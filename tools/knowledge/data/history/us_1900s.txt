what were the roaring twenties | why were the 1920s called the roaring twenties => The Roaring Twenties were the 1920s in the US, a time of fast economic growth, jazz music, new cars, radios and movies, and changing styles like flappers.
what was a flapper | what were flappers => Flappers were young women in the 1920s who wore shorter dresses and bobbed hair, danced to jazz and broke old rules about how women should act.
what was prohibition like | what was a speakeasy | who were bootleggers => During Prohibition (1920 to 1933) alcohol was illegal, so people drank at secret bars called speakeasies and gangsters made fortunes smuggling it, called bootlegging.
who was al capone | what is al capone famous for => Al Capone was a Chicago gangster who got rich from illegal alcohol during Prohibition. He was finally jailed in 1931 for not paying his taxes.
what was the jazz age | where did jazz music come from => Jazz began among Black musicians in New Orleans in the early 1900s. The 1920s are called the Jazz Age because the music became hugely popular.
who was louis armstrong | what is louis armstrong famous for => Louis Armstrong was a jazz trumpeter and singer from New Orleans, one of the most important musicians of the 1900s. He is known for songs like "What a Wonderful World."
what was the great depression | when was the great depression | how long did the great depression last => The Great Depression was the worst economic crash in modern history, starting in 1929 and lasting through the 1930s. At its worst, about a quarter of American workers had no job.
what caused the great depression | why did the stock market crash in 1929 => Causes included a stock market bubble where people bought stocks with borrowed money, bank failures, too much debt and falling trade. When stock prices crashed in October 1929, the economy spiraled down.
what was black tuesday | what happened on october 29 1929 => Black Tuesday, October 29, 1929, was the worst day of the stock market crash. Panicked investors sold about 16 million shares and prices collapsed.
what were hoovervilles | explain hoovervilles => Hoovervilles were shantytowns of shacks built by homeless people during the Great Depression, named mockingly after President Herbert Hoover.
what was the dust bowl | what caused the dust bowl | when was the dust bowl => The Dust Bowl was a disaster in the 1930s when drought and over-plowing let winds blow away topsoil on the Great Plains, creating huge dust storms. Many families moved west to California.
what was black sunday dust storm | explain black sunday dust storm => On April 14, 1935, called Black Sunday, a giant dust storm swept the Great Plains and turned day into night. A reporter's story about it gave the Dust Bowl its name.
what is social security | when did social security start => Social Security is a government program that pays money to retired people, people with disabilities and some families. It was created in 1935 as part of the New Deal.
what was the ccc | what was the civilian conservation corps => The Civilian Conservation Corps (1933 to 1942) was a New Deal program that gave young men jobs planting trees, building trails and working in parks. About 3 million men served.
what was the wpa | what was the works progress administration => The Works Progress Administration was a New Deal program started in 1935 that hired millions of people to build roads, bridges, schools and parks, and even paid artists and writers.
when did the great depression end | what ended the great depression => The Great Depression ended around 1939 to 1941, when huge spending on World War 2 created millions of jobs.
what was the gi bill | explain the gi bill => The GI Bill of 1944 helped World War 2 veterans pay for college and buy homes. Millions went to college because of it.
what was the baby boom | who are baby boomers => The baby boom was a big jump in births in the US from 1946 to 1964, after World War 2. People born then are called baby boomers.
what was life like in the 1950s | what happened in the 1950s => The 1950s in the US brought a growing economy, new suburbs, television in most homes, rock and roll music, and the early civil rights movement, all during the Cold War.
what was mccarthyism | what was the red scare => The Red Scare was a fear of communism in the US. In the early 1950s Senator Joseph McCarthy accused many people of being communists without real evidence, ruining careers. This is called McCarthyism.
what was the korean war | when was the korean war | who fought in the korean war => The Korean War (1950 to 1953) began when communist North Korea invaded South Korea. The US and UN helped the South, and China helped the North. It ended in an armistice with Korea still divided.
what was the vietnam war | when was the vietnam war | why did the us fight in vietnam => The Vietnam War was fought between communist North Vietnam and South Vietnam, which the US supported. US combat troops fought there from 1965 to 1973 to stop communism from spreading.
how did the vietnam war end | who won the vietnam war => North Vietnam won. US troops left in 1973, and in April 1975 North Vietnamese forces captured Saigon, uniting Vietnam under communist rule.
how many americans died in vietnam | vietnam war deaths => About 58,000 Americans died in the Vietnam War. Vietnamese deaths were far higher, estimated at more than a million soldiers and civilians.
why did people protest the vietnam war | vietnam war protests => Many Americans thought the war was wrong or unwinnable, opposed the draft and were upset by TV images of the violence. Huge protests happened, especially on college campuses.
what was kent state | explain kent state => On May 4, 1970, Ohio National Guard soldiers fired on students protesting the Vietnam War at Kent State University, killing four students.
what is the vietnam veterans memorial | what is the vietnam wall => The Vietnam Veterans Memorial in Washington, D.C., is a black granite wall with the names of over 58,000 Americans who died in the war. It was designed by Maya Lin, a 21 year old student, and opened in 1982.
what was the draft | what does being drafted mean => The draft is when the government requires people to serve in the military. The US used a draft in the Civil War, both world wars, Korea and Vietnam, but has used only volunteers since 1973.
what was woodstock | explain woodstock => Woodstock was a huge music festival in New York State in August 1969, with about 400,000 people and stars like Jimi Hendrix. It became a symbol of 1960s youth culture.
what were the 1960s known for | what happened in the 60s => The 1960s brought the civil rights movement, the Vietnam War and protests, the Moon landing, the Beatles, and the assassinations of JFK, Malcolm X, Martin Luther King Jr. and Robert Kennedy.
who was robert kennedy | what happened to bobby kennedy => Robert F. Kennedy was JFK's brother, US Attorney General and later a senator from New York. He was assassinated in Los Angeles in June 1968 while running for president.
what was the iran hostage crisis | explain the iran hostage crisis => In November 1979 Iranian students seized the US embassy in Tehran and held 52 Americans hostage for 444 days. They were released on January 20, 1981, minutes after Reagan took office.
what was the energy crisis in the 1970s | why were there gas lines in the 70s => In 1973 and 1979, oil shortages made gas prices jump, and Americans waited in long lines at gas stations. This pushed the US to save energy and build smaller cars.
what happened in the 1980s | what were the 80s known for => The 1980s brought the Reagan presidency, the end of the Cold War, personal computers, video games, MTV and the Space Shuttle program.
what was the challenger disaster | when did the challenger explode => On January 28, 1986, the Space Shuttle Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch, killing all seven crew, including teacher Christa McAuliffe. A rubber O-ring seal had failed in the cold.
what was the columbia disaster | what happened to space shuttle columbia => On February 1, 2003, Space Shuttle Columbia broke apart while returning to Earth, killing all seven astronauts. Foam had damaged its wing during launch.
what was the gulf war | when was the gulf war | operation desert storm => The Gulf War in 1991 was fought by a US-led coalition of about 35 countries to push Iraq's army out of Kuwait, which Iraq had invaded in 1990. The ground war lasted only about 100 hours.
what happened in the 1990s | what were the 90s known for => The 1990s brought the end of the Soviet Union, the Gulf War, a strong US economy, the rise of the internet and the World Wide Web, and cell phones becoming common.
what was y2k | what was the y2k bug => Y2K was a worry that computers would fail on January 1, 2000, because many stored years with only two digits, so 2000 would look like 1900. Huge efforts to fix the code meant few problems happened.
what was the oklahoma city bombing | explain the oklahoma city bombing => On April 19, 1995, a truck bomb set off by an American extremist destroyed a federal building in Oklahoma City, killing 168 people, including 19 children. It was the deadliest act of homegrown terrorism in US history.
what happened on 9/11 | what was september 11 | what happened on september 11 2001 => On September 11, 2001, terrorists from the group al-Qaeda took over four passenger planes. Two hit the World Trade Center towers in New York, one hit the Pentagon, and one crashed in a Pennsylvania field after passengers fought back.
how many people died on 9/11 | 9/11 death toll => Nearly 3,000 people died in the September 11 attacks, including more than 400 firefighters, police officers and other first responders who rushed in to help.
what was flight 93 | explain flight 93 => United Flight 93 was the fourth plane taken over on September 11, 2001. Passengers learned of the other attacks and fought the hijackers, and the plane crashed near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, instead of reaching Washington.
what is the 9/11 memorial | what replaced the twin towers => The National September 11 Memorial in New York has two huge square pools where the Twin Towers stood, with all the victims' names around them. One World Trade Center, opened in 2014, is the tallest building in the US.
why did the us go to war in afghanistan | how long was the war in afghanistan => After 9/11, the US invaded Afghanistan in October 2001 to fight al-Qaeda and the Taliban government that sheltered it. It became the longest US war, ending when US troops left in August 2021.
what was the iraq war | when was the iraq war => The Iraq War began in March 2003 when a US-led coalition invaded Iraq and removed Saddam Hussein. The US said Iraq had dangerous weapons, but none were found. US combat troops left in 2011.
who was osama bin laden | what happened to osama bin laden => Osama bin Laden led al-Qaeda, the terrorist group behind the 9/11 attacks. US Navy SEALs found and killed him in Pakistan on May 2, 2011.
what was hurricane katrina | when was hurricane katrina => Hurricane Katrina hit the Gulf Coast in August 2005. Levees failed in New Orleans and about 80 percent of the city flooded. Around 1,800 people died, and the slow response was widely criticized.
what was the great recession | what happened in 2008 => The Great Recession was a severe economic downturn from 2007 to 2009, triggered by risky home loans and a banking crisis. Millions of Americans lost jobs and homes.

The World Health Organization declared it a pandemic in March 2020, and schools and businesses around the world shut down.
how many people died from covid in the us | covid deaths in america => The CDC counted more than 1.1 million deaths from COVID-19 in the United States by 2023, the deadliest disease outbreak in US history.
