# tag: history.us.constitution
what is the constitution | what is the us constitution | what does the constitution do => The US Constitution is the supreme law of the United States. It sets up the three branches of government, limits their power and protects people's rights.
when was the constitution written | when was the constitution signed | how old is the constitution => The Constitution was written at the Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia in the summer of 1787 and signed on September 17, 1787. It took effect in 1789.
what was the constitutional convention | who went to the constitutional convention => The Constitutional Convention met in Philadelphia from May to September 1787. 55 delegates from 12 states attended, with George Washington presiding. Rhode Island refused to send anyone.
who wrote the constitution | who is the father of the constitution => Many delegates shaped it, but James Madison is called the Father of the Constitution for his planning and notes. Gouverneur Morris wrote the final wording, including the Preamble.
how many people signed the constitution | signers of the constitution => 39 delegates signed the Constitution on September 17, 1787, including George Washington, Benjamin Franklin, James Madison and Alexander Hamilton.
what is the preamble | what does the preamble say | what does we the people mean => The Preamble is the opening of the Constitution. It begins "We the People of the United States" and lists its goals, like forming a more perfect union, justice, peace, defense, welfare and liberty.
how many articles are in the constitution | what are the seven articles => The Constitution has 7 articles. Articles I, II and III set up Congress, the president and the courts, and the others cover the states, amendments, federal supremacy and ratification.
what does article 1 of the constitution do | what is article one about => Article I creates Congress, the legislative branch, with a House of Representatives and a Senate, and lists its powers, like making laws, taxing and declaring war.
what does article 2 of the constitution do | what is article two about => Article II creates the executive branch, led by the president. It explains how the president is chosen and their powers, like commanding the military and making treaties.
what does article 3 of the constitution do | what is article three about => Article III creates the judicial branch, with the Supreme Court and lower courts that Congress creates. It also defines treason.
what was the great compromise | what was the connecticut compromise => The Great Compromise of 1787 settled how states get seats in Congress: the House is based on population, and in the Senate every state gets two senators.
what was the virginia plan | what was the new jersey plan => The Virginia Plan wanted seats in Congress based on population, which helped big states. The New Jersey Plan wanted equal seats for every state. The Great Compromise mixed them.
what was the three fifths compromise | why was the three fifths compromise bad => The Three-Fifths Compromise counted three out of every five enslaved people when deciding a state's population for seats in the House. It treated enslaved people as less than whole persons and gave slave states more power.
who were the federalists and anti federalists | federalists vs anti federalists => Federalists wanted to approve the new Constitution and a strong national government. Anti-Federalists feared it gave too much power to the government and wanted a bill of rights.
what are the federalist papers | who wrote the federalist papers => The Federalist Papers are 85 essays written in 1787 and 1788 by Alexander Hamilton, James Madison and John Jay under the name "Publius" to convince New Yorkers to approve the Constitution.
how many states had to ratify the constitution | when was the constitution ratified | what does ratify mean => Ratify means officially approve. Nine of the 13 states had to ratify the Constitution, and New Hampshire became the ninth on June 21, 1788.
which state ratified the constitution first | why is delaware the first state => Delaware was the first state to ratify the Constitution, on December 7, 1787, so it is called the First State.
which state was last to ratify the constitution | last state to approve the constitution => Rhode Island was the last of the original 13 states to ratify the Constitution, on May 29, 1790.
what is an amendment | how many amendments are there | how many amendments does the constitution have => An amendment is a change or addition to the Constitution. There are 27 amendments, and the last was ratified in 1992.
how do you amend the constitution | how is an amendment made | how hard is it to change the constitution => Usually two-thirds of both the House and the Senate must propose an amendment, and then three-fourths of the states, 38 of 50, must ratify it. It is meant to be hard.
what is the bill of rights | what are the first ten amendments | why was the bill of rights added => The Bill of Rights is the first 10 amendments to the Constitution, ratified in 1791. They protect freedoms like speech, religion and a fair trial, and were added because many people demanded them.
who wrote the bill of rights | when was the bill of rights ratified => James Madison wrote the Bill of Rights, and it was ratified on December 15, 1791.
what does the first amendment say | what is the 1st amendment | what rights are in the first amendment => The 1st Amendment protects freedom of religion, speech and the press, the right to gather peacefully, and the right to petition the government.
does freedom of speech mean you can say anything | what does freedom of speech protect => Freedom of speech stops the government from punishing you for your views. It doesn't cover everything, like true threats or lies that hurt someone, and private places like schools or websites can have their own rules.
what is the 2nd amendment | what does the second amendment say => The 2nd Amendment protects the right of the people to keep and bear arms, and mentions a well-regulated militia. People debate how far that right goes.
what is the 3rd amendment | what does the third amendment say => The 3rd Amendment says the government can't make people let soldiers live in their homes in peacetime without the owner's permission.
what is the 4th amendment | what does the fourth amendment say | can police search your house => The 4th Amendment protects people from unreasonable searches and seizures. Usually police need a warrant, approved by a judge, to search your home.
what is the 5th amendment | what does pleading the fifth mean | what does the fifth amendment say => The 5th Amendment says you don't have to testify against yourself, which is called "pleading the Fifth." It also bans being tried twice for the same crime and requires due process of law.
what is the 6th amendment | what does the sixth amendment say => The 6th Amendment gives people accused of crimes the right to a speedy, public trial by jury, a lawyer, and the right to know the charges and question witnesses.
what is the 7th amendment | what does the seventh amendment say => The 7th Amendment gives the right to a jury trial in many civil cases, which are lawsuits between people, not crimes.
what is the 8th amendment | what does the eighth amendment say => The 8th Amendment bans excessive bail, excessive fines and cruel and unusual punishment.
what is the 9th amendment | what does the ninth amendment say => The 9th Amendment says that just because a right is not listed in the Constitution doesn't mean people don't have it.
what is the 10th amendment | what does the tenth amendment say => The 10th Amendment says powers not given to the federal government, and not banned for the states, belong to the states or the people.
what is the 11th amendment | what does the eleventh amendment say => The 11th Amendment, ratified in 1795, limits people from suing a state in federal court, especially people from another state or country.
what is the 12th amendment | what does the twelfth amendment say => The 12th Amendment, ratified in 1804, makes electors cast separate votes for president and vice president, after the 1800 tie between Jefferson and Burr.
what is the 13th amendment | what amendment ended slavery | when was slavery abolished => The 13th Amendment, ratified in December 1865, abolished slavery in the United States, except as punishment for a crime.
what is the 14th amendment | what does the fourteenth amendment say => The 14th Amendment, ratified in 1868, made everyone born in the US a citizen, including formerly enslaved people, and promised all people equal protection of the laws.
what is the 15th amendment | what does the fifteenth amendment say => The 15th Amendment, ratified in 1870, said the right to vote can't be denied because of race or color. Many Southern states still blocked Black voters for decades with unfair rules.
what is the 16th amendment | when did income tax start => The 16th Amendment, ratified in 1913, lets Congress collect a federal income tax.
what is the 17th amendment | how are senators elected => The 17th Amendment, ratified in 1913, lets voters elect their senators directly. Before that, state legislatures chose them.
what is the 18th amendment | what was prohibition | when did prohibition start => The 18th Amendment, ratified in 1919, started Prohibition, a nationwide ban on making and selling alcohol, which took effect in 1920.
what is the 19th amendment | when did women get the right to vote | when could women vote => The 19th Amendment, ratified on August 18, 1920, gave women the right to vote nationwide.
what is the 20th amendment | what does the twentieth amendment say => The 20th Amendment, ratified in 1933, moved the start of the president's term to January 20 and Congress's to January 3, shortening the wait after elections.
what is the 21st amendment | when did prohibition end | which amendment ended prohibition => The 21st Amendment, ratified in December 1933, ended Prohibition. It is the only amendment that cancels another one.
what is the 23rd amendment | can washington dc vote for president => The 23rd Amendment, ratified in 1961, gives Washington, D.C. electoral votes in presidential elections.
what is the 24th amendment | what is a poll tax => The 24th Amendment, ratified in 1964, banned poll taxes in federal elections. A poll tax was a fee to vote, used to keep poor and Black citizens from voting.
what is the 25th amendment | what happens if the president is sick => The 25th Amendment, ratified in 1967, explains who takes over if the president dies, resigns or can't do the job, and how to fill an empty vice presidency.
what is the 26th amendment | when did the voting age become 18 | why can 18 year olds vote => The 26th Amendment, ratified in 1971, lowered the voting age from 21 to 18. People argued that if 18 year olds could be drafted to fight in Vietnam, they should be able to vote.
what is the 27th amendment | what was the last amendment | newest amendment => The 27th Amendment, ratified in 1992, says a pay raise Congress votes itself can't start until after the next election. It was first proposed in 1789, so it took over 200 years.
which amendment took the longest to pass | longest time to ratify an amendment => The 27th Amendment took the longest. It was proposed in 1789 and finally ratified in 1992, more than 202 years later.
what was the equal rights amendment | did the era pass => The Equal Rights Amendment would guarantee equal rights regardless of sex. Congress passed it in 1972, but not enough states ratified it before the deadline, so it is not in the Constitution.
what is the supremacy clause | are federal laws above state laws => The Supremacy Clause, in Article VI, says the Constitution and federal laws are the highest law of the land, above state laws that conflict with them.
what is due process | what does due process mean => Due process means the government has to follow fair, legal steps before taking away someone's life, freedom or property. It is in the 5th and 14th Amendments.
what is equal protection | what does equal protection mean => Equal protection, from the 14th Amendment, means states must treat people the same under the law. It was used in cases like Brown v. Board of Education to strike down segregation.
what is a natural born citizen | what does natural born citizen mean => A natural-born citizen is generally someone who is a US citizen from birth. The Constitution requires the president to be one.
what is the elastic clause | what is the necessary and proper clause => The Necessary and Proper Clause, nicknamed the elastic clause, lets Congress make laws needed to carry out its listed powers, so its power can stretch to new situations.
what are enumerated powers | what is an expressed power => Enumerated, or expressed, powers are the powers the Constitution lists for Congress, like taxing, printing money, regulating trade between states and declaring war.
where is the constitution kept | can you see the real constitution => The original Constitution is on display at the National Archives in Washington, D.C., in a special case filled with argon gas to protect it.
what is constitution day | when is constitution day => Constitution Day is September 17, the day the Constitution was signed in 1787. Schools often learn about it that day.
how long is the constitution | how many words is the constitution => The original Constitution is about 4,400 words, making it one of the shortest national constitutions. With all 27 amendments it's about 7,600 words.
what is the oldest constitution | is the us constitution the oldest => The US Constitution is the oldest written national constitution still in use, in effect since 1789.
what is federalism | what does federalism mean => Federalism means power is shared between a national government and state governments. In the US, both levels make laws and collect taxes.
what is separation of powers | why is power split into three branches => Separation of powers means the government is split into three branches, legislative, executive and judicial, so no single group has all the power.
what are checks and balances | how do checks and balances work | example of checks and balances => Checks and balances let each branch limit the others. The president can veto bills, Congress can override vetoes and impeach, and courts can strike down unconstitutional laws.
what is popular sovereignty | what does popular sovereignty mean => Popular sovereignty means the power of government comes from the people. Before the Civil War, the phrase also meant letting settlers in a territory vote on slavery.
what is limited government | what does limited government mean => Limited government means the government has only the powers the people give it in the constitution, and it has to follow the law like everyone else.
what is the rule of law | what does rule of law mean => The rule of law means everyone, including leaders, must follow the same laws, and laws are applied fairly by courts.
what is judicial review | what is marbury v madison => Judicial review is the power of courts to strike down laws that break the Constitution. The Supreme Court claimed this power in Marbury v. Madison in 1803.
