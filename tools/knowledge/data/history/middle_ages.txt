# tag: history.medieval
what were the middle ages | when were the middle ages => The Middle Ages, or medieval period, is the time in Europe from the fall of the Western Roman Empire around AD 476 to about 1500. Medieval comes from Latin for "middle age."
what were the dark ages | why are they called the dark ages => The "Dark Ages" is an old name for the early Middle Ages, about 500 to 1000, because fewer written records survive. Historians now avoid it, since there was plenty of learning and art.
what was feudalism | how did feudalism work => Feudalism was a system in medieval Europe where kings gave land to lords in return for loyalty and soldiers, and peasants farmed the land for lords in return for protection.
what was a serf | what is a peasant | what was life like for peasants => Most medieval people were peasants who farmed. Serfs were peasants tied to a lord's land who couldn't leave without permission and had to work the lord's fields.
what was a knight | how did you become a knight => Knights were mounted warriors in armor who served a lord. A boy might become a page at about 7, a squire at about 14, and be knighted around 21.
what was chivalry | what was the knights code => Chivalry was the ideal code for knights, which said they should be brave, loyal, generous and protect the weak. Real knights didn't always live up to it.
how heavy was a suit of armor | could knights move in armor => A full suit of plate armor weighed about 15 to 25 kilograms (33 to 55 pounds), spread over the body. Trained knights could run, climb and get on a horse in it.
what was a castle for | why were castles built | when were castles built => Castles were fortified homes for lords and kings, built mostly from about 1000 to 1500. They protected owners from attack and controlled the land around them.
what is a moat | what is a drawbridge => A moat is a deep ditch, often filled with water, around a castle. A drawbridge could be raised to block the entrance.
what is a motte and bailey castle | explain a motte and bailey castle => A motte and bailey was an early castle with a wooden tower on a mound of earth, the motte, and a fenced yard, the bailey. The Normans built many in England after 1066.
what is a trebuchet | how does a trebuchet work => A trebuchet is a big siege weapon that uses a heavy counterweight to swing a long arm and throw stones at castle walls. It was used in the Middle Ages.
who was charlemagne | what did charlemagne do => Charlemagne was king of the Franks who united much of western Europe. He was crowned emperor by the pope on Christmas Day, AD 800, and encouraged learning and schools.
what was the holy roman empire | explain the holy roman empire => The Holy Roman Empire was a group of territories in central Europe, mostly Germany, ruled by an elected emperor, from 962 to 1806. Voltaire joked it was neither holy, nor Roman, nor an empire.
who were the anglo saxons | tell me about the anglo saxons => The Anglo-Saxons were Germanic peoples who settled in England from the 400s and ruled it until 1066. English grew from their language, Old English.
who was alfred the great | what is alfred the great famous for => Alfred the Great was king of Wessex from 871 to 899. He defended England against the Vikings and encouraged education and laws.
when did the vikings live | when was the viking age => The Viking Age lasted from about AD 793, when Vikings raided the monastery at Lindisfarne in England, to about 1066.
did vikings wear horned helmets | viking helmet horns myth => No. There's no evidence Vikings wore horned helmets in battle. The idea came from costumes for operas and art in the 1800s.
who was leif erikson | did vikings discover america => Leif Erikson was a Norse explorer who reached North America around AD 1000, about 500 years before Columbus. A Viking site was found at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland, Canada.
what were viking longships | explain viking longships => Longships were long, narrow wooden Viking boats with a sail and oars. They were fast and could sail in shallow rivers, letting Vikings raid far inland.
who was erik the red | what is erik the red famous for => Erik the Red was a Norse explorer who founded the first Norse settlement in Greenland around AD 985. He was Leif Erikson's father.
what was the battle of hastings | when was the battle of hastings | 1066 => At the Battle of Hastings on October 14, 1066, William of Normandy defeated King Harold II of England. William became king and was called William the Conqueror.
who was william the conqueror | what is william the conqueror famous for => William the Conqueror was a duke of Normandy who won the Battle of Hastings in 1066 and became the first Norman king of England.
what is the bayeux tapestry | explain the bayeux tapestry => The Bayeux Tapestry is an embroidered cloth about 70 meters long showing the story of the Norman conquest of England in 1066. It was made in the 1070s.
what is the domesday book | explain the domesday book => The Domesday Book was a huge survey of land and property in England ordered by William the Conqueror and finished in 1086, so he knew what everyone owned and could tax it.
what were the crusades | why did the crusades happen | when were the crusades => The Crusades were religious wars from 1095 to 1291 in which European Christians tried to capture Jerusalem and the Holy Land from Muslim rulers. The First Crusade took Jerusalem in 1099.
who won the crusades | how did the crusades end => In the end Muslim rulers kept the Holy Land. Crusaders held Jerusalem from 1099 to 1187, when Saladin retook it, and the last crusader city, Acre, fell in 1291.
who was saladin | what is saladin famous for => Saladin was a Muslim sultan of Egypt and Syria who recaptured Jerusalem from the crusaders in 1187. Even his enemies respected him for his fairness.
who was richard the lionheart | what is richard the lionheart famous for => Richard I, called the Lionheart, was king of England from 1189 to 1199. He led the Third Crusade against Saladin but couldn't take Jerusalem.
what was the childrens crusade | explain the childrens crusade => The Children's Crusade of 1212 was a movement of young people and poor peasants who set out to free Jerusalem. Most never reached it, and many were sold into slavery or died.
who were the knights templar | tell me about the knights templar => The Knights Templar were a group of warrior monks founded around 1119 to protect pilgrims to the Holy Land. They became rich and powerful, and the French king had them arrested in 1307.
what was the black death | when was the black death | how many people died in the black death => The Black Death was a plague pandemic that hit Europe from 1347 to 1351. It killed about a third to half of Europe's people, perhaps 25 to 50 million, and many more in Asia and Africa.
what caused the black death | how did the plague spread => The Black Death was caused by a bacterium, Yersinia pestis, spread mainly by fleas on rats and from person to person. People back then didn't know about germs.
what did plague doctors wear | why did plague doctors have beaks => The beaked mask was used by some doctors in the 1600s, not during the Black Death. The beak was stuffed with herbs because people thought bad smells spread disease.
what was the hundred years war | how long was the hundred years war => The Hundred Years' War was a series of wars between England and France from 1337 to 1453, about 116 years, over who should rule France. France won in the end.
who was joan of arc | what did joan of arc do => Joan of Arc was a French peasant girl who said saints told her to help France. At 17 she helped lift the siege of Orléans in 1429. She was captured, burned at the stake in 1431, and later made a saint.
what was the battle of agincourt | explain the battle of agincourt => At Agincourt in 1415, a small, tired English army under Henry V beat a much larger French army, largely thanks to English longbow archers and muddy ground.
what was the magna carta signed by king john | explain the magna carta signed by king john => King John of England was forced by his barons to agree to the Magna Carta in 1215 at Runnymede. It said even the king had to follow the law.
who was robin hood | was robin hood real => Robin Hood is a legendary outlaw who robbed the rich to give to the poor in Sherwood Forest. Stories about him go back to the 1300s, but there's no proof he was a real person.
who was king arthur | was king arthur real => King Arthur is a legendary British king with the Knights of the Round Table and the sword Excalibur. Historians debate whether a real leader inspired the stories, but there's no solid evidence.
what was a guild | what were medieval guilds => Guilds were groups of craftspeople or merchants in a town, like bakers or blacksmiths, who set rules, prices and training. Young people learned a trade as apprentices.
what was an apprentice | what is a journeyman => An apprentice was a young person who worked for a master craftsman for years to learn a trade. Next they became a journeyman, paid by the day, and could become a master.
what was a monastery | what did monks do => A monastery is a place where monks live, pray and work. In the Middle Ages monks copied books by hand, ran schools and hospitals and kept learning alive.
what is an illuminated manuscript | explain an illuminated manuscript => An illuminated manuscript is a handwritten book decorated with bright colors, gold and pictures. Monks made many in the Middle Ages.
what is the book of kells | explain the book of kells => The Book of Kells is a richly illustrated copy of the four Christian Gospels made by monks around AD 800, probably in Scotland or Ireland. It is kept at Trinity College Dublin.
what was the church like in the middle ages | how powerful was the church in medieval times => The Catholic Church was the most powerful institution in medieval western Europe. It owned huge amounts of land, collected taxes called tithes, and its leader, the pope, could challenge kings.
what are gothic cathedrals | what is a flying buttress => Gothic cathedrals were built in Europe from the 1100s, with tall pointed arches, huge stained glass windows and flying buttresses, outside supports that let walls be thinner and taller.
when was notre dame built | what happened to notre dame => Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris was built mostly from 1163 to the 1260s. A fire in April 2019 destroyed its roof and spire. It reopened in December 2024 after rebuilding.
when were the first universities | oldest university in the world => The University of Bologna in Italy, founded in 1088, is often called the oldest university in continuous operation in Europe. The University of al-Qarawiyyin in Morocco, founded in 859, is older.
what was the islamic golden age | explain the islamic golden age => The Islamic Golden Age, from about the 700s to the 1200s, was a time when scholars in cities like Baghdad, Cairo and Córdoba made big advances in math, medicine, astronomy and philosophy.
what was the house of wisdom | explain the house of wisdom => The House of Wisdom was a center of learning in Baghdad in the 800s where scholars translated Greek, Persian and Indian works into Arabic and did new research.
who was al khwarizmi | where does the word algebra come from => Al-Khwarizmi was a Persian mathematician in Baghdad in the 800s. His book gave us the word algebra, and the word algorithm comes from his name.
who was ibn sina | who was avicenna => Ibn Sina, called Avicenna in Europe, was a Persian doctor and philosopher who died in 1037. His Canon of Medicine was used as a medical textbook in Europe for centuries.
who was ibn battuta | what is ibn battuta famous for => Ibn Battuta was a Moroccan traveler who from 1325 to 1354 journeyed about 120,000 kilometers across Africa, the Middle East, Asia and Europe, and wrote about it.
who was ibn al haytham | what is ibn al haytham famous for => Ibn al-Haytham was an Arab scientist in Cairo around AD 1000 who studied light and vision with careful experiments. He is often called the father of optics.
what was al andalus | when did muslims rule spain => Al-Andalus was the part of Spain and Portugal ruled by Muslims from 711. Its last kingdom, Granada, fell to Christian Spain in 1492.
what is the alhambra | explain the alhambra => The Alhambra is a palace and fortress in Granada, Spain, built mainly in the 1200s and 1300s by Muslim rulers, famous for its detailed tiles, carvings and gardens.
what was the reconquista | explain the reconquista => The Reconquista was the long series of wars, over about 780 years, in which Christian kingdoms took back the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rulers, ending with Granada in 1492.
who were the ottomans | what was the ottoman empire => The Ottoman Empire was founded around 1299 in what is now Turkey. It became a huge empire across southeast Europe, the Middle East and North Africa and lasted until 1922.
when did constantinople fall | who captured constantinople => Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II captured Constantinople on May 29, 1453, ending the Byzantine Empire. The city became the Ottoman capital and is now Istanbul.
who was suleiman the magnificent | what is suleiman the magnificent famous for => Suleiman the Magnificent was Ottoman sultan from 1520 to 1566, when the empire was at its peak. He expanded it, reformed its laws and supported art and architecture.
what was medieval japan like | who were the samurai => Samurai were Japanese warriors who served lords called daimyo, from about the 1100s to the 1800s. They followed a code of honor later called bushido.
what was a shogun | explain a shogun => A shogun was the top military ruler of Japan. From 1192 to 1867 shoguns usually held the real power, while the emperor was mostly a symbol.
who were ninjas | were ninjas real => Ninjas, or shinobi, were real spies and secret agents in feudal Japan, especially in the 1400s to 1600s. The black outfits and magic powers come mostly from later stories and movies.
what was the tokugawa shogunate | what was the edo period => The Tokugawa shogunate ruled Japan from 1603 to 1868, a long peaceful time called the Edo period. Japan cut off most contact with the outside world.
when did japan open to the world | who was commodore perry => In 1853 and 1854 US Commodore Matthew Perry sailed warships to Japan and forced it to open trade, ending over 200 years of near isolation.
what was the meiji restoration | explain the meiji restoration => The Meiji Restoration in 1868 returned power to Japan's emperor and began fast modernization, with railways, factories and a modern army.
what was medieval food like | what did people eat in medieval times => Medieval peasants ate mostly bread, pottage (a thick soup of grains and vegetables), beans and some cheese, and drank ale. Rich people ate more meat and used costly spices.
did people in medieval times take baths | were medieval people dirty => Medieval people did wash. Many towns had public bathhouses, and people washed with water and soap at home, though full baths were less common than today.
what was a jester | what did a jester do => A jester was an entertainer at a royal or noble court who told jokes, sang, juggled and did tricks. Some were allowed to joke about the king in ways others couldn't.
what was the printing press used for first | what was the gutenberg bible => Johannes Gutenberg's first major printed book was the Gutenberg Bible, finished around 1455 in Mainz, Germany. About 49 copies still exist.
what were the wars of the roses | explain the wars of the roses => The Wars of the Roses (1455 to 1487) were civil wars for the English throne between two families, York and Lancaster. Henry Tudor won and became Henry VII.
what was the great schism | explain the great schism => The Great Schism of 1054 split Christianity into the Roman Catholic Church in the west and the Eastern Orthodox Church in the east.
