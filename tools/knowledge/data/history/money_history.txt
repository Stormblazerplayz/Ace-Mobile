# tag: history.money
what was bartering | what is barter => Bartering is trading goods or services directly without money, like swapping eggs for a pair of shoes. It was common before money and is still used sometimes.
why is it called a dollar | where does the word dollar come from => Dollar comes from the "thaler," a large silver coin first made in Joachimsthal, in what is now the Czech Republic, in the 1500s. Spanish silver dollars were widely used in early America.
when did the us start making its own money | when was the us dollar created => The Coinage Act of 1792 created the US dollar and the US Mint in Philadelphia. The first US coins were made in 1792 and 1793.
what was continental currency | what does not worth a continental mean => During the American Revolution, Congress printed paper money called Continentals. So many were printed that they lost almost all their value, giving the saying "not worth a Continental."
when did the us start printing paper money | first us paper dollars => The US government first issued national paper money, called "greenbacks," in 1861 and 1862 to pay for the Civil War.
who is on the one dollar bill | whose face is on the 1 dollar bill => George Washington, the first president, is on the $1 bill.
who is on the five dollar bill | whose face is on the 5 dollar bill => Abraham Lincoln is on the $5 bill, and the Lincoln Memorial is on the back.
who is on the ten dollar bill | whose face is on the 10 dollar bill => Alexander Hamilton, the first Secretary of the Treasury, is on the $10 bill. He's one of two non-presidents on US paper money in use today.
who is on the twenty dollar bill | whose face is on the 20 dollar bill => Andrew Jackson, the seventh president, is on the $20 bill.
who is on the fifty dollar bill | whose face is on the 50 dollar bill => Ulysses S. Grant, Civil War general and 18th president, is on the $50 bill.
who is on the hundred dollar bill | whose face is on the 100 dollar bill => Benjamin Franklin, a Founding Father who was never president, is on the $100 bill.
who is on the two dollar bill | whose face is on the 2 dollar bill => Thomas Jefferson is on the $2 bill. The back shows the signing of the Declaration of Independence. It's still made but rarely used.
who is on the penny | whose face is on a penny => Abraham Lincoln has been on the penny since 1909, the 100th anniversary of his birth. In 2025 the US Mint was told to stop making new pennies.
who is on the nickel | whose face is on a nickel => Thomas Jefferson is on the nickel, with his home, Monticello, on the back.
who is on the dime | whose face is on a dime => Franklin D. Roosevelt has been on the dime since 1946. He helped found the March of Dimes to fight polio.
who is on the quarter | whose face is on a quarter => George Washington is on the quarter. Since 1999, special designs on the back have honored states, parks and famous women.
what was the largest us bill ever | was there a 100000 dollar bill => The largest US bill ever printed was the $100,000 gold certificate with Woodrow Wilson, made in 1934 and used only between Federal Reserve banks.
what was the gold standard | explain the gold standard => The gold standard meant a country's money could be traded for a set amount of gold. The US mostly left it in 1933 and fully ended the link to gold in 1971.
what was the euro | when did europe start using the euro => The euro is the shared currency of many European Union countries. It started in 1999 for banks and businesses, and euro coins and bills were used from January 1, 2002.
what was the stock market crash | what is a stock market crash => A stock market crash is when stock prices fall very fast. The most famous was in October 1929, which helped start the Great Depression.
what is the oldest stock exchange | explain the oldest stock exchange => The Amsterdam Stock Exchange, started in 1602 to trade shares of the Dutch East India Company, is often called the first modern stock exchange.
what was the first bank | when were banks invented => Early banking began in ancient Mesopotamia, where temples stored grain and made loans. Modern banking grew in Italian cities like Florence and Venice in the 1300s and 1400s.
what were taxes like long ago | did ancient people pay taxes => Taxes are very old. Ancient Egyptians paid taxes in grain and labor, and Romans paid taxes on land and sales. Medieval peasants often paid their lords in crops and work.
what was the whiskey rebellion | explain the whiskey rebellion => The Whiskey Rebellion was a 1794 protest by farmers in western Pennsylvania against a federal tax on whiskey. President Washington led troops to end it, showing the new government could enforce its laws.
what was the silk road trade | what was traded on the silk road => The Silk Road was a network of trade routes linking China, Central Asia, the Middle East and Europe. Traders carried silk, spices, tea, horses, paper and ideas along it.
what was mercantilism | explain mercantilism => Mercantilism was the idea, common from the 1500s to 1700s, that countries grow rich by exporting more than they import and by using colonies for raw materials.
what was the new deal | what did the new deal do for money => The New Deal was Franklin Roosevelt's set of programs in the 1930s to fight the Great Depression. It created jobs, Social Security and the FDIC, which protects bank savings.
what was the first cryptocurrency | when did bitcoin start => Bitcoin, created by an unknown person using the name Satoshi Nakamoto, was launched in January 2009. It was the first cryptocurrency.
