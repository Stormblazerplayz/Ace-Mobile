# tag: history.us.courts
what was mcculloch v maryland | explain mcculloch v maryland => In McCulloch v. Maryland (1819) the Supreme Court said Congress could create a national bank under the Necessary and Proper Clause and that states could not tax it.
what was gibbons v ogden | explain gibbons v ogden => Gibbons v. Ogden (1824) said only Congress, not the states, controls trade between states, in a case about steamboats on New York waters.
what was worcester v georgia | explain worcester v georgia => In Worcester v. Georgia (1832) the Supreme Court ruled that Georgia had no power over Cherokee land. President Jackson ignored the ruling, and the Cherokee were later forced out.
what was korematsu v united states | explain korematsu v united states => In Korematsu v. United States (1944) the Supreme Court allowed the forced removal of Japanese Americans during World War 2. It is now seen as a terrible mistake, and the Court said in 2018 it was wrong.
what was mapp v ohio | explain mapp v ohio => Mapp v. Ohio (1961) ruled that evidence police get through an illegal search can't be used in state courts.
what was gideon v wainwright | explain gideon v wainwright => Gideon v. Wainwright (1963) said states must give a free lawyer to anyone charged with a serious crime who can't afford one. Clarence Gideon wrote his appeal by hand from prison.
what was miranda v arizona | what are miranda rights | you have the right to remain silent => Miranda v. Arizona (1966) said police must tell people under arrest their rights before questioning, like the right to remain silent and to have a lawyer. These are called Miranda rights.
what was tinker v des moines | can students protest in school => Tinker v. Des Moines (1969) said students keep free speech rights at school. Students who wore black armbands to protest the Vietnam War could not be punished, as long as it didn't seriously disrupt school.
what was new york times v united states | what were the pentagon papers => In 1971 the Supreme Court let newspapers publish the Pentagon Papers, a secret study of the Vietnam War, saying the government couldn't stop them in advance.
what was roe v wade | explain roe v wade => Roe v. Wade (1973) was a Supreme Court decision that said the Constitution protected a right to abortion. In 2022 the Court overturned it in Dobbs v. Jackson, letting each state make its own laws.
what was united states v nixon | explain united states v nixon => In United States v. Nixon (1974) the Supreme Court ruled unanimously that President Nixon had to hand over his secret tapes for the Watergate investigation. He resigned weeks later.
what was new jersey v tlo | can schools search your backpack => New Jersey v. T.L.O. (1985) said schools can search students' belongings if they have a reasonable suspicion of breaking a rule or law, a lower bar than police need.
what was texas v johnson | is flag burning legal => Texas v. Johnson (1989) said burning the American flag as a political protest is protected free speech under the 1st Amendment.
what was hazelwood v kuhlmeier | explain hazelwood v kuhlmeier => Hazelwood v. Kuhlmeier (1988) said schools can edit or limit what goes in a school-sponsored student newspaper for good educational reasons.
what was bush v gore | explain bush v gore => Bush v. Gore (2000) was the Supreme Court case that stopped the Florida recount in the presidential election, which meant George W. Bush won.
what was engel v vitale | can schools make students pray => Engel v. Vitale (1962) said public schools can't lead official prayers, because that would be the government supporting a religion.
what is the supreme court | how many justices are on the supreme court => The Supreme Court is the highest court in the US. It has nine justices, one Chief Justice and eight associate justices, appointed for life.
how do you become a supreme court justice | who picks supreme court justices => The president nominates a justice and the Senate votes to confirm them. Justices serve for life unless they retire, resign or are impeached and removed.
who was the first chief justice of the supreme court | longest serving chief justice => John Jay was the first Chief Justice, starting in 1789. John Marshall served the longest, from 1801 to 1835, and made the Court much stronger.
what is a landmark case | what does landmark case mean => A landmark case is a court decision that sets an important new rule or changes how the law is understood, like Brown v. Board of Education.
what does v mean in court cases | what does versus mean in a case name => The "v." stands for versus, Latin for "against." The first name is usually the side that brought the case or appeal, and the second is the side answering it.
how does a case get to the supreme court | does the supreme court hear every case => Most cases reach the Supreme Court by appeal from lower courts. The Court chooses which to hear, taking only about 60 to 80 cases a year out of thousands of requests.
