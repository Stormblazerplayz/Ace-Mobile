# tag: history.pirates
what was the golden age of piracy | when was the golden age of pirates => The Golden Age of Piracy lasted from about 1650 to 1730, when pirates attacked ships in the Caribbean, along the American coast, and in the Indian Ocean.
who was blackbeard | what is blackbeard famous for => Blackbeard, probably named Edward Teach or Thatch, was an English pirate who terrorized the Caribbean and American coast around 1717 and 1718. He was killed in a battle with the Royal Navy off North Carolina in 1718.
what was blackbeards ship | what was the queen annes revenge => Blackbeard's flagship was the Queen Anne's Revenge, a captured French slave ship. It ran aground in North Carolina in 1718, and its wreck was found in 1996.
who was anne bonny | who were anne bonny and mary read => Anne Bonny and Mary Read were two of the few known women pirates. They sailed with Calico Jack Rackham in the Caribbean and were captured in 1720.
who was captain kidd | did captain kidd bury treasure => William Kidd was a Scottish sea captain hired to hunt pirates who was accused of becoming one himself. He was hanged in London in 1701. Legends say he buried treasure, but only a small amount was ever found.
who was henry morgan | who was captain morgan => Henry Morgan was a Welsh privateer who raided Spanish cities in the Caribbean in the 1660s and 1670s, including Panama City. He later became lieutenant governor of Jamaica.
who was ching shih | who was the most successful pirate => Ching Shih, also called Zheng Yi Sao, was a Chinese pirate leader in the early 1800s who commanded tens of thousands of pirates. She negotiated a pardon and retired, which few pirates managed.
who was black bart | who was bartholomew roberts => Bartholomew Roberts, called Black Bart, was a Welsh pirate who captured over 400 ships between 1719 and 1722, more than any other pirate of the Golden Age.
what is a privateer | what is the difference between a pirate and a privateer => A privateer was a sailor with a government license, called a letter of marque, to attack enemy ships during war. Pirates attacked any ship without permission from anyone.
what is the jolly roger | what is a pirate flag => The Jolly Roger is the name for pirate flags, most famously a skull and crossbones on black. Flags warned ships to surrender without a fight.
did pirates really make people walk the plank | is walking the plank real | what is walking the plank => Walking the plank was very rare. Only a few cases are recorded, mostly from after the Golden Age of Piracy. The idea was made famous by stories like Peter Pan.
did pirates bury treasure | did pirates have treasure maps => Pirates almost never buried treasure. They usually spent or split their loot quickly. Buried treasure and treasure maps were made popular by the novel Treasure Island in 1883.
what were the barbary pirates | explain the barbary pirates => The Barbary pirates sailed from North African ports like Algiers and Tripoli, capturing ships and people in the Mediterranean for centuries. The US fought two Barbary Wars against them in the early 1800s.
what was pirate life like | did pirates have rules => Pirate crews often voted for their captain and signed a written code, splitting treasure in shares and giving payment to injured crew. Life at sea was still dangerous, cramped and often hungry.
what did pirates eat | pirate food | what food did pirates have on ships => Pirates ate hardtack, a hard dry biscuit, salted meat, and whatever they captured. Lack of fresh fruit could cause scurvy, a disease from missing vitamin C.
# tag: history.knights-castles
how did someone become a knight | what were the steps to become a knight => A boy training to be a knight usually began as a page at about age 7, became a squire at about 14 and helped a knight, and could be knighted around age 21 in a ceremony called dubbing.
what is a squire | explain a squire => A squire was a teenage knight in training who cared for a knight's armor, weapons and horse and followed the knight into battle.
what was a page in the middle ages | explain a page in the middle ages => A page was a young boy, about 7 to 14, sent to live in a noble's household to learn manners, riding and fighting, the first step toward becoming a knight.
what is jousting | what was a joust => Jousting was a contest where two knights on horses charged at each other with long wooden lances, trying to knock the other off. It was a popular event at medieval tournaments.
what was a tournament in the middle ages | explain a tournament in the middle ages => A medieval tournament was a big festival where knights competed in jousting and mock battles to show their skill and win prizes and fame.
how heavy was a knights armor | how much did knight armor weigh | was knight armor heavy => A full suit of plate armor from the 1400s weighed about 45 to 55 pounds (20 to 25 kilograms). Its weight was spread over the body, so a trained knight could run, climb and even get up off the ground.
what is chain mail | explain chain mail => Chain mail is armor made of thousands of small metal rings linked together like fabric. It was common before full plate armor became popular in the 1300s and 1400s.
what is a portcullis | explain a portcullis => A portcullis is a heavy gate made of wood or iron bars that slides down from above to block a castle's entrance.
what is a castle keep | explain a castle keep => The keep was the strongest tower of a castle, where the lord lived and where defenders made their last stand if the walls fell.
what is a castle moat | why did castles have moats => A moat was a deep ditch around a castle, often filled with water, that made it hard for attackers to reach the walls or dig under them.
what is a battlement | explain a battlement => Battlements are the tops of castle walls with gaps, called crenellations, that let defenders shoot arrows and then hide behind the raised parts.
what is a siege | how did people attack castles => A siege was when an army surrounded a castle to cut off food and supplies until the defenders gave up. Attackers also used catapults, battering rams, ladders and tunnels.
what is a coat of arms in history | what is heraldry => A coat of arms is a design of colors and symbols on a shield that identified a knight or family. Heraldry is the system of designing and recording them.
what was the code of chivalry | explain the code of chivalry => Chivalry was the ideal code knights were supposed to follow, including bravery, loyalty, protecting the weak and courtesy to women. Real knights didn't always live up to it.
what is the biggest castle in the world | explain the biggest castle in the world => Malbork Castle in Poland, built by the Teutonic Knights starting in the 1200s, is the largest castle in the world by land area.
# tag: history.spies-codes
who were famous spies in history | tell me about famous spies in history => Famous spies include Nathan Hale and the Culper Ring in the American Revolution, Harriet Tubman as a Union scout, Mata Hari in World War 1 and Virginia Hall in World War 2.
what was the culper ring | explain the culper ring => The Culper Ring was a secret spy network that George Washington used in New York during the American Revolution. Its members used code numbers and invisible ink.
who was mata hari | what is mata hari famous for => Mata Hari was a Dutch dancer in Paris who was accused of spying for Germany in World War 1. France executed her in 1917, though historians doubt she gave away much.
who was virginia hall | what is virginia hall famous for => Virginia Hall was an American spy in World War 2 who helped the French Resistance against the Nazis, even with an artificial leg. The Germans called her one of the most dangerous Allied spies.
who was james armistead lafayette | what is james armistead lafayette famous for => James Armistead Lafayette was an enslaved Black man who spied on the British for the Americans during the Revolution, helping win at Yorktown in 1781. He was later granted his freedom.
what is invisible ink | explain invisible ink => Invisible ink is writing that can't be seen until it's heated or treated with a chemical. Spies used it for centuries, including during the American Revolution.
what is a cipher | what is a code in spying => A cipher scrambles letters of a message using a rule, such as shifting each letter, so only someone who knows the rule can read it. Codes replace whole words or phrases with other words or numbers.
what is the caesar cipher | explain the caesar cipher => The Caesar cipher shifts every letter of a message a set number of places in the alphabet, like A becoming D. Julius Caesar is said to have used it.
what was the cambridge five | explain the cambridge five => The Cambridge Five were a group of British men, recruited at Cambridge University in the 1930s, who secretly spied for the Soviet Union while working in British government.
what was the u2 incident | explain the u2 incident => In 1960, the Soviet Union shot down an American U-2 spy plane and captured its pilot, Francis Gary Powers. He was traded back to the US in 1962.
# tag: history.famous-animals
who was balto | what is balto famous for => Balto was a sled dog who led the final stretch of a relay that carried medicine to Nome, Alaska, during a diphtheria outbreak in 1925. A statue of him stands in New York's Central Park.
who was togo the sled dog | what is togo the sled dog famous for => Togo was the lead sled dog who ran the longest and most dangerous part of the 1925 medicine relay to Nome, Alaska, about 260 miles. Many mushers consider him the true hero of the run.
who was sergeant stubby | what is sergeant stubby famous for => Sergeant Stubby was a stray dog who served with American soldiers in World War 1. He warned them of gas attacks and found wounded soldiers, becoming the most decorated dog of the war.
who was smokey bear | what is smokey bear famous for => Smokey Bear is the mascot of US wildfire prevention, created in 1944. A real bear cub rescued from a New Mexico wildfire in 1950 became the living Smokey and lived at the National Zoo.
who was seabiscuit | what is seabiscuit famous for => Seabiscuit was a small, underestimated racehorse who became a hero during the Great Depression. In 1938 he beat the Triple Crown winner War Admiral in a famous two-horse race.
who was greyfriars bobby | what is greyfriars bobby famous for => Greyfriars Bobby was a Skye terrier in Edinburgh, Scotland, said to have guarded his owner's grave for 14 years until his own death in 1872.
who was old abe the eagle | what is old abe the eagle famous for => Old Abe was a bald eagle carried into battle by a Wisconsin regiment during the Civil War. He became a famous symbol of the Union.
who was marengo | what is marengo famous for => Marengo was Napoleon's famous gray warhorse, named after a battle he won in 1800. The horse's skeleton is in a museum in London.
what were war pigeons | explain war pigeons => Pigeons carried messages in both World Wars because they could fly home over long distances. One named Cher Ami saved nearly 200 American soldiers in 1918.
