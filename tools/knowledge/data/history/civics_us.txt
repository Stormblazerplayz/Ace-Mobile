# tag: civics.us
what are the three branches of government | name the 3 branches of government | what are the branches of the us government => The three branches are the legislative branch (Congress), which makes laws, the executive branch (the president), which carries out laws, and the judicial branch (the courts), which explains laws.
what does the legislative branch do | what is the legislative branch => The legislative branch, Congress, makes federal laws, decides how to spend tax money, declares war and approves treaties and top appointments.
what does the executive branch do | what is the executive branch => The executive branch, led by the president, carries out and enforces laws. It includes the vice president, the Cabinet and agencies like the FBI and NASA.
what does the judicial branch do | what is the judicial branch => The judicial branch, the Supreme Court and other federal courts, decides cases, interprets laws and can rule that a law breaks the Constitution.
what is congress | what are the two parts of congress | what is a bicameral legislature => Congress is the law-making branch of the US government. It's bicameral, meaning it has two parts: the House of Representatives and the Senate.
how many senators are there | how many senators does each state get => There are 100 senators, two from each of the 50 states, no matter how big the state is.
how many representatives are in the house | how many members of the house of representatives => The House of Representatives has 435 voting members. Each state gets seats based on its population, with at least one.
how long is a senators term | how many years does a senator serve => A senator's term is six years. About a third of the Senate is up for election every two years.
how long is a representatives term | how often are house members elected => Members of the House of Representatives serve two-year terms, so all 435 seats are up for election every two years.
how old do you have to be to be a senator | requirements to be a senator => A senator must be at least 30 years old, a US citizen for at least 9 years, and live in the state they represent.
how old do you have to be in the house of representatives | requirements to be a representative => A representative must be at least 25 years old, a US citizen for at least 7 years, and live in the state they represent.
which state has the most representatives | which state has the most seats in the house => California has the most House seats, 52 after the 2020 census. Six states have just one representative.
what is the census | how often is the census | why does the census matter => The census is a count of everyone living in the US, done every 10 years since 1790. It decides how many House seats each state gets and how some federal money is shared.
who is the speaker of the house | what does the speaker of the house do => The Speaker of the House is the leader of the House of Representatives, chosen by its members. The Speaker controls which bills get votes and is second in line to become president after the vice president.
who leads the senate | who is president of the senate => The vice president is the president of the Senate but votes only to break a tie. The Senate majority leader runs its day-to-day business.
how does a bill become a law | how are laws made in the us | steps for a bill to become a law => A member of Congress introduces a bill, a committee studies it, and then both the House and Senate must pass the same version. The president then signs it into law or vetoes it.
what is a bill | what is the difference between a bill and a law => A bill is a proposed law. It becomes a law only after Congress passes it and the president signs it, or Congress overrides a veto.
what is a filibuster | what does filibuster mean => A filibuster is a way senators can delay or block a vote by keeping debate going. Usually 60 of 100 senators must vote to end it.
what is impeachment | what happens when a president is impeached | does impeachment mean removed from office => Impeachment is when the House of Representatives formally charges an official with serious wrongdoing. Then the Senate holds a trial, and two-thirds of senators must agree to remove them.
what is a constituent | who do members of congress represent => A constituent is a person who lives in the area an official represents. Members of Congress are supposed to listen to and help their constituents.
what is a congressional district | how are districts drawn => A congressional district is an area that elects one member of the House. States redraw them after each census to keep populations about equal.
what is gerrymandering | why is gerrymandering bad => Gerrymandering is drawing voting district lines to give one party or group an unfair advantage. The name comes from Governor Elbridge Gerry and an 1812 district shaped like a salamander.
what is the capitol building | where does congress meet => Congress meets in the US Capitol building in Washington, D.C., with the House in the south wing and the Senate in the north wing under the big dome.
why is washington dc the capital | when did washington dc become the capital => Washington, D.C. became the capital in 1800. The location on the Potomac River was chosen by a 1790 deal, and George Washington picked the exact site.
what does dc stand for | why is dc not a state => D.C. stands for District of Columbia. The Constitution set up a federal district not part of any state so no single state would control the national capital.
what were the first capitals of the us | was philadelphia the capital => Before Washington, D.C., the US capital was New York City from 1789 to 1790 and Philadelphia from 1790 to 1800. Congress also met in several other cities during the Revolution.
what is an election | why do we have elections => An election is when people vote to choose leaders or decide on issues. Elections let citizens have a say in their government and replace leaders peacefully.
when are presidential elections | how often do we vote for president => Presidential elections are held every four years, on the Tuesday after the first Monday in November.
what is election day | why is election day on a tuesday => Election Day is the Tuesday after the first Monday in November. Congress picked it in 1845 partly because farmers needed a day to travel to vote that wasn't Sunday or market day.
what is the electoral college | how does the electoral college work => The Electoral College is how the US elects its president. Each state gets electors equal to its number of senators and representatives, and a candidate needs 270 of the 538 electoral votes to win.
how many electoral votes are there | how many electoral votes do you need to win => There are 538 electoral votes in total, and a candidate needs 270, a majority, to become president.
can you win the popular vote and lose | what is the popular vote => The popular vote is the total number of individual votes. A candidate can win it and still lose the Electoral College, which happened in 1824, 1876, 1888, 2000 and 2016.
what happens if no one gets 270 electoral votes | electoral college tie => If no one gets 270, the House of Representatives picks the president, with each state's delegation getting one vote. This happened in 1824, when the House chose John Quincy Adams.
what is a primary election | what is a caucus => A primary is an election where party members choose their candidate for the general election. A caucus is a meeting where party members pick candidates by discussion and voting.
what is a midterm election | what are midterms => Midterm elections happen halfway through a president's four-year term. All House seats and about a third of Senate seats are on the ballot.
what is a political party | why are there political parties => A political party is a group of people with similar ideas about government who work together to win elections and pass laws.
what are the two main political parties | what is the difference between democrats and republicans => The two main US parties are the Democrats and the Republicans. In general, Democrats favor more government programs and Republicans favor lower taxes and smaller government, though views vary a lot.
what is a third party | have third parties ever won => A third party is any party besides the two biggest ones, like the Libertarian or Green Party. No third party candidate has won the presidency since the Republican Party first won in 1860.
why is the democratic party a donkey | why is the republican party an elephant => The donkey and elephant symbols were made popular by cartoonist Thomas Nast in the 1870s. Andrew Jackson's opponents had called him a jackass, and he used it as a symbol.
what is a red state and a blue state | explain a red state and a blue state => On election maps, red states usually vote Republican and blue states usually vote Democratic. These colors became standard after the 2000 election.
what is a swing state | what is a battleground state => A swing state is a state where support for the two parties is close, so either one could win it. Candidates spend lots of time there.
how old do you have to be to vote | when can i vote | what is the voting age => In the US you must be 18 to vote in federal elections. Some states let 17-year-olds vote in primaries if they will turn 18 by the general election.
how do you register to vote | what is voter registration => To vote in most states you must register first with your name, address and age. Many states let you register online, at the motor vehicle office or by mail. North Dakota doesn't require registration.
what is a ballot | what is an absentee ballot => A ballot is the paper or screen where you mark your votes. An absentee or mail-in ballot lets people vote by mail instead of going to a polling place.
what is a polling place | where do people vote => A polling place is where people go to vote on Election Day, often a school, library or community center.
why is voting important | does my vote matter | importance of voting => Voting lets people choose the leaders who make laws about schools, taxes, roads and more. Many close elections have been decided by just a few votes.
what is a secret ballot | explain a secret ballot => A secret ballot means no one can see how you voted. It protects voters from being pressured or punished for their choices.
what is a referendum | what is a ballot measure => A referendum or ballot measure lets voters decide directly on a law or issue, instead of elected officials.
what is a governor | what does a governor do => A governor is the elected leader of a state's executive branch, like a president for the state. They sign or veto state laws and run state agencies.
what is a mayor | what does a mayor do => A mayor is the leader of a city or town government. Mayors oversee city services like police, fire departments, parks and trash pickup.
what does a city council do | what is a city council => A city council is a group of elected people who make local laws, called ordinances, and decide how the city spends its money.
what are the levels of government | what is local state and federal government => The US has three levels of government: federal (the whole country), state, and local (counties, cities and towns). Each handles different things.
what does the state government do | what do states control => State governments run things like public schools, driver's licenses, state roads, state police and many criminal laws.
what does the federal government do | what is the federal government in charge of => The federal government handles national matters like the military, foreign relations, printing money, the post office and national parks.
what does local government do | what does a city government handle => Local governments run things close to home like police and fire departments, libraries, parks, garbage pickup, local roads and zoning.
what is a county | what does a county do => A county is a part of a state with its own local government, often running courts, jails, roads and records. Louisiana calls them parishes and Alaska calls them boroughs.
who are the members of the cabinet | name the cabinet departments => The Cabinet includes the vice president and the heads of 15 departments, like State, Treasury, Defense, Justice, Interior, Agriculture, Commerce, Labor, Education and Homeland Security.
what does the secretary of state do | secretary of state job => The Secretary of State is the president's top adviser on foreign affairs and leads US diplomacy with other countries.
what is the department of defense | what is the pentagon => The Department of Defense runs the US military. Its headquarters, the Pentagon in Virginia, is one of the largest office buildings in the world, finished in 1943.
what is the fbi | what does the fbi do => The FBI, or Federal Bureau of Investigation, investigates federal crimes like terrorism, spying and cybercrime. It began in 1908.
what is the irs | what does the irs do => The IRS, or Internal Revenue Service, is the federal agency that collects taxes and enforces tax laws.
what is a jury | what does a jury do => A jury is a group of ordinary citizens, often 12 people, who listen to evidence in a trial and decide whether someone is guilty or who should win a case.
what is jury duty | who has to do jury duty => Jury duty is when citizens are called to serve on a jury. Adult US citizens can be called, and it's considered an important civic duty.
what is a judge | what does a judge do => A judge runs a courtroom, makes sure trials are fair, explains the law to juries and, in some cases, decides the outcome and the sentence.
what is the difference between a civil case and a criminal case | explain the difference between a civil case and a criminal case => A criminal case is when the government charges someone with a crime. A civil case is a dispute between people or companies, like over money or a broken contract.
what does innocent until proven guilty mean | presumption of innocence => It means a person accused of a crime is treated as innocent unless the government proves they are guilty beyond a reasonable doubt.
what is a lawyer | what is the difference between a prosecutor and a defense attorney => A lawyer gives legal advice and represents people in court. A prosecutor represents the government against an accused person, and a defense attorney represents the accused.
what is a law | why do we need laws => A law is a rule made by a government that everyone must follow. Laws keep people safe, protect rights and settle disagreements fairly.
what is a citizen | how do you become a us citizen => A citizen is a full member of a country with rights like voting. You can become a US citizen by being born in the US, having citizen parents, or through naturalization.
what is naturalization | what is the citizenship test => Naturalization is how an immigrant becomes a US citizen. They usually must live in the US for 5 years as a permanent resident, pass an English and civics test, and take an oath.
what is the pledge of allegiance | who wrote the pledge of allegiance => The Pledge of Allegiance is a promise of loyalty to the flag and the country. Francis Bellamy wrote it in 1892, and "under God" was added in 1954.
what is the national anthem of the us | when did the star spangled banner become the anthem => The US national anthem is "The Star-Spangled Banner," written by Francis Scott Key in 1814. It became the official national anthem in 1931.
what is the national bird of the us | us national bird => The bald eagle is the national bird and appears on the Great Seal of the United States, adopted in 1782. It stands for strength and freedom.
what does e pluribus unum mean | what is the us motto => E pluribus unum is Latin for "out of many, one," meaning many states form one nation. It's on the Great Seal. The official national motto since 1956 is "In God We Trust."
what is the great seal of the united states | explain the great seal of the united states => The Great Seal is the official emblem of the US, adopted in 1782. The front shows a bald eagle holding an olive branch and arrows. The back, with a pyramid and an eye, is printed on the $1 bill.
what is uncle sam | explain uncle sam => Uncle Sam is a cartoon figure who stands for the US government, usually shown with a white beard and a top hat in stars and stripes. The name dates to the War of 1812.
what are civic duties | what are the responsibilities of a citizen => Citizens have duties like obeying laws, paying taxes and serving on juries, and responsibilities like voting, staying informed and helping their community.
what are rights | what is the difference between rights and responsibilities => Rights are freedoms that are protected, like free speech and a fair trial. Responsibilities are things people should do, like following laws and respecting others' rights.
what is a petition | how do you start a petition => A petition is a written request signed by many people asking a government or organization to do something. The 1st Amendment protects the right to petition.
how can kids get involved in government | how can a kid make a difference => Kids can write to their representatives, speak at school board or city council meetings, join student government, volunteer, and learn about issues so they're ready to vote.
how do i write to my senator | how can i contact my representative => You can find your senators and representative on official government websites and write them a letter or email with your name, address and a short, polite message about what you care about.
what is the school board | what does a school board do => A school board is a group of elected local leaders who set rules and budgets for public schools, hire the superintendent and make big decisions about schools.
what is student government | explain student government => Student government is a group of elected students who represent their classmates, plan events and share ideas with teachers and principals. It's practice for real democracy.
what is a lobbyist | what is lobbying => Lobbying is trying to convince lawmakers to support certain laws. Lobbyists are people paid by groups, companies or causes to do this.
what is a campaign | how do people run for office => A campaign is the effort to win an election, with speeches, ads, debates and meeting voters. Candidates raise money and gather supporters.
what is a debate in politics | why do candidates debate => In a political debate, candidates answer the same questions in front of voters so people can compare their ideas. The first televised presidential debate was Kennedy vs. Nixon in 1960.
what is a term limit | do senators have term limits => A term limit caps how many terms someone can serve. The president is limited to two terms, but members of Congress have no term limits.
what is the national guard | explain the national guard => The National Guard is part of the US military made up of part-time soldiers in each state. Governors can call them up for emergencies like floods, and the president can call them for national service.
what are the branches of the military | how many branches of the us military are there => The US armed forces have six branches: the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Space Force and Coast Guard. The Space Force was created in 2019.
what is the bill of rights day | when is bill of rights day => Bill of Rights Day is December 15, marking the day in 1791 when the Bill of Rights was ratified.
what is memorial day | what is the difference between memorial day and veterans day => Memorial Day, the last Monday in May, honors military members who died in service. Veterans Day, November 11, honors everyone who has served.
what is presidents day | why do we have presidents day => Presidents' Day is the third Monday in February. It started as a celebration of George Washington's birthday, February 22, and now often honors all presidents.
why do we celebrate the fourth of july | what happened on the fourth of july => Independence Day, July 4, celebrates the adoption of the Declaration of Independence in 1776, when the colonies declared freedom from Britain.
what is columbus day | what is indigenous peoples day => The second Monday in October is Columbus Day, a federal holiday since 1937. Many states and cities now also or instead celebrate Indigenous Peoples' Day to honor Native Americans.
what are the federal holidays | how many federal holidays are there => As of 2025 there are 11 federal holidays, including New Year's Day, MLK Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas.
