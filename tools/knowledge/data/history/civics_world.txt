# tag: civics.world
what is government | why do we need government | what does government do => A government is the group of people and institutions that make and enforce rules for a country or community. It keeps order, protects people, and provides services like schools and roads.
what are the types of government | different kinds of government => Main types include democracy (people vote), monarchy (a king or queen), dictatorship (one ruler with total power), oligarchy (a small group rules) and theocracy (religious leaders rule).
what is a democracy | how does democracy work => A democracy is a government where the people hold power, usually by voting for leaders and laws. Most democracies protect rights like free speech and fair elections.
what is the difference between direct and representative democracy | explain the difference between direct and representative democracy => In a direct democracy, citizens vote on laws themselves, like ancient Athens. In a representative democracy, citizens elect people to make laws for them, like the US.
what is a republic | is the us a democracy or a republic => A republic is a country with no king where leaders are elected and the head of state isn't a monarch. The US is both: a democratic republic, where people elect representatives.
what is a monarchy | what is a king => A monarchy is a government led by a king, queen, emperor or other monarch who usually inherits the job from their family.
what is a constitutional monarchy | does the king of england have power => In a constitutional monarchy, like the UK, Japan or Spain, the monarch is head of state but elected leaders and a constitution hold the real power.
what is an absolute monarchy | which countries have absolute monarchs => In an absolute monarchy the ruler has almost total power. As of 2025, examples include Saudi Arabia, Brunei, Oman, Eswatini and Vatican City.
what is a dictatorship | what is a dictator => A dictatorship is a government where one person or small group has total power, often taken by force, with no fair elections and little freedom for people.
what is totalitarianism | what is an authoritarian government => An authoritarian government has strong central power and limits freedoms. A totalitarian one goes further and tries to control almost every part of people's lives, like Nazi Germany or Stalin's Soviet Union.
what is an oligarchy | what does oligarchy mean => An oligarchy is rule by a small group of powerful people, often the rich, military leaders or a single party. The word comes from Greek for "rule by few."
what is a theocracy | which countries are theocracies => A theocracy is a government led by religious leaders or based on religious law. Iran and Vatican City are examples.
what is anarchy | what does anarchy mean | anarchism explained => Anarchy means having no government at all. Anarchism is the idea that people could live cooperatively without rulers.
what is a parliament | how does a parliamentary system work => A parliament is a group of elected lawmakers. In a parliamentary system, like the UK, Canada or India, the leader, the prime minister, comes from the party that has the most support in parliament.
what is a prime minister | what is the difference between a president and a prime minister => A prime minister leads the government in a parliamentary system and is chosen by parliament. A president in a system like the US is elected separately and is both head of state and head of government.
who leads the united kingdom | how does the uk government work => The UK is a constitutional monarchy. The king is head of state, but the prime minister, the leader of the largest party in the House of Commons, runs the government.
what is the house of commons | what is the house of lords => The UK Parliament has two houses. The House of Commons has 650 elected members and holds most power. The House of Lords is appointed and reviews laws.
what is the united nations | what does the un do | when was the un created => The United Nations is an international organization founded on October 24, 1945, after World War 2, to keep peace, protect human rights and help countries work together. As of 2025 it has 193 member countries.
where is the un headquarters | where is the united nations located => The UN headquarters is in New York City, on land along the East River. It is international territory managed by the UN.
what is the un security council | what are the permanent members of the security council => The Security Council is responsible for international peace. It has 15 members, including 5 permanent ones that can veto decisions: the US, the UK, France, Russia and China.
what is the un general assembly | explain the un general assembly => The General Assembly is the UN's main meeting where every member country gets one vote. It meets every year in New York to discuss world issues.
who is the un secretary general | what does the secretary general do => The Secretary-General leads the UN's staff and speaks for the organization. As of 2025, António Guterres of Portugal holds the job, which he started in 2017.
what is unicef | what does unicef do => UNICEF is the UN agency that helps children around the world with food, clean water, vaccines, education and protection. It was created in 1946.
what is the world health organization | what does the who do => The World Health Organization is the UN agency for global health, founded in 1948. It fights diseases, helps with vaccines and gives health advice to countries.
what is unesco | what is a world heritage site => UNESCO is the UN agency for education, science and culture. It lists World Heritage Sites, places of special value to humanity like the Pyramids of Giza and Machu Picchu.
what is the universal declaration of human rights | explain the universal declaration of human rights => The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted by the UN on December 10, 1948. It lists basic rights everyone should have, like life, freedom, education and equality.
what are human rights | examples of human rights => Human rights are basic rights every person has just for being human, like the right to life, freedom from slavery and torture, freedom of speech, education and equal treatment.
what are the rights of a child | what is the convention on the rights of the child => The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, adopted in 1989, says children have rights to be safe, healthy, educated, heard and protected from harm.
what is the international court of justice | what is the world court => The International Court of Justice is the UN's main court, in The Hague, Netherlands. It settles legal disputes between countries.
what is the red cross | when was the red cross founded => The International Red Cross was founded in 1863 in Geneva, Switzerland, by Henry Dunant and others to help wounded soldiers. Today the Red Cross and Red Crescent help people in wars and disasters worldwide.
what is the geneva convention | geneva conventions laws of war => The Geneva Conventions are international treaties, first signed in 1864 and updated in 1949, that set rules for war, like protecting wounded soldiers, prisoners and civilians.
what is a treaty | how do treaties work => A treaty is a formal written agreement between countries, like a peace deal or trade deal. In the US, treaties need approval from two-thirds of the Senate.
what is diplomacy | what is a diplomat | what is an ambassador => Diplomacy is countries talking and negotiating to solve problems peacefully. An ambassador is a top diplomat who represents their country in another country.
what is an embassy | explain an embassy => An embassy is the official office of one country in another country's capital, led by an ambassador. It helps citizens abroad and handles relations between the countries.
what is a sanction | what are economic sanctions => Sanctions are penalties, like banning trade or freezing money, that countries put on another country to pressure it to change its behavior.
what is a country | what makes something a country => A country, or sovereign state, usually has a defined territory, a permanent population, its own government and the ability to deal with other countries.
how many countries are there in the world | number of countries on earth => There are 195 countries widely recognized: 193 UN members plus two observer states, Vatican City and Palestine. Some places, like Taiwan and Kosovo, are recognized by only some countries.
what is nationalism | nationalism vs patriotism => Patriotism is love and loyalty for your country. Nationalism is a strong belief in your nation's interests and identity, which can unite people but in extreme forms has led to conflict.
what is the left and right in politics | what does left wing and right wing mean => Left and right describe political views. Left usually means favoring more government help and change, and right usually means favoring tradition, lower taxes and less government. The terms come from seating in France's 1789 assembly.
what is a liberal and a conservative | explain a liberal and a conservative => In the US, liberals generally want government to do more to help people and protect rights, while conservatives generally want smaller government, lower taxes and to keep traditions.
what is propaganda | explain propaganda => Propaganda is information, often biased or misleading, spread to push people toward a certain view, especially by governments. Posters in the world wars are famous examples.
what is freedom of the press | why is a free press important => Freedom of the press means newspapers and other media can report news and criticize the government without being censored. It helps citizens know the truth and hold leaders accountable.
what is censorship | what does censorship mean => Censorship is when a government or group blocks or removes information, books, art or speech it doesn't want people to see.
what is a coup | what does coup mean => A coup, or coup d'état, is a sudden, illegal takeover of a government, often by the military.
what is a revolution | what is the difference between a revolution and a rebellion => A revolution is a big, often fast change in government or society, like the American or French Revolution. A rebellion is an uprising against authority that may or may not succeed.
what is civil disobedience | explain civil disobedience => Civil disobedience is peacefully refusing to obey a law you think is unfair and accepting the punishment, like Gandhi's Salt March or Rosa Parks staying in her seat.
what is a protest | is protesting legal => A protest is a public way of showing disagreement, like a march or rally. In the US, peaceful protest is protected by the 1st Amendment, though cities can set rules on time and place.
what is a strike | what does going on strike mean => A strike is when workers stop working together to pressure an employer for better pay or conditions.
what is the g7 | what is the g20 => The G7 is a group of seven large wealthy democracies: the US, Canada, the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Japan. The G20 adds big economies like China, India and Brazil, plus the EU and the African Union.
what is the world bank | what is the imf => The World Bank lends money to poorer countries for projects like roads and schools. The International Monetary Fund helps keep the world money system stable and lends to countries in crisis. Both started in 1944.
what is the african union | explain the african union => The African Union is a group of 55 African countries formed in 2002, replacing the Organization of African Unity, to promote peace, cooperation and development.
what is asean | explain asean => ASEAN, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, is a group of Southeast Asian countries founded in 1967 to work together on trade, peace and culture.
what is the olympic truce | olympic rings meaning and history => The five Olympic rings, designed by Pierre de Coubertin in 1913, stand for the five inhabited continents joined together. The Olympic Truce is an ancient Greek tradition of pausing wars during the Games.
