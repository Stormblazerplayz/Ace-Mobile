why do people say bless you when you sneeze | why do we say bless you | where does bless you come from => No one knows for sure. One popular story links it to Pope Gregory I during a plague in Rome in 590, and other old beliefs held that a sneeze could let evil spirits in or out.
where did the high five come from | who invented the high five | first high five => Many people trace the high five to 1977, when Los Angeles Dodgers players Dusty Baker and Glenn Burke slapped hands after a home run. Similar hand slaps existed before that.
why do people shake hands | where did handshakes come from => Handshakes go back thousands of years, appearing in ancient Greek art. One common theory is that holding out an empty right hand showed you weren't carrying a weapon.
why are there seven days in a week | who made the seven day week => The seven day week goes back to ancient Babylon, possibly linked to the phases of the Moon and the seven moving objects they saw in the sky. The Jewish Sabbath tradition and later the Romans spread it.
why are there twelve months in a year | why does a year have 12 months | where do months come from => There are 12 months because there are about 12 cycles of the Moon in a year. Our months come from the Roman calendar, which Julius Caesar reformed in 46 BC.
why is september the ninth month | what does september mean | why is october not the eighth month => September comes from the Latin word for seven because the early Roman calendar started in March. When January and February were moved to the start, September became the ninth month.
which months are named after people | who is july named after | who is august named after => July is named after Julius Caesar and August after Emperor Augustus. January is named after Janus, the Roman god of doorways, and March after Mars, the god of war.
why do we celebrate new year on january 1 | why does the year start in january | who made january 1 new years => Julius Caesar's calendar made January 1 the start of the year in 45 BC. Many European countries used other dates in the Middle Ages, but January 1 became standard with the Gregorian calendar.
when did people start using forks | who invented the fork | history of the fork => People in ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome had forks for cooking and serving. Eating forks spread from the Byzantine Empire to Italy around the 1000s and became common in England only in the 1600s and 1700s.
when did people start celebrating birthdays | history of birthday parties | why do we have birthday cakes => Ancient Egyptians celebrated the "birthdays" of pharaohs, and ancient Greeks and Romans honored birthdays too. Birthday cakes with candles for children grew from German celebrations called Kinderfeste in the 1700s.
who wrote the happy birthday song | where does the happy birthday song come from | who made happy birthday to you => The tune of "Happy Birthday to You" comes from "Good Morning to All," a song by sisters Mildred and Patty Hill, two Kentucky teachers, published in 1893.
when did kids start going to school | when was school invented | who invented school => The earliest known schools were in ancient Sumer about 4,500 years ago, where boys learned to write on clay tablets. In the US, Boston Latin School opened in 1635, and by 1918 every state required children to attend school.
what is the oldest map | explain the oldest map => One of the oldest known world maps is the Babylonian Map of the World, carved on a clay tablet around 600 BC. It shows Babylon in the middle of a round world surrounded by water.
when was the first restaurant | who invented restaurants | where was the first restaurant => The modern restaurant, where customers chose food from a menu at their own tables, began in Paris in the 1760s. Inns and food stalls had served travelers for thousands of years before that.
why do people wear wedding rings | why is a wedding ring on the fourth finger | history of wedding rings => Ancient Egyptians and Romans exchanged rings as signs of marriage. The Romans believed a vein ran from the fourth finger of the left hand straight to the heart.
why do brides wear white | why are wedding dresses white | who started white wedding dresses => White wedding dresses became popular after Queen Victoria wore one to marry Prince Albert in 1840. Before then, brides usually wore their best dress in any color.
why are school buses yellow | who made school buses yellow | what color are school buses and why => In 1939, a conference led by Frank Cyr chose a yellow-orange color, now called National School Bus Glossy Yellow, because it's easy to see in early morning light.
when did people start using white flags to surrender | why does a white flag mean surrender | what does a white flag mean in war => White flags have been used to show surrender or truce for about 2,000 years, mentioned by ancient Roman and Chinese writers.
why do men wear neckties | where did ties come from => Neckties grew from scarves worn by Croatian soldiers in the 1600s. The French liked the look and called them cravats, a word that comes from "Croat."
why does a week start on sunday | is sunday the first day of the week | does the week start on monday or sunday => In the US, calendars start on Sunday because of Jewish and Christian tradition, where Sunday is the first day. Many other countries and an international standard start the week on Monday.
why is it called the weekend | when did the weekend start => The two-day weekend grew in the 1800s and 1900s. Henry Ford gave his workers Saturdays and Sundays off in 1926, and the US set a 40-hour workweek in 1940.
where does the word salary come from | were roman soldiers paid in salt | what does salary mean in latin => Salary comes from the Latin word "salarium," linked to salt. A popular story says Roman soldiers were paid in salt, but historians aren't sure that's true.
why do we clink glasses when we toast | why do people make a toast | where does toasting come from => No one knows for sure. Toasting goes back to ancient Greece and Rome. The word "toast" comes from putting a piece of toasted bread in a drink to improve its flavor.
# tag: history.documents
what was the four freedoms speech | explain the four freedoms speech => In January 1941, President Franklin Roosevelt said everyone in the world deserved four freedoms: freedom of speech, freedom of worship, freedom from want and freedom from fear.
what was the atlantic charter | explain the atlantic charter => The Atlantic Charter was a 1941 statement by Franklin Roosevelt and Winston Churchill of their goals for the world after World War 2, like self-government and free trade. It helped inspire the United Nations.
what was the quit india movement | explain the quit india movement => The Quit India Movement was a campaign launched by Gandhi in August 1942 demanding that Britain leave India immediately. British authorities arrested Gandhi and other leaders.
what was the balfour declaration | explain the balfour declaration => The Balfour Declaration was a 1917 letter from Britain's foreign secretary, Arthur Balfour, supporting a homeland for the Jewish people in Palestine, while saying the rights of others there should be protected.
what was the edict of nantes | explain the edict of nantes => The Edict of Nantes, signed by France's King Henry IV in 1598, gave Protestant Huguenots religious freedom. King Louis XIV canceled it in 1685, and many Huguenots fled France.
what was the edict of milan | explain the edict of milan => The Edict of Milan in AD 313 was an agreement by Roman emperors Constantine and Licinius to allow Christians, and people of all religions, to worship freely.
what was the treaty of ghent | explain the treaty of ghent => The Treaty of Ghent, signed in December 1814, ended the War of 1812 between the US and Britain, mostly returning things to how they were before the war.
what was the clean air act | explain the clean air act => The Clean Air Act of 1970 is a US law that lets the government limit air pollution from factories and cars. It has greatly reduced smog and harmful gases.
what was the endangered species act | explain the endangered species act => The Endangered Species Act, signed in 1973 by President Nixon, protects plants and animals at risk of extinction and their habitats. It helped save the bald eagle and grizzly bear.
what was the pure food and drug act | explain the pure food and drug act => The Pure Food and Drug Act of 1906 was a US law banning harmful or falsely labeled food and medicine. It led to the creation of the FDA.
what was the sherman antitrust act | explain the sherman antitrust act => The Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890 was a US law against monopolies, when one company controls a whole industry and blocks competition.
what was the fair labor standards act | explain the fair labor standards act => The Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 set the first US national minimum wage, limited the workweek and greatly limited child labor.
what was the 1924 indian citizenship act | explain the 1924 indian citizenship act => The Indian Citizenship Act of 1924 made all Native Americans born in the US citizens. Some states still kept many from voting for decades.
what was the immigration act of 1965 | explain the immigration act of 1965 => The Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 ended US immigration limits based on national origin that had favored Europeans, opening the door to more immigrants from Asia, Africa and Latin America.
what was the 1862 morrill act | what are land grant colleges => The Morrill Act of 1862 gave states federal land to sell to fund colleges teaching farming, engineering and other practical subjects. Schools like Iowa State and Michigan State began as land-grant colleges.
what was the pacific railway act | explain the pacific railway act => The Pacific Railway Act of 1862, signed by Lincoln, gave land and loans to companies to build the first transcontinental railroad, finished in 1869.
what was the national park service act | explain the national park service act => The National Park Service was created by a law signed by Woodrow Wilson on August 25, 1916, to manage America's national parks. Yellowstone had become the first national park in 1872.
what was the antiquities act | explain the antiquities act => The Antiquities Act of 1906 lets presidents protect historic and natural places as national monuments. Theodore Roosevelt used it first, for Devils Tower in Wyoming.
