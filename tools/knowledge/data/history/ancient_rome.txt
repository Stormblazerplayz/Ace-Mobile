what was the battle of cannae | explain the battle of cannae => At Cannae in 216 BC, Hannibal surrounded and destroyed a much bigger Roman army, killing tens of thousands in a single day. It's still studied as a perfect battle plan.

how did julius caesar die | who killed julius caesar | what are the ides of march => Julius Caesar was stabbed to death by a group of senators, including Brutus and Cassius, on the Ides of March, March 15, 44 BC. They feared he wanted to rule as a king.
what does crossing the rubicon mean | what did caesar do at the rubicon => In 49 BC Julius Caesar led his army across the Rubicon River into Italy, breaking Roman law and starting a civil war. Crossing the Rubicon now means making a choice you can't take back.
what does veni vidi vici mean | i came i saw i conquered => Veni, vidi, vici is Latin for "I came, I saw, I conquered." Julius Caesar wrote it in 47 BC after a very quick victory.

what was the pax romana | explain the pax romana => The Pax Romana, or Roman Peace, was about 200 years of mostly peaceful, stable times in the Roman Empire, from Augustus in 27 BC to about AD 180.

who was caligula | what is caligula famous for => Caligula was emperor from AD 37 to 41, known for cruel and strange behavior. Stories say he planned to make his horse a consul. He was killed by his own guards.
who was marcus aurelius | what was meditations => Marcus Aurelius was emperor from AD 161 to 180 and a Stoic philosopher. His private notes, called Meditations, are still read today.

who was trajan | what is trajan famous for => Trajan was emperor from AD 98 to 117, known as a good ruler, under whom the Roman Empire reached its largest size.
who was augustus caesar | what did augustus do => Augustus, born Octavian, was the first Roman emperor, ruling from 27 BC to AD 14. He brought peace after civil wars and rebuilt Rome. The month August is named for him.

what is the colosseum | when was the colosseum built | how many people did the colosseum hold => The Colosseum is a giant amphitheater in Rome, finished in AD 80. It held about 50,000 people, who watched gladiator fights, animal hunts and public shows.
who were gladiators | did gladiators fight to the death => Gladiators were fighters who battled in arenas to entertain crowds. Most were enslaved people, prisoners or volunteers. Many fights didn't end in death, since trained gladiators were valuable.
what did thumbs up mean in rome | gladiator thumbs down meaning => Nobody is sure. Romans used a thumb signal to decide a defeated gladiator's fate, but which gesture meant life or death is still debated.
what is the pantheon in rome | explain the pantheon in rome => The Pantheon in Rome is a temple finished around AD 125 under Hadrian. Its concrete dome, with a hole on top called the oculus, is still the largest unreinforced concrete dome in the world.
what is the roman forum | explain the roman forum => The Roman Forum was the center of ancient Rome, a big open square with temples, government buildings and markets where people met and did business.
what was the circus maximus | explain the circus maximus => The Circus Maximus was a giant stadium in Rome for chariot races, which could hold over 150,000 people.

how did the romans make concrete | why is roman concrete so strong => Romans made concrete from lime, volcanic ash and rock. Some of it heals small cracks over time, which helps explain why Roman buildings and harbors have lasted 2,000 years.
did the romans build roads | all roads lead to rome => Romans built over 80,000 kilometers of paved roads to move armies and trade. The saying "all roads lead to Rome" comes from that huge network.

what did romans eat | ancient roman food => Ordinary Romans ate bread, porridge, beans, vegetables, olives and some cheese and fish. Rich Romans held big dinners and loved a fish sauce called garum.
