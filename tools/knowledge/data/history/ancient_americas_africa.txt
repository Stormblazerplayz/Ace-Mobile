# tag: history.ancient.americas
who were the olmecs | what were the olmec heads => The Olmecs lived on Mexico's Gulf Coast from about 1200 to 400 BC and are one of the earliest civilizations in Mesoamerica. They carved giant stone heads, some nearly 3 meters tall.
who were the maya | where did the maya live | when did the maya live => The Maya built cities in southern Mexico and Central America. Their classic period was about AD 250 to 900, and millions of Maya people still live there today.
what happened to the maya | why did the maya cities get abandoned => Many big Maya cities in the southern lowlands were abandoned between about AD 800 and 1000, probably from droughts, too many people for the land, and wars. Maya cities in the north lasted longer, and the Maya people never disappeared.
what did the maya invent | what were the maya good at => The Maya developed a full writing system, a very accurate calendar, a number system with a zero, and great astronomy. They built tall stone pyramids and temples.
what is chichen itza | explain chichen itza => Chichén Itzá is a large Maya city in Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, at its height around AD 900 to 1200. Its famous pyramid, El Castillo, shows a shadow like a snake on its stairs at the equinoxes.
what is tikal | explain tikal => Tikal, in the rainforest of Guatemala, was one of the most powerful Maya cities, with temple pyramids over 60 meters tall.
did the maya predict the end of the world in 2012 | maya calendar 2012 => No. A Maya calendar cycle ended in December 2012, but the Maya saw it as the start of a new cycle, not the end of the world.
what was the mesoamerican ball game | explain the mesoamerican ball game => The Mesoamerican ballgame was played with a heavy rubber ball that players hit with their hips. Courts were in many Maya and Aztec cities, and some games had religious meaning.
who were the aztecs | where did the aztecs live | when did the aztecs live => The Aztecs, or Mexica, built a powerful empire in central Mexico in the 1400s and early 1500s. Their capital, Tenochtitlan, stood on an island in a lake where Mexico City is now.
what was tenochtitlan | how big was tenochtitlan => Tenochtitlan was the Aztec capital, founded around 1325 on an island in Lake Texcoco. By 1519 it had perhaps 200,000 people, with canals, causeways and floating gardens.
what is the legend of the eagle on the cactus | mexican flag eagle => Aztec legend says their god told them to build their city where they saw an eagle on a cactus eating a snake. They saw it on an island in Lake Texcoco, and that image is on Mexico's flag.
what were chinampas | what were floating gardens => Chinampas were raised farm plots built in shallow lake water from mud and plants, used by the Aztecs to grow corn, beans and squash all year.
who was montezuma | who was moctezuma => Moctezuma II was the Aztec emperor when Hernán Cortés arrived in 1519. He was taken captive by the Spanish and died in 1520.
who conquered the aztecs | how did cortes defeat the aztecs => Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés conquered the Aztecs in 1521 with a few hundred Spaniards and many thousands of Native allies who hated Aztec rule. Smallpox also killed a huge number of Aztecs.
what did the aztecs eat | did the aztecs invent chocolate => Aztecs ate corn tortillas, beans, squash, chili peppers and tomatoes. They drank a bitter chocolate drink, made from cacao, which people in Mesoamerica had made for thousands of years.
what was teotihuacan | explain teotihuacan => Teotihuacan was a huge ancient city near Mexico City, at its height around AD 100 to 550, with the giant Pyramid of the Sun. The Aztecs found it already in ruins and named it.
who were the inca | where did the inca live | when was the inca empire => The Inca built the largest empire in the Americas in the 1400s and early 1500s, stretching along the Andes mountains of South America. Their capital was Cusco, in Peru.
what is machu picchu | when was machu picchu built | who built machu picchu => Machu Picchu is an Inca estate high in the Andes of Peru, built around 1450 for the emperor Pachacuti. The Spanish never found it, and it became famous worldwide after Hiram Bingham visited in 1911.
how did the inca communicate without writing | what is a quipu => The Inca didn't have a writing system like ours. They kept records with quipus, bundles of knotted strings where the knots and colors stood for numbers and information.
what were inca roads like | explain inca roads like => The Inca built about 40,000 kilometers of roads through mountains, with rope bridges and rest houses. Relay runners called chasquis carried messages quickly along them.
who conquered the inca | who was francisco pizarro => Spanish conquistador Francisco Pizarro conquered the Inca starting in 1532, capturing and later killing the emperor Atahualpa. A civil war and smallpox had already weakened the empire.
what language did the inca speak | inca language quechua => The Inca spoke Quechua, which millions of people in Peru, Bolivia and Ecuador still speak today.
who were the mound builders | what is cahokia => Cahokia, near St. Louis, was the largest city in North America north of Mexico before Europeans came, at its height around AD 1050 to 1200 with perhaps 10,000 to 20,000 people and huge earthen mounds.
who were the ancestral puebloans | who lived in mesa verde => Ancestral Puebloans lived in the Four Corners area of the US Southwest. Around the 1200s they built cliff dwellings at places like Mesa Verde in Colorado.
what was the iroquois confederacy | haudenosaunee => The Haudenosaunee, or Iroquois Confederacy, is an alliance of Native nations in what is now New York. It began with five nations and became six in 1722, with a council that made decisions together.
how many native americans were there before columbus | native american population in 1492 => Estimates vary widely. Many scholars think tens of millions of people lived in the Americas in 1492, but disease, war and enslavement after Europeans arrived killed a huge share of them.
why did so many native americans die after europeans came | what diseases did europeans bring to america => The biggest killer was disease. Europeans brought smallpox, measles and flu, which Native peoples had never faced, and epidemics killed huge numbers, often before Europeans even arrived.
what was the columbian exchange | explain the columbian exchange => The Columbian Exchange was the swap of plants, animals, people and diseases between the Americas and the rest of the world after 1492. Corn, potatoes and tomatoes went to Europe, and horses, wheat and smallpox came to the Americas.
# tag: history.ancient.africa
what was the kingdom of mali | who was mansa musa => The Mali Empire in West Africa was rich from gold and trade from about 1235 to 1600. Its ruler Mansa Musa, who reigned in the early 1300s, is often called one of the richest people in history.
what was mansa musas trip to mecca | explain mansa musas trip to mecca => In 1324 Mansa Musa traveled to Mecca with thousands of people and loads of gold. He gave away so much gold in Cairo that its value there dropped for years.
what was timbuktu famous for | explain timbuktu famous for => Timbuktu, in Mali, was a great center of trade and Islamic learning in the 1300s to 1500s, with famous mosques and libraries full of manuscripts.
what was the ghana empire | explain the ghana empire => The Ghana Empire was a West African trading empire from about the 300s to the 1200s, rich from gold. Modern Ghana took its name but is in a different place.
what was the songhai empire | explain the songhai empire => The Songhai Empire controlled West Africa's trade in the 1400s and 1500s and was one of the largest empires in African history. It fell to a Moroccan army in 1591.
what was great zimbabwe | explain great zimbabwe => Great Zimbabwe was a stone city in southern Africa built from the 1000s to the 1400s, with huge walls made without mortar. The country Zimbabwe is named after it.
what was the kingdom of aksum | what was axum => Aksum was a powerful trading kingdom in what is now Ethiopia and Eritrea from about AD 100 to 940. It made its own coins, built tall stone obelisks and became Christian in the 300s.
what are the churches of lalibela | explain the churches of lalibela => Lalibela in Ethiopia has 11 churches carved down into solid rock, built around the 1100s and 1200s. They are still used for worship.
what was the kingdom of benin | what are the benin bronzes => The Kingdom of Benin, in what is now Nigeria, was famous for metal sculptures called the Benin Bronzes. British soldiers looted thousands of them in 1897, and some are now being returned.
what was the atlantic slave trade | how many africans were taken in the slave trade => From the 1500s to the 1800s, about 12.5 million Africans were forced onto ships and taken across the Atlantic to the Americas. Nearly 2 million died on the voyage.
when did britain end slavery | who was william wilberforce => Britain banned the slave trade in 1807 and slavery in most of its colonies in 1833. William Wilberforce led the long campaign in Parliament.
what was the scramble for africa | explain the scramble for africa => The Scramble for Africa was when European powers rushed to take control of almost all of Africa between about 1880 and 1914. By 1914 only Ethiopia and Liberia were independent.
what was the berlin conference | explain the berlin conference => At the Berlin Conference of 1884 to 1885, European countries set rules for dividing Africa among themselves. No Africans were invited.
what was the battle of adwa | explain the battle of adwa => At the Battle of Adwa in 1896, Ethiopia's army under Emperor Menelik II defeated an invading Italian army, keeping Ethiopia independent.
who was shaka zulu | what is shaka zulu famous for => Shaka was a Zulu king in southern Africa from about 1816 to 1828 who built the Zulu nation into a powerful military kingdom.
what was the battle of isandlwana | explain the battle of isandlwana => At Isandlwana in 1879, a Zulu army defeated a British force, one of the worst defeats of the British army by an African force.
