when did ancient china start | how old is chinese civilization | first chinese dynasty => Chinese civilization grew along the Yellow River. The Shang dynasty, from about 1600 to 1046 BC, is the first dynasty with written records, carved on bones.
what is a dynasty | what does dynasty mean | chinese dynasties list => A dynasty is a series of rulers from the same family. Chinese history is often divided by dynasties like the Han, Tang, Song, Ming and Qing.
what were oracle bones | oldest chinese writing => Oracle bones were turtle shells and ox bones that Shang priests carved with questions, then heated until they cracked to read answers from ancestors. They hold the oldest known Chinese writing.
what is the mandate of heaven | mandate of heaven meaning => The Mandate of Heaven was a Chinese belief that heaven gave a ruler the right to rule as long as he ruled well. Disasters and revolts were seen as signs he had lost it.
who was the first emperor of china | who was qin shi huang => Qin Shi Huang united China in 221 BC and became its first emperor. He set one writing system, money and measurement for all of China and began linking walls into an early Great Wall.
what is the terracotta army | how many terracotta soldiers are there | who made the terracotta warriors => The Terracotta Army is about 8,000 life-size clay soldiers, plus horses and chariots, buried with China's first emperor, Qin Shi Huang, around 210 BC. Farmers digging a well found them in 1974.
why does china have the name china | where does the name china come from => The name China probably comes from the Qin dynasty, pronounced "chin," which first united the country in 221 BC.
which dynasty built the great wall | when was the great wall built | how long did the great wall take to build => The Great Wall was built over about 2,000 years by many dynasties. Qin Shi Huang linked older walls around 220 BC, and most of what tourists see today was built by the Ming dynasty, 1368 to 1644.
can you see the great wall from space | is the great wall visible from the moon => No, you can't see the Great Wall from the Moon, and it's very hard to spot from low Earth orbit with the naked eye, because it's long but narrow and similar in color to the land around it.
what was the han dynasty | explain the han dynasty => The Han dynasty ruled China from 206 BC to AD 220, a golden age when paper was invented, the Silk Road opened and Confucian ideas guided government. Most Chinese people still call themselves Han.
what was the silk road | why was it called the silk road => The Silk Road was a network of trade routes linking China to the Middle East and Europe from about 130 BC. Traders carried silk, spices, paper and ideas, and religions like Buddhism spread along it.
who invented paper | when was paper invented => Paper was invented in China. A court official named Cai Lun improved papermaking around AD 105, using tree bark, hemp, rags and fishing nets, though simpler paper existed earlier.
what are the four great inventions of china | explain the four great inventions of china => The Four Great Inventions of ancient China are paper, printing, gunpowder and the compass.
who invented gunpowder | when was gunpowder invented => Gunpowder was invented in China, probably by people searching for a potion for long life, by the 800s AD. It was first used for fireworks and then weapons.
who invented the compass | when was the compass invented => The compass was invented in China. Spoons of magnetic lodestone pointed south by around the 200s BC, and magnetic needles were used for navigation at sea by about the 1000s AD.
who invented printing | first printed book => Woodblock printing began in China by the 600s AD. The Diamond Sutra, printed in 868, is the oldest dated printed book. Bi Sheng invented movable type around 1040.
who was confucius | what did confucius teach => Confucius was a Chinese teacher who lived from 551 to 479 BC. He taught respect for family, elders and good manners, and that rulers should be fair and kind. His ideas shaped China for 2,000 years.
what is taoism | who was laozi | what is daoism => Taoism, or Daoism, is a Chinese philosophy and religion that teaches living in harmony with the Tao, the natural way of the universe. Its key book, the Tao Te Ching, is credited to Laozi.
what is yin and yang | explain yin and yang => Yin and yang is a Chinese idea that opposite forces, like dark and light or cold and hot, are connected and balance each other. The symbol shows two swirls each with a dot of the other.
who was sun tzu | what is the art of war => Sun Tzu was a Chinese general and thinker credited with The Art of War, a book on strategy written around 500 BC. It is still read by military and business leaders.
what was the tang dynasty | explain the tang dynasty => The Tang dynasty ruled China from 618 to 907, a golden age of poetry, art and trade. Its capital Chang'an was one of the largest cities in the world.
what was the song dynasty | explain the song dynasty => The Song dynasty ruled China from 960 to 1279. It saw paper money, movable type, the compass at sea and a booming economy.
who was wu zetian | only female emperor of china => Wu Zetian was the only woman to rule China as emperor in her own name, from 690 to 705, during the Tang era.
who was genghis khan | what did genghis khan do => Genghis Khan united the Mongol tribes in 1206 and began conquests that created the largest connected land empire in history. He died in 1227.
how big was the mongol empire | largest land empire ever => At its peak in the late 1200s the Mongol Empire stretched from Korea to Eastern Europe, about 24 million square kilometers, the largest connected land empire ever.
who was kublai khan | what is kublai khan famous for => Kublai Khan was Genghis Khan's grandson who conquered all of China and founded the Yuan dynasty in 1271. Marco Polo described visiting his court.
who was marco polo | what did marco polo do => Marco Polo was a Venetian merchant who traveled to China in the 1270s and served Kublai Khan for about 17 years. His book about his travels made Europeans curious about Asia.
what was the ming dynasty | explain the ming dynasty => The Ming dynasty ruled China from 1368 to 1644. It rebuilt the Great Wall, built the Forbidden City and sent huge treasure fleets across the Indian Ocean.
who was zheng he | what is zheng he famous for => Zheng He was a Chinese admiral who led seven huge voyages from 1405 to 1433, with hundreds of ships, as far as East Africa and Arabia.
what is the forbidden city | why is it called the forbidden city => The Forbidden City in Beijing was the palace of Chinese emperors from 1420 to 1912. It was called forbidden because ordinary people couldn't enter without permission. It's now the Palace Museum.
what was the qing dynasty | who was the last emperor of china => The Qing dynasty, founded by the Manchus, was China's last imperial dynasty, from 1644 to 1912. The last emperor, Puyi, gave up the throne in 1912 at age 6.
what were the opium wars | explain the opium wars => The Opium Wars (1839 to 1842 and 1856 to 1860) were fought when China tried to stop British traders from selling opium there. Britain won and forced China to open ports and give up Hong Kong.
when did hong kong go back to china | hong kong handover 1997 => Britain returned Hong Kong to China on July 1, 1997, after ruling it for over 150 years.
what was the boxer rebellion | explain the boxer rebellion => The Boxer Rebellion (1899 to 1901) was an uprising in China against foreign influence and Christian missionaries. An army of eight foreign nations crushed it.
when did china become a republic | who was sun yat sen => China became a republic in 1912 after a revolution ended the Qing dynasty. Sun Yat-sen led the movement and is called the father of modern China.
when did china become communist | who was mao zedong => Mao Zedong led the Communist Party to victory in China's civil war and founded the People's Republic of China on October 1, 1949. He ruled until he died in 1976.
what was the great leap forward | explain the great leap forward => The Great Leap Forward (1958 to 1962) was Mao's plan to quickly industrialize China. It caused a terrible famine in which tens of millions of people died.
what was the cultural revolution | explain the cultural revolution => The Cultural Revolution (1966 to 1976) was a movement started by Mao to purge his opponents. Teens called Red Guards attacked teachers and officials, and millions suffered.
what happened at tiananmen square | tiananmen square 1989 => In spring 1989 students and workers protested for more freedom in Beijing's Tiananmen Square. On June 3 and 4 the army cleared the area by force, and many people were killed.
what was the long march | explain the long march => The Long March was a retreat of the Chinese Communist army in 1934 and 1935, covering about 10,000 kilometers. It made Mao the party's leader.

what was mohenjo daro | explain mohenjo daro => Mohenjo-daro was one of the largest Indus Valley cities, built around 2500 BC in what is now Pakistan. It had a grid of streets, covered drains and a big public bath.
can we read indus valley writing | indus script => No. The Indus script, found on small seals, has not been deciphered, so we can't read what the Indus people wrote.
what are the vedas | explain the vedas => The Vedas are ancient sacred texts of Hinduism, composed in Sanskrit starting around 1500 BC. The oldest is the Rigveda.
what is sanskrit | explain sanskrit => Sanskrit is an ancient language of India, used for Hindu, Buddhist and Jain texts. It is related to Latin, Greek and English.
what was the maurya empire | who was chandragupta maurya => The Maurya Empire, founded by Chandragupta Maurya around 321 BC, was the first empire to unite most of India.
who was ashoka | what did ashoka do => Ashoka was a Maurya emperor who ruled about 268 to 232 BC. After a bloody war in Kalinga, he turned to Buddhism and nonviolence and carved messages of kindness on pillars across India.
what is the lion capital of ashoka | explain the lion capital of ashoka => The Lion Capital of Ashoka is a sculpture of four lions on top of a pillar Ashoka set up at Sarnath. It's India's national emblem, and its wheel is on India's flag.
what was the gupta empire | golden age of india => The Gupta Empire ruled much of northern India from about AD 320 to 550, a golden age of science, math, art and literature.

where do our numbers come from | what are arabic numerals => The digits 0 to 9 we use came from India and spread to Europe through Arab scholars, which is why they're called Hindu-Arabic numerals.

what was the mughal empire | who were the mughals => The Mughal Empire ruled much of the Indian subcontinent from 1526 to the 1700s, declining until 1857. It was founded by Babur, and its rulers were Muslim, ruling a mostly Hindu land.
who was akbar | what is akbar famous for => Akbar was a Mughal emperor from 1556 to 1605 known for wise rule and religious tolerance, inviting scholars of many faiths to debate at his court.
who built the taj mahal | why was the taj mahal built | when was the taj mahal built => Mughal emperor Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in Agra as a tomb for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died in 1631. It took about 20,000 workers roughly 20 years, finishing around 1653.

what was the east india company | explain the east india company => The British East India Company was a trading company started in 1600 that grew so powerful it had its own army and ruled large parts of India until 1858.
what was the indian rebellion of 1857 | explain the indian rebellion of 1857 => In 1857 Indian soldiers and others rose up against the British East India Company. The revolt was crushed, and the British government took over direct rule of India in 1858.
