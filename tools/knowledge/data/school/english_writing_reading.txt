# Essay and paragraph writing, citing sources, and reading skills. Written by hand.
# tag: school.english.writing
what is the writing process | steps of the writing process | prewriting drafting revising editing publishing => The writing process has five steps: prewriting (brainstorm and plan), drafting, revising (improve ideas and organization), editing (fix grammar and spelling), and publishing (share the final copy).
what is the difference between revising and editing | revising vs editing | revise or edit => Revising improves the content: ideas, order, details and clarity. Editing fixes the surface: spelling, grammar, punctuation and capitalization. Revise first, then edit.
how do you brainstorm ideas for writing | brainstorming techniques | ways to brainstorm => Try listing, freewriting for five minutes without stopping, drawing a mind map, or asking who, what, when, where, why and how about your topic.
what is a graphic organizer | types of graphic organizers | graphic organizer for writing => A graphic organizer is a chart for planning or understanding ideas, like a mind map, Venn diagram, story map, timeline or hamburger paragraph chart.
what is the hamburger paragraph | hamburger paragraph model | paragraph like a sandwich => The hamburger model compares a paragraph to a burger: the top bun is the topic sentence, the fillings are supporting details, and the bottom bun is the concluding sentence.
what is a concluding sentence | how do you end a paragraph | closing sentence => A concluding sentence wraps up a paragraph by restating the main idea in new words or linking to the next paragraph.
what are supporting details | supporting sentences | how to support a main idea => Supporting details are facts, examples, reasons, quotes and explanations that back up the topic sentence.
what is the five paragraph essay | 5 paragraph essay structure | five paragraph essay format => It has an introduction with a thesis, three body paragraphs that each support one point, and a conclusion that restates the thesis and wraps up.
what is a body paragraph | how do you write a body paragraph | body paragraph structure => A body paragraph supports one point of your thesis. Start with a topic sentence, give evidence, explain how the evidence supports your point, and end with a link back.
how to write a hook | types of hooks => A hook is an opening line that grabs the reader. Try a surprising fact, a question, a short story, a vivid description or a strong quote.
what are transition words | transition words list | linking words => Transition words connect ideas: first, next, then, finally (order); also, in addition (adding); however, on the other hand (contrast); because, as a result (cause and effect); for example (examples).
what is a narrative essay | how to write a narrative essay | personal narrative => A narrative essay tells a true story, usually from your own life, with a clear beginning, middle and end, vivid details, and a point or lesson.
what is an expository essay | how to write an expository essay | informative essay => An expository (informative) essay explains a topic using facts, examples and clear organization, without giving your opinion.
what is a persuasive essay | how to write a persuasive essay | opinion essay => A persuasive essay tries to convince the reader. State your position, give strong reasons with evidence, answer the other side, and finish with a call to action.
what is an argumentative essay | argumentative vs persuasive essay | how to write an argument essay => An argumentative essay makes a claim and supports it with evidence and reasoning, and addresses counterclaims fairly. Persuasive essays may lean more on emotion; argumentative ones rely on logic and evidence.
what is a counterargument | how do you write a counterclaim | rebuttal in an essay => A counterargument is the other side's best point. Mention it fairly, then give a rebuttal explaining why your position is still stronger.
what is a compare and contrast essay | how to write a compare and contrast essay | block vs point by point => It explains how two things are alike and different. Organize by block (all about A, then all about B) or point by point (compare A and B on each point in turn).
what is a cause and effect essay | cause and effect writing | how to write cause and effect => It explains why something happened (causes) and what happened because of it (effects), using transitions like because, so, therefore and as a result.
what is a descriptive essay | how to write a descriptive essay | describing a place in writing => A descriptive essay paints a picture of a person, place or event using sensory details, vivid adjectives, strong verbs and figurative language.
what is a literary analysis essay | how to write about a book | literary analysis structure => A literary analysis makes a claim about a text, like how an author develops a theme, and supports it with quotes and explanation. Use present tense when discussing the book.
what is the claim evidence reasoning | cer writing | claim evidence reasoning format => CER stands for Claim, Evidence, Reasoning: make a claim, back it with evidence, then explain how the evidence supports the claim.
what is race writing strategy | race strategy | restate answer cite explain => RACE stands for Restate the question, Answer it, Cite evidence from the text, and Explain how the evidence supports your answer.
how do you use a quote in an essay | integrating quotes | quote sandwich => Use a quote sandwich: introduce the quote, give the quote with a citation, then explain what it shows and why it matters.
what is paraphrasing | how do you paraphrase | paraphrase vs summary => Paraphrasing restates a passage in your own words and sentence structure, about the same length. A summary is shorter and gives only the main ideas. Both still need a citation.
what is an outline | how do you write an outline | essay outline format => An outline is a plan of your writing in order, using headings and indented points, often with Roman numerals for main sections and letters for details.
how do you make writing more interesting | improve my writing | tips for better writing => Use specific nouns and strong verbs, vary sentence length, show rather than tell, cut unneeded words, and read your work aloud to catch awkward parts.
what does show don't tell mean | show not tell | show don't tell examples => It means letting readers see details instead of stating feelings. Instead of "She was scared," write "Her hands shook as she reached for the door."
how do you write dialogue in a story | writing good dialogue | dialogue tips => Keep it natural and short, give each character their own voice, use "said" most of the time, and start a new paragraph for each speaker.
what is a formal vs informal tone | formal writing | informal writing => Formal writing avoids slang, contractions and "you", and uses complete sentences, as in essays. Informal writing is conversational, like texts or friendly emails.
how do you write a news article | news article structure | inverted pyramid => Start with a headline and a lead that answers who, what, when, where and why. Put the most important facts first and details later, called the inverted pyramid.
what is a writing prompt | how do you respond to a writing prompt | answering a prompt => A writing prompt is a question or topic to write about. Underline what it asks you to do, plan briefly, answer every part, and reread the prompt before finishing.
what is a word count | how long is a 500 word essay | how many words per page => A typed, double-spaced page with standard font is about 250 to 300 words, so a 500-word essay is roughly two pages.
# tag: school.english.citing
what is a citation | what does cite mean => A citation tells the reader where information came from. Citing gives credit to the author, lets readers check your facts, and avoids plagiarism.
what is mla format | mla style | how do you format an mla paper => MLA style is used mostly in English and humanities. Papers use 12 pt font, double spacing, 1-inch margins, a header with your last name and page number, and a Works Cited page.
what is apa format | apa style | when do you use apa => APA style is used mostly in science and social sciences. It uses author-date citations like (Smith, 2020) and a References page.
how do you cite a book in mla | mla book citation | mla works cited book => Author's last name, first name. Title of Book in italics. Publisher, year. For example: Lowry, Lois. The Giver. Houghton Mifflin, 1993.
how do you cite a website in mla | mla website citation | mla works cited website => Author (if known). "Title of Page." Website Name, date published, URL. Add an access date if there's no publication date.
what is an in text citation | parenthetical citation | how to cite in the middle of an essay => An in-text citation is a short note in your sentence pointing to the full source. In MLA it's the author's last name and page: (Lowry 45). In APA it's author and year: (Lowry, 1993).
what is a works cited page | works cited vs bibliography | how to make a works cited page => A Works Cited page lists every source you cited, in alphabetical order by author's last name, with a hanging indent. A bibliography may also list sources you read but didn't cite.
what is a hanging indent | how to do a hanging indent | hanging indent citation => A hanging indent keeps the first line of each citation at the margin and indents the following lines. Word processors have a setting for it under paragraph options.
what is a primary source | secondary source | primary vs secondary sources => A primary source is firsthand evidence from the time, like a diary, photo, speech or original data. A secondary source interprets or discusses primary sources, like a textbook or article.
how do you know if a website is reliable | credible sources | how to check if a source is trustworthy => Check who wrote it and their expertise, when it was updated, whether facts are backed by evidence, and whether other trusted sources agree. Sites from universities, museums and established news outlets are often good starts.
can i use wikipedia for research | is wikipedia a good source | citing wikipedia => Wikipedia is a good starting point to learn about a topic and find sources in its references, but most teachers want you to cite the original sources instead.
what is a quotation in an essay | when to quote vs paraphrase | should i quote or paraphrase => Quote when the exact words matter, are powerful, or are being analyzed. Paraphrase when the idea matters more than the wording. Cite either way.
# tag: school.english.reading
what is the main idea | how do you find the main idea | main idea vs theme => The main idea is what a passage is mostly about. Look at the title, first and last sentences, and repeated ideas, then ask "What's the point?" Theme is a story's life lesson.
what is a key detail | key details in reading | main idea and details => Key details are the facts, examples and descriptions that support the main idea.
how do you make an inference | what is inferring | making inferences in reading => An inference is a smart guess using clues from the text plus what you already know. If a character's umbrella is dripping, you can infer it's raining.
what is a prediction in reading | making predictions | how to predict in reading => A prediction is a guess about what will happen next, based on clues. Check it as you read and change it if needed.
what is context clues | how to use context clues | figuring out words from context => Context clues are hints in nearby words that help you work out an unknown word, like definitions, examples, synonyms or opposites in the same sentence.
what is author's purpose | pie author's purpose | persuade inform entertain => Author's purpose is why the author wrote: to Persuade, Inform or Entertain (PIE). Some add "explain" or "describe".
what is text structure | types of text structure | nonfiction text structures => Text structure is how information is organized. Common types are description, sequence, compare and contrast, cause and effect, and problem and solution.
what are text features | nonfiction text features | examples of text features => Text features help you find and understand information: titles, headings, captions, bold words, glossaries, tables of contents, indexes, diagrams, maps and charts.
what is a glossary | glossary vs index | what is an index in a book => A glossary is an alphabetical list of key words with definitions, near the back of a book. An index lists topics with the page numbers where they appear.
how do you summarize a story | somebody wanted but so then | summarizing fiction => Use "Somebody Wanted But So Then": who the character is, what they wanted, what got in the way, what they did, and how it ended.
what is close reading | how to do close reading | annotating a text => Close reading means reading a short passage carefully more than once, annotating key words and ideas, asking questions, and looking for patterns and evidence.
how do you annotate a text | annotation tips | how to mark up a book => Underline key ideas, circle unfamiliar words, write short questions or reactions in the margin, and mark important quotes. Use sticky notes if you can't write in the book.
what is citing textual evidence | how to find text evidence | text evidence examples => Textual evidence is a quote or detail from the text that supports your answer. Use phrases like "The text says..." or "According to the author..." and give the page.
what is fluency in reading | how to read more fluently | reading fluency => Reading fluency is reading smoothly, at a good pace, with expression. Reading aloud, rereading favorite books and listening to audiobooks while following along help.
how do i find books i like | how to find a good book | what book should i read next => Think about books, shows and games you liked, ask a librarian or friends, try different genres, and read the first chapter before deciding. It's fine to stop a book you don't enjoy.
what is the five finger rule for books | is this book too hard | choosing a just right book => Open to a page and read it, putting up a finger for each word you don't know. Zero or one is easy, two or three is just right, and four or five might be too hard for now.
what is a reading log | reading journal | how to keep a reading log => A reading log records what you read: title, author, pages or minutes, and sometimes a short reaction. It helps you track progress and remember books.
