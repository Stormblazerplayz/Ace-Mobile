# Statistics and probability, with tiny worked examples. Written by hand.
# tag: school.math.statistics
how do you find the mean | how to calculate the mean | how do you work out the average of numbers => Add all the values and divide by how many there are. For 6, 8, 10 and 12: the sum is 36 and there are 4 numbers, so the mean is 9.
how do you find the median | how to calculate the median | how do you find the middle number => Put the numbers in order and pick the middle one. For 3, 9, 4, 7, 5 in order (3, 4, 5, 7, 9), the median is 5.
how do you find the median with an even number of values | median of an even set | median when there are two middle numbers => Order the numbers and average the two middle ones. For 2, 4, 6, 10, the middle two are 4 and 6, so the median is 5.
how do you find the mode | how to calculate the mode | what if there are two modes => The mode is the value that appears most often. In 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 7, 7 the mode is 7. A set can have two modes (bimodal), or no mode if nothing repeats.
how do you find the range of a data set | range in statistics | how to work out the range => Subtract the smallest value from the biggest. For 12, 5, 19, 8, the range is 19 - 5 = 14.
how do you remember mean median mode and range | mean median mode range rhyme | trick to remember mean median mode => A popular rhyme: "Hey diddle diddle, the median's the middle, you add and divide for the mean. The mode is the one that appears the most, and the range is the difference between."
which is better mean or median | when to use mean or median | why use the median instead of the mean => Use the median when there are outliers, because one huge or tiny value pulls the mean but barely moves the median. Use the mean when the data is fairly even with no extreme values.
how do outliers affect the mean | does an outlier change the median | effect of outliers => An outlier pulls the mean toward it but hardly changes the median. For 2, 3, 4, 5, 100, the mean is 22.8 but the median is 4.
how do you find a missing number when you know the mean | mean missing value | what number do i need to get an average of => Multiply the mean by how many values there should be to get the total, then subtract the ones you know. To average 80 over 4 tests with 75, 82 and 78 so far, you need 320 - 235 = 85.
how do you find the mean from a frequency table | mean of grouped data | average from a frequency table => Multiply each value by its frequency, add those up, and divide by the total frequency. Values 1, 2, 3 with frequencies 4, 5, 1: (4 + 10 + 3) ÷ 10 = 1.7.
what is a weighted average | how do you calculate a weighted average | weighted grade average => Multiply each value by its weight, add them, and divide by the total weight. If tests count 70% and homework 30%, scores of 80 and 100 give 0.7 × 80 + 0.3 × 100 = 86.
how do you find the interquartile range | how to calculate iqr | interquartile range steps => Order the data, find the median of the lower half (Q1) and of the upper half (Q3), and subtract: IQR = Q3 - Q1. It shows how spread out the middle half of the data is.
how do you make a box plot | how to draw a box and whisker plot | box plot steps => Find the minimum, Q1, median, Q3 and maximum. Draw a number line, a box from Q1 to Q3 with a line at the median, and whiskers out to the minimum and maximum.
how do you find outliers using iqr | 1.5 iqr rule | outlier rule => A value is usually called an outlier if it's more than 1.5 × IQR below Q1 or above Q3. If Q1 = 10 and Q3 = 20, the IQR is 10, so anything under -5 or over 35 is an outlier.
what is the mean absolute deviation | how do you find mad | mean absolute deviation steps => MAD is the average distance of the values from the mean. Find the mean, find how far each value is from it, then average those distances. For 2, 4, 6, 8 (mean 5): distances 3, 1, 1, 3, so MAD = 2.
how do you calculate standard deviation | standard deviation steps | how to work out standard deviation => Find the mean, subtract it from each value and square the results, average those squares (that's the variance), then take the square root. For 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 the mean is 5 and the standard deviation is 2.
what is variance | define variance | variance vs standard deviation => Variance is the average of the squared distances from the mean. Standard deviation is the square root of the variance, which puts it back in the same units as the data.
what is a normal distribution | bell curve | what does a bell curve mean => A normal distribution is a symmetric, bell-shaped curve where most values are near the mean. About 68% of values fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean and about 95% within 2.
what does skewed mean in statistics | skewed left vs skewed right | what is a skewed distribution => A skewed distribution has a long tail on one side. Skewed right means the tail stretches to the higher values (mean usually above the median); skewed left means it stretches to the lower values.
what is a biased sample | define sampling bias | how to avoid bias in a survey => A biased sample doesn't fairly represent the whole group. Asking only people at a gym how often they exercise is biased. Random sampling helps avoid it.
what is a random sample | define random sample | why use a random sample => In a random sample, every member of the population has an equal chance of being chosen, like drawing names from a hat. It helps the sample represent the whole group.
what is a survey question that is biased | leading question | example of a biased question => A biased (leading) question pushes people toward an answer, like "Don't you agree that school lunches are too short?" A fair version is "How do you feel about the length of school lunch?"
how do you read a pie chart | how to find a value from a pie chart | pie chart percentages => Each slice's angle or percent shows its share of the whole. If 200 people were surveyed and a slice is 25%, that slice is 50 people. Each 1% is 3.6° of the circle.
how do you make a pie chart | how to work out angles for a pie chart | pie chart angles => Divide each category's count by the total and multiply by 360° to get its angle. If 10 of 40 students like soccer, that slice is 10/40 × 360 = 90°.
what makes a graph misleading | misleading graphs | how can a graph be misleading => A graph can mislead by not starting the axis at 0, using uneven scales, using pictures of different sizes, or leaving out data. Always check the axis labels and scales.
what is the difference between a bar graph and a histogram | bar chart vs histogram | when to use a histogram => A bar graph compares separate categories, like favorite sports, with gaps between bars. A histogram shows how numerical data falls into ranges, like heights from 140 to 150 cm, and its bars touch.
what does correlation not causation mean | correlation vs causation | correlation does not mean causation => Two things can rise and fall together without one causing the other. Ice cream sales and sunburns both go up in summer, but ice cream doesn't cause sunburn; hot sunny weather affects both.
what is a two way table | how to read a two way table | two way frequency table => A two-way table counts data in two categories at once, with rows for one and columns for the other, like grade level versus favorite subject. The totals are at the ends of each row and column.
what is a cumulative frequency | how do you find cumulative frequency | running total frequency => Cumulative frequency is a running total. If frequencies are 3, 5 and 2, the cumulative frequencies are 3, 8 and 10.
what are measures of center | measures of central tendency | what are measures of spread => Measures of center (mean, median, mode) describe a typical value. Measures of spread (range, IQR, MAD, standard deviation) describe how spread out the data is.
what is a census | census vs sample | define census in statistics => A census collects data from every member of a population, while a sample collects from only some. A census is more complete but usually much harder and costlier.
what is the difference between discrete and continuous data | discrete vs continuous data | is height discrete or continuous => Discrete data can only be certain values, usually counts like the number of pets. Continuous data can be any value in a range, like height or time. Height is continuous.
# tag: school.math.probability
how do you calculate probability | probability formula | how to find the probability of something => Probability = number of ways it can happen ÷ total number of equally likely outcomes. The chance of rolling a 4 on a die is 1/6.
what is the probability of rolling a 6 | chance of rolling a six on a dice | probability of rolling a certain number => On a fair six-sided die, each number has a probability of 1/6, about 16.7%.
what is the probability of flipping heads | chance of getting heads | coin flip probability => For a fair coin, the probability of heads is 1/2, or 50%. Each flip is independent, so past flips don't change it.
what is the probability of flipping two heads in a row | chance of two heads | probability of heads twice => Multiply the chances: 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4, or 25%. For three heads in a row it's 1/8.
what is the probability of rolling a sum of 7 with two dice | most common roll with two dice | two dice sum probability => 7 is the most likely sum with two dice. There are 6 ways to make it out of 36 outcomes, so the probability is 6/36 = 1/6.
how many outcomes are there with two dice | two dice sample space | how many ways can two dice land => There are 6 × 6 = 36 outcomes when you roll two six-sided dice.
what is the probability of drawing an ace | chance of drawing an ace from a deck | deck of cards probability => A standard deck has 52 cards with 4 aces, so the probability is 4/52 = 1/13, about 7.7%.
how many cards are in a deck | what is in a deck of cards | standard deck of cards => A standard deck has 52 cards: 4 suits (hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades) with 13 cards each (ace, 2 to 10, jack, queen, king). Hearts and diamonds are red; clubs and spades are black.
what is the probability of not happening | complement probability | probability of something not happening => Subtract from 1. If the chance of rain is 0.3, the chance of no rain is 1 - 0.3 = 0.7.
what is theoretical probability | theoretical vs experimental probability | what is experimental probability => Theoretical probability is what should happen based on math, like 1/2 for heads. Experimental probability is what actually happened in trials, like 27 heads in 50 flips = 27/50.
what is the law of large numbers | why do more trials get closer to the real probability | law of large numbers example => The law of large numbers says that the more times you repeat an experiment, the closer the results get to the theoretical probability. 10 coin flips might give 7 heads, but 10,000 flips will be very close to half.
what are independent events | define independent events | independent events probability => Independent events don't affect each other, like flipping a coin and rolling a die. To find both happening, multiply: P(heads and 6) = 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.
what are dependent events | define dependent events | dependent events probability example => Dependent events affect each other, like drawing two cards without putting the first back. The chance of two aces is 4/52 × 3/51 = 1/221.
what are mutually exclusive events | define mutually exclusive | mutually exclusive probability => Mutually exclusive events can't happen at the same time, like rolling a 2 and rolling a 5 on one roll. To find either one, add: 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3.
what is the addition rule for probability | probability of a or b | or rule probability => P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). For drawing a heart or a king: 13/52 + 4/52 - 1/52 = 16/52 = 4/13.
what is a tree diagram | how do you use a tree diagram | probability tree => A tree diagram shows every outcome by branching for each step. Multiply along a path for that combined outcome, and add paths for "or". Two coin flips make 4 paths: HH, HT, TH, TT.
what is the fundamental counting principle | counting principle | how many outfits can i make => Multiply the number of choices at each step. With 3 shirts and 4 pants, you can make 3 × 4 = 12 outfits.
what is a permutation | permutation formula | how many ways can you arrange => A permutation is an arrangement where order matters. The ways to arrange 3 books on a shelf are 3! = 6. Choosing and ordering 2 from 5 is 5 × 4 = 20.
what is a combination | combination formula | how many ways can you choose => A combination is a selection where order doesn't matter. Choosing 2 friends from 5 gives 5 × 4 ÷ 2 = 10 combinations, written 5C2 = 10.
what is the difference between permutation and combination | permutation vs combination | when does order matter => In a permutation order matters (1st, 2nd, 3rd place); in a combination it doesn't (picking a team). There are always at least as many permutations as combinations.
what does odds mean | odds vs probability | how do you find odds => Odds compare ways to win against ways to lose, while probability compares ways to win against all outcomes. Rolling a 6 has odds of 1 to 5 but a probability of 1/6.
what is expected value | define expected value | how do you calculate expected value => Expected value is the average result you'd expect over many tries: multiply each outcome by its probability and add. A game paying $6 on a roll of 6 (and $0 otherwise) has an expected value of 6 × 1/6 = $1 per roll.
what is the gamblers fallacy | does a coin remember past flips | if i got tails 5 times is heads due => No, a fair coin has no memory. Even after 5 tails, the next flip is still 50% heads. Thinking it's "due" is called the gambler's fallacy.
what is the probability scale | probability words impossible to certain | likely unlikely probability => Probability goes from 0 to 1. 0 is impossible, around 0.25 is unlikely, 0.5 is an even chance, around 0.75 is likely, and 1 is certain.
can probability be more than 1 | can a probability be negative | probability range => No. A probability is always from 0 to 1 (0% to 100%). If you get more than 1 or a negative number, there's a mistake in the working.
what is the birthday problem | birthday paradox | how many people for two to share a birthday => In a group of just 23 people, there's slightly over a 50% chance that two share a birthday. With 70 people it's over 99.9%. It feels surprising because there are so many possible pairs.
what is conditional probability | probability of a given b | conditional probability formula => Conditional probability is the chance of A happening given that B already happened, written P(A|B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B). If you know a card is red, the chance it's a heart is 1/2.
what is a simulation in probability | how do you simulate an experiment | probability simulation => A simulation uses a model, like a random number generator, coins or a spinner, to act out an event many times and estimate its probability.
what is the monty hall problem | should you switch doors | monty hall explained => In the Monty Hall game, you pick 1 of 3 doors, the host opens a losing door, and you can switch. Switching wins 2/3 of the time, because your first pick only had a 1/3 chance.
