# More grammar terms and literary terms. Written by hand.
# tag: school.english.grammar-terms
what is a noun phrase | noun phrase examples | define noun phrase => A noun phrase is a noun plus the words that describe it, acting as one unit: "the tall old tree" or "a box of crayons".
what is a verb phrase | verb phrase examples | define verb phrase => A verb phrase is a main verb plus its helping verbs: "has been running" or "will finish".
what is a phrasal verb | phrasal verb examples | define phrasal verb => A phrasal verb is a verb plus a small word like up, out or off that changes its meaning: give up (quit), look after (care for), run out (have none left).
what is a possessive adjective | my your his her | possessive determiners => Possessive adjectives (also called possessive determiners) come before a noun to show ownership: my, your, his, her, its, our, their. "My bike" but "the bike is mine".
what is an object pronoun | object pronouns list | me him her us them => Object pronouns receive the action or follow prepositions: me, you, him, her, it, us, them. "She called me." "Give it to them."
what is a quantifier in grammar | quantifiers examples | some any much many => Quantifiers tell how much or how many: some, any, many, much, few, little, several, a lot of, all and enough.
when do you use much or many | much vs many | many or much => Use many with countable nouns (many books) and much with uncountable nouns (much water). In questions and negatives: "How much time?" "Not many people."
when do you use some or any | some vs any | any or some => Use some in positive statements (I have some) and offers. Use any in most questions and negatives (Do you have any? I don't have any).
what is a conjunctive adverb | however therefore moreover | conjunctive adverbs list => A conjunctive adverb links two ideas, like however, therefore, meanwhile, moreover and otherwise. Between two sentences, use a semicolon before it and a comma after.
what is a superlative | superlative examples | define superlative => A superlative shows the highest degree, comparing three or more: biggest, best, most beautiful. It usually goes with "the": "the tallest tree".
what is a comparative | comparative examples | define comparative => A comparative compares two things: bigger, better, more careful. It's often followed by "than": "taller than me".
what is an adverbial | adverbial phrase | adverbial clause => An adverbial is a word, phrase or clause that works like an adverb, telling when, where, how or why: "after school", "very quickly", "because it rained".
what is a fronted adverbial | fronted adverbial examples | adverbial at the start => A fronted adverbial is an adverbial moved to the start of a sentence, followed by a comma: "Later that day, we went swimming."
what is a modifier | modifier in grammar | define modifier => A modifier is a word or phrase that describes or limits another word, like an adjective or adverb. In "the very old house", "very" and "old" are modifiers.
what is an expletive in grammar | there is sentences | it is sentences grammar => In grammar, an expletive is a filler word like "there" or "it" that starts a sentence without meaning anything itself: "There are three cats." "It is raining."
what is ellipsis in grammar | leaving words out grammar | elliptical sentence => Grammatical ellipsis leaves out words that are understood: "I can swim, and she can too" means "she can swim too".
what is a complement in grammar | object complement | complement examples => A complement completes the meaning of a verb. An object complement describes the object: in "They named the dog Max," Max is the object complement.
what is the base form of a verb | bare infinitive | root form of a verb => The base form is the verb with nothing added, like go, eat or play. It's used after "to" (to go) and after modals (can go).
what is a stative verb | action vs stative verbs | state verbs examples => Stative verbs describe states, not actions: know, love, believe, belong, seem. They usually aren't used in -ing forms: say "I know", not "I am knowing".
what is reported speech | direct vs indirect speech | indirect speech examples => Direct speech quotes exact words: She said, "I'm tired." Reported (indirect) speech retells them: She said that she was tired. The tense often shifts back.
what is a tag question | question tags | isn't it questions => A tag question is a short question added to the end of a statement: "It's cold, isn't it?" "You like pizza, don't you?"
what is a gerund phrase | gerund phrase examples | define gerund phrase => A gerund phrase is a gerund with its objects and modifiers, acting as a noun: "Reading comic books is my hobby."
what is an infinitive phrase | infinitive phrase examples | define infinitive phrase => An infinitive phrase is an infinitive with its objects and modifiers: "To win the game, we practiced every day."
what is a participial phrase | participial phrase examples | define participial phrase => A participial phrase starts with a participle and describes a noun: "Running down the street, the dog chased the ball."
what is a noun clause | noun clause examples | define noun clause => A noun clause is a dependent clause that acts as a noun: "I know that you're right." "What she said surprised me."
what is word order in english | subject verb object | english sentence order => English usually follows Subject-Verb-Object order: "The cat (S) chased (V) the mouse (O)." Changing the order can change the meaning.
what is a sentence opener | ways to start a sentence | varying sentence beginnings => Sentence openers vary how sentences start: with an adverb (Suddenly,), a prepositional phrase (In the morning,), a dependent clause (When it rained,) or an -ing phrase (Smiling,).
what makes a complete sentence | complete sentence rules | is this a complete sentence => A complete sentence has a subject, a verb, and expresses a complete thought. It starts with a capital letter and ends with a period, question mark or exclamation mark.
# tag: school.english.literary-terms
what is an allegory | allegory examples | define allegory => An allegory is a story where characters and events stand for bigger ideas, often moral or political. Animal Farm is an allegory about power and government.
what is a parable | define parable | parable vs fable => A parable is a short story that teaches a moral or spiritual lesson using people. A fable usually uses talking animals.
what is a motif | motif in literature | motif vs theme => A motif is an image, idea or symbol that keeps coming back in a story, like repeated mentions of birds or darkness. Motifs help develop the theme.
what is juxtaposition | juxtaposition examples | define juxtaposition => Juxtaposition places two very different things side by side to highlight their contrast, like a beggar outside a palace.
what is a paradox | paradox examples | define paradox => A paradox is a statement that seems to contradict itself but may be true, like "Less is more" or "I'm a compulsive liar."
what is anthropomorphism | anthropomorphism vs personification | anthropomorphism examples => Anthropomorphism makes animals or objects act fully like humans, as in talking, dressed-up animals. Personification just gives something a human quality for a moment, like "the wind howled".
what is a prologue | epilogue | prologue vs epilogue => A prologue is an introduction before the main story begins. An epilogue comes after the ending and shows what happened later.
prequel | sequel vs prequel => A sequel continues a story after it ends. A prequel tells events that happened before the original story.
what is a trilogy | define trilogy | trilogy examples => A trilogy is a set of three related books or films, like The Lord of the Rings, which was published in three volumes.
what is an anthology | define anthology | anthology book => An anthology is a collection of stories, poems or essays by different writers, gathered in one book.
what is a plot twist | define plot twist | twist ending => A plot twist is a surprising change in the direction of a story that the reader didn't expect.
what is a red herring | red herring in literature | red herring in mystery => A red herring is a false clue that leads readers or characters away from the real answer, common in mysteries.
what is in medias res | starting in the middle of the action | in media res meaning => In medias res is Latin for "in the middle of things". A story that starts in medias res begins in the middle of the action and explains earlier events later.
what is a frame story | story within a story | frame narrative examples => A frame story is an outer story that holds one or more inner stories, like a character telling tales to others, as in One Thousand and One Nights.
what is stream of consciousness | stream of consciousness writing | define stream of consciousness => Stream of consciousness is a writing style that follows a character's thoughts as they flow, often jumping between ideas without neat order.
what is an epigraph | define epigraph | quote at the start of a book => An epigraph is a short quotation at the start of a book or chapter that hints at its theme.
what is an anecdote | define anecdote | anecdote examples => An anecdote is a short, interesting true story about a real person or event, often used to make a point in a speech or essay.
what is a narrative hook | define narrative hook | hook in a story => A narrative hook is an opening that grabs the reader's attention and makes them want to keep reading, like a mystery, danger or a surprising line.
what is pacing in a story | define pacing | how do writers control pacing => Pacing is how fast or slow a story moves. Short sentences and quick action speed it up; long descriptions and reflection slow it down.
what is atmosphere in literature | atmosphere vs mood | define atmosphere => Atmosphere is the feeling created by the setting and description, like a spooky old house at night. It's closely related to mood.
what is diction | define diction | word choice in writing => Diction is an author's word choice. Formal or casual, simple or fancy, it shapes the tone and voice of a piece.
what is syntax in writing | define syntax | syntax vs diction => Syntax is how words are arranged into sentences: length, order and structure. Diction is which words are chosen.
what is a dramatic monologue | define dramatic monologue | dramatic monologue poem => A dramatic monologue is a poem where one character speaks to a silent listener, revealing their personality as they talk.
what is a protagonist's flaw | tragic flaw | define hamartia => A tragic flaw (hamartia) is a weakness in a hero, like pride or jealousy, that leads to their downfall.
what is a character arc | define character arc | how characters change => A character arc is the change a character goes through from the beginning to the end of a story.
what is a subplot | define subplot | subplot examples => A subplot is a smaller, side story that runs alongside the main plot, often involving secondary characters.
what is a stock character | define stock character | stock character examples => A stock character is a familiar type used over and over, like the evil stepmother, the mad scientist or the wise old mentor.
what is a hero vs antihero | define antihero | antihero examples => A hero is brave and good. An antihero is a main character who lacks some usual heroic qualities, like being selfish or rule-breaking, but readers still root for them.
what is a dystopia | utopia vs dystopia | dystopian fiction => A utopia is an imagined perfect society. A dystopia is an imagined society that's frightening or unfair, often presented as perfect, like in The Giver or The Hunger Games.
what is a graphic novel | graphic novel vs comic | define graphic novel => A graphic novel tells a full, book-length story in comic form, with panels of art and speech bubbles.
what is a novel in verse | verse novel | define novel in verse => A novel in verse tells a full story through a series of poems instead of prose, like The Crossover by Kwame Alexander.
what is prose | prose vs poetry | define prose => Prose is ordinary written language in sentences and paragraphs, like novels and essays. Poetry is arranged in lines and often uses rhythm and sound patterns.
what is a playwright | parts of a play | acts and scenes => A playwright writes plays. Plays are divided into acts and smaller scenes, and the script includes dialogue and stage directions.
define stage directions | stage directions in a play => Stage directions are notes in a play's script, often in italics or brackets, telling actors how to move, speak or what the set looks like.
what is a dramatic irony example in romeo and juliet | romeo and juliet irony | irony in shakespeare => In Romeo and Juliet, the audience knows from the opening lines that the lovers will die, but the characters don't, which creates dramatic irony throughout.
