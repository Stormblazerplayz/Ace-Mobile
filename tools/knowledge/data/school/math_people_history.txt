# Famous mathematicians and math history. Written by hand; dates are well established.
# tag: school.math.mathematicians
who was euclid | what is euclid famous for | father of geometry => Euclid was a Greek mathematician in Alexandria around 300 BC, often called the father of geometry. His book Elements was used to teach geometry for over 2,000 years.
what is euclid's elements | elements by euclid | what book did euclid write => Elements is Euclid's 13-book work on geometry and numbers, written around 300 BC. It builds everything from a few basic assumptions and was one of the most widely used textbooks ever.
what does eureka mean | why did archimedes say eureka | eureka story => "Eureka" is Greek for "I have found it!" The story says Archimedes shouted it when he realized, in the bath, that he could measure an object's volume by the water it pushes aside.
how did archimedes estimate pi | archimedes and pi | archimedes polygon method => Archimedes fitted 96-sided polygons inside and outside a circle and showed pi is between 3 10/71 and 3 1/7, about 3.1408 to 3.1429.
how to find primes with a sieve | sieve method primes => List the numbers from 2 up, circle 2 and cross out its multiples, then circle the next uncrossed number and cross out its multiples, and repeat. The circled numbers are the primes.
who was hypatia | what is hypatia famous for | first famous woman mathematician => Hypatia (died AD 415) was a mathematician, astronomer and teacher in Alexandria. She is the earliest woman mathematician whose life is fairly well recorded.
who was fibonacci | what is fibonacci famous for | leonardo of pisa => Fibonacci (Leonardo of Pisa, about 1170 to 1250) was an Italian mathematician. His book Liber Abaci helped bring Hindu-Arabic numerals to Europe and includes the rabbit puzzle behind the Fibonacci sequence.
who was brahmagupta | who invented zero as a number | who made rules for zero => Brahmagupta was an Indian mathematician who, in AD 628, wrote rules for calculating with zero and negative numbers. He was one of the first to treat zero as a number in its own right.
who was pierre de fermat | what is fermat famous for | fermat mathematician => Pierre de Fermat (1601 to 1665) was a French lawyer who did math as a hobby. He helped start probability theory and number theory and is famous for Fermat's Last Theorem.
what is fermat's last theorem | who proved fermat's last theorem | aⁿ + bⁿ = cⁿ no solutions => It says aⁿ + bⁿ = cⁿ has no whole-number solutions when n is greater than 2. Fermat wrote it in a margin around 1637, and Andrew Wiles finally proved it in 1994.
who was blaise pascal | what is pascal famous for | pascal mathematician => Blaise Pascal (1623 to 1662) was a French mathematician and scientist. He built one of the first mechanical calculators, worked with Fermat on probability, and studied the number triangle named after him.
who invented calculus | newton or leibniz | who discovered calculus => Isaac Newton and Gottfried Leibniz each developed calculus on their own in the late 1600s. The notation we use today, like dy/dx and the ∫ sign, mostly comes from Leibniz.
what is euler's identity | e to the i pi plus 1 equals 0 | most beautiful equation in math => Euler's identity is e^(iπ) + 1 = 0. People call it beautiful because it links five key numbers (e, i, π, 1 and 0) in one short equation.
what are the seven bridges of konigsberg | konigsberg bridge problem | euler bridges puzzle => The puzzle asked if you could walk through Königsberg crossing each of its 7 bridges exactly once. In 1736 Euler proved it was impossible, starting graph theory.
who was sophie germain | what is sophie germain famous for | sophie germain primes => Sophie Germain (1776 to 1831) was a French mathematician who taught herself and at first wrote under a man's name. She worked on Fermat's Last Theorem and the math of vibrating surfaces.
who was ada lovelace | first computer programmer | what is ada lovelace famous for => Ada Lovelace (1815 to 1852) was an English mathematician. She wrote notes on Charles Babbage's planned Analytical Engine, including steps to calculate numbers, so she's often called the first computer programmer.
who was george boole | what is boolean logic named after | boole mathematician => George Boole (1815 to 1864) was an English mathematician who created an algebra of logic using true and false. Boolean logic, used in every computer, is named after him.
who was evariste galois | what is galois famous for | galois theory => Évariste Galois (1811 to 1832) was a French mathematician who created group theory ideas to explain which equations can be solved with formulas. He died in a duel at age 20.
who was bernhard riemann | what is the riemann hypothesis | riemann mathematician => Bernhard Riemann (1826 to 1866) was a German mathematician who shaped geometry and calculus. The Riemann hypothesis, about where the zeros of a special function lie, is one of the most famous unsolved problems.
who was georg cantor | different sizes of infinity | cantor infinity => Georg Cantor (1845 to 1918) was a German mathematician who founded set theory. He proved some infinities are bigger than others, for example there are more real numbers than whole numbers.
who was emmy noether | what is emmy noether famous for | noether theorem => Emmy Noether (1882 to 1935) was a German mathematician who transformed abstract algebra. Noether's theorem links symmetry in physics to conservation laws, like conservation of energy.
who was srinivasa ramanujan | what is ramanujan famous for | the man who knew infinity => Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887 to 1920) was a mostly self-taught Indian mathematician. He found thousands of remarkable formulas, many about infinite series and partitions, and worked with G. H. Hardy in Cambridge.
what is the hardy ramanujan number | why is 1729 special | taxicab number => 1729 is the smallest number that's the sum of two cubes in two different ways: 1³ + 12³ and 9³ + 10³. Ramanujan noticed this when Hardy mentioned his taxi's number.
who was kurt godel | godel incompleteness | godel mathematician => Kurt Gödel (1906 to 1978) was an Austrian logician. His incompleteness theorems showed that in any rich enough math system there are true statements that can't be proved within it.
who was maryam mirzakhani | first woman fields medal | mirzakhani mathematician => Maryam Mirzakhani (1977 to 2017) was an Iranian mathematician at Stanford. In 2014 she became the first woman to win the Fields Medal, for work on curved surfaces.
who is terence tao | terence tao mathematician | youngest math olympiad gold => Terence Tao (born 1975) is an Australian-American mathematician at UCLA. He won the Fields Medal in 2006 and, at 13, became the youngest gold medalist in International Math Olympiad history.
who was john napier | who invented logarithms | napier's bones => John Napier (1550 to 1617) was a Scottish mathematician who invented logarithms, which made big calculations much faster. He also made "Napier's bones", rods for multiplying.
who was andrew wiles | who solved fermat's last theorem | andrew wiles proof => Andrew Wiles (born 1953) is an English mathematician who proved Fermat's Last Theorem in 1994, after working on it in secret for about seven years. He first read about the problem at age 10.
who was grigori perelman | poincare conjecture solved | perelman refused prize => Grigori Perelman (born 1966) is a Russian mathematician who proved the Poincaré conjecture around 2003. He turned down both the Fields Medal and a million-dollar prize.
who was paul erdos | what is an erdos number | erdos mathematician => Paul Erdős (1913 to 1996) was a Hungarian mathematician who wrote around 1,500 papers with hundreds of partners. Your Erdős number is how many co-author links separate you from him.
who was thales | thales of miletus => Thales of Miletus (about 624 to 546 BC) was one of the first Greek mathematicians. Thales' theorem says any triangle drawn in a semicircle, with the diameter as one side, has a right angle.
who was zeno | zeno's paradoxes | achilles and the tortoise => Zeno of Elea was a Greek thinker around 450 BC who made paradoxes about motion. In one, speedy Achilles seems never to catch a tortoise, because he must first reach where it was. Infinite series explain it.
who was omar khayyam | omar khayyam math | khayyam cubic equations => Omar Khayyam (1048 to 1131) was a Persian mathematician, astronomer and poet. He found geometric ways to solve cubic equations and helped make a very accurate calendar.
who was david hilbert | hilbert's problems | hilbert hotel => David Hilbert (1862 to 1943) was a German mathematician. In 1900 he listed 23 big unsolved problems that guided math for a century, and his "Hilbert's hotel" puzzle shows how strange infinity is.
who was florence nightingale in math | florence nightingale statistics | nightingale rose chart => Besides nursing, Florence Nightingale (1820 to 1910) was a pioneer of statistics. She used her "rose" diagrams to show that most soldiers in the Crimean War died from disease, not wounds.
who was john nash | a beautiful mind mathematician | nash equilibrium => John Nash (1928 to 2015) was an American mathematician known for the Nash equilibrium in game theory. He won the Nobel economics prize in 1994, and the film A Beautiful Mind tells his story.
who was mary somerville | mary somerville scientist | somerville mathematician => Mary Somerville (1780 to 1872) was a Scottish mathematician and science writer. She and Caroline Herschel became the first women members of the Royal Astronomical Society, in 1835.
who was augusta ada king | is ada lovelace the same as ada king | ada lovelace real name => Yes. Ada Lovelace's full name was Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace. She was the daughter of the poet Lord Byron.
who was benoit mandelbrot | who discovered fractals | mandelbrot set => Benoit Mandelbrot (1924 to 2010) was a Polish-born French-American mathematician who named and popularized fractals. The Mandelbrot set is a famous fractal shape named after him.
who was niels henrik abel | abel prize named after | abel mathematician => Niels Henrik Abel (1802 to 1829) was a Norwegian mathematician who proved that general equations of degree 5 can't be solved by a formula with roots. The Abel Prize is named after him.
who was john venn | who invented venn diagrams | venn diagram inventor => John Venn (1834 to 1923) was an English logician who popularized the overlapping-circle diagrams now called Venn diagrams, in 1880.
who was august mobius | mobius strip inventor | who discovered the mobius strip => August Möbius (1790 to 1868) was a German mathematician. He described the Möbius strip, a loop with a half twist that has only one side and one edge. Johann Listing found it independently the same year.
# tag: school.math.history
what is the fields medal | biggest prize in math | is there a nobel prize for math => There's no Nobel Prize for math. The Fields Medal, given every four years to up to four mathematicians under 40, is often called math's top honor, along with the Abel Prize.
what is the abel prize | abel prize math | abel prize vs fields medal => The Abel Prize is a yearly award for lifetime achievement in math, given by Norway since 2003. Unlike the Fields Medal, it has no age limit.
what are the millennium prize problems | million dollar math problems | unsolved math problems with prizes => In 2000 the Clay Mathematics Institute named seven hard problems with a $1 million prize each. Only one, the Poincaré conjecture, has been solved so far, by Grigori Perelman.
where did zero come from | history of zero => Zero as a placeholder was used by the Babylonians and Maya, but treating zero as a number to calculate with developed in India. Brahmagupta wrote rules for it in AD 628.
hindu arabic numerals | why are they called arabic numerals => The digits 0 to 9 were developed in India, spread through the Arab world, and reached Europe around the 1200s. That's why they're called Hindu-Arabic numerals.
base 60 history | babylonian number system => The Babylonians counted in base 60 about 4,000 years ago. Their system survives in 60 seconds per minute, 60 minutes per hour and the 360 degrees of a circle.
what is an abacus | how does an abacus work | history of the abacus => An abacus is a counting frame with beads on rods, each rod standing for a place value. People across Asia, Europe and the Middle East used versions of it for thousands of years.
what is the rhind papyrus | ancient egyptian math | egyptian math papyrus => The Rhind papyrus is an Egyptian math scroll from about 1550 BC, copied from an older text. It shows fractions, area and volume problems, and an estimate of pi near 3.16.
how did ancient egyptians write fractions | egyptian fractions | unit fractions => Ancient Egyptians wrote most fractions as sums of different unit fractions (fractions with 1 on top). For example, 3/4 was written as 1/2 + 1/4.
what is a mayan number system | how did the maya count | maya numerals => The Maya counted mostly in base 20, using dots for 1, bars for 5 and a shell sign for zero. They were one of the first peoples to use a zero.
who first used the equals sign | where did the equals sign come from | history of equals sign => Robert Recorde, a Welsh mathematician, introduced the = sign in 1557. He used two parallel lines because "no two things can be more equal".
where did the plus and minus signs come from | history of plus sign | who invented plus and minus => The + and - signs first appeared in print in a German book by Johannes Widmann in 1489. The + probably comes from a shortened Latin "et", meaning "and".
when was pi first called pi | who named pi | history of the pi symbol => William Jones first used the Greek letter π for the circle ratio in 1706, and Euler made it popular in the 1730s. The ratio itself had been studied for thousands of years.
how many digits of pi are known | how far has pi been calculated | digits of pi record => Computers have calculated pi to well over 100 trillion digits. For most science, about 40 digits is more than enough.
first mechanical calculator | history of calculators => Wilhelm Schickard designed a calculating machine in 1623, and Blaise Pascal built his "Pascaline" in the 1640s. Pocket electronic calculators became common in the 1970s.
eniac | history of computers math => ENIAC, finished in 1945 in the US, was one of the first general-purpose electronic computers. Earlier, Charles Babbage designed mechanical computers in the 1800s that were never fully built in his lifetime.
when did people start using the decimal point | history of decimals | who invented decimals => Simon Stevin, a Flemish mathematician, popularized decimal fractions in Europe in 1585. The dot style of decimal point spread in the 1600s, partly through John Napier.
what is the golden ratio history | why is it called the golden ratio | phi number => The golden ratio, about 1.618, is written with the Greek letter φ (phi). Euclid described it around 300 BC, and the name "golden" became popular in the 1800s.
what is the four color theorem | four colour map problem | how many colors to color a map => The four color theorem says any flat map can be colored with just four colors so no neighboring regions share a color. It was proved in 1976 with heavy help from a computer.
what is the poincare conjecture | poincare conjecture simple | who was henri poincare => Henri Poincaré was a French mathematician who in 1904 asked whether a certain kind of 3D space must be a "sphere". Grigori Perelman proved it was, around 2003.
what is the twin prime conjecture | twin primes unsolved | are there infinite twin primes => Twin primes are primes 2 apart, like 11 and 13. The twin prime conjecture says there are infinitely many, but nobody has proved it yet.
what is goldbach's conjecture | every even number is two primes | goldbach unsolved => Goldbach's conjecture says every even number greater than 2 is the sum of two primes, like 10 = 3 + 7. It's been checked for huge numbers but never proved.
what is the collatz conjecture | 3n plus 1 problem | collatz unsolved => Start with any whole number: if it's even, halve it; if odd, triple it and add 1. The Collatz conjecture says you always reach 1 eventually. It's easy to state but still unproved.
what is the international math olympiad | imo math competition | math olympiad => The International Mathematical Olympiad is a yearly problem-solving contest for high school students from over 100 countries. It started in Romania in 1959.
