# More math topics: number systems, number theory, more statistics and probability, money math, estimation, logic puzzles. Written by hand.
# tag: school.math.number-systems
what is base 10 | why do we count in tens | what is the decimal number system => Base 10 (the decimal system) uses ten digits, 0 to 9, and each place is worth 10 times the place to its right. We probably count in tens because we have ten fingers.
what is a number base | what does base mean in number systems | different number bases => A number base is how many digits a system uses before moving to the next place. Base 10 uses 0 to 9, base 2 (binary) uses 0 and 1, and base 16 (hexadecimal) uses 0 to 9 and A to F.
how do you read a binary number | binary place values | what is 1011 in binary => Binary place values double from the right: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16... So 1011 in binary is 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11.
what are ordinal numbers | cardinal vs ordinal numbers | ordinal number examples => Cardinal numbers tell how many (one, two, three). Ordinal numbers tell position (first, second, third, or 1st, 2nd, 3rd).
what is a nominal number | numbers used as names | nominal number examples => A nominal number names or identifies something instead of counting it, like a jersey number or a zip code.
what is the difference between a number and a numeral | numeral vs number | what is a numeral => A number is the idea of an amount; a numeral is the symbol used to write it. The number five can be written with the numeral 5 or V.
what is the hindu arabic number system | why is our number system place value | place value system history => Our number system uses ten digits and place value, so the same digit means different amounts in different places. It came from India through the Arab world.
# tag: school.math.number-theory
what is modular arithmetic | clock math | what does mod mean in math => Modular arithmetic works with remainders, like a clock that wraps around. 15 mod 12 = 3, which is why 15:00 is 3 o'clock.
what is a digital root | how do you find a digital root | add the digits until one digit => Add a number's digits, and keep adding until you get one digit. For 4,729: 4 + 7 + 2 + 9 = 22, and 2 + 2 = 4. It's a quick check for divisibility by 9 and by 3.
what is casting out nines | casting out nines check | check multiplication with nines => Casting out nines checks arithmetic using digital roots. If the digital roots of the problem don't match the digital root of your answer, you made a mistake.
what are relatively prime numbers | coprime numbers | what does coprime mean => Two numbers are relatively prime (coprime) if their only common factor is 1, like 8 and 15. They don't have to be prime themselves.
what is the fundamental theorem of arithmetic | unique prime factorization | every number is a product of primes => It says every whole number greater than 1 is either prime or can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way, apart from order. 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.
are there infinitely many primes | euclid proof of infinite primes | how do we know primes never end => Yes. Euclid proved it around 300 BC: multiply any list of primes together and add 1. The result has a prime factor not on the list, so no list can contain them all.
what is a mersenne prime | mersenne primes examples | 2 to the n minus 1 primes => A Mersenne prime is a prime of the form 2ⁿ - 1, like 3, 7, 31 and 127. The largest known primes are usually Mersenne primes, found by computers.
what are amicable numbers | friendly numbers | 220 and 284 => Amicable numbers are two numbers where each one's factors (not counting itself) add up to the other. The smallest pair is 220 and 284.
what is an abundant number | deficient number | abundant vs deficient numbers => If a number's factors (not counting itself) add up to more than the number, it's abundant, like 12 (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 6 = 16). If they add to less, it's deficient, like 8.
# tag: school.math.statistics-more
what is a percentile | percentile meaning | what does 90th percentile mean => A percentile tells what percent of values are at or below a score. If you're in the 90th percentile, you scored as well as or better than 90% of the group.
what is a z score | z score meaning | how do you find a z score => A z-score tells how many standard deviations a value is from the mean: z = (value - mean) ÷ standard deviation. A z-score of 2 means 2 standard deviations above average.
what is a stratified sample | systematic sample | types of sampling => A random sample picks members by chance. A systematic sample picks every nth member. A stratified sample splits the group into types, like grades, and samples from each. A convenience sample just uses whoever is easy to reach, and is often biased.
what is margin of error | margin of error meaning | plus or minus in polls => The margin of error shows how much a poll result might differ from the true value. "52% ± 3%" means the real number is probably between 49% and 55%.
what is the 68 95 99.7 rule | empirical rule | normal distribution percentages => In a normal (bell-shaped) distribution, about 68% of values are within 1 standard deviation of the mean, 95% within 2, and 99.7% within 3.
what is a positive and negative correlation | correlation types | no correlation scatter plot => A positive correlation means both go up together (study time and scores). A negative correlation means one goes up as the other goes down (speed and travel time). No correlation means no clear pattern.
what is an average | is mean the same as average | mean vs average => "Average" usually means the mean: add the values and divide by how many. But median and mode are also kinds of averages, or measures of center.
what is a trend in data | how do you describe a trend | trend line => A trend is the general direction of data over time: increasing, decreasing or staying steady. A trend line sketches that direction through a scatter plot.
what is an experiment vs observational study | observational study | experiment in statistics => In an experiment, researchers change something on purpose and measure the effect. In an observational study, they just watch and record without changing anything.
what is a control group | control group meaning | why have a control group => A control group doesn't get the treatment being tested. Comparing it with the treatment group shows whether the treatment really made a difference.
# tag: school.math.probability-more
what is a fair game | how do you know if a game is fair | fair vs unfair game => A game is fair if every player has the same chance of winning. Rolling a die where evens win for one player and odds for the other is fair.
how do you use a probability to predict | expected number of outcomes | predict how many times => Multiply the probability by the number of trials. If P(rolling a 6) = 1/6, in 60 rolls you'd expect about 10 sixes.
what is a compound event | compound probability | probability of two events => A compound event combines two or more events. For independent events, multiply: the chance of heads and then a 6 is 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.
what is the probability of at least one | at least one probability | probability of at least one six => Use the complement: P(at least one) = 1 - P(none). The chance of at least one 6 in two rolls is 1 - (5/6)² = 11/36, about 31%.
what is a random number | is anything truly random | how do computers make random numbers => A random number is chosen so every possible value is equally likely and unpredictable. Computers usually use formulas that only look random, called pseudorandom numbers.
# tag: school.math.money
what is percent error | percent error formula | how to calculate percent error => Percent error = |measured - actual| ÷ actual × 100. If you measured 9.5 cm and the real length was 10 cm, the error is 0.5 ÷ 10 × 100 = 5%.
how do you calculate a percent of a price after two discounts | stacking discounts | 20 percent off then 10 percent off => Apply them one after the other, not added together. 20% off then 10% off is 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72 of the price, a 28% total discount, not 30%.
how do you calculate hourly pay | hourly wage math | how much do i earn per hour => Multiply the hourly rate by the hours worked. At $12 an hour for 15 hours, you earn $180. To find the hourly rate, divide total pay by hours.
# tag: school.math.estimation
what is a fermi problem | fermi estimate | how do you estimate big numbers => A Fermi problem asks for a rough estimate of something hard to count, like how many piano tuners live in a city. Break it into smaller guesses you can reason about and multiply them.
what is compatible numbers estimation | estimating with compatible numbers | compatible numbers division => Compatible numbers are nearby numbers that are easy to work with. To estimate 437 ÷ 6, use 420 ÷ 6 = 70.
what is a reasonable answer | how do you know if an answer is reasonable | reasonableness in math => An answer is reasonable if it fits the situation and is close to your estimate. A pencil 3 meters long or 1.5 people would be a sign to recheck.
how do you estimate a sum | estimating addition | round to estimate a sum => Round each number to a convenient place, then add. 389 + 512 is about 400 + 500 = 900.
how do you estimate a product | estimating multiplication | round to estimate a product => Round each factor, then multiply. 48 × 21 is about 50 × 20 = 1,000; the exact answer is 1,008.
what is the difference between exact and estimate | exact answer vs estimate | when to estimate => An exact answer is precisely right; an estimate is close enough for the purpose. Estimate when checking work, planning, or when exact numbers aren't needed.
# tag: school.math.logic-puzzles
what is a logic puzzle | types of logic puzzles | logic grid puzzle => A logic puzzle is solved by reasoning from clues. In a logic grid, you mark which pairings are possible or impossible until only one answer fits each row.
what is a kenken puzzle | kenken rules | how to play kenken => KenKen is a grid puzzle where each row and column uses each number once, and outlined groups of cells must make a target number with a given operation.
what is the tower of hanoi | tower of hanoi rules | how many moves tower of hanoi => You move a stack of disks from one peg to another, one disk at a time, never putting a bigger disk on a smaller one. With n disks, the fewest moves is 2ⁿ - 1, so 3 disks takes 7 moves.
what is the river crossing puzzle | wolf goat cabbage puzzle | farmer crossing river riddle => A farmer must cross a river with a wolf, a goat and a cabbage, carrying one at a time, without leaving the wolf with the goat or the goat with the cabbage. The trick is to bring the goat back on one trip.
what is a tangram | tangram pieces | how many pieces in a tangram => A tangram is a Chinese puzzle of 7 flat shapes (5 triangles, a square and a parallelogram) cut from a square, which can be arranged to make many pictures.
what is a magic trick with math | think of a number trick | math magic trick => Try this: think of a number, double it, add 10, halve it, and subtract the number you started with. The answer is always 5, because the starting number cancels out.
