# Basic trigonometry, sets and logic. Written by hand.
# tag: school.math.trig
what is trigonometry | define trigonometry | what is trig used for => Trigonometry is the math of triangles, especially how their angles relate to their side lengths. It's used in building, navigation, physics, video games and anything with angles or waves.
what is sohcahtoa | what does soh cah toa mean | how to remember sin cos tan => SOH CAH TOA: Sine = Opposite/Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent/Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite/Adjacent. It works for the acute angles of a right triangle.
what is sine | define sine | what does sin mean in math => In a right triangle, the sine of an angle is the opposite side divided by the hypotenuse. If the opposite side is 3 and the hypotenuse is 5, sin = 3/5 = 0.6.
what is cosine | define cosine | what does cos mean in math => In a right triangle, the cosine of an angle is the adjacent side divided by the hypotenuse. If the adjacent side is 4 and the hypotenuse is 5, cos = 4/5 = 0.8.
what is tangent in trig | define tangent trig | what does tan mean in math => In a right triangle, the tangent of an angle is the opposite side divided by the adjacent side. If they're 3 and 4, tan = 3/4 = 0.75. It also equals sin ÷ cos.
which side is opposite and which is adjacent | opposite vs adjacent side | how to label sides of a right triangle => The hypotenuse is across from the right angle. The opposite side is across from the angle you're using, and the adjacent side is the other leg that touches that angle.
how do you find a missing side with trig | using sin cos tan to find a side | find the opposite side with sine => Pick the ratio that uses the side you know and the one you want, then solve. If the angle is 30° and the hypotenuse is 10, the opposite side = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5.
how do you find a missing angle with trig | inverse sine | how to use sin to the minus 1 => Find the ratio of two sides, then use the inverse function on a calculator. If opposite = 1 and hypotenuse = 2, sin θ = 0.5, so θ = sin⁻¹(0.5) = 30°.
what are the exact trig values | sin cos tan of 30 45 60 | special angle values => The key values are sin 30° = 1/2, cos 30° = √3/2, tan 30° = 1/√3. sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2, tan 45° = 1. sin 60° = √3/2, cos 60° = 1/2, tan 60° = √3.
what is sin 90 | what is cos 0 | what is sin 0 => The value of sin 0° is 0 and cos 0° = 1. sin 90° = 1 and cos 90° = 0. tan 90° is undefined.
what is the law of sines | sine rule | when do you use the law of sines => The law of sines says a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C for any triangle, where each side is across from its matching angle. Use it when you know two angles and a side, or two sides and an angle opposite one of them.
what is the law of cosines | cosine rule | when do you use the law of cosines => The law of cosines says c² = a² + b² - 2ab cos C. Use it when you know two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides. With C = 90° it becomes the Pythagorean theorem.
what is an angle of elevation | angle of depression | elevation vs depression => An angle of elevation is measured up from the horizontal to something above you; an angle of depression is measured down from the horizontal to something below. They're used in height and distance problems.
how do you find the height of a building with trig | angle of elevation problem | trig height problem => Use tangent. If you stand 40 m from a building and the angle of elevation to the top is 35°, the height is 40 × tan 35°, about 28 m (plus your eye height if needed).
what is the unit circle | define unit circle | how does the unit circle work => The unit circle has radius 1 and is centered at the origin. For an angle θ measured from the positive x-axis, the point on the circle is (cos θ, sin θ).
what is the pythagorean identity | sin squared plus cos squared | sin squared x plus cos squared x equals 1 => For any angle, sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. It comes from the Pythagorean theorem on the unit circle.
degrees to radians formula | radians to degrees => Multiply degrees by π/180 to get radians, and radians by 180/π to get degrees. 90° = π/2 radians, and π radians = 180°.
what does a sine wave look like | sine graph | graph of y equals sin x => The graph of y = sin x is a smooth wave that goes between 1 and -1 and repeats every 360° (2π radians). The cosine graph is the same wave shifted by 90°.
what are cosecant secant and cotangent | csc sec cot | reciprocal trig functions => They're the reciprocals: csc θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ, and cot θ = 1/tan θ.
should my calculator be in degrees or radians | deg vs rad mode | why is my trig answer wrong on the calculator => If the question uses degrees, set the calculator to DEG mode. A common mistake is being in RAD mode, which makes sin 30 give about -0.988 instead of 0.5.
# tag: school.math.sets
what does union mean in sets | define union | what does the u symbol mean in math => The union of two sets (A ∪ B) contains everything in either set. {1, 2, 3} ∪ {3, 4} = {1, 2, 3, 4}.
what does intersection mean in sets | define intersection of sets | upside down u symbol in math => The intersection of two sets (A ∩ B) contains only what's in both. {1, 2, 3} ∩ {2, 3, 5} = {2, 3}.
what is a subset | define subset | what does subset mean => A set A is a subset of B if everything in A is also in B. {1, 2} is a subset of {1, 2, 3}, written {1, 2} ⊆ {1, 2, 3}. Every set is a subset of itself.
what is the complement of a set | define set complement | what does a prime mean in sets => The complement of A is everything in the universal set that's not in A. If the universal set is {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} and A = {1, 2}, the complement is {3, 4, 5}.
what is a universal set | define universal set | what does the rectangle in a venn diagram mean => The universal set is everything being considered in a problem. In a Venn diagram, it's the rectangle around the circles.
how many subsets does a set have | number of subsets formula | subsets of a set with 3 elements => A set with n elements has 2ⁿ subsets, counting the empty set and itself. {a, b, c} has 2³ = 8 subsets.
what is set builder notation | how do you read set builder notation | x such that => Set-builder notation describes a set by a rule, like {x | x > 5}, read "the set of all x such that x is greater than 5".
what are the number sets symbols | what does a double struck r mean | n z q r symbols => ℕ is the natural numbers, ℤ is the integers, ℚ is the rationals, ℝ is the real numbers and ℂ is the complex numbers. ℤ comes from the German word Zahlen, meaning numbers.
how do you solve a venn diagram problem | venn diagram word problem | how to fill in a venn diagram => Start with the middle overlap, then fill each circle's remaining part by subtracting the overlap, and finally anything outside. If 20 like cats, 15 like dogs and 5 like both, then 15 like only cats and 10 only dogs.
what is cardinality | define cardinality | size of a set => The cardinality of a set is how many elements it has, written |A| or n(A). The set {a, e, i, o, u} has cardinality 5.
# tag: school.math.logic
what is a conditional statement | define if then statement | hypothesis and conclusion in math => A conditional is an "if p, then q" statement. In "If it rains, the ground gets wet," "it rains" is the hypothesis and "the ground gets wet" is the conclusion.
what is a converse | define converse | converse of a statement => The converse swaps the hypothesis and conclusion. The converse of "If it's a square, then it has 4 sides" is "If it has 4 sides, then it's a square," which is false.
what is the inverse of a statement | contrapositive | inverse vs contrapositive => The inverse negates both parts ("If not p, then not q"). The contrapositive swaps and negates them ("If not q, then not p"). A statement and its contrapositive are always both true or both false.
what is inductive reasoning | deductive reasoning | inductive vs deductive => Inductive reasoning makes a general guess from patterns you notice. Deductive reasoning uses facts and rules to reach a conclusion that must be true. Proofs use deductive reasoning.
what is a biconditional | if and only if | what does iff mean => A biconditional means "p if and only if q": both the statement and its converse are true. "A triangle is equilateral if and only if all its sides are equal."
what is a truth table | how do truth tables work | and or not truth table => A truth table lists every true/false combination of statements and the result. "p AND q" is true only when both are true; "p OR q" is true when at least one is true.
what does therefore symbol mean | what do the three dots mean in math | three dots triangle symbol => The ∴ symbol means "therefore". A related symbol, ∵ (dots the other way), means "because".
