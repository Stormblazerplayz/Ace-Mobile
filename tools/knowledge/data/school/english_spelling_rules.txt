# Spelling rules and word-study basics. Written by hand.
# tag: school.english.spelling-rules
what is the i before e rule | i before e except after c | does i before e always work => "I before e, except after c, or when sounding like a as in neighbor and weigh" works for words like believe, receive and eight. There are many exceptions, like weird, seize, their and science.
when do you double the last letter before adding ing | doubling rule spelling | why is running spelled with two n's => For short words ending in one vowel then one consonant, double the consonant before adding a vowel suffix: run → running, hop → hopped, big → bigger. Don't double w, x or y (snowing, fixed).
when do you drop the e before adding ing | silent e rule suffix | why is making spelled without e => Drop a final silent e before a suffix starting with a vowel: make → making, hope → hoped. Keep it before a consonant suffix: hope → hopeful. Some words keep it to protect a soft c or g sound, like noticeable and courageous.
how do you add ed to a word ending in y | change y to i rule | why is cried spelled with i => If a word ends in consonant + y, change the y to i before most suffixes: cry → cried, happy → happiness. Keep the y before -ing (crying) and after a vowel (played).
what does a silent e do | magic e rule | bossy e => A silent e at the end of a word often makes the vowel before it say its name (long sound): cap → cape, kit → kite, hop → hope.
what are silent letters | silent letter words | examples of silent letters => Silent letters are written but not spoken, like the k in knee, w in write, b in lamb, g in gnome, h in hour, t in listen and l in walk.
what is a long vowel | short vowel | long vs short vowels => A long vowel says its own name, like the a in cake or the i in bike. A short vowel makes its other sound, like the a in cat or the i in sit.
what is a vowel team | vowel teams list | two vowels together => A vowel team is two or more letters that make one vowel sound, like ai (rain), ay (play), ee (tree), ea (team), oa (boat) and igh (night).
what is a digraph | consonant digraph | digraph examples => A digraph is two letters that make one sound, like sh, ch, th, wh, ph and ck. For example, ph in phone sounds like f.
what is a blend in phonics | consonant blends | blend examples => A blend is two or three consonants where you still hear each sound, like bl, st, tr, spr and str in blue, stop, tree, spring and string.
what is an r controlled vowel | bossy r | ar er ir or ur => An r-controlled vowel is a vowel followed by r that changes its sound: ar (car), or (corn), and er, ir and ur, which usually sound the same (her, bird, turn).
what is a syllable | how do you count syllables | how to split words into syllables => A syllable is a part of a word with one vowel sound. Clap as you say the word or feel your chin drop: "but-ter-fly" has 3 syllables.
when do you use ck or k | ck vs k spelling | ck rule => Use -ck right after a short vowel at the end of a one-syllable word (back, duck, sick). Use -k after a consonant or a long vowel sound (milk, bake, cook).
when do you use c or k at the start of a word | c or k spelling rule | k before e and i => Use k before e, i and y (keep, kite, sky) and c before a, o, u and consonants (cat, cot, cut, clap), since c before e or i usually sounds like s.
what is a soft c and soft g | hard c vs soft c | when does c sound like s => C and g are usually "soft" before e, i and y: c sounds like s (city, cent) and g often sounds like j (gem, giant). Before a, o and u they're "hard" (cat, go).
when do you use able or ible | able vs ible | able or ible spelling => -able is more common and often goes on whole words (readable, enjoyable). -ible often goes on word parts that can't stand alone (visible, possible). There are exceptions, so check a dictionary.
when do you use ance or ence | ant vs ent spelling | ance or ence => There's no simple rule, so it's best to learn common ones: importance, distance, appearance (-ance), and difference, patience, existence (-ence).
how do you spell words ending in cede ceed and sede | cede vs ceed | ceed words => Only three common words end in -ceed: exceed, proceed and succeed. Only supersede ends in -sede. Others use -cede: precede, concede, recede.
what is the plural of words ending in y after a vowel | plural of monkey | plural of day => If a vowel comes before the y, just add -s: monkeys, days, toys, keys.
what is the plural of compound words | plural of mother in law | plural of passerby => Make the main word plural: mothers-in-law, passersby, attorneys general. For closed compounds, add -s at the end: toothbrushes, cupcakes.
why is english spelling so hard | why doesn't english spell how it sounds | english spelling history => English borrowed words from many languages, kept old spellings after pronunciations changed, and printers fixed spellings in the 1400s and 1500s, so sounds and letters often don't match.
what is american vs british spelling | color or colour | center or centre => American English often uses -or (color), -er (center), -ize (organize) and single l (traveled). British English often uses -our (colour), -re (centre), -ise (organise) and double l (travelled).
how can i get better at spelling | spelling tips | how to improve spelling => Read a lot, learn rules and their exceptions, break words into syllables, make up memory tricks for hard words, use look-say-cover-write-check, and keep a list of words you often miss.
what is look say cover write check | spelling practice method | lscwc spelling => Look at the word, Say it aloud, Cover it, Write it from memory, then Check it. Repeat until you get it right a few times in a row.
what is a homograph | homograph examples | words spelled the same different meaning => A homograph is spelled the same as another word but has a different meaning, and sometimes a different sound: "lead" (to guide) and "lead" (a metal), or "bow" (a ribbon) and "bow" (bend forward).
what is a homonym | homonym vs homophone | homonym examples => Homonyms are words that sound and are spelled the same but mean different things, like "bat" (animal) and "bat" (for baseball). Homophones only sound alike, like "flour" and "flower".
what is a compound word | compound word examples | two words together => A compound word joins two words into one, like sunflower, football, rainbow and backpack.
what is a contraction spelling | where does the apostrophe go in a contraction | won't spelling => The apostrophe goes where letters are left out: do not → don't, she will → she'll. "Won't" comes from an old form, "wol not", which is why it isn't "willn't".
what is a palindrome word | palindrome examples | words that read the same backwards => A palindrome reads the same forward and backward, like racecar, level, kayak and noon. Phrases can be palindromes too: "Never odd or even."
what is an anagram | anagram examples | how do anagrams work => An anagram rearranges the letters of a word or phrase to make another, like listen → silent and night → thing.
what is an acronym | acronym vs abbreviation | acronym examples => An acronym is formed from first letters and said as a word, like NASA or scuba. An initialism is said letter by letter, like FBI. Both are kinds of abbreviation.
what is a portmanteau | portmanteau examples | blended words => A portmanteau blends two words into one: brunch (breakfast + lunch), smog (smoke + fog) and emoticon (emotion + icon).
