# More commonly confused words and homophones. Written by hand.
# tag: school.english.confused-words
your vs you're | when to use your or you're | is it your or you're => Your shows ownership: "your bike". You're is short for "you are": "You're fast." If "you are" fits, use you're.
there their or they're | difference between there their and they're | when to use there their they're => There is a place (over there). Their shows ownership (their dog). They're means "they are" (they're late).
bad vs badly | feel bad or feel badly | difference between bad and badly => Bad is an adjective and badly is an adverb. After feel, look or seem, use bad: "I feel bad." Use badly for actions: "He sang badly."
adapt vs adopt | difference between adapt and adopt | adapt or adopt => Adapt means to change to fit a new situation: "Animals adapt to the cold." Adopt means to take something as your own: "We adopted a puppy."
beside vs besides | difference between beside and besides | beside or besides => Beside means next to: "Sit beside me." Besides means in addition to or except: "Besides math, I like art."
continual vs continuous | difference between continual and continuous | continual or continuous => Continual means happening again and again with breaks, like continual interruptions. Continuous means going on without stopping, like a continuous hum.
discreet vs discrete | difference between discreet and discrete | discreet or discrete => Discreet means careful not to attract attention or reveal secrets. Discrete means separate and distinct, as in discrete parts.
disinterested vs uninterested | difference between disinterested and uninterested | disinterested meaning => Uninterested means not interested or bored. Disinterested traditionally means fair and not taking sides, like a disinterested referee.
envelop vs envelope | difference between envelop and envelope | envelop or envelope => Envelop (verb) means to wrap or surround: "Fog enveloped the town." An envelope (noun) is the paper holder for a letter.
everyone vs every one | difference between everyone and every one | every one or everyone => Everyone means all people: "Everyone laughed." Every one means each single item: "Every one of the cookies was burnt."
anyone vs any one | difference between anyone and any one | any one or anyone => Anyone means any person: "Can anyone help?" Any one means any single item from a group: "Pick any one of these cards."
sometime vs sometimes vs some time | difference between sometime and sometimes | sometime or some time => Sometime means at an unknown time: "Visit sometime." Sometimes means now and then: "I sometimes walk." Some time means a period of time: "It took some time."
historic vs historical | difference between historic and historical | historic or historical => Historic means important in history: "a historic moon landing". Historical means about or from the past: "a historical novel".
lend vs loan | difference between lend and loan | loan or lend => Lend is a verb: "Can you lend me a pencil?" Loan is usually a noun: "a bank loan", though in American English loan is also used as a verb.
peak vs peek vs pique | difference between peak peek and pique | peak or peek => A peak is a top point, like a mountain peak. A peek is a quick look. Pique means to stir up, as in "pique your interest".
palate vs palette vs pallet | difference between palate and palette | pallet or palette => Your palate is the roof of your mouth or your sense of taste. A palette is an artist's paint board or a set of colors. A pallet is a wooden platform for moving goods.
pedal vs peddle | difference between pedal and peddle | pedal or peddle => You pedal a bike with your feet. To peddle means to sell things, usually by going from place to place.
prophecy vs prophesy | difference between prophecy and prophesy | prophecy or prophesy => A prophecy (noun, rhymes with see) is a prediction. To prophesy (verb, rhymes with sigh) is to make one.
rational vs rationale | difference between rational and rationale | rationale meaning => Rational means reasonable or logical. A rationale is the reason behind a decision.
troop vs troupe | difference between troop and troupe | troop or troupe => A troop is a group of soldiers or scouts. A troupe is a group of performers, like a dance troupe.
vary vs very | difference between vary and very | vary or very => Vary is a verb meaning to change or differ: "Prices vary." Very is an adverb meaning extremely: "very cold".
wander vs wonder | difference between wander and wonder | wonder or wander => To wander is to walk around with no set path. To wonder is to think curiously about something.
altar vs alter | difference between altar and alter | alter or altar => An altar is a table used in religious ceremonies. To alter means to change.
later vs latter | difference between later and latter | latter or later => Later means after some time. Latter means the second of two things mentioned: "Of tea and juice, I prefer the latter."
worse vs worst | difference between worse and worst | worse or worst => Worse compares two things: "My cold is worse today." Worst is the most bad of three or more: "the worst day ever".
device vs devise | difference between device and devise | devise or device => A device (noun) is a tool or gadget. To devise (verb) is to plan or invent something.
cloth vs clothe | difference between cloth and clothe | clothe or cloth => Cloth (noun) is fabric. Clothe (verb) means to dress someone. Clothes are the things you wear.
bath vs bathe | difference between bath and bathe | bathe or bath => Bath is a noun: "Take a bath." Bathe is a verb: "Bathe the dog." Same pattern as breath and breathe.
allude vs elude | difference between allude and elude | elude or allude => To allude is to mention indirectly. To elude is to escape or avoid: "The fox eluded the hounds."
statue vs statute | difference between statue and statute | stature meaning => A statue is a sculpture. A statute is a written law. Stature means height or importance.
alright vs all right | is alright a word | all right or alright => All right is the standard spelling in formal writing. Alright is common in casual writing but many teachers mark it wrong.
cannot vs can not | is cannot one word | can not or cannot => Cannot is usually written as one word. Can not appears only in rare cases, like "You can not only swim but also dive."
a while vs awhile | difference between a while and awhile | awhile or a while => Awhile is an adverb meaning for a short time: "Stay awhile." A while is a noun phrase, used after for or in: "for a while".
setup vs set up | login vs log in | one word or two words => The one-word forms are nouns: "the setup", "your login". The two-word forms are verbs: "set up the game", "log in to your account". Same for backup and back up.
# tag: school.english.homophones
ate vs eight | ate or eight | homophones ate eight => Ate is the past tense of eat. Eight is the number 8.
berry vs bury | berry or bury | homophones berry bury => A berry is a small fruit. To bury is to put in the ground.
blew vs blue | blew or blue | homophones blew blue => Blew is the past tense of blow. Blue is a color.
cell vs sell | cell or sell | homophones cell sell => A cell is a tiny unit of life or a small room. To sell is to trade for money.
cent vs scent vs sent | cent or scent | homophones cent scent sent => A cent is a penny. A scent is a smell. Sent is the past tense of send.
for vs four vs fore | four or for | homophones for four fore => For is a preposition (a gift for you). Four is the number 4. Fore means front, and golfers shout it as a warning.
grate vs great | grate or great | homophones grate great => To grate is to shred food, like cheese; a grate is also a metal frame. Great means very good or large.
groan vs grown | groan or grown | homophones groan grown => A groan is a low sound of pain or annoyance. Grown is a form of grow.
hair vs hare | hare or hair | homophones hair hare => Hair grows on your head. A hare is an animal like a large rabbit.
hymn vs him | hymn or him | homophones hymn him => A hymn is a religious song. Him is a pronoun.
knot vs not | knot or not | homophones knot not => A knot is a tied loop in rope. Not makes a statement negative.
made vs maid | maid or made | homophones made maid => Made is the past tense of make. A maid is a person who cleans.
morning vs mourning | mourning or morning | homophones morning mourning => Morning is the early part of the day. Mourning is grieving for someone who died.
oar vs or vs ore | ore or oar | homophones oar or ore => An oar is a paddle for rowing. Or joins choices. Ore is rock with metal in it.
one vs won | won or one | homophones one won => One is the number 1. Won is the past tense of win.
pain vs pane | pane or pain | homophones pain pane => Pain is hurt. A pane is a sheet of glass in a window.
patience vs patients | patients or patience | homophones patience patients => Patience is the ability to wait calmly. Patients are people getting medical care.
pause vs paws | paws or pause | homophones pause paws => A pause is a short stop. Paws are animal feet.
presence vs presents | presents or presence | homophones presence presents => Presence means being there. Presents are gifts.
prey vs pray | pray or prey | homophones prey pray => Prey is an animal hunted by another. To pray is to speak to a god.
road vs rode vs rowed | rode or road | homophones road rode rowed => A road is a street. Rode is the past tense of ride. Rowed is the past tense of row.
sew vs so vs sow | sew or sow | homophones sew so sow => To sew is to stitch with thread. So means therefore or very. To sow is to plant seeds.
some vs sum | sum or some | homophones some sum => Some means an amount. A sum is the answer to an addition problem.
tide vs tied | tied or tide | homophones tide tied => The tide is the rise and fall of the sea. Tied is the past tense of tie.
whine vs wine | wine or whine | homophones whine wine => To whine is to complain in a high voice. Wine is a drink made from grapes.
yolk vs yoke | yoke or yolk | homophones yolk yoke => A yolk is the yellow part of an egg. A yoke is a wooden bar joining two animals pulling a load.
colonel vs kernel | colonel or kernel | homophones colonel kernel => A colonel is a military officer, said like "kernel". A kernel is a seed, like a corn kernel.
muscle vs mussel | mussel or muscle | homophones muscle mussel => A muscle is body tissue that moves you. A mussel is a shellfish.
naval vs navel | navel or naval | homophones naval navel => Naval means about a navy. Your navel is your belly button.
root vs route | route or root | homophones root route => A root is the part of a plant underground. A route is a way to get somewhere; it can also be said "rowt" in American English.
whale vs wail | wail or whale | homophones whale wail => A whale is a huge sea mammal. To wail is to cry loudly.
flew vs flu vs flue | flu or flew | homophones flew flu flue => Flew is the past tense of fly. Flu is an illness (short for influenza). A flue is a chimney pipe.
guessed vs guest | guest or guessed | homophones guessed guest => Guessed is the past tense of guess. A guest is a visitor.
hall vs haul | haul or hall | homophones hall haul => A hall is a hallway or big room. To haul is to pull or carry something heavy.
higher vs hire | hire or higher | homophones higher hire => Higher means more high. To hire is to give someone a job or rent something.
chews vs choose | choose or chews | homophones chews choose => Chews is what a mouth does to food. Choose means to pick.
bridal vs bridle | bridle or bridal | homophones bridal bridle => Bridal means about a bride. A bridle is the headgear used to guide a horse.
plum vs plumb | plumb or plum | homophones plum plumb => A plum is a fruit. Plumb means exactly straight up and down, and is where the word plumber comes from.
profit vs prophet | prophet or profit | homophones profit prophet => Profit is money gained. A prophet is a person said to speak for God or predict the future.
cheap vs cheep | cheep or cheap | homophones cheap cheep => Cheap means low in price. A cheep is a baby bird's sound.
