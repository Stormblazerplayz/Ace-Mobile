# Commonly confused words. Written by hand.
# tag: school.english.confused-words
to too or two | to vs too vs two | when do you use too => "To" shows direction or comes before a verb (go to school, to eat). "Too" means also or very (me too, too hot). "Two" is the number 2.
then vs than | then or than | when do you use than => "Than" compares (taller than me). "Then" is about time or order (first eat, then play).
accept vs except | accept or except | difference between accept and except => "Accept" means to receive or agree (accept a gift). "Except" means leaving something out (everyone except Sam).
lose vs loose | lose or loose | difference between lose and loose => "Lose" means to misplace or not win (lose a game). "Loose" means not tight (a loose tooth).
whose vs who's | whose or who's | difference between whose and who's => "Who's" means "who is" or "who has" (Who's coming?). "Whose" asks or shows ownership (Whose bag is this?).
were vs where vs we're | were or where => "Were" is a past form of be (we were late). "Where" asks about place (Where is it?). "We're" means "we are".
weather vs whether | weather or whether | difference between weather and whether => "Weather" is rain, sun and temperature. "Whether" introduces choices (whether or not to go).
hear vs here | hear or here | difference between hear and here => "Hear" is what you do with your ears. "Here" means this place. Tip: you hear with your ear.
know vs no | know or no | difference between know and no => "Know" means to have information (I know the answer). "No" is the opposite of yes.
new vs knew | knew or new | difference between knew and new => "Knew" is the past of know (I knew it). "New" means not old.
write vs right vs rite | write or right | difference between write and right => "Write" means to put words down. "Right" means correct or the opposite of left. A "rite" is a ceremony.
buy vs by vs bye | buy or by | difference between buy and by => "Buy" means to purchase. "By" means near or through (by the door, written by me). "Bye" is short for goodbye.
peace vs piece | peace or piece | difference between peace and piece => "Peace" means calm or no war. "Piece" is a part of something. Tip: a piece of pie.
principal vs principle | principal or principle | difference between principal and principle => A "principal" is the head of a school, or main (the principal reason). A "principle" is a rule or belief. Tip: the principal is your pal.
stationary vs stationery | stationary or stationery | difference between stationary and stationery => "Stationary" means not moving. "Stationery" is paper and envelopes. Tip: stationery has e for envelope.
desert vs dessert | desert or dessert | difference between desert and dessert => A "desert" is a dry place (or, as a verb, to abandon). "Dessert" is the sweet course after a meal. Tip: dessert has two s's because you want seconds.
compliment vs complement | compliment or complement | difference between compliment and complement => A "compliment" is praise. A "complement" completes or goes well with something (the sauce complements the pasta).
allowed vs aloud | aloud or allowed | difference between aloud and allowed => "Aloud" means out loud. "Allowed" means permitted.
brake vs break | brake or break | difference between brake and break => A "brake" stops a vehicle. "Break" means to smash, or a rest (take a break).
cereal vs serial | cereal or serial | difference between cereal and serial => "Cereal" is grain or breakfast food. "Serial" means in a series (a serial number).
fewer vs less | fewer or less | difference between fewer and less => Use "fewer" for things you can count (fewer cookies) and "less" for amounts you can't (less sugar). Supermarket signs saying "10 items or less" are informal.
farther vs further | farther or further | difference between farther and further => "Farther" is for physical distance (walk farther). "Further" is for extent or more (further questions). Further is often used for both.
lie vs lay | lay or lie | difference between lie and lay => "Lie" means to recline (I lie down) and has no object. "Lay" means to put something down (lay the book on the desk). Confusingly, the past of lie is lay.
sit vs set | set or sit | difference between sit and set => "Sit" means to be seated (sit down). "Set" means to place something (set the cup down).
rise vs raise | raise or rise | difference between rise and raise => "Rise" means to go up by itself (the sun rises). "Raise" means to lift something (raise your hand).
borrow vs lend | lend or borrow | difference between borrow and lend => You "borrow" from someone (can I borrow your pen?) and "lend" to someone (can you lend me your pen?).
teach vs learn | difference between teach and learn => A teacher "teaches" you, and you "learn" from them. "She learned me math" is incorrect; say "She taught me math."
bring vs take | take or bring | difference between bring and take => "Bring" means carrying toward the speaker (bring it here). "Take" means carrying away (take it to school).
can vs may | may or can | difference between can and may => "Can" is about ability (I can swim). "May" is about permission or possibility (May I go? It may rain). In everyday speech, "can" is also used for permission.
among vs between | between or among | difference between among and between => Use "between" for two things or clearly separate items (between you and me). Use "among" for a group (among friends).
amount vs number | number or amount | difference between amount and number => Use "number" for countable things (a number of people) and "amount" for things you can't count (an amount of water).
advice vs advise | advise or advice | difference between advice and advise => "Advice" is a noun, the suggestion you get. "Advise" is the verb, to give advice. "I advise you to take my advice."
breath vs breathe | breathe or breath | difference between breath and breathe => "Breath" is a noun (take a deep breath). "Breathe" is the verb (breathe in slowly).
quiet vs quite vs quit | quite or quiet | difference between quiet and quite => "Quiet" means silent. "Quite" means very or completely. "Quit" means to stop.
chose vs choose | choose or chose | difference between choose and chose => "Choose" is the present (I choose red). "Chose" is the past (I chose red yesterday).
loose vs lose spelling tip | how to remember lose and loose | loose has two o's => Think of "loose" as having extra room, an extra o, like a loose shirt. "Lose" lost an o.
than vs then spelling tip | how to remember than and then | then is for time => "Then" and "when" both have e and talk about time. "Than" and "compare" both have a.
its vs it's tip | how to remember its and it's | it's always means it is => If you can replace the word with "it is" or "it has", write it's. Otherwise write its, which shows possession, like his or hers (no apostrophe).
a lot vs alot | is alot a word | how to spell a lot => "A lot" is always two words. "Alot" isn't a word. "Allot" is a different word meaning to give out shares.
all ready vs already | already or all ready | difference between already and all ready => "Already" means before now (I already ate). "All ready" means completely ready (we're all ready to go).
all together vs altogether | altogether or all together | difference between altogether and all together => "Altogether" means completely or in total. "All together" means everyone in one place (sing all together).
anyway vs anyways | is anyways a word | anyway or anyways => "Anyway" is standard. "Anyways" is casual speech and should be avoided in school writing.
everyday vs every day | every day or everyday | difference between everyday and every day => "Everyday" is an adjective meaning ordinary (everyday shoes). "Every day" means each day (I read every day).
maybe vs may be | may be or maybe | difference between maybe and may be => "Maybe" means perhaps (maybe tomorrow). "May be" is a verb phrase (it may be late).
into vs in to | in to or into => "Into" shows movement inside (jump into the pool). "In to" appears when "in" belongs with the verb and "to" with what follows (hand your work in to the teacher).
onto vs on to | on to or onto | difference between onto and on to => "Onto" means on top of (climb onto the roof). "On to" is used when "on" goes with the verb (move on to the next page).
could of vs could have | is could of correct | should of or should have => Always write "could have", "should have" and "would have". "Could of" comes from how "could've" sounds and is incorrect.
emigrate vs immigrate | immigrate or emigrate | difference between emigrate and immigrate => You "emigrate" from a country (leave) and "immigrate" to a country (arrive). Tip: emigrate = exit, immigrate = in.
ensure vs insure | insure or ensure | difference between ensure and insure => "Ensure" means make sure. "Insure" means to buy insurance for something.
explicit vs implicit | implicit or explicit | difference between explicit and implicit => "Explicit" means stated clearly. "Implicit" means suggested but not said directly.
imply vs infer | infer or imply | difference between imply and infer => A speaker or writer "implies" (hints). A reader or listener "infers" (figures it out). "She implied I was late, so I inferred she was annoyed."
illusion vs allusion | allusion or illusion | difference between allusion and illusion => An "allusion" is a brief reference to something else (an allusion to a famous story). An "illusion" is a false image or belief (an optical illusion).
elicit vs illicit | illicit or elicit | difference between elicit and illicit => "Elicit" means to draw out (elicit a response). "Illicit" means not allowed or illegal.
eminent vs imminent | imminent or eminent | difference between eminent and imminent => "Eminent" means famous and respected. "Imminent" means about to happen.
council vs counsel | counsel or council | difference between council and counsel => A "council" is a group that makes decisions. "Counsel" is advice, or to give advice.
capital vs capitol | capitol or capital | difference between capital and capitol => A "capital" is a city where a government sits, a capital letter, or money. A "capitol" is the building where lawmakers meet.
cite vs site vs sight | site or cite | difference between cite site and sight => "Cite" means to quote or name a source. A "site" is a place or website. "Sight" is seeing, or something seen.
coarse vs course | course or coarse | difference between coarse and course => "Coarse" means rough (coarse sand). A "course" is a path, a class, or part of a meal.
dual vs duel | duel or dual | difference between dual and duel => "Dual" means two (dual screens). A "duel" is a fight between two people.
flour vs flower | flower or flour | difference between flour and flower => "Flour" is ground grain for baking. A "flower" is the bloom of a plant.
hole vs whole | whole or hole | difference between hole and whole => A "hole" is an opening or gap. "Whole" means complete or all of something.
mail vs male | male or mail | difference between mail and male => "Mail" is letters and packages. "Male" refers to boys, men or male animals.
meat vs meet | meet or meat | difference between meat and meet => "Meat" is animal flesh used as food. "Meet" means to come together with someone.
pair vs pear vs pare | pear or pair | difference between pair and pear => A "pair" is two of something. A "pear" is a fruit. "Pare" means to peel or trim.
passed vs past | past or passed | difference between passed and past => "Passed" is the past of pass (she passed the test). "Past" means earlier times or beyond (in the past, walk past the store).
plain vs plane | plane or plain | difference between plain and plane => "Plain" means simple, or flat land. A "plane" is an airplane, a flat surface in math, or a woodworking tool.
role vs roll | roll or role | difference between role and roll => A "role" is a part someone plays. A "roll" is bread, a list of names, or to turn over and over.
scene vs seen | seen or scene | difference between scene and seen => A "scene" is a part of a play or a view. "Seen" is a form of see (I have seen it).
sole vs soul | soul or sole | difference between sole and soul => "Sole" means only (the sole winner), or the bottom of a foot or shoe; it's also a fish. "Soul" is a person's spirit.
steal vs steel | steel or steal | difference between steal and steel => "Steal" means to take without permission. "Steel" is a strong metal.
tail vs tale | tale or tail | difference between tail and tale => A "tail" is the end part of an animal. A "tale" is a story.
threw vs through | through or threw | difference between threw and through => "Threw" is the past of throw. "Through" means in one side and out the other, or finished.
waist vs waste | waste or waist | difference between waist and waste => Your "waist" is the middle of your body. "Waste" means to use carelessly, or trash.
weak vs week | week or weak | difference between weak and week => "Weak" means not strong. A "week" is seven days.
wear vs where vs ware | wear or where | difference between wear and where => "Wear" means to have clothes on. "Where" asks about place. "Ware" means goods for sale, like software or glassware.
which vs witch | witch or which | difference between which and witch => "Which" asks about a choice. A "witch" is a character with magic powers in stories.
board vs bored | bored or board | difference between board and bored => A "board" is a flat piece of wood or a group that runs something. "Bored" means not interested.
horse vs hoarse | hoarse or horse | difference between horse and hoarse => A "horse" is the animal. "Hoarse" means a rough, croaky voice, like after shouting.
dyeing vs dying | dying or dyeing | difference between dying and dyeing => "Dyeing" means coloring with dye. "Dying" means coming to the end of life.
lead vs led | led or lead | past tense of lead => The past of the verb lead is "led" (she led the team). "Lead" (rhymes with bed) is also a metal, which causes the mix-up.
lightning vs lightening | lightening or lightning | difference between lightning and lightening => "Lightning" is the flash in a storm. "Lightening" means making lighter.
personal vs personnel | personnel or personal | difference between personal and personnel => "Personal" means private or about one person. "Personnel" means the people who work somewhere.
precede vs proceed | proceed or precede | difference between precede and proceed => "Precede" means to come before. "Proceed" means to go forward or continue.
respectfully vs respectively | respectively or respectfully | difference between respectfully and respectively => "Respectfully" means with respect. "Respectively" means in the order given: "Ana and Ben got A and B, respectively."
sense vs since | since or sense | difference between since and sense => "Since" means from a time or because (since Monday). "Sense" is a feeling or meaning (it makes sense).
conscience vs conscious | conscious or conscience | difference between conscience and conscious => Your "conscience" is your sense of right and wrong. "Conscious" means awake or aware.
moral vs morale | morale or moral | difference between moral and morale => A "moral" is a lesson or about right and wrong. "Morale" is the mood or confidence of a group (team morale).
loath vs loathe | loathe or loath | difference between loath and loathe => "Loath" means unwilling (I'm loath to leave). "Loathe" means to hate.
literally vs figuratively | what does literally mean | is literally used wrong => "Literally" means exactly as stated. "Figuratively" means not exactly, as a figure of speech. Many people use "literally" for emphasis in speech, but avoid that in writing.
irregardless vs regardless | is irregardless a word | regardless or irregardless => Use "regardless". "Irregardless" appears in some dictionaries as nonstandard, but it's considered incorrect in school writing.
i.e. vs e.g. | difference between ie and eg | when to use ie and eg => "E.g." means "for example" and introduces examples. "I.e." means "that is" and restates or clarifies. Fruits, e.g., apples; my favorite fruit, i.e., mangoes.
who vs that | who or that for people | can you use that for people => Use "who" for people (the girl who won) and "that" or "which" for things. "That" for people is common in speech but "who" is preferred in writing.
bear vs bare | bare or bear | difference between bare and bear => A "bear" is an animal, and to bear means to carry or put up with. "Bare" means uncovered (bare feet).
dear vs deer | deer or dear | difference between deer and dear => A "deer" is an animal. "Dear" means loved, or starts a letter.
fair vs fare | fare or fair | difference between fair and fare => "Fair" means just, or a festival. A "fare" is the price of a ride.
heal vs heel | heel or heal | difference between heal and heel => "Heal" means to get better. Your "heel" is the back of your foot.
knight vs night | night or knight | difference between knight and night => A "knight" was an armored soldier in the Middle Ages. "Night" is when it's dark.
rain vs reign vs rein | reign or rein | difference between reign and rein => "Rain" falls from clouds. A king or queen "reigns" (rules). "Reins" are straps used to guide a horse; you "rein in" something to control it.
sail vs sale | sale or sail | difference between sail and sale => A "sail" catches wind on a boat. A "sale" is selling, often at lower prices.
sea vs see | see or sea | difference between sea and see => The "sea" is a big body of salt water. "See" means to look.
son vs sun | sun or son | difference between son and sun => A "son" is a male child. The "sun" is our star.
stair vs stare | stare or stair | difference between stair and stare => A "stair" is a step. To "stare" is to look at something for a long time.
toe vs tow | tow or toe | difference between toe and tow => A "toe" is on your foot. To "tow" is to pull a vehicle or boat.
wait vs weight | weight or wait | difference between wait and weight => "Wait" means to stay until something happens. "Weight" is how heavy something is.
way vs weigh | weigh or way | difference between way and weigh => "Way" is a path or method. "Weigh" means to measure how heavy something is.
wood vs would | would or wood | difference between wood and would => "Wood" comes from trees. "Would" is a helping verb (I would like that).
our vs are vs hour | are or our => "Our" shows something belongs to us. "Are" is a form of be (we are here). An "hour" is 60 minutes.
of vs off | off or of | difference between of and off => "Of" shows belonging or part (a cup of tea). "Off" means not on, or away (turn it off).
through vs thorough vs though | thorough or through | difference between through and thorough => "Through" means from one side to the other. "Thorough" means careful and complete. "Though" means however or even if.
how to remember where | where vs were spelling tip | where has here in it => Tip: "where" has "here" inside it, and both are about places.
vain vs vein vs vane | vein or vain | difference between vain and vein => "Vain" means too proud of yourself, or useless (in vain). A "vein" carries blood. A "vane" shows wind direction.
idle vs idol | idol or idle | difference between idle and idol => "Idle" means not working or doing nothing. An "idol" is someone admired greatly, or an image worshipped.
aisle vs isle | isle or aisle | difference between aisle and isle => An "aisle" is a walkway between rows of seats or shelves. An "isle" is a small island.
die vs dye | dye or die | difference between die and dye => "Dye" is a coloring. "Die" means to stop living; a die is also one of a pair of dice.
pore vs pour vs poor | pour or pore | difference between pour and pore => "Pour" means to let liquid flow. A "pore" is a tiny opening in the skin, and to pore over something means to study it closely. "Poor" means having little money.
