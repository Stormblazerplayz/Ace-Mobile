# English punctuation and capitalization. Written by hand (American usage, with British differences noted).
# tag: school.english.punctuation
what are the main punctuation marks | list of punctuation marks | punctuation marks names => The main marks are the period (.), comma (,), question mark (?), exclamation mark (!), colon (:), semicolon (;), apostrophe ('), quotation marks (" "), hyphen (-), dash, parentheses ( ) and ellipsis (...).
when do you use a period | what is a full stop | period rules => Use a period at the end of a statement or a mild command, and in many abbreviations like Mr. and etc. British English calls it a full stop.
when do you use a question mark | question mark rules | do indirect questions need a question mark => Use a question mark after a direct question: "Where are you going?" An indirect question ends with a period: "She asked where I was going."
when do you use an exclamation mark | exclamation point rules | how many exclamation marks => Use an exclamation mark for strong feeling or a sharp command: "Watch out!" Use one, not several, and don't overuse them in formal writing.
what are the comma rules | when do you use a comma | comma rules list => Use commas to separate items in a list, before FANBOYS joining two sentences, after an introductory part, around extra information, in dates and addresses, and before or after a quote's speaker tag.
how do you use commas in a list | commas in a series | comma before and in a list => Put commas between items in a list of three or more: "eggs, milk, and bread." The comma before "and" is the Oxford comma; it's optional in many styles, but use it consistently.
what is the oxford comma | serial comma | should i use the oxford comma => The Oxford (serial) comma is the comma before "and" or "or" in a list. It can prevent confusion: "I thanked my parents, Ana, and Ben" makes clear Ana and Ben aren't your parents.
do you need a comma before and | comma before but | comma with fanboys => Use a comma before and, but, or, so, yet, for or nor when it joins two complete sentences: "I was tired, so I slept." Don't use one when it just joins two verbs: "I ate and slept."
when do you use a comma after an introductory word | comma after however | comma after a dependent clause => Put a comma after an introductory word, phrase or dependent clause: "However, it rained." "After lunch, we left." "When I got home, I ate."
how do you use commas with extra information | commas around a clause | nonessential clause commas => Put commas around words that add extra, non-essential information: "My brother, who lives in Ohio, is visiting." If removing it changes the meaning, don't use commas.
how do you use commas in dates | comma in a date | comma in an address => In US dates, put a comma between the day and year: July 4, 1776. In addresses, separate the city and state: Austin, Texas.
how do you use a comma when talking to someone | direct address comma | let's eat grandma => Use commas around the name of someone you're speaking to: "Let's eat, Grandma." Without the comma, "Let's eat Grandma" means something very different!
how do you use commas between adjectives | coordinate adjectives | comma between two adjectives => Use a comma between adjectives that each describe the noun equally, if you could swap them or put "and" between: "a long, boring movie." Not for "a big red ball" (no "and" fits).
when do you use a semicolon | semicolon rules | how to use a semicolon => Use a semicolon to join two closely related complete sentences without a conjunction: "I love reading; it relaxes me." It also separates list items that already contain commas.
semicolon with however | however semicolon or comma | how to punctuate however between sentences => When "however" joins two sentences, put a semicolon before it and a comma after: "It rained; however, we played." The same goes for therefore, moreover and otherwise.
when do you use a colon | colon rules | how to use a colon => Use a colon after a complete sentence to introduce a list, an explanation or a quote: "Bring three things: a pen, paper, and water." Also use it in times (3:30) and ratios.
what is the difference between a colon and a semicolon | colon vs semicolon | semicolon or colon => A semicolon joins two equal sentences. A colon introduces something that explains or lists what came before. Both usually follow a complete sentence.
how do you use an apostrophe | apostrophe rules | when to use an apostrophe => Use an apostrophe for contractions (don't, it's) and possession (Sam's bike, the girls' room). Never use one just to make a word plural: "apples", not "apple's".
how do you make a plural noun possessive | apostrophe after s | girls or girl's or girls' => For plurals ending in s, add just an apostrophe: the girls' room. For plurals not ending in s, add 's: the children's toys.
how do you make a name ending in s possessive | james's or james' | possessive of names ending in s => Both are seen, but most US style guides prefer adding 's: James's book, Chris's phone. Be consistent.
what is a contraction | contraction examples | how do contractions work => A contraction shortens two words with an apostrophe where letters are left out: do not → don't, I am → I'm, they are → they're, will not → won't.
how do you punctuate dialogue | dialogue punctuation | how to write speech in a story => Put spoken words in quotation marks, start a new paragraph for each new speaker, and put the comma or end mark inside the quotes: "I'm hungry," said Leo. "Let's eat!"
do commas go inside quotation marks | period inside or outside quotes | punctuation with quotation marks => In American English, commas and periods always go inside quotation marks. Question and exclamation marks go inside only if they belong to the quote: Did she say "yes"?
when do you use quotation marks | quotation marks rules | quotes for titles => Use quotation marks for exact words someone said or wrote, and for titles of short works like songs, poems, short stories and articles.
when do you use single quotation marks | single vs double quotes | quote inside a quote => In American English, use single quotation marks for a quote inside a quote: "She told me, 'Be careful,' before leaving." British English often does the opposite.
should book titles be in italics or quotes | how to write a book title | underline titles => Italicize long works: books, movies, TV shows, albums, magazines and newspapers. Use quotation marks for short works inside them: chapters, episodes, songs, poems and articles. When handwriting, underline instead of italicizing.
what is a hyphen | when do you use a hyphen | hyphen rules => A hyphen (-) joins words. Use it in compound adjectives before a noun (a well-known author), in some compound words (mother-in-law), in numbers from twenty-one to ninety-nine, and with some prefixes (ex-friend).
what is a dash | en dash vs em dash | how to use a dash => An em dash (the long dash) sets off a sudden break or extra thought, a bit like strong parentheses. A shorter en dash shows ranges, like pages 10 to 20. A hyphen is shorter still and joins words.
when do you use parentheses | parentheses rules | brackets in writing => Use parentheses around extra information that isn't essential, like an aside or an example: "The test (worth 20%) is Friday." If you remove them, the sentence should still make sense.
what are square brackets used for | brackets in a quote | how to use square brackets => Square brackets show words you added or changed inside a quotation: "He [the coach] was proud." They're also used inside parentheses.
what is an ellipsis | how to use an ellipsis | three dots in writing => An ellipsis (...) shows words left out of a quote, or a pause or trailing thought in dialogue: "Well... maybe."
what is a slash in writing | when to use a slash | and/or slash => A slash (/) can mean "or" (yes/no), "per" (km/h), or separate lines of poetry in a quote. Avoid it in formal sentences when "or" works.
what is an asterisk | what is the asterisk used for | star symbol in writing => An asterisk (*) points the reader to a footnote or note. Online it's also used for emphasis or to correct a typo.
what is an ampersand | what does & mean | ampersand meaning => The ampersand (&) means "and". It's fine in names and logos (Barnes & Noble) but should be written out as "and" in school writing.
# tag: school.english.capitalization
what are the capitalization rules | when do you capitalize | capital letter rules => Capitalize the first word of a sentence, the pronoun I, proper nouns (names, places, days, months, holidays, languages), and the main words in titles.
do you capitalize days of the week | capitalize months | capitalize seasons => Capitalize days (Monday), months (March) and holidays (Thanksgiving). Don't capitalize seasons (spring, winter) unless they're part of a name.
how do you capitalize a title | title case rules | which words to capitalize in a title => In title case, capitalize the first and last words and all main words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns). Short articles, conjunctions and prepositions (a, the, and, of, in) stay lowercase unless first or last.
do you capitalize mom and dad | when to capitalize mom | capitalize grandma => Capitalize Mom or Dad when used as a name ("Hi, Mom!"), but not after a possessive word ("my mom").
do you capitalize directions | is north capitalized | capitalize the south => Don't capitalize directions (drive north), but do capitalize them as names of regions (the South, the Midwest).
do you capitalize subjects in school | is math capitalized | capitalize english class => Capitalize languages (English, Spanish) and specific course names (Algebra 1), but not general subjects (math, science, history).
do you capitalize after a colon | capital after colon | lowercase after colon => In American English, use lowercase after a colon unless what follows is a full sentence (some styles capitalize then), a proper noun, or a quote.
