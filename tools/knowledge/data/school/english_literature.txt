# Figurative language, literary terms and poetry forms. Written by hand.
# tag: school.english.figurative-language
what is figurative language | types of figurative language | figurative vs literal => Figurative language uses words in a non-literal way to create images or effects. Types include similes, metaphors, personification, hyperbole, idioms, onomatopoeia and alliteration.
what is personification | personification examples | define personification => Personification gives human qualities to things that aren't human. "The wind whispered through the trees" and "The sun smiled down" are examples.
what is hyperbole | hyperbole examples | define hyperbole => Hyperbole is extreme exaggeration for effect, not meant literally. "I'm so hungry I could eat a horse" and "I've told you a million times" are examples.
what is an idiom | idiom examples | define idiom => An idiom is a phrase whose meaning isn't clear from its words. "It's raining cats and dogs" means it's raining hard, and "break a leg" means good luck.
what is assonance | assonance examples | define assonance => Assonance is the repetition of vowel sounds in nearby words, like "the light of the fire is a sight".
what is consonance | consonance examples | define consonance => Consonance is the repetition of consonant sounds, often at the ends or middles of words, like "pitter-patter" or "strike a match".
what is an oxymoron | oxymoron examples | define oxymoron => An oxymoron puts two opposite words together, like "jumbo shrimp", "deafening silence" or "bittersweet".
what is a pun | pun examples | define pun => A pun is a joke that plays on a word with two meanings or words that sound alike. "I used to be a banker, but I lost interest."
what is understatement | understatement examples | define understatement => Understatement makes something sound less important than it is, often for humor. Calling a hurricane "a bit of a breeze" is understatement.
what is irony | types of irony | define irony => Irony is a gap between what's expected and what happens. Verbal irony says the opposite of what's meant, situational irony is an unexpected outcome, and dramatic irony is when the audience knows something a character doesn't.
what is dramatic irony | dramatic irony examples | define dramatic irony => Dramatic irony is when the audience knows something a character doesn't. In a scary movie, we know the monster is behind the door while the character opens it.
what is situational irony | situational irony examples | define situational irony => Situational irony is when the opposite of what's expected happens, like a fire station burning down.
what is verbal irony | verbal irony examples | is sarcasm verbal irony => Verbal irony is saying the opposite of what you mean, like saying "Lovely weather!" in a storm. Sarcasm is verbal irony meant to mock or criticize.
what is imagery | imagery examples | define imagery in writing => Imagery is descriptive language that appeals to the five senses, helping readers see, hear, smell, taste or feel a scene. "The crisp, cold air stung my cheeks."
what is symbolism | symbolism examples | define symbolism => Symbolism is using an object, color or event to stand for a bigger idea. A dove often symbolizes peace, and a journey can symbolize growing up.
what is an allusion in literature | allusion examples | define allusion => An allusion is a short reference to a well-known person, story or event. Calling someone "a Romeo" alludes to Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet.
what is an analogy | analogy examples | define analogy => An analogy explains something by comparing it to something similar in a few ways. "A cell is like a factory: the nucleus is the manager."
what is a cliche | cliche examples | define cliche => A cliché is a phrase used so often it feels stale, like "at the end of the day" or "busy as a bee". Writers try to find fresher ways to say things.
what is a euphemism | euphemism examples | define euphemism => A euphemism is a gentle or polite way to say something unpleasant, like "passed away" instead of "died".
what is an extended metaphor | extended metaphor examples | define extended metaphor => An extended metaphor carries one comparison through several lines or a whole piece, like describing life as a road with turns, hills and dead ends.
what is the difference between a simile and a metaphor | simile vs metaphor | metaphor or simile => A simile compares using "like" or "as" (brave as a lion). A metaphor says one thing is another (he is a lion).
what is repetition in writing | repetition literary device | why do writers repeat words => Repetition is using a word or phrase more than once for emphasis or rhythm. Martin Luther King Jr. repeated "I have a dream" to make his speech powerful.
what is a rhetorical question | rhetorical question examples | define rhetorical question => A rhetorical question is asked for effect, not for an answer. "Who doesn't love summer?"
what is foreshadowing | foreshadowing examples | define foreshadowing => Foreshadowing is a hint about what will happen later in a story. Dark clouds at the start of a chapter might foreshadow trouble.
what is a flashback | flashback in literature | define flashback => A flashback is a scene that jumps back in time to show something that happened before the main story.
what is suspense | how do writers build suspense | define suspense => Suspense is the tense feeling of wanting to know what happens next. Writers build it with cliffhangers, short sentences, hidden information and danger.
what is a cliffhanger | cliffhanger meaning | define cliffhanger => A cliffhanger ends a chapter or episode at an exciting, unresolved moment, so you want to keep going.
what is mood in literature | mood vs tone | define mood in a story => Mood is the feeling a story creates in the reader, like spooky or cheerful. Tone is the author's attitude toward the subject, like serious or playful.
what is tone in writing | tone examples | define tone in literature => Tone is the author's attitude, shown through word choice. The same event could be told in a humorous, angry or sad tone.
what is voice in writing | author's voice | define voice in writing => Voice is the personality that comes through in writing: the word choice, rhythm and attitude that make it sound like a particular writer or narrator.
# tag: school.english.story-elements
what are the elements of a story | story elements | parts of a story => The main elements are characters, setting, plot, conflict, theme and point of view.
what is a plot | plot of a story | define plot => The plot is the sequence of events in a story. It usually has an exposition, rising action, climax, falling action and resolution.
what is a plot diagram | freytag's pyramid | story mountain => A plot diagram (story mountain) shows the plot as a hill: exposition at the start, rising action going up, the climax at the top, then falling action and resolution coming down.
what is exposition in a story | define exposition | exposition literature => The exposition is the beginning of a story, where the characters, setting and situation are introduced.
what is rising action | define rising action | rising action in a story => Rising action is the series of events that build tension and lead up to the climax.
what is the climax of a story | define climax | climax in literature => The climax is the turning point of the story, its most intense moment, when the main conflict comes to a head.
what is falling action | define falling action | falling action in a story => Falling action is what happens right after the climax, as the story's problems start to wind down.
what is the resolution of a story | define resolution literature | denouement => The resolution (or denouement) is the ending, where the conflict is settled and loose ends are tied up.
what is conflict in a story | types of conflict | define conflict literature => Conflict is the struggle that drives a story. Types include character vs. character, vs. self, vs. nature, vs. society, and vs. technology or the supernatural.
what is internal conflict | external conflict | internal vs external conflict => Internal conflict happens inside a character's mind, like fear or a hard choice. External conflict is a struggle with something outside, like another person, nature or society.
what is the setting of a story | define setting | setting in literature => The setting is where and when a story takes place, including the time period, place, and sometimes the weather or mood.
what is a protagonist | antagonist | protagonist vs antagonist => The protagonist is the main character. The antagonist is the character or force that opposes them.
what is a static character | dynamic character | static vs dynamic character => A dynamic character changes during the story, learning or growing. A static character stays basically the same.
what is a flat character | round character | flat vs round character => A round character is complex, with many traits. A flat character has just one or two traits and isn't developed much.
what is characterization | direct and indirect characterization | how do authors show character => Characterization is how an author reveals a character. Direct characterization tells you outright ("she was kind"); indirect characterization shows it through actions, speech, thoughts and others' reactions.
what is a foil character | define foil in literature | character foil => A foil is a character whose traits contrast with another's, making the other character's qualities stand out.
what is a theme | how do you find the theme | theme vs topic => A theme is the story's message or lesson about life, like "friendship takes effort". A topic is just a subject (friendship). Find the theme by asking what the main character learns.
what is a moral of a story | moral vs theme | define moral => A moral is a lesson the story teaches directly, common in fables, like "slow and steady wins the race". A theme is usually broader and not stated outright.
what is point of view | types of point of view | define point of view => Point of view is who tells the story. First person uses "I", second person uses "you", and third person uses "he", "she" or "they".
what is first person point of view | first person narrator | define first person pov => In first person, a character tells the story using "I" and "we", so we only know what that narrator knows and thinks.
what is third person limited | third person omniscient | third person limited vs omniscient => Third person limited follows one character's thoughts. Third person omniscient is an all-knowing narrator who can share any character's thoughts.
what is second person point of view | second person examples => Second person speaks to the reader as "you", as in "You walk into the room." It's rare in stories but common in instructions and choose-your-own-adventure books.
what is an unreliable narrator | define unreliable narrator | unreliable narrator examples => An unreliable narrator tells the story in a way we can't fully trust, because they lie, misunderstand or leave things out.
what is a narrator | narrator vs author | define narrator => The narrator is the voice telling the story. It isn't always the author; it can be a character the author invented.
what is dialogue | define dialogue in literature | why do authors use dialogue => Dialogue is the words characters say to each other. It reveals personality, moves the plot along and makes scenes feel real.
what is an aside in drama | define aside | aside in a play => An aside is a short remark a character makes to the audience that other characters on stage supposedly can't hear.
what is a genre | literary genres | types of genres => A genre is a type of writing with shared features. Examples include fantasy, science fiction, mystery, historical fiction, realistic fiction, biography and poetry.
what is fiction vs nonfiction | fiction or nonfiction | difference between fiction and nonfiction => Fiction is invented, made up by the author (though it may feel real). Nonfiction is about real people, events and facts.
what is realistic fiction | define realistic fiction | realistic fiction examples => Realistic fiction is made up but could really happen, with believable characters and settings, like Wonder by R. J. Palacio.
what is historical fiction | define historical fiction | historical fiction examples => Historical fiction is a made-up story set in a real past time, often including real events, like Number the Stars by Lois Lowry, set in World War II Denmark.
what is science fiction | define science fiction | sci fi vs fantasy => Science fiction imagines future science and technology, like space travel or robots. Fantasy uses magic and impossible creatures that science can't explain.
fairy tale vs folktale | difference between a fairy tale and a folktale => A folktale is any story passed down by word of mouth. Fairy tales are folktales with magic, like Cinderella. Tall tales exaggerate a hero's feats, like Paul Bunyan.
what is a biography | autobiography | biography vs autobiography => A biography is the true story of someone's life written by another person. An autobiography is written by the person themselves. A memoir focuses on certain memories.
what is a novel | novella | short story vs novel => A novel is a long fictional story, usually over 40,000 words. A novella is shorter, and a short story can often be read in one sitting.
what is a protagonist's motivation | character motivation | why does a character do things => Motivation is the reason behind a character's actions, what they want or fear. Understanding it helps explain the plot.
what is an archetype | define archetype | archetype examples => An archetype is a character type or pattern that appears in many stories, like the hero, the wise mentor, the trickster or the hero's journey.
what is the hero's journey | define hero's journey | hero's journey steps => The hero's journey is a common story pattern: a hero leaves the ordinary world, faces trials with help from a mentor, wins a great challenge, and returns changed.
what is a tragedy | define tragedy | comedy vs tragedy => In drama, a tragedy ends in disaster for the main character, often because of a flaw. A comedy usually ends happily.
what is satire | define satire | satire examples => Satire uses humor, exaggeration or irony to criticize people, ideas or society. Political cartoons are often satire.
what is a parody | define parody | parody vs satire => A parody imitates a work or style in an exaggerated, funny way. Satire aims to criticize; parody aims mainly to mock a specific work, though they often overlap.
# tag: school.english.poetry
what is a stanza | define stanza | stanza in a poem => A stanza is a group of lines in a poem, like a paragraph in prose. A two-line stanza is a couplet, three is a tercet, and four is a quatrain.
what is a couplet | define couplet | rhyming couplet => A couplet is two lines of poetry together, often rhyming: "Twinkle, twinkle, little star, / How I wonder what you are."
what is a quatrain | define quatrain | four line stanza => A quatrain is a stanza of four lines, often with a rhyme scheme like ABAB or AABB.
what is a rhyme scheme | how do you find the rhyme scheme | abab rhyme scheme => A rhyme scheme is the pattern of rhymes at line ends, labeled with letters. Give the first sound A, the next new sound B, and so on: "cat, hat, dog, log" is AABB.
what is rhythm in poetry | define rhythm poem | rhythm vs meter => Rhythm is the beat of a poem, made by stressed and unstressed syllables. Meter is a regular, repeating rhythm pattern.
what is meter in poetry | define meter | what is a foot in poetry => Meter is the regular pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables. Each unit is a foot, like the iamb (da-DUM) or trochee (DUM-da).
what is iambic pentameter | define iambic pentameter | shakespeare meter => Iambic pentameter is a line of five iambs, ten syllables in a da-DUM pattern: "But SOFT, what LIGHT through YONder WINdow BREAKS." Shakespeare often used it.
what is free verse | define free verse | poem that doesn't rhyme => Free verse is poetry without a regular rhyme or meter. It follows the natural rhythms of speech.
what is blank verse | define blank verse | blank verse vs free verse => Blank verse has a regular meter, usually iambic pentameter, but no rhyme. Free verse has neither.
what is a limerick | how do you write a limerick | limerick rhyme scheme => A limerick is a funny five-line poem with an AABBA rhyme scheme. Lines 1, 2 and 5 are longer, and lines 3 and 4 are shorter.
what is a sonnet | how many lines in a sonnet | shakespearean sonnet => A sonnet is a 14-line poem, usually in iambic pentameter. A Shakespearean sonnet has three quatrains and a final couplet, rhyming ABAB CDCD EFEF GG.
what is a cinquain | how do you write a cinquain | cinquain poem => A cinquain is a five-line poem. A popular school form uses 2, 4, 6, 8 and 2 syllables; another uses a word pattern of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 1 words.
what is an acrostic poem | how do you write an acrostic | acrostic poem examples => In an acrostic poem, the first letters of each line spell a word, often the topic, read down the left side.
what is a diamante poem | how to write a diamante | diamante poem format => A diamante is a seven-line diamond-shaped poem: noun, 2 adjectives, 3 -ing verbs, 4 nouns, 3 -ing verbs, 2 adjectives, noun. It often moves from one idea to its opposite.
what is a concrete poem | shape poem | define concrete poem => A concrete (shape) poem is arranged so its words form a picture of the subject, like a poem about rain shaped like falling drops.
what is a narrative poem | define narrative poem | narrative poem examples => A narrative poem tells a story with characters and a plot, like "The Raven" by Edgar Allan Poe or "Paul Revere's Ride" by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow.
what is a lyric poem | define lyric poem | lyric poetry => A lyric poem expresses personal thoughts and feelings, usually in a musical way. Most short poems are lyric poems.
what is an ode | define ode | ode poem examples => An ode is a poem of praise for a person, object or idea, often formal and full of feeling, like "Ode to a Nightingale" by John Keats.
what is an elegy | define elegy | elegy poem => An elegy is a poem mourning someone who has died.
what is a ballad | define ballad poem | ballad poetry => A ballad is a poem or song that tells a story, often in quatrains with a simple rhyme scheme and a refrain.
what is an epic poem | define epic | epic poem examples => An epic is a long narrative poem about a hero's great deeds, like Homer's Odyssey or the ancient Epic of Gilgamesh.
what is a refrain in poetry | define refrain | chorus in a poem => A refrain is a line or group of lines repeated in a poem or song, often at the end of each stanza, like a chorus.
what is enjambment | define enjambment | run on line in poetry => Enjambment is when a sentence runs past the end of a line into the next without a pause or punctuation.
what is a slant rhyme | near rhyme | define slant rhyme => A slant rhyme (near rhyme) uses words with similar but not identical sounds, like "worm" and "swarm" or "soul" and "all".
what is internal rhyme | define internal rhyme | internal rhyme examples => Internal rhyme is rhyme within a single line, like "Once upon a midnight dreary, while I pondered, weak and weary."
what is end rhyme | define end rhyme | end rhyme examples => End rhyme is rhyme at the ends of lines, the most common kind in poems and songs.
what is a tanka | define tanka | tanka poem syllables => A tanka is a Japanese form with five lines of 5, 7, 5, 7 and 7 syllables, 31 in all. It's like a haiku with two extra lines.
what is a villanelle | define villanelle | villanelle form => A villanelle is a 19-line poem with five tercets and a final quatrain, using two rhymes and two lines that repeat throughout. "Do Not Go Gentle into That Good Night" is a famous one.
what is a speaker in a poem | speaker vs poet | define speaker in poetry => The speaker is the voice talking in a poem. It may not be the poet; the poet can write as an invented character.
how do you analyze a poem | poem analysis steps | how to understand a poem => Read it aloud twice, look up unknown words, notice the speaker, mood, imagery and sound devices, check structure and rhyme, then ask what the poem is saying about its subject.
how do you write a poem | tips for writing poetry | how to start a poem => Pick a small, specific subject, use sensory details and strong verbs, play with line breaks and sound, and cut words you don't need. It doesn't have to rhyme.
