# tag: science.myths
is the great wall of china visible from space | can you see the great wall from the moon | great wall from orbit => No, not with the naked eye from the Moon, and it's very hard even from low orbit, because the wall is narrow and similar in color to the land around it. Astronauts can see cities and big highways more easily.
10 percent brain myth | is it true we use 10 percent of our brain => No, that's a myth. Brain scans show we use virtually all of our brain, just not every part at the same moment.
goldfish memory myth | how long do goldfish remember => No. Experiments show goldfish can remember things for months, and they can be trained to respond to sounds, colors or feeding times.
does lightning never strike the same place twice | lightning same spot myth => That's a myth. Lightning often hits the same spot, especially tall objects. The Empire State Building is struck about 20 to 25 times a year.
do bulls hate the color red | are bulls angry at red | why do matadors use red capes => No. Bulls are red-green colorblind. They charge at the cape because it moves, not because it's red.
does sugar make kids hyper | sugar hyperactivity myth | is sugar rush real => Careful studies have found that sugar doesn't make kids hyperactive. Exciting events like parties, where sugary food is common, are likely the real cause.
do we lose most body heat through our head | heat loss through head myth | should you wear a hat => No. Your head loses heat about as much as any other uncovered part of your body. A hat helps because your head is often the only uncovered part.
do you swallow spiders in your sleep | swallow 8 spiders a year myth | spiders in your mouth while sleeping => No. That's a myth. Sleeping people make vibrations and breathe out warm air, which spiders avoid.
do bats really go blind | blind as a bat myth => Bats aren't blind. All bats can see, and many fruit bats have excellent eyesight. Many bats also use echolocation to hunt in the dark.
is a penny dropped from a skyscraper deadly | penny from the empire state building => No. A penny is light and flat, so air resistance keeps it slow, about 40 to 80 km/h. It might sting, but it can't kill.
does water drain the other way in the southern hemisphere | coriolis effect toilet | sink drain direction => No. The Coriolis effect is far too weak to steer a sink or toilet. The direction depends on the shape of the basin and how the water was moving.
do chameleons change color to match their background | chameleon camouflage myth => Not mainly. Chameleons change color mostly to show mood, signal other chameleons and control temperature. Their usual colors already blend in.
is glass a liquid | do old windows sag because glass flows | is glass a slow liquid => No. Glass is an amorphous solid. Old windows are sometimes thicker at the bottom because of how the glass was made back then, not because it flowed.
dark side of the moon myth | is the far side of the moon dark => The far side of the Moon isn't dark. It gets just as much sunlight as the near side. We just never see it from Earth because the Moon is tidally locked.
is the sun yellow from space | the sun is yellow myth => The Sun is actually white. It gives off all colors of light and looks yellow only because our atmosphere scatters away some blue light.
does a duck quack echo | duck quack no echo myth => Yes, a duck's quack echoes like any sound. The myth probably started because quacks are soft and fade out, so the echo is hard to notice.
do hair and nails keep growing after death | hair grows after death myth => No. After death, skin dries and shrinks back, which makes hair and nails look longer, but they don't grow.
does reading in dim light damage your eyes | reading in the dark myth | is reading in low light bad => No. Reading in dim light can make your eyes tired, but it doesn't cause lasting damage.
does sitting close to the tv ruin your eyes | is sitting close to the screen bad | tv eyesight myth => There's no evidence it causes permanent damage, though it can tire your eyes. Taking breaks, like looking far away every 20 minutes, helps.
can you see the stars from the bottom of a well | stars in daytime from a well myth => No. A well blocks out most of the sky, but the patch you see is still lit by sunlight scattered in the air, so stars stay hidden in daytime.
are diamonds made from coal | do diamonds come from coal myth => Almost never. Most diamonds formed deep in the mantle, far below where coal is found, and many are older than land plants, which coal comes from.
heavy things fall faster myth | galileo falling objects => Without air resistance, heavy and light objects fall at the same rate. A feather falls slowly on Earth only because air slows it down.
does humidity mean the air is heavier | is humid air heavier | moist air density => Surprisingly, humid air is lighter than dry air at the same temperature and pressure, because water vapor molecules weigh less than the nitrogen and oxygen they replace.
