# tag: science.chem.lab
what is a beaker used for | what does a beaker look like | beaker in science => A beaker is a wide glass cup with a flat bottom and a lip for pouring. It's used to hold, mix and heat liquids. Its markings are only rough measurements.
what is an erlenmeyer flask | erlenmeyer flask used for => A conical or Erlenmeyer flask has a flat bottom and a narrow neck, so you can swirl liquids without spilling. It was invented by Emil Erlenmeyer in 1860.
what is a test tube used for | test tube rack => A test tube holds small amounts of chemicals for reactions. When heating one, hold it with a test tube holder, tilt it, and point the open end away from everyone.
what is a bunsen burner | who invented the bunsen burner | how does a bunsen burner work => A Bunsen burner is a gas burner used to heat things in the lab. Robert Bunsen and his technician Peter Desaga made it popular in 1855. The air hole controls how hot the flame is.
what is a blue flame vs yellow flame | which bunsen flame is hottest => With the air hole closed, the Bunsen flame is yellow, wobbly and easy to see: the safety flame. With it open, it's a roaring blue flame, much hotter, used for heating.
what is a pipette used for | pipette vs dropper | how to use a pipette => A pipette moves a measured amount of liquid. A volumetric pipette measures one exact volume, like 25.0 mL. Always use a pipette filler, never your mouth.
what is a burette | what is a burette used for | titration equipment => A burette is a long glass tube with a tap and markings, used to add exact amounts of liquid during a titration.
what is a titration | acid base titration => A titration finds the concentration of a solution by slowly adding another solution of known concentration from a burette until the reaction is complete, often shown by an indicator changing color.
what is an evaporating dish | how to evaporate a solution | evaporating basin => An evaporating dish is a shallow porcelain bowl used to heat a solution so the water evaporates and leaves solid crystals behind.
what is a crucible | what is a crucible used for | crucible tongs => A crucible is a small heat-proof cup, often porcelain, used to heat solids to very high temperatures. You move it with crucible tongs.
what is a tripod and gauze | why use a gauze in the lab | tripod stand => A tripod is a three-legged metal stand that holds equipment over a Bunsen burner. A wire gauze on top spreads the heat and supports beakers.
what is a clamp stand | retort stand | what holds a test tube in place => A clamp stand, or retort stand, is a heavy base with a rod, and clamps that hold glassware like burettes and flasks steady.
what is a petri dish | who invented the petri dish | growing bacteria in a dish => A Petri dish is a shallow, round dish with a lid, used to grow bacteria or fungi on a jelly called agar. Julius Petri invented it in 1887.
what is a watch glass | watch glass used for | what is a watch glass in chemistry => A watch glass is a round, slightly curved piece of glass used to hold small amounts of solids, cover beakers, or let liquids evaporate.
what is a spatula in chemistry | how to measure powders in the lab | lab spatula => A lab spatula is a small metal or plastic scoop used to move small amounts of solid chemicals.
what is a mortar and pestle | why grind chemicals | mortar and pestle used for => A mortar is a heavy bowl and a pestle is a club-shaped tool. They're used to crush and grind solids into powder, which makes them dissolve and react faster.
what is a funnel used for | filter funnel | how to filter with filter paper => A funnel guides liquids into narrow openings. For filtering, fold filter paper into a cone inside the funnel, and the solid stays on the paper while liquid passes through.
what is a condenser in chemistry | liebig condenser | how does distillation equipment work => A condenser is a glass tube surrounded by a jacket of flowing cold water. In distillation, it cools vapor back into liquid.
what is an electronic balance | how to zero a balance | tare button => An electronic balance measures mass precisely. Pressing tare sets it to zero with an empty container on it, so you only measure what you add.
what is a thermometer in the lab | digital vs liquid thermometer | lab thermometer range => Lab thermometers measure temperature, often from minus 10 to 110 °C. Read a liquid thermometer at eye level, and don't use it to stir.
what are safety goggles for | why wear goggles in science | lab eye protection => Goggles protect your eyes from splashes, flying glass and fumes. Wear them whenever chemicals, heating or anything that could break or splash is involved.
what does corrosive mean | corrosive symbol | corrosive chemicals examples => Corrosive chemicals destroy living tissue and can eat through some materials, like strong acids and alkalis. Their symbol shows liquid dripping onto a hand and a metal bar.
flammable vs inflammable | flammable symbol => Flammable means it catches fire easily. Confusingly, inflammable means the same thing. The hazard symbol is a black flame.
how do you test for hydrogen gas | squeaky pop test | hydrogen gas test => Hold a lit splint at the mouth of the test tube. If hydrogen is there, it burns with a squeaky pop.
how do you test for oxygen gas | glowing splint test | oxygen gas test => Put a glowing splint (one that has just been blown out) into the gas. If oxygen is there, the splint relights.
how do you test for carbon dioxide | limewater test | what turns limewater milky => Bubble the gas through limewater (calcium hydroxide solution). If carbon dioxide is there, the limewater turns milky or cloudy.
how do you test for chlorine gas | chlorine bleaches litmus | test for chlorine => Hold damp blue litmus paper in the gas. Chlorine turns it red and then bleaches it white. Only do this in a fume cupboard.
how do you test for water | anhydrous copper sulfate test | cobalt chloride paper => White anhydrous copper sulfate turns blue when water is added. Blue cobalt chloride paper turns pink with water.
what color does lithium burn | lithium flame test color | lithium flame => Lithium compounds give a bright crimson red flame in a flame test.
what color does sodium burn | sodium flame test color | why are street lights orange => Sodium gives a strong yellow-orange flame. Old orange street lights glowed that color because they used sodium vapor.
what color does potassium burn | potassium flame test color | lilac flame => Potassium compounds give a lilac, pale purple flame. It's often hidden by any sodium, so it's viewed through blue cobalt glass.
what color does copper burn | copper flame test color | green flame chemistry => Copper compounds give a blue-green flame.
what color does calcium burn | calcium flame test color | brick red flame => Calcium compounds give an orange-red, often called brick red, flame.
what color does strontium burn | strontium flame color | red fireworks chemical => Strontium gives a bright red flame and is used to make red fireworks and road flares.
what color does barium burn | barium flame test color | green fireworks chemical => Barium gives a pale green flame and is used for green fireworks.
what is the balanced equation for making water | hydrogen and oxygen equation | 2h2 + o2 => 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. Two hydrogen molecules react with one oxygen molecule to make two water molecules.
what is the equation for burning methane | methane combustion equation | natural gas burning equation => CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O. Methane burns with oxygen to make carbon dioxide and water, releasing heat.
what is the equation for burning propane | propane combustion equation | barbecue gas equation => C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O. Propane burns with oxygen to make carbon dioxide and water.
respiration chemical equation | glucose and oxygen equation => C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O, plus energy. Glucose and oxygen make carbon dioxide and water.
what is the equation for rusting | iron rust equation | iron and oxygen equation => A simple version is 4Fe + 3O2 → 2Fe2O3. In real life, water is needed too, and rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide.
what is the equation for burning magnesium | magnesium and oxygen equation | 2mg + o2 => 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO. Magnesium burns with a brilliant white flame to make white magnesium oxide powder.
what is the equation for sodium and chlorine | how is salt made from elements | sodium chloride formation equation => 2Na + Cl2 → 2NaCl. A soft, reactive metal and a poisonous gas combine into ordinary table salt.
what is the equation for baking soda and vinegar | sodium bicarbonate and acetic acid equation | volcano reaction equation => NaHCO3 + CH3COOH → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2. Baking soda and vinegar make sodium acetate, water and carbon dioxide gas.
what is the equation for neutralization | hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide equation | hcl + naoh => HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O. An acid and a base make a salt and water.
what is the equation for zinc and hydrochloric acid | metal and acid equation | zn + hcl => Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2. A reactive metal and an acid make a salt and hydrogen gas.
what is the equation for heating limestone | calcium carbonate decomposition | thermal decomposition of limestone => CaCO3 → CaO + CO2. Heating limestone breaks it into quicklime and carbon dioxide.
what is the equation for hydrogen peroxide decomposition | peroxide breaking down | 2h2o2 => 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2. Hydrogen peroxide breaks down into water and oxygen, faster with a catalyst.
what is the equation for electrolysis of water | splitting water equation | water into hydrogen and oxygen => 2H2O → 2H2 + O2. Passing electricity through water splits it into hydrogen and oxygen, with twice as much hydrogen gas.
what is the haber process | how is ammonia made | n2 + 3h2 => The Haber process makes ammonia: N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3, using an iron catalyst, high pressure and about 450 °C. Most of it becomes fertilizer, which feeds billions of people.
what is the equation for sodium and water | 2na + 2h2o => 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2. Sodium fizzes, melts into a ball and zooms around on water, making sodium hydroxide and hydrogen.
what is the thermite reaction | thermite equation | aluminium and iron oxide => 2Al + Fe2O3 → Al2O3 + 2Fe. Aluminium takes oxygen from iron oxide so violently that it makes molten iron at over 2,500 °C. It's used to weld railway tracks.
what is the limewater reaction equation | carbon dioxide and calcium hydroxide | why does limewater go cloudy => Ca(OH)2 + CO2 → CaCO3 + H2O. The cloudiness is tiny particles of calcium carbonate that don't dissolve.
what is the copper and silver nitrate reaction | silver tree experiment | cu + agno3 => Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag. A copper wire in silver nitrate grows shiny silver crystals while the solution turns blue.
how to write a word equation | word equation example => A word equation names the reactants and products, like magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide. Symbol equations then use formulas.
what are state symbols | what does aq mean in chemistry | s l g aq meaning => State symbols show each substance's state: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas and (aq) aqueous, meaning dissolved in water.
how to use a balanced equation | stoichiometry meaning => Stoichiometry uses balanced equations to work out amounts. In 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O, two moles of hydrogen react with one mole of oxygen, a 2 to 1 ratio.
