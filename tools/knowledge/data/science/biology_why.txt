# tag: science.bio.plants
why do trees lose their leaves in winter | why are some trees bare in winter | deciduous trees winter => Losing leaves helps deciduous trees save water and avoid frost damage in winter, when there's little sunlight and frozen ground makes water hard to get.
why are evergreen trees green all winter | conifer needles => Conifer needles are narrow and coated in wax, so they lose little water and can survive freezing. Evergreens do shed old needles, just a few at a time.
phototropism | why do plants bend to the window => A plant hormone called auxin collects on the shady side of a stem and makes those cells grow longer, so the stem bends toward the light. This is phototropism.
gravitropism | why do roots grow downward => Roots sense gravity using tiny dense grains in their tip cells that settle downward. Hormones then steer growth so roots grow down and shoots grow up.
why do plants need sunlight | can plants grow without light | plants in the dark => Plants use light energy to make sugar by photosynthesis. In the dark, a seedling grows tall, thin and pale as it searches for light, and eventually runs out of stored food.
why do cactuses have spines | why do cacti have spikes | cactus adaptations => Cactus spines are modified leaves. They protect the cactus from animals, shade it, and lose much less water than flat leaves would. The thick green stem does the photosynthesis.
why do fruits have seeds | why do plants make fruit => Fruits help plants spread their seeds. Animals eat the tasty fruit and drop the seeds somewhere else, often in their droppings, which also act as fertilizer.
why do some seeds have wings | seed dispersal => Seeds spread in many ways: maple seeds spin like helicopters, dandelion seeds float on fluffy parachutes, burrs hitch rides on fur, and coconuts float across the sea.
why do onions grow in layers | what is a bulb plant | how do bulbs work => A bulb, like an onion or tulip, is a short underground stem wrapped in thick, fleshy leaves that store food, letting the plant survive winter and regrow in spring.
why do sunflowers face the sun | heliotropism => Young sunflowers turn east to west through the day to follow the Sun and swing back at night. Once they bloom, most stop moving and face east.
why does grass grow back after mowing | how does grass survive mowing | where grass grows from => Grass grows from near its base, not from the tips, so cutting off the tops doesn't stop it growing. That's also why it survives grazing animals.
why do mushrooms grow after rain | when do mushrooms come up | mushrooms after rain => The main part of a fungus is a hidden web of threads in the soil or wood. After rain, it has the water it needs to quickly push up mushrooms, which release spores.
why are mushrooms not plants | are fungi plants | why are fungi their own kingdom => Fungi can't make their own food by photosynthesis. They absorb food from their surroundings, and their cell walls are made of chitin, like insect shells. They're actually more closely related to animals than plants.
why do venus flytraps eat bugs | how does a venus flytrap catch flies => Venus flytraps grow in boggy soil low in nutrients, so they catch insects for extra nitrogen. Trigger hairs inside the trap must be touched twice quickly to make it snap shut.
why do some plants close at night | why do leaves fold at night => Some plants, like prayer plants and many legumes, fold their leaves at night by moving water in special joints at the leaf base. It may help them save water and avoid nighttime pests.
# tag: science.bio.body
why do fingers wrinkle in water | pruney fingers | why do you get wrinkly in the bath => After a few minutes in water, nerves make blood vessels in your fingers shrink, which wrinkles the skin. Some scientists think wrinkly fingers help grip wet things.
why does your foot fall asleep | why do legs go numb => Sitting in one position can squeeze nerves and blood vessels, so messages from your foot stop getting through. When you move, the nerves wake up and send fizzy pins-and-needles signals.
what causes ice cream headache | how to stop brain freeze => Brain freeze happens when something cold touches the roof of your mouth, quickly changing blood flow in nearby vessels. Pressing your tongue to the roof of your mouth helps it pass faster.
why do our eyes blink | why do we need to blink => Blinking spreads tears over your eyes to keep them moist and clean and protects them from dust. People blink about 15 to 20 times a minute.
what makes you left handed | percent left handed => About 10% of people are left-handed. It's linked to how the brain is organized, influenced by many genes and the environment before birth.
what does a fever do | why does your body heat up when sick => A fever is your body raising its temperature to fight infection. Higher temperatures help immune cells work and make it harder for some germs to multiply.
why do cuts form scabs | how does skin heal => When you cut yourself, platelets and a protein called fibrin form a clot that dries into a scab. It protects the wound while new skin grows underneath.
why do bruises turn green | bruise colors => A bruise is blood leaking under the skin. As your body breaks down hemoglobin, the bruise changes from red or purple to blue, then green and yellow before fading.
why does your heart beat faster when you run | why does exercise raise heart rate | heart rate exercise => Working muscles need more oxygen and fuel. Your heart pumps faster and harder to deliver more blood to them and to carry away carbon dioxide.
why do we breathe faster when exercising | why do you pant after running | breathing exercise => Exercising muscles make more carbon dioxide. Your brain senses it building up in your blood and makes you breathe faster and deeper to get rid of it and take in oxygen.
what causes muscle soreness | doms => Soreness a day or two after hard exercise comes from tiny damage to muscle fibers as they work in new ways. As they repair, they get stronger.
what do vitamins do | what happens without vitamins => Vitamins are nutrients your body needs in small amounts to work properly. For example, vitamin C helps heal wounds, and without enough of it for weeks, people get scurvy.
why does your voice crack in puberty | why do voices get deeper | voice change => During puberty, the larynx (voice box) grows and the vocal folds get longer and thicker, so voices deepen. While it adjusts, a voice can suddenly crack or squeak.
why do kids grow so fast | when do you stop growing => Bones grow at growth plates near their ends. They're very active during childhood and puberty, then harden and close, usually by the late teens or early twenties, when growth stops.
why do we lose baby teeth | how many baby teeth do kids have => Kids have 20 baby teeth, which make room for the adult teeth forming underneath. Adult teeth push them out from about age 6 to 12, and adults have up to 32 teeth.
runny nose in winter | why does cold air make your nose drip => Your nose warms and moistens the air you breathe. In cold air, it makes extra mucus, and water from your warm breath condenses in your nose, so it drips.
why do we get freckles | what causes freckles | freckles and sun => Freckles are small spots with extra melanin. They're mostly genetic and get darker with sun exposure, which is why they often show up more in summer.
why does skin tan in the sun | how does tanning work | is a tan healthy => Sunlight's ultraviolet rays make skin cells produce more melanin, a pigment that absorbs UV. A tan is a sign of skin damage, so sunscreen and shade are better protection.
why do we get sunburned | how to prevent sunburn => Sunburn is skin damage from the Sun's ultraviolet rays. It can happen on cloudy days too. Sunscreen with SPF 30 or more, hats and shade help prevent it.
