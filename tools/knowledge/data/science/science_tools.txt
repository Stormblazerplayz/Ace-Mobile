# tag: science.tools
how does an ear thermometer work | how do forehead thermometers work | infrared thermometer => Ear and forehead thermometers measure the invisible infrared radiation your body gives off. Warmer objects give off more infrared, so the sensor can work out the temperature without touching.
what does a hygrometer measure | how is humidity measured | humidity meter => A hygrometer measures humidity, how much water vapor is in the air. Old ones used human hair, which gets longer in damp air.
what is weather radar | how does doppler radar work | how does radar see rain => Weather radar sends out radio pulses that bounce off raindrops, snow and hail. Doppler radar also measures how fast the drops move toward or away from it, which helps spot rotating storms.
what does a seismograph record | how do they measure earthquakes => A seismometer has a heavy mass that stays still while the ground shakes around it, and the motion between them is recorded. The squiggly record is called a seismogram.
how does an electron microscope work | how small can you see => Electron microscopes use beams of electrons instead of light, so they can magnify over a million times and show viruses and even large molecules.
what is a microscope slide | onion cell slide => A slide is a thin glass rectangle that holds a sample under a microscope. A thin piece of onion skin with a drop of iodine and a cover slip on top is a classic first slide.
what is a test tube | lab glassware => A beaker is a wide glass cup with a spout for mixing and heating liquids. A test tube is a narrow glass tube for small amounts. A measuring cylinder is tall and narrow for measuring volume accurately.
what is a pipette | measuring small amounts of liquid => A pipette is a thin tube for moving small, exact amounts of liquid. A burette is a long glass tube with a tap and scale, used to add liquid drop by drop.
what is a balance in science | how do you measure mass | digital scale vs balance => A balance measures mass. Lab balances can measure to a thousandth of a gram or better, and they need to be zeroed, or tared, before use.
what is a spring balance | how to measure force => A newton meter, or spring balance, measures force. A spring inside stretches more with more force, moving a pointer along a scale in newtons.
what is a stopwatch used for in science | how to measure time in an experiment | reaction time error => Scientists time events with stopwatches or sensors. Human reaction time adds about 0.2 seconds of error, so it helps to time several repeats and take an average.
what is a data logger | how do sensors record data | data logging experiment => A data logger is a device that records readings from sensors, like temperature or light, at set times. It can collect data for hours or days without anyone watching.
what is a spectroscope | how do you split light | diffraction grating glasses => A spectroscope splits light into its colors so you can study the spectrum. Each element gives its own pattern of bright lines, which helps identify it.
how do you detect radiation | geiger counter clicks => A Geiger counter detects ionizing radiation. Each time radiation passes through its gas-filled tube, it makes a tiny current pulse, heard as a click.
what is a particle accelerator | lhc => A particle accelerator speeds up particles close to the speed of light and smashes them together. The Large Hadron Collider near Geneva is the biggest, a ring about 27 km around, where the Higgs boson was found in 2012.
what is a wind tunnel | why do engineers use wind tunnels | wind tunnel testing => A wind tunnel blows air past a model plane, car or building so engineers can measure drag and lift and see how air flows around it.
how to grow bacteria in a petri dish | agar plate => A petri dish is a shallow, round dish with a lid, often filled with agar jelly that feeds microbes. Julius Petri, a German scientist, developed it in 1887.
what is an autoclave | how are lab tools sterilized | sterilizing with steam => An autoclave sterilizes equipment using high-pressure steam at about 121 °C, which kills bacteria, viruses and spores.
what is a sonar | how do ships map the seafloor => Sonar sends out sound pulses underwater and listens for the echoes. Timing the echoes shows how deep the water is or where objects are, which is how most of the seafloor has been mapped.
what is a satellite used for in science | earth observation satellites | how do satellites study earth => Earth-watching satellites measure clouds, sea temperature, ice, forests, pollution and more from orbit, giving scientists a view of the whole planet every day.
what is a hydrophone | listening underwater | whale song recording => A hydrophone is an underwater microphone. Scientists use them to record whale songs, earthquakes under the sea and ships.
what is a sundial | how does a sundial tell time | how to make a sundial => A sundial tells the time from the shadow of a pointer called a gnomon. As the Sun moves across the sky, the shadow moves around marked hours.
