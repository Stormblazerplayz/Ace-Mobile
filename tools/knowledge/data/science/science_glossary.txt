# tag: science.chem.matter
what is a freezing point | freezing point of water | freezing point vs melting point => The freezing point is the temperature where a liquid turns solid. For pure water at sea level, it's 0 °C (32 °F), the same as ice's melting point.
what is a monomer | monomer vs polymer | monomer examples => A monomer is a small molecule that can link up with many others like it to form a polymer. Ethene molecules join to make polyethylene, and glucose units join to make starch.
what is a metalloid | list of metalloids => Metalloids have properties between metals and nonmetals. Boron, silicon, germanium, arsenic, antimony and tellurium are usually included. Silicon's semiconductor behavior makes computer chips possible.
what are lanthanides | rare earths uses => The lanthanides are the 15 elements from lanthanum to lutetium. With scandium and yttrium, they're called rare earth elements and are used in strong magnets, phone screens and wind turbines.
what are actinides | actinide series | radioactive actinides => The actinides are the 15 radioactive elements from actinium to lawrencium. Only thorium and uranium are found in large amounts in nature. Most of the rest are made in reactors or labs.
what is molarity | how do you calculate molarity | moles per liter => Molarity is concentration in moles of solute per liter of solution. Dissolving 1 mole of salt to make 1 liter of solution gives a 1 molar (1 M) solution.
what is dilution | how do you dilute a solution | diluting an acid safely => Dilution is adding more solvent to make a solution weaker. When diluting acid, always add the acid slowly to water, not water to acid, because it can heat up and splash.
what is an unsaturated solution | saturated vs unsaturated solution => An unsaturated solution can still dissolve more solute. A saturated one can't dissolve any more at that temperature. A supersaturated one holds more than it normally could and crystallizes if disturbed.
what is an aerogel | lightest solid | frozen smoke => Aerogel is a solid made mostly of air, up to about 99.8%, nicknamed frozen smoke. It's an amazing insulator and was used by NASA's Stardust mission to catch comet dust.
what is a nanoparticle | what is nano size | nanoparticles uses => A nanoparticle is between about 1 and 100 nanometers across. At that size, materials can behave differently: gold nanoparticles look red or purple, and some sunscreens use nanoparticles of zinc oxide.
what is a superfluid | superfluid helium | liquid that climbs walls => A superfluid flows with zero friction. Helium cooled below about minus 271 °C becomes superfluid and can creep up and over the sides of its container.
what is permeability in rocks | what is porosity | porous vs permeable => Porosity is how much empty space a rock has, and permeability is how easily water flows through it. Sandstone is porous and permeable, while clay is porous but lets water through very slowly.
# tag: science.physics.atoms
what is radioactive decay | why do atoms decay | what happens in radioactive decay => Radioactive decay is when an unstable nucleus breaks down by itself, giving off radiation and often turning into a different element. Nobody can predict when one atom will decay, only the average rate.
what is an alpha particle | alpha radiation | how far does alpha radiation travel => An alpha particle is two protons and two neutrons, the same as a helium nucleus. It's stopped by a sheet of paper or a few centimeters of air, but is dangerous if swallowed or breathed in.
what is a beta particle | beta radiation | what stops beta radiation => A beta particle is a fast electron (or positron) shot out of a nucleus when a neutron turns into a proton. A few millimeters of aluminium stops it.
what is a gamma ray made of | gamma radiation | what stops gamma rays => Gamma rays are very high-energy electromagnetic waves, with no mass or charge. They pass through a lot and need thick lead or concrete to block them.
what is an interferometer | how does an interferometer work | ligo interferometer => An interferometer combines waves, usually light, to measure tiny differences. LIGO uses laser beams in 4 km long arms to detect length changes smaller than a thousandth of a proton.
# tag: science.space.universe
what is a blazar | blazar vs quasar | black hole jet pointed at earth => A blazar is a quasar whose jet from its supermassive black hole points almost straight at Earth, making it look extremely bright and flickering.
what is a lagrange point | where is the james webb telescope | l2 point => Lagrange points are spots where the gravity of two big objects, like the Sun and Earth, balances so a spacecraft can stay put relative to them. Webb orbits around L2, about 1.5 million km from Earth.
what is the roche limit | why do moons break apart | how are rings formed => The Roche limit is how close a moon can get to a planet before tides tear it apart. Saturn's rings may have formed when a moon or comet got too close.
what is a tidal force | tidal forces in space | how tides stretch things => A tidal force happens because gravity pulls harder on the near side of an object than the far side, stretching it. It causes ocean tides and can heat moons like Io.
what is an accretion disk | disk around a black hole | how black holes glow => An accretion disk is a swirling disk of gas and dust spiraling into a star or black hole. Friction heats it to millions of degrees, so it glows brightly.
what is a singularity in a black hole | center of a black hole | what is inside a black hole => In theory, a black hole's mass is crushed into a singularity, a point of infinite density at the center. Most physicists think a future theory of quantum gravity will explain what's really there.
how does gravity act like a lens | einstein ring => Gravitational lensing is when a galaxy or black hole bends light from something behind it, like a lens, making it look magnified, stretched into arcs, or even a full circle called an Einstein ring.
what is a hypergiant star | biggest stars in the universe | largest known star => Hypergiants are the biggest, brightest stars known. Some red supergiants, like Stephenson 2-18 and UY Scuti, may be over 1,500 times wider than the Sun, though their exact sizes are hard to measure.
what is a kilonova | neutron star collision | where does gold come from in space => A kilonova is the flash from two neutron stars colliding. In 2017, astronomers saw one along with gravitational waves, and it made heavy elements like gold and platinum.
what is a gamma ray burst | most powerful explosions | grb => Gamma-ray bursts are the brightest explosions known, flashes of gamma rays from collapsing giant stars or merging neutron stars. One can release more energy in seconds than the Sun will in its lifetime.
what is a sunspot | why are sunspots dark | how big are sunspots => Sunspots are cooler, darker patches on the Sun caused by strong magnetic fields. They're still about 3,500 °C and some are bigger than Earth.
what is the chromosphere | layers of the sun | photosphere chromosphere corona => The Sun's visible surface is the photosphere. Above it is the thin, reddish chromosphere, and then the huge, very hot corona, seen during total eclipses.
what is the magnetosphere | earths magnetic shield | how does earths magnetic field protect us => The magnetosphere is the region around Earth controlled by its magnetic field. It deflects most of the solar wind and cosmic rays, protecting the atmosphere and life.
what are the van allen belts | radiation belts around earth | who discovered the van allen belts => The Van Allen belts are two doughnut-shaped zones of charged particles trapped by Earth's magnetic field. They were discovered in 1958 by James Van Allen using America's first satellite, Explorer 1.
what is the ionosphere | how do radio waves bounce | ionosphere layer => The ionosphere is part of the upper atmosphere, about 60 to 1,000 km up, full of charged particles made by sunlight. It can bounce radio waves around the world and is where auroras glow.
# tag: science.earth.geology
what is an ice core | how do ice cores show past climate | oldest ice core => Ice cores are long tubes of ice drilled from glaciers and ice sheets. Air bubbles trapped inside show past climate and carbon dioxide levels, going back about 800,000 years in Antarctica.
what is stratigraphy | studying rock layers | stratigraphy meaning => Stratigraphy is the study of rock layers, their order and what they reveal about Earth's history.
what is isostasy | why do continents float | rebound after ice age => Isostasy is how Earth's crust floats on the mantle like ice on water. When heavy ice sheets melted, land such as Scandinavia and Canada began slowly rising, and it's still rising today.
what is a mantle plume | where do hot spots come from | plume volcanoes => A mantle plume is a column of unusually hot rock rising from deep in the mantle. Where it reaches the surface, it can make hot spot volcanoes like Hawaii and Iceland.
what is orogeny | mountain building | how long do mountains take to form => Orogeny means mountain building, usually when tectonic plates collide. It takes millions of years, and mountains are slowly worn down by erosion at the same time.
what is a batholith | what is a dike in geology | what is a sill in geology => These are bodies of magma that cooled underground. A batholith is a huge mass, like the granite of Yosemite. A dike cuts across rock layers, and a sill slides between them.
what is a fumarole | volcanic vents steam | fumarole gases => A fumarole is a vent near a volcano that lets out steam and gases like sulfur dioxide. Some leave yellow crusts of sulfur around them.
what is tephra | volcanic bombs | what does a volcano throw out => Tephra is all the rock fragments a volcano throws into the air, from fine ash to lapilli (pea- to walnut-sized) to volcanic bombs bigger than 64 mm.
what is karst | karst landscape | limestone landscapes => Karst is a landscape shaped by water dissolving rock like limestone, with caves, sinkholes, underground rivers and disappearing streams.
what is a cenote | cenotes in mexico | natural sinkhole pools => A cenote is a natural sinkhole filled with groundwater, common in Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, where the limestone collapsed into underground rivers. The Maya considered them sacred.
what is an artesian well | artesian aquifer | water that flows up by itself => An artesian well taps an aquifer trapped between layers of rock under pressure, so water rises up the well on its own, sometimes even gushing out.
what is alluvium | alluvial soil | why are river floodplains fertile => Alluvium is sand, silt and clay deposited by rivers. Floods spread it across floodplains, making very fertile soil, as along the Nile.
what is an esker | what is a drumlin => These are landforms left by glaciers. Moraines are ridges of rock and soil the glacier pushed or dropped, eskers are long winding ridges from rivers under the ice, and drumlins are smooth, egg-shaped hills.
what is a cirque | what is a tarn | glacier carved bowl => A cirque is a bowl-shaped hollow carved by a glacier high on a mountain. When the ice melts, it often holds a small lake called a tarn.
what is loess | wind blown soil | loess plateau => Loess is fine, yellowish silt blown by the wind, often after the ice ages. It makes very fertile soil, like China's Loess Plateau.
what is a playa | dry lake bed | racetrack playa sliding rocks => A playa is a flat, dry lake bed in a desert. On Racetrack Playa in Death Valley, rocks slide across the mud, pushed by wind on thin sheets of ice, which was finally filmed in 2014.
what is a hoodoo rock | bryce canyon hoodoos | rock pillars => A hoodoo is a tall, thin spire of rock, often with a harder cap protecting softer rock beneath. Bryce Canyon in Utah has thousands of them.
what is a tombolo | what is a spit in geography | sandbar connecting island => A spit is a long ridge of sand that sticks out from the shore. A tombolo is a sandbar that connects an island to the mainland.
what is the abyssal plain | what is the hadal zone | deepest ocean zones => The abyssal plain is the flat deep-sea floor, about 3,000 to 6,000 meters down, covering much of the Earth. The hadal zone is the trenches deeper than about 6,000 meters.
what is a guyot | flat topped seamount | sunken islands => A guyot is a flat-topped underwater mountain. It was once a volcanic island whose top was worn flat by waves before it sank.
# tag: science.earth.weather
what is a chinook wind | what is a foehn wind | snow eater wind => Chinook and foehn winds are warm, dry winds that blow down the side of mountains. They can raise temperatures very fast and melt snow quickly, which is why chinooks are called snow eaters.
what is a sirocco | what is a mistral | named winds of the world => The sirocco is a hot, dusty wind blowing from the Sahara into southern Europe. The mistral is a cold, strong wind blowing down France's Rhône valley to the Mediterranean.
what is a microburst | what is a downburst | why are microbursts dangerous for planes => A downburst is a strong rush of sinking air from a thunderstorm that spreads out at the ground. A small, intense one is a microburst, dangerous for planes taking off or landing.
what is wind shear | wind shear and planes | wind shear in storms => Wind shear is a change in wind speed or direction over a short distance or height. It can make planes lose lift suddenly, and it helps supercell thunderstorms rotate.
what is graupel | soft hail | snow pellets => Graupel is soft, white pellets that form when supercooled water droplets freeze onto falling snowflakes. They're softer than hail and crumble easily.
what is virga | rain that doesnt reach the ground | streaks under clouds => Virga is rain or snow that falls from a cloud but evaporates before reaching the ground, appearing as wispy streaks hanging under the cloud.
what is a brocken spectre | glory around your shadow | shadow in the clouds => A Brocken spectre is your giant shadow cast onto mist or cloud below you, often surrounded by colored rings called a glory. Mountain climbers and plane passengers can see it.
what is the green flash | green flash at sunset | why is the sun green at sunset => The green flash is a brief green spot or flash seen at the top of the Sun just as it sets or rises, caused by the atmosphere bending colors differently. A clear, flat horizon like the sea helps.
what are crepuscular rays | sunbeams through clouds | god rays => Crepuscular rays are beams of sunlight streaming through gaps in clouds, often at sunrise or sunset. They're actually parallel but look like they spread out because of perspective.
what is alpenglow | why do mountains glow pink | mountain sunset glow => Alpenglow is the rosy pink or orange glow on mountains just before sunrise or after sunset, when they're lit by light scattered through the atmosphere.
what is a fogbow | what is a moonbow | white rainbow => A fogbow is a pale, almost white rainbow made by tiny fog droplets. A moonbow is a faint rainbow made by moonlight, usually looking white to our eyes.
what is petrichor | why does rain smell good | smell after rain => Petrichor is the earthy smell after rain on dry ground. It comes mainly from a chemical called geosmin made by soil bacteria and oils from plants, released into the air by raindrops.
what is hoarfrost | rime ice | feathery frost => Hoarfrost is feathery ice crystals that grow directly from water vapor on cold surfaces. Rime is ice formed when supercooled fog droplets freeze on contact, building up on the windward side.
what is black ice | why is black ice dangerous | how to spot black ice => Black ice is a thin, clear layer of ice on roads that looks just like wet pavement, so drivers don't see it. It often forms on bridges and in shady spots first.
what are noctilucent clouds | night shining clouds | highest clouds => Noctilucent clouds are the highest clouds, about 80 km up in the mesosphere, made of ice on meteor dust. They glow electric blue after sunset in summer at high latitudes.
what are polar stratospheric clouds | nacreous clouds | mother of pearl clouds => Polar stratospheric clouds form very high over the poles in extreme cold, often shimmering with rainbow colors. They help destroy ozone in the ozone hole.
# tag: science.bio.ecology
what is a chaparral | mediterranean climate biome | shrubland biome => Chaparral is a biome of dense, tough shrubs in places with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters, like California. Many of its plants are adapted to regrow after fires.
what is a fen | bog vs fen => A bog gets water mainly from rain and is acidic and mossy. A fen is fed by groundwater and is less acidic. A marsh is a wetland dominated by grasses and reeds.
what is a chloroplast | chloroplast structure | how many chloroplasts in a leaf cell => A chloroplast is the organelle where photosynthesis happens. It's packed with stacks of membranes called thylakoids containing chlorophyll, and a leaf cell can have dozens of them.
