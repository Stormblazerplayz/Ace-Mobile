why does a balloon stick to the wall after rubbing it on your hair | balloon static on wall | why does a rubbed balloon stick => Rubbing moves electrons from your hair onto the balloon, giving it a negative charge. That charge pulls on the charges in the wall, which is enough to hold up a light balloon.
why does your hair stand up after taking off a hat | static hair | why is my hair staticky => Rubbing your hair gives each strand the same electric charge. Like charges push apart, so the strands spread away from each other. It's worse in dry winter air.
static shock from door handle | why do i get zapped in winter => Walking on carpet can build up static charge on your body. When you touch metal, the charge jumps across as a tiny spark. Dry winter air makes it worse because moist air lets charge leak away.
why does a spoon look bent in water | why do things look bent underwater => Light bends, or refracts, when it passes from water into air. Your brain assumes light travels in straight lines, so the part of the spoon underwater appears to be in a different place.
why does water look shallow | swimming pool depth illusion => Light from the bottom bends as it leaves the water, making the bottom look closer than it is. A pool looks only about three quarters as deep as it really is.
why do things look smaller far away | why do distant objects look small | perspective size => Light from a far object reaches your eye at a smaller angle, so its image on your retina is smaller. Your brain usually knows the real size, but it can be fooled.
how does drinking through a straw work | how do straws suck up liquid => When you suck, you lower the air pressure inside the straw. The higher air pressure pushing down on the drink outside then pushes liquid up the straw.
why does a bicycle stay up | how do bikes balance | why dont bikes fall over when moving => A moving bike stays up mostly because the rider and the front fork's design steer the wheel under the bike whenever it starts to lean. Spinning wheels help a little, but steering is the main reason.
why does spinning a top keep it upright | how do spinning tops work | why do tops fall when they slow down => A spinning top has angular momentum, which resists changes to the direction of its spin. As friction slows it, it wobbles more and finally falls.
why do you feel pushed back in a car | why do you lean when a car turns | why do i feel pulled sideways in a car => Your body tends to keep moving in a straight line because of inertia. When the car speeds up or turns, the seat has to push you to change your motion, so you feel pushed the opposite way.
how do seat belts work physics | why wear a seat belt => In a crash, the car stops suddenly but your body keeps moving. A seat belt stops you more gently and spreads the force across your strong hip and chest bones, instead of letting you hit the dashboard or windshield.
how do airbags protect you | airbag physics => An airbag makes you stop over a longer time and a larger area. Spreading out the stopping force means less force on any one part of your body.
why do helmets protect your head | how do bike helmets work | helmet foam => A helmet's foam crushes during a hit, making your head stop more slowly. That lowers the force on your skull and brain.
why do ice skates glide | how do ice skates work | why do skates have thin blades => A skate's thin blade glides on the slippery surface layer of ice. The narrow blade also bites into the ice when tipped, which lets skaters push and turn.
why do cars have treads on tires | why do tires have grooves | tire tread purpose => Tire grooves channel water out from under the tire, so the rubber can still grip the road. Bald tires can float on a film of water, called hydroplaning.
why do race car tires have no tread | what are slick tires | why are racing tires smooth => Smooth racing slicks put more rubber on the road for extra grip when it's dry. In rain, racers switch to grooved tires to clear the water.
why do balls bounce less when cold | cold tennis ball bounce | does temperature affect bounce => Cold makes rubber stiffer and less springy, and the air inside has lower pressure. Both mean a cold ball bounces lower.
how do paper airplanes fly | why do paper planes glide => A paper airplane glides by trading height for speed. Its wings make lift as air flows over them, and a good design has its weight balanced slightly toward the nose.
why do kites stay in the air | kite physics => Wind pushing against a tilted kite is deflected downward, which pushes the kite up. The string holds it at the right angle, and the tail keeps it steady.
why does helium float | what makes helium balloons rise => Helium is much less dense than air, so the air around a helium balloon pushes it up more than gravity pulls it down.
why do helium balloons shrink | why do balloons go flat after a few days | why do helium balloons sink after a day => Helium atoms are tiny and slowly leak through the rubber. Foil balloons hold helium for much longer, often weeks.
how do fish float | what is a swim bladder | why dont fish sink => Many fish have a swim bladder, a gas-filled sac they adjust to float at any depth. Sharks have no swim bladder, so they use an oily liver and keep swimming.
why do ships float but rocks sink | how can a steel ship float | why do boats float => A ship floats because its hollow shape pushes aside a lot of water. If the weight of that water equals the ship's weight, it floats, even though steel itself is denser than water.
ear popping on airplanes | why do ears pop in the mountains => As the air pressure around you changes, the air trapped behind your eardrum stays at the old pressure. Swallowing or yawning opens tubes to your throat, letting the pressure even out with a pop.
why do chip bags puff up on planes | why do bags swell at altitude | snack bag on a mountain => Air pressure is lower at high altitude, so the air sealed inside the bag pushes outward and puffs it up. Bring it back down and it shrinks again.
why does a can crush when cooled | why do soda cans implode => If you heat a little water in a can and flip it into cold water, the steam inside condenses. The pressure inside drops, and outside air pressure crushes the can instantly.
why does a metal spoon feel colder than a wooden one | why does metal feel cold | why do some things feel colder => Both are the same temperature, but metal conducts heat away from your hand much faster, so it feels colder. Wood is a poor conductor.
why do pans have plastic or wooden handles | why are pot handles not metal | heat insulating handles => Plastic and wood are insulators, so they don't carry the heat from the pan to your hand.
why do we wear dark clothes in winter | do black clothes make you hotter => Dark colors absorb more sunlight and turn it into heat, while white and light colors reflect it. That's why light clothing feels cooler in strong sun.
why do puffy jackets keep you warm | how do down jackets work | why does trapped air insulate => Puffy jackets trap lots of still air in their fluffy filling. Air is a poor conductor of heat, so it slows the loss of your body heat.
why do we feel cold when wet | why does wind make you colder => Evaporating water takes heat from your skin. Wind speeds up evaporation and blows away the warm layer of air next to your skin, so you feel colder.
how does sweat cool you | evaporative cooling => As sweat evaporates, the fastest molecules escape and take heat with them, cooling your skin. Sweat cools less in humid air because it evaporates more slowly.
why does blowing on hot food cool it | why do people blow on soup | how does blowing cool food => Blowing moves away the warm, damp air above the food and speeds up evaporation, which carries heat away faster.
why does your breath steam in cold weather | why can i see my breath | breath fog => Your breath is warm and full of water vapor. In cold air it cools quickly, and the vapor condenses into a tiny cloud of droplets.
do power lines get longer in heat | sagging wires => Metal wires expand when they're hot, so they get slightly longer and sag more in summer heat.
why does a jar lid open after hot water | how to open a stuck jar lid | hot water on jar lid => Hot water makes the metal lid expand more than the glass jar, loosening its grip so it twists off more easily.
why do glass cups crack in hot water | why does glass break with sudden heat | thermal shock => When one part of glass heats faster than another, it expands unevenly and the strain can crack it. That's called thermal shock. Heat-resistant glass expands much less.
how do microwaves cook food | why does food get hot in a microwave => Microwaves make water molecules in food rapidly twist back and forth, and this motion becomes heat. That's why dry dishes stay cool and food can heat unevenly.
why does metal spark in the microwave | can you put foil in a microwave | microwave sparks => Microwaves push electrons around in metal, and sharp edges or thin foil can build up enough charge to spark. Some microwave-safe metal is designed for it, but forks and crumpled foil can spark.
how do induction stoves work | why do induction hobs only heat pans | induction cooking => An induction stove uses a fast-changing magnetic field to make electric currents flow in an iron or steel pan, which heats the pan itself. The cooktop stays cooler, and pans that aren't magnetic won't heat.
how does a fridge keep food cold | how does a refrigerator work | fridge physics => A fridge pumps a special fluid around in pipes. It evaporates inside the fridge, soaking up heat, then is squeezed by a compressor and gives off the heat through coils at the back.
why does a guitar string make sound | guitar physics => Plucking a string makes it vibrate. The vibrations pass into the guitar's hollow body, which makes the surrounding air vibrate more strongly, so the sound is louder.
why do thicker strings sound lower | tighter string higher pitch => A string's pitch rises when it's shorter, tighter or thinner. That's why pressing a guitar string against a fret, which shortens it, makes a higher note.
how do drums make sound | why do drums sound different | drum physics => Hitting a drum makes its stretched skin vibrate, pushing the air back and forth. Tighter or smaller drumheads vibrate faster and sound higher.
why does a bottle whistle when you blow over it | blowing over a bottle | bottle note => Blowing across the top makes the air inside vibrate. Adding water leaves less air space, so the note gets higher.
is the ocean sound in a seashell real | seashell sound => The sound isn't the ocean. The shell's hollow shape makes some of the background noise around you louder, which sounds like waves.
why does sound echo in empty rooms | furniture and echoes => Hard, bare walls reflect sound back. Furniture, carpets and curtains absorb sound, so a furnished room echoes much less.
why is thunder long | why does thunder sound different near and far => Lightning heats air along its whole path, which can be many kilometers long. Sound from different parts reaches you at different times, making a long rumble. Close lightning makes a sharp crack.
why does a siren sound different when it passes | why does a car sound change as it passes => As a siren comes toward you, its sound waves bunch up, so the pitch is higher. As it moves away, they stretch out and the pitch drops. This is the Doppler effect.
why do some sounds hurt your ears | can loud noise damage hearing => Very loud sounds can damage the tiny hair cells in your inner ear, which don't grow back. Long exposure above about 85 decibels, like heavy traffic, can hurt hearing over time.
active noise cancellation | anti noise => Microphones on the headphones listen to outside noise and make an opposite sound wave. When the two waves meet, they cancel each other out, especially for steady low sounds like engines.
why is lightning before thunder | light vs sound speed => Light travels about 300,000 km per second, while sound travels about 343 meters per second. The flash reaches you almost instantly, but the thunder takes about 3 seconds per kilometer.
why does a magnifying glass make things bigger | how does a magnifying glass work | convex lens magnify => A magnifying glass is a convex lens that bends light inward. When you hold it close to an object, it makes a larger, upright image that your eye sees.
how do glasses help you see | glasses lenses => Glasses bend light so it focuses right on your retina. Nearsighted people need concave lenses, and farsighted people need convex lenses.
what causes red eye in pictures | red eye flash => A camera flash right next to the lens lights up the blood-rich back of the eye, which reflects red into the camera. Red eye is more likely in dim light, when pupils are wide.
why do bubbles have rainbow colors | bubble colors => Light reflects off both the inside and outside of a bubble's thin film. The two reflections interfere, strengthening some colors and canceling others, depending on how thick the film is.
why are bubbles round | why do bubbles form spheres => Surface tension pulls a bubble's film into the shape with the smallest surface for the air inside, which is a sphere.
why do bubbles pop | how long can a bubble last | what makes bubbles burst => A bubble pops when its film gets too thin, as water evaporates or drains down, or when something dry or dusty touches it. Adding glycerin or sugar makes bubbles last longer.
why does oil make rainbows on puddles | why do oily puddles look colorful => A thin layer of oil on water reflects light from its top and bottom surfaces. These reflections interfere and show different colors where the film is different thicknesses.
why do cds and dvds show rainbows | why does a cd make rainbows | cd rainbow => A CD's surface has a tight spiral of tiny tracks. They act like a diffraction grating, spreading white light into its colors.
why does a candle flame point up | why do flames go up | flame in space => Hot gases from the flame are less dense than the air around them, so they rise and stretch the flame upward. In space, with no up, a flame is a round, blue ball.
why do fireworks have different colors | firework chemicals colors => Firework colors come from metal compounds that glow when heated. Strontium makes red, barium green, copper blue, sodium yellow and orange, and calcium orange.
why does a bouncy ball bounce higher on a hard floor | carpet vs floor bounce | why do balls bounce less on grass => A hard floor barely squashes, so most of the energy goes back into the ball. Carpet and grass squash and absorb energy, so the ball bounces lower.
why does a slinky walk down stairs | how does a slinky work | slinky physics => A Slinky's coils flip over one by one, with gravity pulling each new loop down to the next step. Momentum carries the top coils over the top.
why does a swing go higher when you pump | how to swing higher | swing physics => Pumping your legs and leaning shifts your weight at the right moments in each swing. This adds energy, so each swing goes a little higher.
why does a yo yo come back up | how does a yo yo work | yo yo physics => As a yo-yo falls, its energy turns into spin. When it reaches the bottom, the spinning winds the string back up, carrying the yo-yo back to your hand.
why is it hard to open a door near the hinges | door torque => A push far from the hinge creates more turning force, called torque. Pushing near the hinge needs much more force to turn the door the same way.
why are wrenches long | how does a longer wrench help | lever and torque => A longer handle lets the same push create more turning force, or torque, so stuck bolts turn more easily.
