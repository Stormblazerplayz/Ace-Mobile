# tag: science.physics.energy
what are the types of energy | different forms of energy | list the kinds of energy | energy types => Main types of energy include kinetic (movement), thermal (heat), light, sound, electrical, chemical, nuclear, gravitational potential and elastic potential energy.
what is kinetic energy | kinetic energy definition => Kinetic energy is the energy something has because it's moving. The faster and heavier it is, the more kinetic energy it has.
how do you calculate kinetic energy | kinetic energy formula | ke equals half mv squared => Kinetic energy = ½ x mass x speed² (KE = ½mv²). Doubling the speed gives four times the kinetic energy.
what is potential energy | potential energy definition | what does potential energy mean => Potential energy is stored energy that something has because of its position or condition, like a book on a high shelf or a stretched spring.
what is gravitational potential energy | how do you calculate gpe | gpe formula => Gravitational potential energy is energy stored by lifting something up. GPE = mass x g x height, so lifting a 1 kg book 2 m stores about 20 joules.
what is elastic potential energy | energy stored in a spring | energy in a stretched rubber band => Elastic potential energy is energy stored when something is stretched, squashed or bent, like a pulled bow, a wound spring or a stretched rubber band.
what is chemical energy | where is chemical energy stored | examples of chemical energy => Chemical energy is stored in the bonds between atoms. Food, fuel, batteries and fireworks all hold chemical energy that's released in reactions.
what is thermal energy | thermal energy definition | what is heat energy => Thermal energy is the energy of the jiggling particles inside a substance. The faster they move, the hotter it is.
what is nuclear energy | where does nuclear energy come from | what is atomic energy => Nuclear energy is stored in the nucleus of atoms. It's released by fission (splitting big atoms) in power plants or fusion (joining small atoms) in the Sun.
what is electrical energy | electrical energy definition | what carries electrical energy => Electrical energy is carried by moving electric charges, usually electrons in a wire. It powers lights, motors and your devices.
what is radiant energy | what is light energy | is light a form of energy => Radiant energy is energy carried by light and other electromagnetic waves, like sunlight, radio waves and X-rays.
what is sound energy | is sound a form of energy | how does sound carry energy => Sound energy is carried by vibrations moving through air, water or solids. It's usually a tiny amount of energy compared to other forms.
what is the law of conservation of energy | can energy be created or destroyed | energy is never lost => Energy can't be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another. The total amount of energy always stays the same.
what is an energy transfer | what is an energy transformation | examples of energy changes => An energy transformation is energy changing form. A light bulb turns electrical energy into light and heat, and a car engine turns chemical energy into kinetic energy and heat.
what energy changes happen in a battery | battery energy transfer | how does a battery store energy => A battery stores chemical energy. When it's connected in a circuit, a chemical reaction turns that into electrical energy.
what energy changes happen when you drop a ball | ball falling energy | energy of a falling object => As a ball falls, gravitational potential energy turns into kinetic energy. When it hits the ground, some turns into sound and heat.
what energy changes happen on a roller coaster | roller coaster energy | why is the first hill the tallest => A roller coaster gets lots of potential energy at the top of the first hill, then swaps it back and forth with kinetic energy. Friction slowly uses it up, so later hills must be lower.
what is wasted energy | what is useful energy | where does wasted energy go => Wasted energy is energy that doesn't do the job you want. It usually spreads out as heat into the surroundings, like the warmth from a laptop charger.
what is a calorie | how many joules in a calorie => A calorie is the energy to heat 1 gram of water by 1 °C, about 4.2 joules. Food labels show kilocalories (1,000 calories), often just called Calories.
how do you calculate the cost of electricity | how much does it cost to run something | electricity cost formula => Multiply the power in kilowatts by the hours used to get kWh, then multiply by the price per kWh. A 2 kW heater for 3 hours uses 6 kWh.
what is heat | is heat the same as temperature | heat vs temperature | difference between heat and temperature => Temperature measures how hot something is, the average energy of its particles. Heat is energy flowing from a hotter thing to a colder one. A bathtub of warm water holds more heat than a cup of boiling water.
what is temperature | how is temperature measured => Temperature measures how fast the particles in something are moving on average. It's measured with a thermometer in degrees Celsius, Fahrenheit, or kelvin.
what is the kelvin scale | what is kelvin => The kelvin scale starts at absolute zero. To convert, add 273.15 to the Celsius temperature, so water freezes at 273.15 K and boils at 373.15 K.
how do liquid thermometers work | why does the liquid in a thermometer rise => In a liquid thermometer, the liquid expands when it warms up and rises in the thin tube. Digital thermometers use a sensor whose electrical resistance changes with temperature.
who made the celsius scale | who made the fahrenheit scale => Galileo made an early thermoscope around 1593. Daniel Fahrenheit made the mercury thermometer and his scale in the 1700s, and Anders Celsius made his scale in 1742.
what are the three ways heat moves | types of heat transfer | conduction convection radiation => Heat moves by conduction (through touching), convection (by flowing liquids or gases) and radiation (by infrared waves, even through space).
what is conduction | how does conduction work | conduction examples => Conduction is heat moving through a material as particles bump into each other. A metal spoon in hot soup gets hot by conduction.
what is convection | how does convection work | what is a convection current => Convection is heat carried by moving liquids or gases. Hot fluid rises because it's less dense, cool fluid sinks, and this makes a loop called a convection current.
what is thermal radiation | how does heat travel through space | how does the sun heat the earth => Thermal radiation is heat carried by infrared waves. It doesn't need any material, which is how the Sun's heat crosses empty space to reach Earth.
what is a thermal conductor | what materials conduct heat well | best heat conductors => Thermal conductors let heat flow through easily. Metals like copper, aluminium and silver are excellent conductors, and diamond is even better.
what is a thermal insulator | what materials are good insulators | best heat insulators => Insulators slow down heat flow. Air, wool, foam, plastic, wood and fiberglass are good insulators, which is why trapped air keeps you warm.
why does metal feel colder than wood | why does a metal bench feel cold | why does tile feel colder than carpet => Metal and wood in the same room are the same temperature. Metal feels colder because it conducts heat away from your hand much faster.
how does a thermos work | how does a vacuum flask keep things hot | thermos flask physics => A thermos has a vacuum between two walls, which stops conduction and convection. Shiny walls reflect heat radiation, so drinks stay hot or cold for hours.
how does home insulation work | how do houses keep heat in | why do houses need insulation => Insulation in walls, lofts and double glazed windows traps air or uses materials that slow heat flow, so less heat escapes in winter and less gets in during summer.
why do dark clothes feel hotter in the sun | do black things absorb more heat | why do white clothes keep you cool => Dark colors absorb more light and turn it into heat, while white and light colors reflect more. That's why a black car gets hotter in the sun.
what is thermal expansion | why do things expand when heated | why do things get bigger when hot => Most materials expand when heated because their particles move faster and spread apart. They shrink again when they cool.
why do bridges have gaps | what are expansion joints | why do train tracks have gaps => Bridges and some rail tracks have small gaps so the metal and concrete can expand on hot days without buckling.
why does water expand when it freezes | why do pipes burst in winter | water expands when frozen => Water expands by about 9% when it freezes, because its molecules lock into an open crystal shape. That push can burst pipes and crack rocks.
what is specific heat capacity | what does specific heat mean | why does water take long to heat up => Specific heat capacity is the energy needed to heat 1 kg of something by 1 °C. Water's is very high, about 4,200 J/kg°C, so it heats and cools slowly.
why is the sea warmer than the land at night | why does the ocean stay warm | water heat capacity weather => Water stores lots of heat and releases it slowly. That keeps coastal places milder, with cooler summers and warmer winters than places far inland.
what is latent heat | why doesnt temperature change when ice melts | hidden heat => Latent heat is energy used to change state, like melting ice or boiling water, without changing the temperature. The energy breaks the bonds between particles instead.
what is melting | what happens when something melts => Melting is a solid turning into a liquid when it's heated. The particles get enough energy to break free from their fixed places.
what is freezing | freezing definition | what happens when water freezes => Freezing is a liquid turning into a solid as it cools. The particles slow down and lock into fixed positions.
what is boiling | boiling vs evaporating | difference between boiling and evaporation => Boiling happens all through a liquid at its boiling point, making bubbles. Evaporation happens only at the surface and can happen at any temperature.
what is sublimation | what substances sublime | what is it called when a solid turns into a gas => Sublimation is a solid turning straight into a gas without melting. Dry ice (solid carbon dioxide) does this, and so does snow on cold, dry, sunny days.
what is deposition in science | gas to solid => Deposition is a gas turning straight into a solid. Frost forms this way when water vapor freezes directly onto cold surfaces.
what are the changes of state | list the state changes | names of phase changes => The changes of state are melting, freezing, evaporating (or boiling), condensing, sublimation and deposition.
what is dry ice | why does dry ice smoke | how cold is dry ice => Dry ice is frozen carbon dioxide at about -78.5 °C. It turns straight into gas, and the cold gas makes water vapor in the air condense into a foggy cloud.
boiling point at high altitude | why is it hard to cook pasta on a mountain => Lower air pressure on mountains lets water boil at a lower temperature. On top of Mount Everest it boils at about 70 °C, so food cooks slowly.
why does a pressure cooker cook faster | pressure cooker physics => A pressure cooker traps steam, raising the pressure inside. Higher pressure raises the boiling point to about 120 °C, so food cooks faster.
why does sweating cool you down | how does evaporation cool things | why do you feel cold getting out of a pool => Evaporation takes heat energy with it. When sweat or pool water evaporates from your skin, it carries heat away and cools you.
how does a fridge work | how do refrigerators keep food cold | refrigerator physics => A fridge pumps a special fluid around pipes. It evaporates inside, soaking up heat, then a compressor squeezes it so it releases that heat out the back.
how does an air conditioner work | air conditioning physics | how does ac cool a room => An air conditioner works like a fridge for a room. It soaks up heat indoors by evaporating a refrigerant, then dumps the heat outside.
what is a heat pump | how does a heat pump work | heat pump vs furnace => A heat pump moves heat instead of making it, like a fridge running in reverse. It can pull heat from cold outdoor air into a house and uses much less energy than a heater.
what is thermodynamics | what are the laws of thermodynamics | thermodynamics basics => Thermodynamics is the science of heat and energy. Its laws say energy is conserved, heat flows from hot to cold, disorder tends to increase, and nothing can reach absolute zero.
does heat flow from hot to cold | why does heat move from hot to cold | does cold move into things => Heat always flows from hotter things to colder ones by itself. Cold isn't a thing that moves in. Your hand feels cold because heat is leaving it.
what is entropy | entropy explained simply | why do things get messier => Entropy is a measure of how spread out energy is, often described as disorder. In a closed system, entropy tends to increase, like a hot drink cooling to room temperature.
examples of fossil fuels | what are fossil fuels made of => Fossil fuels are coal, oil and natural gas. They formed over millions of years from buried remains of ancient plants and sea creatures.
why are fossil fuels bad | problems with fossil fuels | fossil fuel pollution => Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide, which warms the climate, plus pollution that harms health. They're also nonrenewable, so they'll eventually run out.
what are non renewable resources | nonrenewable energy examples | what does non renewable mean => Nonrenewable energy sources can't be replaced in a human lifetime. Coal, oil, natural gas and uranium are examples.
how does solar power work | how do solar panels make electricity | what is a solar cell => Solar panels are made of solar cells, usually silicon. When sunlight hits them, it knocks electrons loose and makes an electric current.
how does wind power work | what is a wind turbine => Wind spins a turbine's blades, which turn a generator inside. The generator uses magnets and coils of wire to make electricity.
how does hydroelectric power work | what is hydropower => Water stored behind a dam flows down through turbines, spinning them. The turbines turn generators that make electricity.
what is tidal energy | how does tidal power work | wave and tidal energy => Tidal power uses the rise and fall of tides to spin turbines. Tides are very predictable, but few places are good for tidal power plants.
what is biomass energy | what is biofuel | examples of biomass => Biomass is energy from plants and other living things, like burning wood or making fuel from corn, sugar cane or waste. The plants can be regrown.
how does a nuclear power plant work | how do nuclear reactors make electricity | nuclear power explained => Uranium atoms split in the reactor (fission), releasing heat. The heat boils water into steam, which spins turbines connected to generators.
is nuclear power safe | pros and cons of nuclear power | is nuclear energy good => Nuclear power makes lots of electricity with almost no carbon emissions, and it has a strong safety record. But it creates radioactive waste, and accidents like Chernobyl and Fukushima were serious.
how do power plants make electricity | how is electricity generated | how does a generator make electricity => Most power plants spin a turbine connected to a generator. Steam, wind or water spins the turbine, and the generator turns that motion into electricity using magnets and wire coils.
what is the difference between energy and power | energy vs power | is power the same as energy => Energy is the total amount of work that can be done, measured in joules. Power is how fast energy is used, measured in watts. A strong motor has more power, not necessarily more energy.
where does all energy on earth come from | is the sun the source of all energy | what is the original source of energy => Almost all energy on Earth's surface comes from the Sun, including food, wind, waves and fossil fuels. Exceptions include geothermal heat, tidal energy and nuclear fuel.
what is an energy flow diagram | what is a sankey diagram | how do you draw energy transfers => A Sankey diagram shows energy transfers with arrows whose width matches the amount of energy. It makes it easy to see how much is useful and how much is wasted.
what is energy efficiency | ways to save energy => Saving energy means using less to do the same job. LED bulbs, turning things off, good insulation and efficient appliances all help.
why are led bulbs better than old bulbs | why do old light bulbs get hot => Old filament bulbs turn about 90% of their energy into heat. LEDs make the same light with a fraction of the electricity and last much longer.
what is a calorimeter | how do you measure energy in food | burning food experiment => A calorimeter measures heat released by a reaction. In school, you can burn a food sample under water and see how much the water heats up.
what happens to particles when you heat them | particle model of heating | how does heat affect molecules => When you heat a substance, its particles gain energy and move faster. In solids they vibrate more, and in liquids and gases they move around faster.
what is the particle model | kinetic theory of matter | what is the kinetic particle theory => The particle model says all matter is made of tiny particles that are always moving. In solids they vibrate in place, in liquids they slide past each other, and in gases they zoom around freely.
why are gases easy to squash | why can you compress a gas | why can't you compress a liquid => Gas particles are far apart with lots of empty space, so you can squash them closer. In liquids and solids the particles are already touching.
what is plasma state of matter | what is plasma | examples of plasma => Plasma is a fourth state of matter where a gas is so hot that electrons are stripped from atoms. Stars, lightning and neon signs contain plasma.
what is a bose einstein condensate | is there a fifth state of matter | bec state of matter => A Bose-Einstein condensate is a strange state made by cooling atoms to almost absolute zero, where they act like one big super atom. It was first made in 1995.
why does diffusion happen faster when hot | does temperature affect diffusion | diffusion and temperature => Hotter particles move faster, so they spread out more quickly. Food coloring spreads faster in hot water than cold water.
what is brownian motion | why do tiny particles jiggle | who discovered brownian motion => Brownian motion is the random jiggling of tiny specks, like pollen in water, as invisible molecules hit them. Robert Brown saw it in 1827, and Einstein explained it in 1905.
what is gas pressure | why does a gas make pressure | what causes pressure in a balloon => Gas pressure comes from gas particles hitting the walls of their container. More particles, or faster ones, mean more hits and more pressure.
why do tires go flat in the cold | why do balloons shrink in the cold | gas and temperature => Cooling a gas slows its particles, so they hit the walls less hard. The pressure drops, so tires go softer and balloons shrink.
what is boyles law | pressure and volume of a gas | boyles law example => Boyle's law says that if a gas stays the same temperature, squeezing it to half the volume doubles its pressure.
what is charles law | gas volume and temperature | charles law example => Charles's law says a gas expands when heated and shrinks when cooled, if its pressure stays the same. Its volume is proportional to its temperature in kelvin.
why does a can of soda explode in a hot car | why do aerosol cans say do not heat | heated cans explode => Heating a sealed can makes the gas inside push harder. If the pressure gets too high, the can can burst or explode.
how does a steam engine work | steam engine history => A steam engine boils water and uses the steam's pressure to push a piston. Thomas Newcomen built a working one in 1712, and James Watt greatly improved it in the 1760s and 1770s.
how does a car engine work | what is an internal combustion engine | how do petrol engines work => In a car engine, fuel and air are squeezed in a cylinder and set alight by a spark. The explosion pushes a piston down, which turns a crankshaft and the wheels.
how does an electric car move | electric car vs gas car => Electric cars store energy in big batteries. The battery powers electric motors that turn the wheels, and they can recover energy when braking.
what is regenerative braking | how do electric cars recharge when braking | regen braking => Regenerative braking uses the motor as a generator when slowing down. It turns kinetic energy back into electricity to recharge the battery instead of wasting it as heat.
how does a hydrogen fuel cell work | what is a fuel cell | fuel cell car => A fuel cell combines hydrogen with oxygen from the air to make electricity. The only waste is water.
what is energy storage | how do we store electricity | grid batteries => Electricity can be stored in big batteries, by pumping water uphill to run back through turbines later, or as heat or hydrogen. Storage helps when the sun and wind aren't available.
why do we need to save energy | why should we conserve energy | benefits of saving energy => Saving energy cuts electricity bills, reduces pollution and greenhouse gases, and makes resources like fossil fuels last longer.
how much energy does the sun give earth | solar energy reaching earth | how powerful is sunlight => About 1,360 watts of sunlight hits each square meter at the top of Earth's atmosphere. In about an hour and a half, Earth receives as much solar energy as all humans use in a year.
what is infrared | is infrared heat | can you see infrared => Infrared is light just past red that our eyes can't see. Warm things give it off, which is how thermal cameras and TV remotes work.
what is a thermal camera | how do thermal cameras work | heat vision cameras => A thermal camera detects infrared light given off by warm objects and shows it as colors, so warm bodies glow even in the dark.
why does a cold drink sweat | why does a glass of ice water get wet outside | condensation on a cold can => Water vapor in the air cools down when it touches the cold glass and condenses into droplets. The water comes from the air, not from inside the glass.
why does breath make fog in winter | why can you see your breath when its cold | breath clouds => Your breath is warm and full of water vapor. In cold air it cools quickly and condenses into tiny droplets, making a little cloud.
how does a microwave oven work | microwave physics => A microwave oven makes microwaves that make water molecules in food spin back and forth very fast. That motion heats the food from the inside and outside.
why does a fan cool you down | does a fan make the air colder | how do fans work => A fan doesn't cool the air. It moves air over your skin, which helps sweat evaporate and carries body heat away faster.
how does a candle burn | what burns in a candle | candle flame science => The flame melts the wax, which climbs the wick and turns into vapor. The wax vapor burns with oxygen, giving off heat, light, carbon dioxide and water.
what is the hottest part of a flame | why are flames different colors => A blue flame is hotter and burns fuel completely with plenty of oxygen. Yellow flames have glowing soot from incomplete burning and are cooler.
