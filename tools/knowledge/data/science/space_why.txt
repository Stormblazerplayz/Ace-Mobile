# tag: science.space.questions
why do stars twinkle but planets dont | how to tell a planet from a star => Stars are so far away that they're tiny points, so moving air bends their light around and makes them twinkle. Planets look like tiny disks, so the flickering averages out and they shine steadily.
why cant we see stars in the daytime | where do stars go during the day | are stars out in the day => The stars are still there in the daytime, but sunlight scattered by the air makes the sky so bright that their faint light gets lost.
why do stars move across the sky | why do stars rise and set | do stars move at night => Stars seem to move across the sky because Earth spins. Their positions relative to each other barely change over a human lifetime.
why does the north star not move | why does polaris stay still | why is polaris always north => Polaris sits almost exactly above Earth's North Pole, along the line Earth spins around. As Earth turns, Polaris stays put while other stars circle around it.
why is the sun yellow | is the sun white => The Sun is really white, giving off all colors. It looks yellow from the ground because air scatters some blue light away. From space, astronauts see it as white.
why does the sun look bigger at sunset | why is the moon bigger near the horizon => The Sun and Moon aren't really bigger near the horizon. It's an illusion: your brain compares them to trees and buildings and judges them as larger. A photo shows they're the same size.
why does the moon follow you | why does the moon move with the car | moon following me => The Moon is so far away that moving a few kilometers barely changes your angle to it, so it seems to stay in the same spot while nearby things rush past.
why can we see the moon in the day | moon during the day | why is the moon out in the morning => The Moon is up in the daytime for part of most days, because it isn't always opposite the Sun. It's bright enough to see against the blue sky.
why does earth have fewer craters than the moon | where are earths craters | why doesnt earth have many craters => Earth has been hit just as much, but wind, water, life and plate tectonics have erased most craters. Our air also burns up small meteoroids. Around 200 impact craters are confirmed on Earth.
why is the moon gray | what color is the moon | why is the moon white => The Moon's surface is mostly gray rock and dust. It looks bright white or yellowish in our sky because it's lit by strong sunlight against a dark background.
why does the moon look orange sometimes | orange moon | why is the moon red near the horizon => When the Moon is low, its light travels through more air, which scatters blue light away and leaves orange and red. Smoke or dust can make it even redder.
why are there footprints on the moon still | do moon footprints last | how long will moon footprints last => The Moon has no wind or rain, so the astronauts' footprints should last for millions of years. Tiny meteorite impacts will slowly wear them away.
why did the flag on the moon look like it was waving | moon flag moving | apollo flag wave => The flags had a rod along the top to hold them out, since there's no air. They looked wavy because they were crumpled from packing, and they only moved while astronauts twisted the poles.
why is space black if there are so many stars | why is the night sky dark => The universe isn't infinitely old, so light from very distant stars hasn't reached us yet, and the expansion of space stretches old light out of view. That leaves the night sky dark between the stars.
why dont astronauts fall off the space station | how does the iss stay up | why doesnt the space station fall => The ISS is falling all the time, but it moves sideways at about 7.7 km per second, so it keeps missing Earth as the ground curves away below it.
why do satellites eventually fall | how long do satellites last | satellite reentry => Even very thin air at orbit height drags on satellites and slows them, so lower ones slowly drop and burn up. The ISS needs regular boosts to stay up.
best place to launch a rocket | why launch east => Earth spins fastest at the equator, about 1,670 km/h, so rockets launched eastward from near the equator get a free speed boost and save fuel.
why do spacecraft get so hot coming back | why do capsules glow on reentry => Returning spacecraft hit the air at very high speed and squash it in front of them, heating it to thousands of degrees. Heat shields protect the crew by slowly burning away or insulating.
do you grow in space | spine in space => Without gravity squeezing their spine, astronauts can get up to about 3% taller. They shrink back after returning to Earth.
why do astronauts lose bone in space | bone loss in space | how do astronauts stay strong => In microgravity, bones and muscles don't have to hold the body up, so they weaken. Astronauts exercise about 2 hours a day on the ISS to fight this.
why do astronauts faces look puffy in space | puffy face space | fluid shift in space => Without gravity pulling fluids down, more blood and fluid move up into the head and chest. Astronauts get puffy faces and skinny legs, sometimes called puffy face bird legs.
why do astronauts wear diapers | how do astronauts go to the bathroom => On the ISS, a space toilet uses airflow to pull waste away, since nothing falls. During launch, landing and spacewalks, astronauts wear absorbent garments because they can't get to a toilet.
why cant you eat bread in space | what food do astronauts eat | why do astronauts eat tortillas => Bread makes crumbs that float away and can get into equipment or eyes, so astronauts often use tortillas instead. Much of their food is packaged and rehydrated.
what makes mars red | rusty planet => Mars is red because its dust and rocks are full of iron oxide, the same chemical as rust.
why is the sky on mars different | what color is the sky on mars | blue sunset on mars => On Mars, fine dust makes the daytime sky look butterscotch or pinkish. At sunset, the dust scatters blue light forward, so the sky around the Sun turns blue, the opposite of Earth.
why does venus spin backwards | does venus spin the wrong way | venus retrograde rotation => Venus spins the opposite way to most planets, very slowly. Scientists think a giant impact or the pull of the Sun on its thick atmosphere may have flipped or slowed it, but nobody knows for sure.
why is uranus tilted on its side | uranus sideways | uranus tilt reason => Uranus is tipped about 98 degrees, so it rolls around the Sun on its side. Scientists think a huge collision long ago, or the pull of a lost moon, knocked it over.
why is jupiter striped | why does jupiter have bands | jupiter stripes => Jupiter's stripes are bands of clouds blown by strong east and west jet streams. Light zones are rising air with high clouds, and dark belts are sinking air where deeper clouds show.
why is neptune so windy | fastest winds in the solar system | neptune wind speed => Neptune has the fastest winds measured in the solar system, about 2,000 km/h. Scientists think its interior heat and the thin friction in its atmosphere let winds build up.
why do comets come back | how often do comets return | periodic comets => Many comets travel on long oval orbits around the Sun, so they return regularly. Halley's Comet comes back about every 76 years, and some comets take thousands of years.
why is the sun round | why are stars spheres => Big objects are pulled into balls by their own gravity, which pulls equally toward the center from all sides. Small asteroids and moons don't have enough gravity, so they can stay lumpy.
why is february 29 a thing | leap year reason => A year is about 365.24 days, not exactly 365. Adding a leap day almost every 4 years keeps the calendar lined up with the seasons. Century years skip it unless divisible by 400.
why does the moon look the same every night | tidal locking => The Moon spins once for each orbit around Earth, so the same side always faces us. This is called tidal locking, and many moons in the solar system are tidally locked.
why is a total solar eclipse rare | how often are total eclipses => The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5 degrees to Earth's, so it usually passes above or below the Sun. A total eclipse happens somewhere on Earth about every 18 months, but any one place waits about 375 years on average.
why do we only see eclipses from some places | eclipse path of totality | why is totality narrow => The Moon's dark shadow is small, usually less than about 270 km wide on Earth, so a total eclipse is only seen in a narrow path. Outside it, you see a partial eclipse.
why is it dangerous to look at the sun | can looking at the sun blind you | solar retinopathy => Sunlight is so intense that staring at the Sun can burn your retina in seconds, and it doesn't hurt while it happens. Only use certified eclipse glasses or a pinhole projector.
how do we know how far away stars are | how do astronomers measure star distance => For nearby stars, astronomers use parallax: they watch how a star seems to shift as Earth moves around the Sun. For farther ones, they use the brightness of special stars and supernovas.
spectroscopy stars | how can we tell what a star contains => Astronomers split starlight into a spectrum. Each element absorbs or gives off light at its own colors, like a barcode, which reveals what the star is made of.
why do we send robots instead of people to mars | why not send humans to mars | robots vs astronauts => Robots don't need air, food or water, can last for years, and can't be harmed by radiation the way people can. They're much cheaper and safer, though people could explore faster.
why do telescopes go into space | why is hubble in space | space telescopes advantages => Earth's air blurs starlight and blocks some kinds of light, like most infrared, ultraviolet and X-rays. Space telescopes get sharp views of all these.
why build telescopes on mountains | best place for telescopes => High mountains are above much of the air, water vapor and clouds, and far from city lights. Places like Mauna Kea in Hawaii and the Atacama Desert in Chile are favorites.
why is it hard to see stars in the city | light pollution effects => Streetlights and buildings light up the sky, washing out faint stars. In a big city you might see a few dozen stars, while a dark site shows thousands.
why does the milky way look like a band | why is the milky way a stripe | how can we see our own galaxy => Our galaxy is a flat disk, and we're inside it. When you look along the disk, you see the light of billions of distant stars as a glowing band across the sky.
