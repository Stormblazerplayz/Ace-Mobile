# tag: science.earth.weather
why is it hotter at the equator | why are the poles cold | why is the equator warm => At the equator, sunlight hits the ground nearly straight on, concentrating its energy. Near the poles, it arrives at a low slant and spreads over a larger area, so it heats less.
why is summer hotter than winter | why do we have seasons and not distance => Seasons come from Earth's tilt, not its distance from the Sun. In summer, your hemisphere tilts toward the Sun, so sunlight is more direct and days are longer. Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January.
why are days longer in summer | why are days shorter in winter | day length seasons => Because Earth is tilted, your hemisphere spends more of each spin in sunlight when it tilts toward the Sun in summer, and less in winter.
why is it windy near the sea | sea breeze and land breeze => On sunny days, land heats up faster than the sea. Warm air rises over the land and cooler sea air blows in to replace it, making a sea breeze. At night it often reverses.
why is the desert cold at night | why do deserts get so cold | desert temperature at night => Desert air is very dry and there are few clouds. Without water vapor and clouds to trap heat, the ground's heat escapes into space quickly at night.
why are cloudy nights warmer | do clouds keep heat in | clear nights cold => Clouds absorb heat rising from the ground and send some back down, like a blanket. On clear nights, more heat escapes to space, so it gets colder and frost is more likely.
why do clouds float | how do clouds stay up => Cloud droplets are so tiny they fall extremely slowly, and rising air keeps them aloft. Even so, a fluffy cumulus cloud can hold water weighing around 500 tonnes, spread over a huge volume.
why are raindrops not tear shaped | real shape of raindrops => Small raindrops are round. Bigger ones get flattened on the bottom by air rushing past, looking more like a hamburger bun. They're never tear-shaped.
why does it smell before it rains | can you smell rain coming | ozone before storms => Before a storm, downdrafts can carry ozone made by lightning, which has a sharp, clean smell. After rain starts, the earthy smell comes from soil chemicals and plant oils.
why do some snowflakes look different | why are no two snowflakes alike | snowflake shapes => Each snowflake takes its own path through the cloud, meeting slightly different temperature and moisture along the way. Since its arms grow according to those conditions, no two complex snowflakes are likely to be exactly the same.
why does snow make things quiet | why is it quiet after snow | snow absorbs sound => Fresh, fluffy snow is full of air pockets that absorb sound waves, making the world sound muffled. Once snow gets packed or icy, it reflects sound instead.
why is snow white | why is snow white if ice is clear | color of snow => Snow is made of ice crystals with many surfaces that scatter all colors of light equally, so it looks white. Very deep snow and glacier ice can look blue.
how can there be ice in hot weather | hail in warm weather => Hail forms inside tall thunderstorm clouds, where strong updrafts carry raindrops high up where it's well below freezing. Summer heat makes these powerful storms.
why does lightning zigzag | why is lightning jagged | lightning shape => Lightning moves in short steps of about 50 meters, each time breaking through the air where it's easiest. That makes a branching, zigzag path.
why does lightning hit tall things | what does lightning strike | does lightning hit the highest point => Lightning often hits tall, pointed objects because they're the closest path for the electric charge to reach the ground. That's why lightning rods are on top of buildings.
how does a lightning rod work | lightning conductor => A lightning rod gives lightning an easy path, a thick metal wire, to carry the electricity safely into the ground instead of through the building.
is a car safe in lightning | why are you safe in a car during lightning | lightning and cars => A hard-topped car is fairly safe, because its metal body carries the electricity around you and into the ground. Rubber tires aren't the reason.
why do tornadoes happen in the us | why does america have so many tornadoes => The US gets the most tornadoes because warm, moist air from the Gulf of Mexico meets cold, dry air from Canada and the Rockies over flat plains, a perfect setup for strong thunderstorms.
why do hurricanes spin | hurricane rotation => Hurricanes spin because of the Coriolis effect from Earth's rotation. They spin counterclockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
why dont hurricanes form at the equator | no hurricanes on the equator => Hurricanes need the Coriolis effect to start spinning, and it's almost zero at the equator. They usually form between about 5 and 20 degrees from the equator, over warm ocean.
why do hurricanes weaken over land | what makes a hurricane die | hurricane landfall => Hurricanes are powered by warm ocean water. Over land, they lose that energy supply and friction slows their winds.
why does fog disappear in the morning | fog burning off | when does fog lift => As the Sun warms the ground and air, the tiny fog droplets evaporate. People say the fog burns off.
why is there dew on the grass | morning dew => On clear, calm nights, grass cools by giving off heat. When it gets colder than the dew point, water vapor in the air condenses onto it as dew.
why is the sky orange during wildfires | why does smoke make the sun red | red sun from smoke => Smoke particles scatter away blue light, letting mostly red and orange light through. That's why skies turn orange and the Sun looks red during big fires.
why is the air hazy in summer | summer haze | what causes haze => Haze comes from tiny particles of dust, smoke, pollen and pollution, plus water that clings to them in humid air. They scatter light, making distant things look faded.
why does weather forecasting get things wrong | why cant they predict weather perfectly => The atmosphere is chaotic, so tiny errors in measurements grow over time. As of 2025, forecasts are quite reliable about 5 days ahead and much less reliable beyond about 10 days.
how do meteorologists predict the weather | how are forecasts made | weather prediction computers => Forecasters gather measurements from weather stations, balloons, satellites, radar and planes, then use supercomputers to calculate how the atmosphere will change.
what does chance of rain mean | 40 percent chance of rain | probability of precipitation => A 40% chance of rain means that, in similar conditions, rain falls at a given spot in the forecast area about 4 times out of 10.
# tag: science.earth.geology
why are there mountains | where do mountains come from => Most big mountain ranges formed when tectonic plates pushed together and crumpled the crust upward over millions of years. Others are volcanoes or blocks of crust lifted along faults.
why is the himalaya still growing | is mount everest getting taller | himalayas rising => The Indian plate is still pushing into Asia by a few centimeters a year, which keeps lifting the Himalayas a few millimeters a year, though erosion and earthquakes work against it.
why are there earthquakes | why does the ground shake => Most earthquakes happen when rocks along a fault get stuck as tectonic plates move. Stress builds up until the rocks suddenly slip, sending shaking waves through the ground.
why cant we predict earthquakes | can scientists predict earthquakes | earthquake warnings => Scientists can say which areas are likely to have big earthquakes, but not exactly when. Some places have early warning systems that give seconds of notice after a quake starts.
what makes a volcano erupt | why does lava come out => Magma is less dense than the rock around it, so it rises. Gases dissolved in it, like water vapor and carbon dioxide, form bubbles as it nears the surface, and the pressure can blast it out.
why are some volcanoes explosive | why do some volcanoes ooze lava | explosive vs gentle eruptions => Thick, sticky magma rich in silica traps gas, so pressure builds until it explodes, like Mount St. Helens. Runny basalt magma lets gas escape easily, so lava pours out gently, like in Hawaii.
why is volcanic soil fertile | why do people live near volcanoes | volcanic ash farming => Volcanic ash and rock break down into soil rich in minerals like potassium and phosphorus, so crops grow well. Many people live near volcanoes for this fertile farmland.
why is lava red | lava colors => Lava glows because it's very hot, usually about 700 to 1,200 °C. The hottest lava glows yellow-orange, and cooler lava glows deep red before turning dark as it hardens.
why is the inside of the earth hot | where does earth's heat come from => Earth's inside is hot from heat left over from when the planet formed and from radioactive elements like uranium, thorium and potassium slowly decaying.
why is earths inner core solid if its hot | inner core pressure => The inner core is about as hot as the Sun's surface, but the pressure there is so enormous that iron and nickel are squeezed into a solid.
why does the earth have a magnetic field | where does earths magnetism come from | geodynamo => Earth's magnetic field is made by molten iron swirling in the outer core. This moving, electrically conducting metal works like a giant generator, called the geodynamo.
why do rocks have layers | how do rock layers form | sedimentary layers => Layers form as sediment like sand and mud settles in water or blows in, bit by bit, over long periods. Each layer is pressed and cemented into rock, with the oldest normally at the bottom.
why are fossils found in rocks | why are fossils mostly in sedimentary rock | how do fossils form in rock => Fossils form when living things are quickly buried in sediment, which later hardens into rock. Heat and pressure in other rocks usually destroy fossils, so most are found in sedimentary rock.
why are there seashells on mountains | fossils on mountains | sea fossils on everest => Rock that formed on ancient seafloors was later pushed up by tectonic plates. The top of Mount Everest is limestone with fossils of sea creatures.
why is sand different colors | what is sand made of | why are some beaches black => Sand is made of broken bits of rock, minerals and shells. Most is pale quartz, but black sand comes from volcanic rock, pink from shells or coral, and green from olivine.
why are some beaches white | what makes white sand | white sand beaches => Many white sand beaches are made of broken coral and shells, and some are nearly pure quartz. Parrotfish make some tropical sand by crunching coral and pooping out fine grains.
why is the soil different colors | what color is soil | red soil => Soil color comes from what it contains. Dark soil is rich in organic matter, red and yellow soils contain iron oxides, and gray soils are often waterlogged.
how are caves made | limestone caves => Most large caves form when slightly acidic rainwater seeps into limestone and slowly dissolves it over hundreds of thousands of years.
why do rivers meander | how do meanders form => A river erodes faster on the outside of a bend, where water flows fastest, and drops sediment on the inside, where it's slow. Over time the bends grow bigger and loop across the valley.
why do rivers flow to the sea | where do rivers go | why do rivers flow downhill => Gravity pulls water downhill, so rivers flow from high ground toward lower areas, usually ending in a sea, ocean or lake.
why are rivers fresh and the sea salty | why is river water not salty | salt in rivers => Rivers carry small amounts of salt into the sea all the time. When seawater evaporates, the salt stays behind, so over millions of years the sea built up its saltiness.
why is the great salt lake salty | why are some lakes salty | salt lakes => Salt lakes have no outlet to the sea. Rivers bring in dissolved salts, and the water only leaves by evaporating, so the salt builds up.
why are glaciers blue | what makes glacier ice blue => Glacier ice is very dense with few air bubbles, so light travels deep into it. Ice absorbs some red light, leaving the blue that shines back out.
why is antarctica colder than the arctic | arctic vs antarctica | which pole is colder => Antarctica is a high, ice-covered continent, averaging over 2 km above sea level. The Arctic is mostly frozen ocean, and the water below keeps it warmer.
why is the dead sea so salty | how salty is the dead sea | dead sea saltiness => The Dead Sea has no outlet and lies in a hot, dry spot where water evaporates fast. It's about 34% salt, nearly 10 times saltier than the ocean.
why does the ocean have waves | what causes waves => Most ocean waves are made by wind blowing over the water. The stronger the wind, the longer it blows and the farther it travels, the bigger the waves.
why do waves curl | how waves break at the beach => As a wave reaches shallow water, its bottom drags on the seabed and slows down while the top keeps going. The top gets too far ahead, tips over and breaks.
why do we have two high tides | tides twice a day => The Moon's pull makes a bulge of water on the side facing it, and another on the far side. Earth spins through both bulges each day, so most places get two high tides.
