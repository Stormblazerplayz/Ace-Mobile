what is the messier catalog | what are messier objects | who was charles messier => The Messier catalog is a list of 110 fuzzy sky objects, like nebulae, star clusters and galaxies. Charles Messier, a French comet hunter, listed them in the 1700s so he wouldn't mistake them for comets.
what is m1 | what is the crab nebula | crab nebula supernova => The Crab Nebula (M1) is the wreck of a star that exploded as a supernova seen from Earth in 1054. At its center is a pulsar spinning about 30 times a second.
what is m31 | what is messier 31 | m31 galaxy => M31 is the Andromeda Galaxy, the nearest big spiral galaxy to the Milky Way, about 2.5 million light years away.
what is m42 | what is the orion nebula | where is the orion nebula => The Orion Nebula (M42) is a huge cloud of gas and dust where new stars are forming, about 1,300 to 1,400 light years away. You can see it as a fuzzy patch in Orion's sword.
what is m45 | what are the pleiades | seven sisters stars => The Pleiades (M45), or Seven Sisters, is a cluster of young, hot blue stars in Taurus, about 440 light years away. Most people can see six or seven of them without a telescope.
what is m57 | what is the ring nebula | ring nebula facts => The Ring Nebula (M57) in Lyra is a glowing shell of gas thrown off by a dying Sun-like star. It's a planetary nebula, about 2,500 light years away.
what is m51 | what is the whirlpool galaxy | whirlpool galaxy facts => The Whirlpool Galaxy (M51) is a beautiful spiral galaxy about 31 million light years away, tugging on a smaller companion galaxy.
what is m13 | great globular cluster in hercules | hercules cluster => M13 is the Great Globular Cluster in Hercules, a ball of several hundred thousand old stars about 22,000 to 25,000 light years away. In 1974, the Arecibo message was beamed toward it.
what is m104 | what is the sombrero galaxy | sombrero galaxy facts => The Sombrero Galaxy (M104) looks like a wide-brimmed hat, with a bright center and a dark dust lane around its edge. It's about 30 million light years away.
what is m87 | m87 black hole | first picture of a black hole galaxy => M87 is a giant elliptical galaxy about 55 million light years away. The black hole at its center was the first ever photographed, by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2019.
what is m16 | what is the eagle nebula | pillars of creation => The Eagle Nebula (M16) holds the famous Pillars of Creation, towers of gas and dust where stars are forming. Hubble photographed them in 1995, and Webb did again in 2022.
what is m8 | what is the lagoon nebula | lagoon nebula facts => The Lagoon Nebula (M8) is a large star-forming cloud in Sagittarius, about 4,000 to 5,000 light years away. It's faintly visible without a telescope from dark places.
what is m20 | what is the trifid nebula | trifid nebula facts => The Trifid Nebula (M20) in Sagittarius is a star-forming cloud split into three parts by dark dust lanes, with glowing red and blue regions.
what is m27 | what is the dumbbell nebula | dumbbell nebula facts => The Dumbbell Nebula (M27) in Vulpecula was the first planetary nebula ever discovered, by Charles Messier in 1764. It's the glowing gas from a dying star.
what is m33 | what is the triangulum galaxy | triangulum galaxy facts => The Triangulum Galaxy (M33) is the third biggest galaxy in our Local Group, after Andromeda and the Milky Way, about 2.7 to 3 million light years away.
what is m81 | what is bodes galaxy | m81 and m82 => M81, Bode's Galaxy, is a bright spiral galaxy about 12 million light years away in Ursa Major. Its neighbor M82, the Cigar Galaxy, is bursting with new stars.
what is m101 | what is the pinwheel galaxy | pinwheel galaxy facts => The Pinwheel Galaxy (M101) is a big face-on spiral galaxy in Ursa Major, about 21 million light years away.
what is m44 | what is the beehive cluster | praesepe => The Beehive Cluster (M44), also called Praesepe, is an open star cluster in Cancer about 600 light years away. It looks like a misty patch to the naked eye.
what is m17 | what is the omega nebula | swan nebula => The Omega Nebula (M17), also called the Swan Nebula, is a bright star-forming cloud in Sagittarius.
what is an open cluster | what is a globular cluster => An open cluster is a loose group of a few hundred to thousands of young stars, like the Pleiades. A globular cluster is a tight ball of hundreds of thousands of very old stars orbiting a galaxy.
what is a planetary nebula | why is it called a planetary nebula | dying star nebula => A planetary nebula is a glowing shell of gas puffed off by a dying Sun-like star. It has nothing to do with planets: early astronomers thought they looked round like planets.
what is the horsehead nebula | horsehead nebula facts | where is the horsehead nebula => The Horsehead Nebula is a dark cloud of dust shaped like a horse's head, outlined against glowing red gas in Orion, about 1,300 to 1,500 light years away.
what is the helix nebula | eye of god nebula | helix nebula facts => The Helix Nebula in Aquarius is one of the closest planetary nebulae, about 650 light years away. Photos of it are sometimes called the Eye of God.
what is the carina nebula | cosmic cliffs | carina nebula facts => The Carina Nebula is a huge star-forming cloud in the southern sky, about 7,500 light years away. Part of it, the Cosmic Cliffs, was one of Webb's first images in 2022.
what is the cats eye nebula | cats eye nebula facts | nebula that looks like an eye => The Cat's Eye Nebula in Draco is a planetary nebula with nested shells and knots of gas. It's one of the most complex nebulae known.
what is omega centauri | biggest globular cluster | omega centauri facts => Omega Centauri is the biggest globular cluster around the Milky Way, with millions of stars. It may be the leftover core of a small galaxy our galaxy swallowed.
what is the double cluster | double cluster in perseus | two star clusters together => The Double Cluster is a pair of open star clusters close together in Perseus, about 7,500 light years away. Binoculars show them well.
what is the jewel box cluster | kappa crucis cluster | jewel box => The Jewel Box is a bright open cluster near the Southern Cross. Its mix of blue-white and orange stars looks like colored gems.
what is the pillars of creation made of | how big are the pillars of creation | pillars of creation size => The Pillars of Creation are columns of cold hydrogen gas and dust. The tallest is about 4 to 5 light years long. Inside them, new stars are forming.
what is the sloan great wall | hercules corona borealis great wall | largest structure in the universe => The largest known structures are huge walls and filaments of galaxies. The Hercules-Corona Borealis Great Wall may stretch about 10 billion light years, though astronomers still debate it.
biggest galaxy cluster | what is the virgo cluster => A galaxy cluster is a group of hundreds to thousands of galaxies held together by gravity. The Virgo Cluster, about 54 million light years away, is the nearest big one.
what is laniakea | what supercluster are we in => Laniakea is the supercluster that includes the Milky Way, defined in 2014. It holds about 100,000 galaxies and its name is Hawaiian for immense heaven.
what is the hubble deep field | hubble ultra deep field | deep field image => The Hubble Deep Field (1995) pointed at a tiny, seemingly empty patch of sky for days and found thousands of galaxies. It showed the universe is packed with galaxies in every direction.
what is the pale blue dot | pale blue dot photo | voyager picture of earth => The Pale Blue Dot is a photo of Earth taken by Voyager 1 on 14 February 1990 from about 6 billion km away. Earth appears as a tiny speck less than a pixel wide.
what is earthrise | earthrise photo | apollo 8 earthrise => Earthrise is a famous photo of Earth rising over the Moon, taken by astronaut Bill Anders on Apollo 8 on 24 December 1968.
what is the blue marble photo | blue marble apollo 17 | first full earth photo => The Blue Marble is a photo of the whole sunlit Earth taken by the Apollo 17 crew on 7 December 1972. It's one of the most reproduced photos in history.
perseids peak | what comet causes the perseids => The Perseids peak around 12 to 13 August each year, often with 50 to 100 meteors an hour from a dark site. They come from dust left by Comet Swift-Tuttle.
geminids peak | what causes the geminids => The Geminids peak around 13 to 14 December, with up to about 120 meteors an hour in ideal conditions. Unusually, they come from an asteroid, 3200 Phaethon.
when is the leonid meteor shower | leonids peak | leonid meteor storm => The Leonids peak around 17 November, from dust left by Comet Tempel-Tuttle. Most years they're modest, but in 1833 and 1966 they produced storms of thousands of meteors an hour.
when is the orionid meteor shower | orionids peak | halleys comet meteor shower => The Orionids peak around 21 October, and the Eta Aquariids peak around early May. Both come from dust left by Halley's Comet.
when is the quadrantid meteor shower | quadrantids peak | january meteor shower => The Quadrantids peak around 3 to 4 January. Their peak is short, only a few hours, but can be strong.
when is the lyrid meteor shower | lyrids peak | april meteor shower => The Lyrids peak around 22 April, from dust left by Comet Thatcher. They've been observed for about 2,700 years.
what is a radiant meteor shower | why are meteor showers named after constellations | meteor shower radiant => All meteors in a shower seem to come from one point in the sky, called the radiant. Showers are named for the constellation where the radiant sits, like the Perseids in Perseus.
how do you watch a meteor shower | best time to see meteors | tips for meteor watching => Go somewhere dark, away from city lights, after midnight when your side of Earth faces forward into the dust. Lie back, let your eyes adjust for 20 to 30 minutes, and look at a wide area of sky.
how often do meteorites hit earth | how many meteorites fall each year | does space dust fall on earth => Tens of tonnes of space dust and rock fall on Earth every day, mostly as tiny grains. Rocks that reach the ground big enough to find land somewhere thousands of times a year, mostly in oceans.
what are the types of meteorites | stony iron meteorites => Meteorites are stony (most common), iron, or stony-iron. Many feel heavy for their size, have a dark melted crust and are attracted to magnets.
what is the zodiacal light | false dawn | glow from space dust => The zodiacal light is a faint cone of light seen after dusk or before dawn, made by sunlight reflecting off dust in the plane of the solar system.
what is airglow | why is the night sky never completely dark | night sky glow => Airglow is a faint light given off by the upper atmosphere at night, as atoms release energy they absorbed from sunlight during the day.
how does light pollution affect stars | dark sky places => Light pollution is too much artificial light brightening the night sky, hiding all but the brightest stars. Dark sky parks protect places where the Milky Way is still visible.
what is the bortle scale | how dark is my sky | dark sky rating => The Bortle scale rates night sky darkness from 1 (excellent dark sky, Milky Way casts shadows) to 9 (inner city sky, only the brightest stars visible).
what is a star party | stargazing for beginners => Start with your eyes and a star map or app: learn bright constellations, then try binoculars on the Moon, Jupiter's moons and the Pleiades. A star party is a gathering where astronomers share telescopes.
what is the best telescope for a beginner | should i buy binoculars or a telescope | first telescope => Many astronomers suggest starting with binoculars, like 7x50 or 10x50, since they're easy to use. A good first telescope is a small Dobsonian reflector, which shows a lot for its price.
what is a refractor telescope | what is a reflector telescope => A refracting telescope uses lenses to gather light, and a reflecting telescope uses curved mirrors. Big research telescopes are reflectors because huge mirrors are easier to build than huge lenses.
who invented the reflecting telescope | newtonian telescope | isaac newton telescope => Isaac Newton built the first practical reflecting telescope in 1668, using a curved metal mirror to avoid the color fringes lenses made.
what does telescope magnification mean | most important telescope spec => Aperture is the width of the main lens or mirror, and it matters most because a bigger one gathers more light and shows more detail. High magnification on a small telescope just gives a blurry image.
what is the very large array | vla telescope | radio telescope array => The Very Large Array in New Mexico has 27 radio dishes, each 25 meters wide, working together as one giant telescope. It appeared in the movie Contact.
what is the square kilometre array | ska telescope | biggest radio telescope project => The Square Kilometre Array is a giant radio telescope being built in South Africa and Australia, with thousands of antennas. As of 2025, it's under construction, with full science expected around 2029 or later.
what was the arecibo telescope | arecibo collapse | arecibo message => The Arecibo telescope in Puerto Rico was a 305 meter radio dish used from 1963. It sent the Arecibo message in 1974 and collapsed in December 2020 after cables failed.
what is an observatory | why are observatories on mountains => An observatory is a place with telescopes for studying the sky. Many are on high, dry mountains, like Mauna Kea in Hawaii and the Atacama Desert in Chile, above most of the air and clouds.
what is spectroscopy in astronomy | star spectrum => Spectroscopy splits starlight into its colors. Dark lines in the spectrum match specific elements, so astronomers can tell what a star is made of without going there.
how was helium discovered in the sun | helium discovered in space | helium named after the sun => Helium was discovered in 1868 as a mystery yellow line in the Sun's spectrum during an eclipse, before it was found on Earth in 1895. Its name comes from helios, Greek for Sun.
what is blueshift | how do we know galaxies are moving away => Redshift is when light from an object moving away is stretched to longer, redder wavelengths. Blueshift is the opposite. Most galaxies are redshifted, showing the universe is expanding.
what is the cosmic distance ladder | how do we measure distance to stars | parallax => Astronomers measure nearby stars by parallax, how they shift as Earth orbits the Sun. For farther objects, they use stars and supernovae of known brightness, like Cepheid variables, as standard candles.
what is a cepheid variable | standard candle => Cepheids are stars that pulse in brightness. Henrietta Leavitt found in 1912 that their pulse rate reveals their true brightness, which lets astronomers measure distances to other galaxies.
what is apparent magnitude | what is absolute magnitude | star brightness scale => Magnitude measures brightness, and smaller numbers mean brighter. Apparent magnitude is how bright a star looks from Earth. Absolute magnitude is how bright it would look from a standard distance of 32.6 light years.
what is the faintest star you can see | naked eye magnitude limit => From a very dark site, people can see stars down to about magnitude 6, which is a few thousand stars at a time. In a city, you might see only a few dozen.
