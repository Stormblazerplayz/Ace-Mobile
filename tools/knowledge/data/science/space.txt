# tag: science.space.sun
what is the sun made of | what elements are in the sun | sun composition => The Sun is about 73% hydrogen and 25% helium by mass, with small amounts of oxygen, carbon, iron and other elements.
how old is the sun | when did the sun form | age of the sun => The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old. It formed from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust.
how long will the sun last | when will the sun die | how much longer will the sun shine => The Sun is about halfway through its life. In roughly 5 billion years it will run low on hydrogen in its core, swell into a red giant, and later become a white dwarf.
will the sun explode | will the sun become a supernova | what will happen to the sun => No. The Sun isn't massive enough to explode as a supernova. It will puff off its outer layers into a planetary nebula and leave behind a white dwarf.
will the sun swallow the earth | what happens to earth when the sun becomes a red giant | earths fate => When the Sun becomes a red giant, it will grow huge and probably swallow Mercury and Venus. Earth may or may not be swallowed, but it will be scorched long before that.
how hot is the core of the sun | how hot is the middle of the sun => The Sun's core is about 15 million °C. That's hot enough for hydrogen nuclei to fuse into helium.
sun surface temperature | temperature of the photosphere => The Sun's visible surface, the photosphere, is about 5,500 °C (about 10,000 °F).
why is the corona hotter than the surface | how hot is the suns corona | sun corona temperature => The Sun's outer atmosphere, the corona, reaches over a million degrees, far hotter than the surface. Scientists think magnetic waves and tiny explosions heat it, but it's still being studied.
parts of the sun | inside the sun => From inside out, the Sun has a core, a radiative zone, a convection zone, the photosphere (visible surface), the chromosphere and the corona.
what are sunspots | why does the sun have dark spots | what causes sunspots => Sunspots are cooler, darker areas on the Sun caused by strong magnetic fields. They're still about 3,500 °C and can be bigger than Earth.
what is the solar cycle | 11 year sun cycle | what is solar maximum => The Sun's activity rises and falls about every 11 years. At solar maximum there are more sunspots, flares and auroras. The latest maximum peaked around 2024 to 2025.
what is a solar flare | what is a coronal mass ejection | can solar flares hurt us => A solar flare is a sudden burst of energy from the Sun. A coronal mass ejection blasts out a huge cloud of charged particles that can cause auroras and disrupt satellites and power grids.
what is the solar wind | how does solar wind work | what does the solar wind do => The solar wind is a constant stream of charged particles flowing out from the Sun at about 400 km per second. Earth's magnetic field shields us from most of it.
how big is the sun compared to earth | how many times bigger is the sun than earth | sun vs earth size => The Sun is about 109 times as wide as Earth, and about 1.3 million Earths could fit inside it.
how heavy is the sun | how much of the solar systems mass is the sun | mass of the sun => The Sun holds about 99.8% of all the mass in the solar system. It's about 333,000 times as massive as Earth.
what kind of star is the sun | is the sun a yellow dwarf | sun star type => The Sun is a G-type main sequence star, often called a yellow dwarf. It's actually white, but looks yellow through our atmosphere.
what color is the sun really | is the sun white or yellow | why does the sun look yellow => The Sun's light is basically white, a mix of all colors. It looks yellow or orange from the ground because our air scatters some of the blue light away.
why shouldnt you look at the sun | is it dangerous to stare at the sun | how to look at the sun safely => Looking at the Sun can burn your retina and permanently damage your eyes, even during a partial eclipse. Only look through certified eclipse glasses or solar filters.
how does the sun make light | how does the sun shine | what powers the sun => The Sun shines because of nuclear fusion in its core. It turns about 4 million tonnes of matter into energy every second, which slowly travels out and leaves as light.
how long does light take to escape the sun | how old is sunlight | how long does energy take to leave the sun core => Energy made in the Sun's core can take tens of thousands of years or more to work its way out to the surface. Then it reaches Earth in about 8 minutes.
what is the parker solar probe | which spacecraft touched the sun | closest spacecraft to the sun => NASA's Parker Solar Probe launched in 2018 to fly through the Sun's corona. In December 2024 it passed about 6.1 million km from the surface, the closest any spacecraft has been, and it's the fastest human-made object.
what is the heliosphere | where does the solar system end | what is the heliopause => The heliosphere is a giant bubble around the solar system made by the solar wind. Its edge, the heliopause, is where the solar wind meets interstellar space. Voyager 1 crossed it in 2012.
# tag: science.space.stars
what is a star | what are stars made of | how do stars shine => A star is a huge ball of hot gas, mostly hydrogen and helium, held together by its own gravity. Nuclear fusion in its core makes it shine.
how do stars form | where do stars come from => Stars form inside cold clouds of gas and dust called nebulae. Gravity pulls clumps together until the center gets hot and dense enough for fusion to start.
what is the life cycle of a star | stages of a star life | star life cycle steps => A star forms in a nebula, becomes a protostar, then a main sequence star. A Sun-like star becomes a red giant and then a white dwarf. A very big star becomes a red supergiant, explodes as a supernova and leaves a neutron star or black hole.
what is a main sequence star | main sequence meaning | how long do stars stay on the main sequence => A main sequence star is fusing hydrogen into helium in its core, like the Sun. Stars spend about 90% of their lives in this stage.
what is a red giant | how does a star become a red giant | red giant star => A red giant is a dying star that has used up the hydrogen in its core. Its outer layers swell up and cool, making it huge and red.
what is a white dwarf | how big is a white dwarf | white dwarf star => A white dwarf is the hot, dense leftover core of a star like the Sun. It's about the size of Earth, but a teaspoon of it would weigh several tonnes.
what is a red dwarf | most common type of star | red dwarf star => Red dwarfs are small, cool, dim stars. They're the most common stars in the Milky Way and can shine for trillions of years.
what is a neutron star | how dense is a neutron star | neutron star size => A neutron star is the collapsed core left after a big star explodes. It's only about 20 km wide but has more mass than the Sun. A teaspoon of it would weigh about a billion tonnes.
what is a pulsar | how do pulsars work | why do pulsars blink => A pulsar is a spinning neutron star that sends out beams of radio waves. As it spins, the beams sweep past Earth like a lighthouse, so we see regular pulses.
what is a magnetar | strongest magnet in the universe | magnetar star => A magnetar is a neutron star with an incredibly strong magnetic field, about a thousand trillion times stronger than Earth's. They're the strongest magnets known.
what is a protostar | baby star | what comes before a star => A protostar is a baby star still gathering gas from its cloud. It glows from the heat of collapsing, before fusion fully begins.
what is a brown dwarf | is a brown dwarf a star | failed star => A brown dwarf is bigger than a planet but too small to fuse hydrogen like a true star. It's sometimes called a failed star and glows dimly from leftover heat.
what is a binary star | do stars have partners | how many stars are in pairs => A binary star is two stars orbiting each other. Many stars are in pairs or groups, like Alpha Centauri, which is actually three stars.
what is a variable star | why do some stars change brightness | cepheid stars => A variable star changes in brightness. Some pulse in and out, like Cepheids, and others dim when a partner star passes in front.
what is a star cluster | what is the pleiades | open vs globular cluster => A star cluster is a group of stars born together. Open clusters, like the Pleiades (Seven Sisters), are loose groups of young stars. Globular clusters are tight balls of hundreds of thousands of old stars.
why do stars twinkle | why do stars flicker | why dont planets twinkle => Stars twinkle because their light is bent back and forth by moving air in our atmosphere. Planets twinkle less because they look like tiny disks, not points.
why do stars have different colors | what do star colors mean | hottest color of star => A star's color shows its temperature. Red stars are coolest, about 3,000 °C, yellow ones like the Sun are medium, and blue stars are hottest, over 10,000 °C.
what is the brightest star in the sky | brightest star at night | which star is the brightest => Sirius is the brightest star in the night sky. Only the Moon and the planets Venus and Jupiter, and sometimes Mars, look brighter.
how hot is a star | hottest stars | star temperatures => Star surfaces range from about 2,500 °C for cool red dwarfs to over 40,000 °C for the hottest blue stars. Their cores are millions of degrees.
are stars all the same size as the sun | how small can a star be | star sizes => No. The smallest true stars are about a tenth the Sun's mass, and the biggest are over 100 times its mass. Their sizes vary from a bit bigger than Jupiter to over a thousand times the Sun's width.
are the stars we see still there | are we seeing dead stars | do stars die before we see them => Almost all the stars you see are still there. They're in our galaxy, so their light is only years to thousands of years old, and stars live for millions to billions of years.
are humans made of stars | where do our atoms come from => Most of the atoms in your body, like carbon, oxygen and iron, were made inside stars that exploded long ago. The hydrogen came from the Big Bang. That's why Carl Sagan said we're made of star stuff.
what is a constellation vs an asterism | what is an asterism | is the big dipper a constellation => An asterism is a star pattern that isn't one of the 88 official constellations. The Big Dipper is an asterism inside the constellation Ursa Major.
how to find polaris with the big dipper | big dipper pointer stars => Find the Big Dipper, then follow the two stars at the front edge of its cup. They point to Polaris, about five times their distance apart.
what is the summer triangle | which stars make the summer triangle | summer triangle stars => The Summer Triangle is an asterism of three bright stars, Vega, Deneb and Altair, high in northern summer skies.
what is orions belt | which stars are in orions belt | three stars in a row => Orion's Belt is a line of three bright stars, Alnitak, Alnilam and Mintaka, in the constellation Orion. It's one of the easiest patterns to spot.
what are the zodiac constellations | how many zodiac constellations | which constellations is the sun in => The Sun appears to pass through 12 traditional zodiac constellations, but it also passes through a 13th one, Ophiuchus.
who named the constellations | where do constellation names come from | how old are constellations => Many constellations come from ancient Greek and Babylonian star lore. Southern ones were added by European explorers. In 1922 the International Astronomical Union set the official list of 88.
can you see constellations from both hemispheres | southern hemisphere constellations | why are stars different in australia => Some constellations can only be seen from one hemisphere. The Southern Cross is visible from Australia but not from most of the US or Europe.
# tag: science.space.galaxies
what is the milky way | what kind of galaxy is the milky way | milky way facts => The Milky Way is our home galaxy, a barred spiral about 100,000 light years across, with an estimated 100 to 400 billion stars.
where is earth in the milky way | where are we in the galaxy | how far is the sun from the galactic center => The Sun is about 26,000 light years from the center of the Milky Way, in a smaller spiral arm called the Orion Arm.
how long does it take the sun to orbit the galaxy | galactic year | how fast does the sun move around the galaxy => The Sun takes about 230 million years to go once around the Milky Way, traveling at about 230 km per second. That's called a galactic year.
what is at the center of the milky way | sagittarius a star | black hole in the middle of our galaxy => At the Milky Way's center is a supermassive black hole called Sagittarius A*. It has about 4 million times the mass of the Sun. Its first image was released in 2022.
why can we see the milky way as a band | why does the milky way look like a stripe | milky way in the night sky => We live inside the flat disk of the galaxy, so when we look along the disk we see millions of distant stars blurred into a glowing band across the sky.
spiral elliptical irregular galaxies | galaxy shapes => Galaxies are usually sorted into spiral (like pinwheels), elliptical (smooth oval balls) and irregular (no clear shape).
what is the andromeda galaxy | how far away is andromeda | can you see andromeda without a telescope => Andromeda is the nearest big galaxy, about 2.5 million light years away. On a dark night it's visible to the naked eye as a faint smudge, the farthest thing most people can see without help.
will the milky way crash into andromeda | milky way andromeda collision | when will andromeda hit us => Andromeda and the Milky Way are moving toward each other. They may merge in about 4 to 5 billion years, though newer studies say it's not certain. Stars are so far apart they'd rarely collide.
what are the magellanic clouds | large magellanic cloud | small galaxies near the milky way => The Large and Small Magellanic Clouds are small galaxies near the Milky Way, visible from the Southern Hemisphere as fuzzy patches.
what is the local group | what galaxies are near us | our galaxy group => The Local Group is our neighborhood of over 50 galaxies, mostly small ones. The biggest are Andromeda, the Milky Way and the Triangulum Galaxy.
what is a galaxy cluster | what is a supercluster | laniakea supercluster => Galaxies gather into clusters, and clusters into superclusters. Our Local Group is part of the Laniakea Supercluster, which holds about 100,000 galaxies.
what is a quasar | brightest objects in the universe | quasar meaning => A quasar is the super bright center of a distant galaxy, powered by a giant black hole swallowing gas. Some quasars outshine their whole galaxy.
what is the farthest galaxy ever seen | most distant galaxy | oldest galaxy found => As of 2025, the James Webb Space Telescope has found galaxies whose light left them less than 300 million years after the Big Bang, like JADES-GS-z14-0 and MoM-z14.
# tag: science.space.universe
what is the big bang | how did the universe begin | big bang theory explained => The Big Bang theory says the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since.
what was before the big bang | what caused the big bang | what came before the universe => Nobody knows. Our physics can't yet describe what happened at or before the very start, and scientists aren't even sure before the Big Bang makes sense.
what is the evidence for the big bang | how do we know the big bang happened | proof of the big bang => Key evidence includes galaxies moving away from us (the expanding universe), the cosmic microwave background (leftover glow from the early universe), and the amount of hydrogen and helium in the universe.
what is the cosmic microwave background | cmb radiation | leftover heat from the big bang => The cosmic microwave background is faint microwave light left over from about 380,000 years after the Big Bang. It was discovered by accident in 1965 by Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson.
how fast is the universe expanding | what is hubbles law => Yes. Galaxies are moving apart, and the farther away they are, the faster they recede. In 1998 scientists found the expansion is speeding up, likely because of dark energy.
how big is the universe | is the universe infinite | size of the observable universe => The observable universe is about 93 billion light years across. Nobody knows how big the whole universe is or whether it goes on forever.
what is the observable universe | why cant we see the whole universe | edge of the universe => The observable universe is the part we can see, because light from farther away hasn't had time to reach us yet. There's probably much more beyond it.
how will the universe end | end of the universe | big freeze big crunch big rip => Scientists aren't sure. The leading idea is the Big Freeze, where the universe keeps expanding and cooling until stars burn out. Other ideas include the Big Crunch and the Big Rip.
are there other universes | what is the multiverse | is there a parallel universe => Some theories suggest our universe could be one of many, called the multiverse. But as of 2025, there's no way to test it or any evidence for it.
what is redshift | why is light from galaxies red shifted | redshift meaning => Redshift is when light from an object moving away from us gets stretched to longer, redder wavelengths. It's how we know distant galaxies are moving away.
# tag: science.space.black-holes
how do black holes form | where do black holes come from | how is a black hole made => Most black holes form when a very massive star runs out of fuel and its core collapses under its own gravity, often in a supernova.
what is an event horizon | what happens at the edge of a black hole | point of no return => The event horizon is the boundary around a black hole where escape is impossible, even for light. Anything that crosses it can't get out.
what is a supermassive black hole | biggest black holes | black holes in the center of galaxies => Supermassive black holes have millions to billions of times the Sun's mass. Almost every big galaxy, including ours, has one at its center.
what was the first picture of a black hole | m87 black hole image | event horizon telescope => In 2019, the Event Horizon Telescope released the first image of a black hole, in the galaxy M87, about 55 million light years away. It showed a glowing ring around a dark shadow.
what is the closest black hole to earth | nearest black hole | gaia bh1 => As of 2025, the closest known black hole is Gaia BH1, about 1,560 light years away. It's quiet and was found by watching a star wobble around it.
can a black hole swallow earth | will a black hole eat the earth | is there a black hole near us => No. There are no black holes close enough to threaten Earth, and the Sun can't become a black hole. A black hole only pulls hard on things that get very close.
do black holes last forever | do black holes evaporate | hawking radiation explained => Stephen Hawking predicted black holes slowly leak energy, called Hawking radiation, and could eventually evaporate. For a star-sized black hole, that would take far longer than the universe's age.
what is a wormhole | are wormholes real | can you travel through a wormhole => A wormhole is a hypothetical tunnel through space and time allowed by Einstein's equations. None has ever been found, and it's not known if they can exist.
what is a white hole | are white holes real | opposite of a black hole => A white hole is an idea from the math of relativity, a region nothing can enter but things can leave. There's no evidence white holes exist.
# tag: science.space.small-bodies
what is the asteroid belt | where is the asteroid belt | how many asteroids are in the asteroid belt => The asteroid belt is a ring of rocky objects between Mars and Jupiter. It has millions of asteroids, but they're spread out, so spacecraft fly through easily.
what is the biggest asteroid | largest asteroid | is vesta an asteroid => Ceres is the biggest object in the asteroid belt, but it's now called a dwarf planet. The biggest true asteroid is Vesta, about 525 km wide.
what asteroid killed the dinosaurs | chicxulub crater => An asteroid about 10 km wide hit near Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula about 66 million years ago, making the Chicxulub crater. It caused a mass extinction that wiped out the non-bird dinosaurs.
will an asteroid hit earth | are asteroids dangerous | how does nasa track asteroids => Small space rocks hit Earth all the time and burn up. NASA and others track near-Earth asteroids, and none of the known big ones is on course to hit Earth in the next 100 years.
what was the dart mission | can we push an asteroid away | did nasa hit an asteroid => In 2022, NASA's DART spacecraft crashed into the small asteroid Dimorphos on purpose and changed its orbit. It was the first test of defending Earth by nudging an asteroid.
what is a near earth object | what is a potentially hazardous asteroid | neo meaning => A near-Earth object is an asteroid or comet whose orbit comes near Earth's. Potentially hazardous ones are large and come within about 7.5 million km of Earth's orbit.
what is the tunguska event | tunguska explosion 1908 | what happened in siberia in 1908 => In 1908, a space rock exploded in the air over Tunguska, Siberia. It flattened about 80 million trees across more than 2,000 square km but left no crater.
what was the chelyabinsk meteor | 2013 meteor russia | meteor explosion russia => In 2013, a meteor about 20 meters wide exploded over Chelyabinsk, Russia. Its shock wave broke windows and injured about 1,500 people, mostly from flying glass.
what are comets made of | what is a comet tail made of | why do comets have tails => Comets are dirty snowballs of ice, dust and rock. When one gets near the Sun, the ice turns to gas, making a glowing cloud and tails that point away from the Sun.
where do comets come from | what is the oort cloud | kuiper belt comets => Short period comets come from the Kuiper belt past Neptune. Long period comets come from the Oort cloud, a huge, distant shell of icy bodies surrounding the solar system.
when will halleys comet come back | when is halleys comet next visible | halleys comet orbit => Halley's Comet returns about every 76 years. It was last seen in 1986 and will be back in 2061.
what is a meteor shower | why do meteor showers happen | when are meteor showers => A meteor shower happens when Earth passes through the trail of dust left by a comet. Lots of tiny bits burn up in the air, making shooting stars that seem to come from one spot.
perseids meteor shower | geminids meteor shower => Two of the best yearly showers are the Perseids, peaking around 12 August, and the Geminids, peaking around 13 to 14 December. Each can show over 50 meteors an hour in dark skies.
what is a fireball meteor | what is a bolide | very bright meteor => A fireball is an extra bright meteor, brighter than the planet Venus. A bolide is a fireball that explodes in the atmosphere.
how big are shooting stars | how big is a meteor | what size are meteors => Most shooting stars are made by bits of dust smaller than a grain of sand, burning up about 80 to 100 km high as they hit our atmosphere at high speed.
are meteorites valuable | how to tell if a rock is a meteorite | how to identify a meteorite => Meteorites are usually heavy for their size, often attract a magnet because of iron, and have a dark, thin crust from burning through the air. Some are worth a lot to collectors and scientists.
what is the biggest meteorite ever found | hoba meteorite | largest meteorite on earth => The biggest known meteorite is the Hoba meteorite in Namibia. It's a slab of iron weighing about 60 tonnes and has never been moved.
what is an exoplanet | how many exoplanets are there | planets around other stars => An exoplanet is a planet orbiting a star other than the Sun. As of 2025, more than 6,000 have been confirmed.
how do we find exoplanets | transit method | how do you detect planets around other stars => Most exoplanets are found by the transit method: a star dims slightly when a planet passes in front of it. Others are found by the star's wobble as the planet tugs it.
what was the first exoplanet discovered | first planet around another star | 51 pegasi b => In 1995, Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz found 51 Pegasi b, the first exoplanet around a Sun-like star. They won the Nobel Prize in 2019.
what is the habitable zone | what is the goldilocks zone | where can life exist around a star => The habitable zone is the area around a star where it's not too hot and not too cold for liquid water to exist on a planet's surface.
what is trappist 1 | trappist 1 planets | star with seven earth sized planets => TRAPPIST-1 is a small red dwarf about 40 light years away with seven Earth-sized planets. Several of them are in its habitable zone.
is there life on other planets | have we found aliens | does alien life exist => As of 2025, no life has been found beyond Earth. Scientists are searching on Mars, icy moons like Europa and Enceladus, and in the air of exoplanets.
what is seti | search for extraterrestrial intelligence | are we listening for aliens => SETI stands for the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence. Scientists use radio telescopes to listen for signals that could come from alien civilizations. None have been confirmed.
what is the wow signal | wow signal 1977 | strange radio signal from space => The Wow! signal was a strong, narrow radio signal picked up in 1977 by Ohio State's Big Ear telescope. It was never detected again, and its source is still unknown.
what is the drake equation | how many alien civilizations are there | drake equation explained => The Drake equation, written by Frank Drake in 1961, estimates how many alien civilizations might exist by multiplying factors like the number of stars and planets. Many of its numbers are still unknown.
what is the fermi paradox | where are all the aliens | why havent we found aliens => The Fermi paradox asks why, if the universe is so huge and old, we haven't seen any signs of alien life. There are many possible answers, but no one knows.
what is a planet | what makes something a planet | iau planet definition => In 2006, the International Astronomical Union said a planet must orbit the Sun, be big enough for gravity to make it round, and have cleared other objects from its orbit.
what are the inner and outer planets | rocky planets vs gas giants | terrestrial planets => The inner planets, Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars, are small and rocky. The outer planets, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune, are giants made mostly of gas and ice.
how was the solar system formed | how did the planets form | solar system formation => About 4.6 billion years ago, a cloud of gas and dust collapsed. Most of it formed the Sun, and the leftover disk clumped into planets, moons, asteroids and comets.
how old is the solar system | age of the solar system | how many years old is the solar system => The solar system is about 4.6 billion years old, based on the ages of the oldest meteorites.
what is a mnemonic for the planets | how to remember the planets order | my very educated mother => A popular one is My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Nachos: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
do all planets orbit in the same direction | which planets spin backwards | retrograde rotation planets => All planets orbit the Sun in the same direction. Most also spin that way, but Venus spins backward and Uranus spins on its side.
why are planets round | why are stars round | why is everything in space a sphere => Big objects are round because gravity pulls equally from all sides toward the center. Small asteroids aren't massive enough, so they can be lumpy.
which planet has the most moons | what planet has the most moons | moon count by planet => As of 2025, Saturn has the most known moons, 274, far more than any other planet. Jupiter is second.
which planet has the longest day | longest day in the solar system | slowest spinning planet => Venus has the longest day, taking 243 Earth days to spin once. That's longer than its year of 225 days.
which planet has the shortest day | fastest spinning planet | shortest day in the solar system => Jupiter has the shortest day, spinning once in just under 10 hours.
which planet is the coldest | coldest planet in the solar system | is neptune or uranus colder => Uranus has recorded the coldest temperatures, about -224 °C, even though Neptune is farther from the Sun.
which planet is the densest | densest planet | which planet is least dense => Earth is the densest planet. Saturn is the least dense, less dense than water.
which planet is most like earth | earths twin planet | most earthlike planet => Venus is called Earth's twin because it's almost the same size and mass, but its surface is a scorching 465 °C. Mars is more like Earth in other ways, like its day length and seasons.
can you see planets without a telescope | which planets can i see with my eyes | naked eye planets => Five planets are easy to see without a telescope: Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. They look like bright stars that don't twinkle much.
what is the morning star | what is the evening star | is venus a star => The morning star and evening star are both Venus, not a star. It's the brightest planet and shines near the Sun just before sunrise or after sunset.
what is a planetary alignment | do planets ever line up | what is a parade of planets => A planetary alignment, or parade, is when several planets appear in the same part of the sky. They never form a perfect line, and it has no effect on Earth.
what is a transit of venus | when is the next transit of venus | venus crossing the sun => A transit of Venus is when Venus passes across the face of the Sun as seen from Earth. The last was in 2012, and the next will be in 2117.
# tag: science.space.earth-moon
why is it day and night | what causes day and night | why does earth have night => Earth spins on its axis once about every 24 hours. The side facing the Sun has day, and the side facing away has night.
how long does earth take to spin once | is a day exactly 24 hours | sidereal day => Earth spins once relative to the stars in about 23 hours 56 minutes. A full sunny day, from noon to noon, averages 24 hours because Earth also moves along its orbit.
when is earth closest to the sun | what is perihelion | earth closest point to the sun => Earth is closest to the Sun, called perihelion, in early January, about 147 million km away. It's farthest, aphelion, in early July.
what is an equinox | equal day and night => An equinox is when day and night are about equal everywhere. They happen around 20 March and 22 September each year.
why is it always cold at the poles | why is the arctic cold => At the poles, sunlight hits at a low angle and spreads over a large area, and for months there's little or no sun at all. The bright ice also reflects much of the sunlight away.
what causes the tides on earth from the moon | does the sun affect tides | spring and neap tides => The Moon's gravity causes most of the tides, and the Sun helps. When they line up at new and full moon, we get extra big spring tides. At half moons, we get small neap tides.
what are the phases of the moon in order | moon phase names | order of moon phases => The Moon's phases are new moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full moon, waning gibbous, last (third) quarter and waning crescent.
what does waxing and waning mean | waxing vs waning moon | is the moon growing or shrinking => Waxing means the lit part of the Moon is growing each night, heading toward full. Waning means it's shrinking after full moon.
what is a new moon | why cant you see the new moon | new moon meaning => At new moon, the Moon is between Earth and the Sun, so its sunlit side faces away from us and it's almost invisible.
why dont we have an eclipse every month | why arent eclipses monthly | moons orbit tilted => The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5 degrees compared to Earth's orbit, so usually the Moon passes a bit above or below the Sun's line. Eclipses only happen when they line up exactly.
what is a total solar eclipse | what is totality | total vs partial eclipse => In a total solar eclipse, the Moon completely covers the Sun, and the sky goes dark for a few minutes. You can see the Sun's glowing corona. Outside the narrow path, people see a partial eclipse.
why is the moon the same size as the sun in the sky | how can the moon cover the sun | eclipse coincidence => The Sun is about 400 times wider than the Moon, but also about 400 times farther away. That coincidence makes them look nearly the same size, allowing total eclipses.
what is an annular eclipse | ring of fire eclipse | annular solar eclipse => An annular eclipse happens when the Moon is farther from Earth and looks a little smaller than the Sun. It leaves a bright ring of sunlight around it, called a ring of fire.
when is the next total solar eclipse | upcoming solar eclipses | next eclipse in the us => Total solar eclipses include 12 August 2026 (Greenland, Iceland, Spain) and 2 August 2027 (Spain, North Africa, Middle East). The next one visible from the contiguous US is on 23 August 2044.
what is earthshine | why can i see the dark part of the moon | da vinci glow => Earthshine is sunlight reflected off Earth that lights up the dark part of the Moon, so you can faintly see the whole Moon during a crescent.
what is a harvest moon | full moon names | what is a wolf moon => Full moons have traditional names. The Harvest Moon is the full moon closest to the September equinox. Others include the Wolf Moon (January) and the Strawberry Moon (June).
