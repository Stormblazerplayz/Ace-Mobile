# tag: science.earth.oceans
name the five oceans | what are the oceans of the world => There are five oceans: the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern and Arctic. They're all connected into one world ocean.
what is the smallest ocean | smallest and shallowest ocean => The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and shallowest ocean. Much of it is covered by sea ice, especially in winter.
when was the southern ocean named | ocean around antarctica => The Southern Ocean surrounds Antarctica. National Geographic began recognizing it as the fifth ocean in 2021, though many scientists had used the name for a long time.
how deep is the ocean on average | average depth of the ocean | how deep is the sea => The ocean's average depth is about 3,700 meters (about 12,100 feet).
who has been to the bottom of the mariana trench | first dive to challenger deep | trieste dive 1960 => Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh first reached the Challenger Deep in the Trieste in 1960. James Cameron went alone in 2012, and Victor Vescovo has made many dives since 2019.
how much of the ocean is explored | how much of the ocean have we explored | is the ocean unexplored => Only a small part of the deep sea has been seen by people or cameras. As of 2025, about a quarter of the seafloor has been mapped in high detail.
what are the ocean zones | layers of the ocean | sunlight twilight midnight zone => The ocean has layers by depth: the sunlight zone (top 200 m), twilight zone (200 to 1,000 m), midnight zone (1,000 to 4,000 m), abyss (4,000 to 6,000 m) and the trenches below.
how far does sunlight go in the ocean | why is the deep sea dark | light in the ocean => Enough sunlight for plants reaches only about 200 meters down. Below about 1,000 meters it's completely dark, except for light made by animals.
how cold is the deep ocean | temperature of the deep sea | is the bottom of the ocean cold => The deep ocean is very cold, usually about 0 to 4 °C, almost everywhere, even under tropical seas.
how much pressure is at the bottom of the ocean | ocean pressure depth | why does pressure increase underwater => Pressure rises by about 1 atmosphere every 10 meters down. At the bottom of the Mariana Trench, it's over 1,000 times the pressure at the surface.
why is the ocean blue | why is the sea blue | what color is the ocean => The ocean looks blue mainly because water absorbs red light and scatters blue light. It also reflects some blue sky.
how much salt is in the ocean | how salty is the sea | ocean salinity => Seawater is about 3.5% salt, which is about 35 grams of salt in every liter.
which sea is the saltiest | why can you float in the dead sea | dead sea salt => The Dead Sea is about 34% salt, nearly 10 times saltier than the ocean. All that salt makes the water so dense that people float easily.
can you drink seawater | why cant you drink salt water | what happens if you drink sea water => No. Seawater is so salty that your kidneys would need more water to get rid of the salt than you drank, so it makes you more dehydrated.
what is desalination | how do you make sea water drinkable | desalination plants => Desalination removes salt from seawater, usually by pushing it through very fine filters (reverse osmosis) or by boiling it and collecting the steam.
what causes ocean waves | how do waves form | why are there waves => Most waves are made by wind blowing across the water. Stronger winds blowing longer over bigger distances make bigger waves.
does the water move with a wave | how do waves move water | waves carry energy not water => In deep water, a wave moves energy forward, but the water itself mostly goes around in circles and stays in roughly the same place.
why do waves break | why do waves curl at the beach | breaking waves => As waves reach shallow water, their bottoms slow down by dragging on the seafloor while their tops keep going, so they get taller and tip over.
what is the biggest wave ever surfed | biggest wave ever recorded | lituya bay tsunami => The biggest wave ever recorded was in Lituya Bay, Alaska, in 1958, when a landslide splashed water 524 meters up a hillside. The biggest surfed waves at Nazaré, Portugal, are around 25 to 26 meters.
what are rogue waves | are rogue waves real | freak waves => Rogue waves are sudden, giant waves far taller than those around them. They were once thought to be sailors' tales, but one was measured at 25.6 meters at the Draupner platform in 1995.
how does a tsunami form | what triggers a tsunami | tsunami warning signs => Tsunamis are usually triggered by undersea earthquakes that push up the seafloor, but landslides and volcanoes can cause them too. If the sea suddenly pulls back, go to high ground right away.
how fast does a tsunami travel | how big are tsunami waves | tsunami speed => In deep ocean, a tsunami can travel at about 800 km/h, as fast as a jet, but may be less than a meter tall. It slows and grows much taller as it reaches shallow water.
what was the 2004 tsunami | indian ocean tsunami | boxing day tsunami => On 26 December 2004, a magnitude 9.1 earthquake off Sumatra caused a tsunami that killed about 230,000 people in 14 countries, the deadliest in history.
what makes tides happen | how does the moon cause tides | why are there two tides a day => The Moon's gravity pulls the ocean into a bulge on the side facing it and another on the far side. As Earth spins through both bulges, most coasts get two high tides and two low tides a day.
what are spring tides | what are neap tides | spring vs neap tides => Spring tides, with the biggest range, happen at new and full moon when the Sun and Moon line up. Neap tides, with the smallest range, happen at quarter moons.
where are the biggest tides | bay of fundy tides | highest tides in the world => The Bay of Fundy in Canada has the world's biggest tides, with up to about 16 meters between low and high tide.
what are ocean currents | why do ocean currents happen | what drives ocean currents => Ocean currents are big flows of water. Surface currents are pushed by winds, while deep currents are driven by differences in temperature and saltiness.
what is the gulf stream | why is britain warmer than canada | gulf stream facts => The Gulf Stream is a warm current flowing from the Gulf of Mexico up the US coast and across the Atlantic. It helps keep western Europe milder than other places as far north.
what is the great ocean conveyor belt | thermohaline circulation | global ocean circulation => The global conveyor belt is a slow, worldwide loop of deep and surface ocean currents driven by temperature and salt. Water may take about 1,000 years to go all the way around.
what is a gyre | great pacific garbage patch | ocean gyres => A gyre is a huge circular system of ocean currents. The Great Pacific Garbage Patch is a zone of floating plastic collected by the North Pacific Gyre, mostly tiny bits, not a solid island.
what is upwelling | why are some ocean areas full of fish | upwelling nutrients => Upwelling is when winds push surface water away and cold, nutrient-rich water rises from below. It feeds plankton, so upwelling zones like off Peru have huge fish populations.
phytoplankton vs zooplankton | why is plankton important => Plankton are tiny living things that drift in water. Phytoplankton are plant-like and make food from sunlight, and zooplankton are tiny animals. Plankton feed almost all ocean life.
how much oxygen comes from the ocean | phytoplankton oxygen => Ocean phytoplankton make roughly half of the oxygen produced on Earth by photosynthesis.
why are coral reefs dying | what causes coral bleaching => When water gets too warm, corals push out the colorful algae living in them and turn white. Without the algae, corals can starve if the heat lasts too long.
what is a coral reef made of | how do coral reefs form | are corals animals => Coral reefs are built by tiny animals called coral polyps, which make hard skeletons of calcium carbonate. Over thousands of years, the skeletons pile up into reefs.
what are hydrothermal vents | black smokers | life at hydrothermal vents => Hydrothermal vents are hot springs on the seafloor where heated, mineral-rich water gushes out. Black smokers can reach about 400 °C. Tube worms, clams and bacteria live there without sunlight.
how can life survive without sunlight | what is chemosynthesis | vent bacteria energy => Near deep-sea vents, bacteria use chemosynthesis, getting energy from chemicals like hydrogen sulfide instead of sunlight. Other animals eat them or live with them.
what is an estuary ecosystem | what is a salt marsh | what are mangroves => Estuaries, salt marshes and mangrove forests are where fresh and salt water meet. They're nurseries for young fish and protect coasts from storms.
what is a continental shelf ecosystem | why do most fish live near coasts | coastal waters life => Most sea life lives in shallow coastal waters over the continental shelves, where sunlight and nutrients are plentiful.
what is the sea floor like | what does the ocean floor look like | features of the ocean floor => The seafloor has mountain ranges (mid-ocean ridges), deep trenches, flat abyssal plains, and seamounts, which are underwater volcanoes.
what is a seamount | underwater mountains | how many seamounts are there => A seamount is an underwater mountain, usually an old volcano, that doesn't reach the surface. There may be over 100,000 of them.
what is an atoll | how do atolls form | ring shaped island => An atoll is a ring of coral reef around a lagoon. It forms when a coral reef grows around a volcanic island that slowly sinks away.
how are islands formed | types of islands | where do islands come from => Islands form from volcanoes (like Hawaii), coral growth (atolls), pieces of continents (like Madagascar), and sand or sediment built up by waves and rivers.
what is a beach made of | where does sand come from | why is some sand black => Most sand is tiny bits of rock, often quartz, worn down by water and weather. Some beaches have white sand from shells and coral, and black sand from volcanic rock.
what is coastal erosion | how do waves shape coastlines | sea arches and stacks => Waves wear away coasts, carving cliffs, caves, arches and stacks. Soft rock erodes faster than hard rock, making bays and headlands.
what is sea foam | why is sea foam made | what makes ocean foam => Sea foam forms when waves churn up water containing dissolved material from algae and plants, which traps air in bubbles like soap.
why does the ocean smell | what is the sea smell | beach smell science => Much of the seaside smell comes from a gas called dimethyl sulfide, made when bacteria break down chemicals from tiny algae.
what is oceanography | what does an oceanographer do | marine biologist vs oceanographer => Oceanography is the science of the ocean: its water, currents, seafloor and life. A marine biologist focuses on the living things.
what is a submersible | how do submarines go deep | alvin submersible => A submersible is a small underwater vehicle built to handle huge pressure. Alvin, first used in 1964, has made thousands of dives and helped discover hydrothermal vents in 1977.
what is sonar | how is the ocean floor mapped | echo sounding => Sonar sends out sound pulses and times their echoes off the seafloor. Ships use multibeam sonar to map the ocean floor in strips.
what is the ocean twilight zone | mesopelagic zone | animals in the twilight zone => The twilight zone, from about 200 to 1,000 meters deep, is dimly lit. Many animals there swim up to the surface at night to feed and go back down by day, the biggest migration on Earth.
what is a dead zone in the ocean | what causes dead zones | gulf of mexico dead zone => A dead zone is an area with too little oxygen for most sea life. Fertilizer runoff feeds huge algae blooms, and when they die, bacteria breaking them down use up the oxygen.
what is an algal bloom | what is a red tide | harmful algae => An algal bloom is when algae grow out of control. Some, called red tides, make toxins that can kill fish and make shellfish unsafe to eat.
how does plastic harm the ocean | what are microplastics | ocean plastic pollution => Plastic can tangle and choke animals, and it breaks into microplastics, pieces smaller than 5 mm, that are now found from the surface to the deepest trenches.
what is overfishing | why is overfishing bad | sustainable fishing => Overfishing is catching fish faster than they can reproduce. It can collapse fish populations, like Atlantic cod off Canada in 1992. Sustainable fishing limits catches to let stocks recover.
what is a marine protected area | ocean reserves | how do we protect the ocean => Marine protected areas limit fishing and other activities so sea life can recover. Reducing plastic, pollution and carbon emissions also helps protect the ocean.
how much of earth is covered by ocean | ocean percentage of earth | how much ocean is there => The ocean covers about 71% of Earth's surface and holds about 97% of its water.
what is sea level | how is sea level measured | why is it called sea level => Sea level is the average height of the ocean surface between high and low tide. Heights of land, like mountains, are measured from it.
what is a strait | what is a gulf | what is a bay => A strait is a narrow waterway joining two seas, like the Strait of Gibraltar. A bay is a curve in a coastline, and a gulf is a large bay, like the Gulf of Mexico.
