# tag: science.physics.everyday
why dont we feel the earth spinning | if the earth spins why dont we fly off | why cant we feel earth moving => We spin along with Earth at a steady speed, and you only feel changes in speed or direction, like a smooth plane ride. Gravity is far stronger than the tiny outward push from the spin.
how fast is the earth spinning | how fast are we moving right now | speed of earths rotation => At the equator, Earth's surface spins at about 1,670 km/h. Earth also orbits the Sun at about 107,000 km/h, and the Sun moves around the galaxy at about 800,000 km/h.
why is ice slippery | what makes ice slippery => Ice is slippery mainly because its surface has a very thin, water-like layer even below freezing. Friction heat from skates adds a little more. Scientists still study the details.
why can you see your breath in winter | foggy breath => Your breath is warm and full of water vapor. In cold air, the vapor cools quickly and condenses into tiny droplets, making a little cloud.
why do windows fog up | why does a mirror fog up after a shower | condensation on windows => Warm, moist air touches the cold glass, cools below its dew point, and the water vapor condenses into tiny droplets on the surface.
why does a car get so hot in the sun | why do cars heat up | hot car greenhouse effect => Sunlight passes through the windows and heats the seats and dashboard, which give off infrared heat that can't easily escape through glass. A closed car can get over 20 °C hotter than outside within an hour, so never leave kids or pets inside.
why does a balloon pop | why do balloons pop when you poke them | balloon popping physics => A blown-up balloon's rubber is stretched tight. A sharp point starts a small tear, and the stretched rubber pulls the tear open almost instantly, releasing the air with a bang.
why does a kettle whistle | how does a whistling kettle work | why does boiling water sing => Steam rushes through two small holes in the whistle. The airflow makes swirling eddies that vibrate at a certain pitch, making the whistle. Before boiling, collapsing bubbles make a rumbling sound.
why does a bike stay up when moving | why cant a bike stand still | bicycle balance physics => A moving bike stays up mostly because you constantly steer slightly into any lean, and the bike's front fork design helps it steer itself. Spinning wheels help a little.
why do you lean when you turn on a bike | why do motorbikes lean in corners | leaning into a turn => Leaning lets gravity and the road's grip work together to provide the sideways force you need to turn, so you don't tip over outward.
why do you feel pushed back when a car speeds up | why do you lurch forward when a car stops | inertia in a car => Your body tends to keep moving the way it was going, which is inertia. When the car speeds up, the seat pushes you forward. When it brakes, you keep moving until the seatbelt stops you.
why do seatbelts save lives | how do seatbelts work | why wear a seatbelt => In a crash, your body keeps moving at the car's speed. A seatbelt stops you with the car and spreads the force over strong parts of your body, instead of you hitting the windshield.
how do airbags work | what makes an airbag inflate | airbag chemistry => Crash sensors trigger a chemical reaction that makes nitrogen gas within about 30 milliseconds, filling the bag. It cushions you by slowing you down over a longer time.
why does a spinning top stay up | how does a gyroscope work | why do spinning things stay balanced => A spinning top has angular momentum, which resists changes to the direction of its spin axis. As friction slows it, it wobbles and falls.
why does a figure skater spin faster | ice skater spin physics | conservation of angular momentum => When a skater pulls their arms in, they spin faster because angular momentum is conserved. Spreading the arms slows the spin down.
why does a ball bounce | why do some balls bounce higher | what makes a ball bouncy => A ball squashes when it hits the ground, storing energy like a spring, then springs back. Bouncy balls lose little energy as heat. Others, like clay, lose most and barely bounce.
why are golf balls bumpy | golf ball dimples physics => Dimples make a thin layer of turbulent air cling to the ball, which shrinks the drag-causing wake behind it. A dimpled ball can fly about twice as far as a smooth one.
why does a boomerang come back | how does a boomerang work | boomerang physics => A boomerang's arms are shaped like wings. As it spins, the top arm moves faster through the air and gets more lift, which tilts it so it curves around in a circle.
why does a frisbee fly | how does a frisbee stay in the air | frisbee physics => A frisbee's curved top creates lift like a wing as it moves forward, and its spin keeps it stable so it doesn't flip over.
why does a paper plane fly | how do paper airplanes work | paper airplane physics => You give it thrust when you throw it. Its wings make lift by pushing air down, gravity pulls it down, and drag slows it. A good design glides slowly and steadily.
why do we have static shocks in winter | why do i get shocked touching a door | static shock doorknob => In dry winter air, static charge builds up on you as you walk, especially on carpet. Touching a metal doorknob lets it jump across in a little spark.
why do clothes stick together from the dryer | static cling | how do dryer sheets work => Clothes rubbing in a dryer swap electrons and become charged, so they cling. Dryer sheets coat fabric with a thin layer that reduces this charge.
why does a spoon in hot tea get hot | why do metal spoons get hot | heat conduction spoon => Metal is a good conductor, so heat travels quickly from the hot tea up the spoon's handle. A wooden spoon stays cooler because wood is a poor conductor.
why does a pot handle not get hot | why are pan handles plastic | insulated handles => Handles are made of plastic or wood, which are poor heat conductors, so heat from the pan doesn't travel to your hand quickly.
why does popcorn need oil | why does oil get hotter than water | why can oil fry food => Water can't get hotter than 100 °C at sea level because it boils, but oil can heat to about 180 °C or more before smoking. That higher heat makes food crispy and brown.
why does food cook faster in a microwave | microwave oven physics => Microwaves make water molecules in the food twist back and forth billions of times a second, which heats the food from inside. Food without much water heats slowly.
why cant you put metal in a microwave | why does foil spark in the microwave | metal in microwave => Microwaves push electrons around in metal, and sharp edges and thin foil can build up charge and spark, which can start a fire. Some smooth, thick metal is fine, but it's best avoided.
why do pipes freeze | frozen pipes physics => Water expands by about 9% when it freezes. In a closed pipe, that expansion builds huge pressure that can split metal or plastic pipes.
why do bridges and sidewalks have gaps | why do railway tracks have gaps => Materials expand when hot and shrink when cold. Gaps called expansion joints give them room, so they don't crack or buckle on hot days.
why do power lines sag in summer | why are wires loose on hot days | thermal expansion power lines => Metal wires expand when they're hot, so they get slightly longer and sag more on hot summer days.
why does a straw look broken in a glass | why does water bend light | why do legs look short in a pool => Light bends when it passes from water into air, called refraction. Your brain assumes light travels in straight lines, so things underwater look bent or closer to the surface.
why does a pool look shallower than it is | how deep is the pool really | refraction depth => Refraction bends light from the bottom as it leaves the water, making the bottom look higher. A pool can look about a quarter shallower than it really is.
why are mirrors reversed left to right | why is writing backwards in a mirror | mirror reversal => A mirror actually reverses front to back, not left to right. Writing looks backwards because you turn the paper around to face the mirror.
why does the road look wet on a hot day | what is a mirage | heat shimmer => On hot days, air just above the road is much hotter than the air above it. Light from the sky bends upward through it, so you see a reflection of the sky that looks like water.
why do we see rainbows in bubbles | why are soap bubbles colorful | oil slick rainbow => Light reflects off the inner and outer surfaces of the thin soap film. The reflections interfere, boosting some colors and canceling others, and the colors shift as the film's thickness changes.
why are bubbles always round | why are bubbles spheres | bubble shape physics => Surface tension pulls the soap film into the shape with the smallest surface area for the air inside, which is a sphere.
why does a seashell sound like the ocean | can you hear the sea in a shell | shell ocean sound => The shell's hollow inside picks up and resonates with background noise around you, making a whooshing sound like waves. A cup over your ear does the same thing.
why does music sound muffled through a wall | why do you hear bass through walls | low sounds travel through walls => Walls block high-pitched sounds more than low ones, because long low-pitched waves pass through and around solid things more easily. That's why you hear a neighbor's bass.
why does a siren change pitch as it passes | ambulance sound changes | doppler effect siren => As the ambulance comes toward you, its sound waves bunch up, giving a higher pitch. As it moves away, they spread out and the pitch drops. This is the Doppler effect.
why does your voice echo in empty rooms | why do empty rooms echo | what makes an echo => Sound bounces off hard walls and floors. Furniture, carpets and curtains absorb sound, so an empty room has more echoes.
why do hot air balloons rise | why does hot air rise | does heat rise => Hot air expands and becomes less dense than the cooler air around it, so it's pushed upward, just like a cork in water. Strictly, it's hot air that rises, not heat.
why does a helium balloon float away | why do helium balloons rise | do helium balloons go to space => Helium is much lighter than air, so the air pushes the balloon up. It doesn't reach space: it rises until the air is thin enough to balance it, or until the balloon expands and pops.
why does a helium balloon deflate | why do helium balloons shrink overnight | helium leaking => Helium atoms are tiny and slowly slip through the rubber of a latex balloon. Foil balloons hold helium much longer because the metal coating blocks it.
why can a ship made of steel float | how do heavy ships float | steel boats float => A ship's hollow shape pushes aside a huge amount of water. If the water it pushes aside weighs more than the ship, the ship floats, even if it's made of steel.
why is it easier to float in the sea | why do you float better in salt water | floating in the ocean => Salt water is denser than fresh water, so it pushes up on you a bit harder, making floating easier.
why do your ears hurt underwater | water pressure on ears | why does diving hurt ears => Water pressure rises as you dive, pressing on your eardrums. Swallowing or gently blowing with your nose pinched lets air into your middle ear to balance the pressure.
why does a drink can crush when cooled | can crush experiment | air pressure can crush => Heat a little water in a can until it steams, then flip it into cold water (with an adult). The steam condenses, leaving low pressure inside, and air pressure outside crushes the can.
why does a suction cup stick | how do suction cups work | suction cup physics => Pressing a suction cup pushes air out. When it springs back, the space inside has lower pressure, and the higher air pressure outside holds it to the surface.
how does a straw work in space | can you drink from a straw on the moon | straw air pressure => Straws work because air pressure pushes the drink up when you lower the pressure in your mouth. On the airless Moon, a straw wouldn't work.
why is it hard to open a jar | how to open a tight jar lid | jar lid physics => Lids can stick because of a vacuum inside from cooling and because of friction. Tapping the lid's edge or running it under hot water expands the metal lid and helps loosen it.
why does a heavy box get easier to push once moving | static vs kinetic friction | why is starting harder => Static friction, which stops things starting to move, is usually stronger than kinetic friction, which acts once they slide. So it takes more force to start pushing than to keep pushing.
why do tires have treads | why are tyres grooved | tire tread physics => Treads channel water out from under the tire so the rubber can grip the road. Without them, a car could slide on a layer of water, called aquaplaning.
why do runners lean forward | why do sprinters crouch at the start | sprint physics => Leaning forward lets sprinters push back against the ground more powerfully, and their bodies speed up faster without tipping backward.
why do cats and kids fall without getting hurt | why do small things survive falls | air resistance small animals => Small, light things have lots of surface for their weight, so air resistance slows them more. A mouse can survive falls that would badly hurt a horse.
can you survive a falling elevator by jumping | jump in a falling elevator | elevator falling physics => No. Jumping only lowers your speed by a few kilometers an hour, which wouldn't save you. Real elevators have emergency brakes and multiple cables, so free falls are extremely rare.
ear pressure flying | airplane ear pain => Cabin pressure changes as the plane climbs and descends. Air trapped in your middle ear pushes on your eardrum until swallowing or yawning opens a tube that lets it balance.
why does a whip crack | how does a whip make noise | whip sound barrier => The tip of a whip moves faster than the speed of sound, making a tiny sonic boom. It was one of the first human-made things to break the sound barrier.
why does thunder rumble | why does thunder last so long | thunder sound physics => A lightning bolt is kilometers long, so sound from different parts reaches you at different times, and it echoes off hills and clouds, making a long rumble.
why does a microwave plate spin | why do microwaves have turntables | hot and cold spots microwave => Microwaves inside the oven form a pattern of hot and cold spots. The turntable moves food through them so it heats more evenly.
why does metal feel colder than plastic at the same temperature | why does tile feel cold on bare feet | cold floor feels => Metal and tile conduct heat away from your skin much faster than plastic or carpet, so they feel colder even though they're the same temperature.
why does stretching a rubber band make it warm | rubber band heat experiment | rubber band temperature => Stretch a rubber band quickly and touch it to your lip: it feels warm. Let it relax and it feels cooler. Stretching lines up its molecules, which releases heat.
why does a cake fall in the middle | why do cakes sink | collapsed cake physics => A cake sinks if gas bubbles escape before the structure sets, often from opening the oven too early, too much raising agent, or underbaking.
why do bubbles in soda rise | why do bubbles form on the side of a glass | nucleation sites => Bubbles form at tiny scratches and specks on the glass, called nucleation sites. Gas is much less dense than liquid, so the bubbles rise.
why does adding salt make water boil faster | salt boiling point => Salt actually raises the boiling point slightly, so it doesn't make water boil faster. The amount used in cooking makes almost no difference.
can you cook pasta on everest | boiling on a mountain => Lower air pressure on mountains lets water boil at a lower temperature, around 70 °C on top of Everest. Food cooks more slowly because the water isn't as hot.
why does your hand stick to a frozen pole | tongue stuck to frozen pole | why does skin stick to ice => Moisture on your skin or tongue freezes instantly against very cold metal, gluing them together. Warm water, not pulling, frees it safely.
