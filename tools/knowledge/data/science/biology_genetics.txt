# tag: science.bio.genetics
what does dna stand for | dna full name | what is deoxyribonucleic acid => DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It's the molecule that carries the genetic instructions for all known living things.
what shape is dna | what is a double helix | why is dna called a double helix => DNA is a double helix, like a twisted ladder. The sides are made of sugar and phosphate, and the rungs are pairs of bases.
who discovered the structure of dna | watson and crick | rosalind franklin dna => James Watson and Francis Crick worked out DNA's double helix in 1953, using X-ray images made by Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins. Franklin's Photo 51 was a key clue.
what are the four bases of dna | dna letters | a t c g => DNA's four bases are adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C) and guanine (G). Their order spells out the genetic code.
how do dna bases pair | base pairing rules | what pairs with adenine => In DNA, A always pairs with T, and C always pairs with G. So if one side reads ATTCG, the other reads TAAGC.
what is a nucleotide | what are nucleotides made of | dna building blocks => A nucleotide is a building block of DNA or RNA, made of a sugar, a phosphate group and one base.
what is a gene | what do genes do | gene definition => A gene is a section of DNA with instructions for making a protein or another molecule. Genes affect traits like eye color and blood type.
how many genes do humans have | number of human genes | do humans have more genes than other animals => Humans have about 20,000 protein-coding genes, about the same as a mouse and fewer than some plants, like rice.
what is a chromosome | what are chromosomes made of | chromosome definition => A chromosome is a long DNA molecule wound tightly around proteins, packed so it fits inside the nucleus. Each one holds hundreds to thousands of genes.
23 pairs of chromosomes | how many chromosomes in a human cell => Humans have 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs in most cells. You get one of each pair from each parent.
what are sex chromosomes | x and y chromosomes | what decides if a baby is a boy or girl => The 23rd pair are the sex chromosomes. Typically, XX develops as female and XY as male. The egg always carries an X, so the sperm's X or Y usually decides.
how long is dna in one cell | how much dna is in your body | dna length => The DNA in one human cell would stretch about 2 meters if unwound. All the DNA in your body would stretch to the Sun and back many times.
what is a genome | human genome size | how many base pairs in human dna => A genome is all the DNA of an organism. The human genome has about 3.1 billion base pairs.
what was the human genome project | when was the human genome sequenced | who mapped human dna => The Human Genome Project was an international effort to read all human DNA. It ran from 1990 and announced a nearly complete sequence in 2003. The fully complete sequence came in 2022.
how much dna do humans share | do we share dna with bananas | dna similarity with chimps => All humans share about 99.9% of their DNA. We share about 98 to 99% with chimpanzees, and many basic genes with mice, flies and even bananas.
what is rna | dna vs rna | how is rna different from dna => RNA is a single strand made with the sugar ribose and the base uracil (U) instead of thymine. It carries copies of genes and helps build proteins.
what is mrna | what does messenger rna do | how does mrna work => Messenger RNA (mRNA) is a temporary copy of a gene that carries instructions from the nucleus to the ribosomes, which read it to build a protein.
what is transcription and translation | how do genes make proteins | protein synthesis => In transcription, a gene's DNA is copied into mRNA. In translation, ribosomes read the mRNA three letters at a time and link amino acids to build a protein.
what is a codon | genetic code three letters | how many codons are there => A codon is a set of three bases that codes for one amino acid or a stop signal. There are 64 codons for 20 amino acids, and the code is almost the same in all living things.
how does dna copy itself | dna replication | why does dna replicate => In DNA replication, the double helix unzips and each strand is used as a template to build a new matching strand. Each new DNA molecule keeps one old strand.
how do you extract dna from strawberries | dna extraction experiment | can you see dna at home => Mash strawberries with dish soap, salt and water, filter it, then gently pour cold rubbing alcohol on top with an adult's help. White, stringy DNA collects where the layers meet.
what is heredity | what is inheritance in biology | how are traits passed down => Heredity is the passing of traits from parents to offspring through genes. That's why children often look like their parents.
what is genetics | what does a geneticist do | study of heredity => Genetics is the study of genes, heredity and how traits vary. Geneticists study DNA to understand diseases, evolution and how living things work.
who is the father of genetics | gregor mendel | mendel pea plants => Gregor Mendel, a monk, is called the father of genetics. He studied about 29,000 pea plants in the 1850s and 1860s and discovered the basic rules of inheritance, published in 1866.
what did mendel discover | mendels laws | law of segregation => Mendel found that traits are passed on as units (now called genes) in pairs, one from each parent, and that the pair separates when sex cells form. Some versions hide others.
what is an allele | allele vs gene | alleles meaning => An allele is a version of a gene. For example, a gene for pea color might have a yellow allele and a green allele.
what is dominant and recessive | dominant vs recessive alleles | what is a recessive trait => A dominant allele shows its trait even if only one copy is present. A recessive allele only shows when there are two copies. Dominant is often written with a capital letter, like B.
what is genotype vs phenotype | genotype meaning | phenotype meaning => Genotype is the alleles an organism has, like Bb. Phenotype is the trait you can see or measure, like brown eyes.
what is homozygous and heterozygous | homozygous meaning | heterozygous meaning => Homozygous means both alleles are the same, like BB or bb. Heterozygous means they're different, like Bb.
what is a punnett square | how do you do a punnett square | punnett square example => A Punnett square is a grid that predicts offspring. Put one parent's alleles across the top and the other's down the side, then fill in each box. Bb times Bb gives BB, Bb, Bb and bb.
what is the ratio for bb times bb | 3 to 1 ratio genetics | monohybrid cross => Crossing two heterozygous parents (Bb x Bb) gives a 1:2:1 ratio of BB, Bb and bb, so a 3:1 ratio of dominant to recessive traits.
what is incomplete dominance | codominance | red and white flowers make pink => In incomplete dominance, the traits blend, like red and white snapdragons making pink. In codominance, both show fully, like AB blood type or a cow with red and white patches.
how is blood type inherited | abo blood groups genetics | can two o parents have an a child => Blood type has three alleles: A, B and O. A and B are codominant and O is recessive. Two type O parents normally have only type O children.
are eye colors controlled by one gene | is brown eyes dominant | eye color genetics => Eye color is controlled by several genes, not just one, so it isn't simple dominant and recessive. Brown is generally more common, and two blue-eyed parents can rarely have a brown-eyed child.
what is a sex linked trait | why are boys more often colorblind | x linked inheritance => Sex-linked traits come from genes on the X chromosome. Males have only one X, so a single recessive allele, like for red-green color blindness, shows up more often in males.
what is a pedigree chart | family tree genetics | how to read a pedigree => A pedigree is a family tree showing who has a trait. Squares are males, circles are females, and shaded shapes have the trait.
what are genetic disorders | examples of genetic diseases | inherited diseases => Genetic disorders are caused by changes in genes or chromosomes. Examples include cystic fibrosis, sickle cell disease, hemophilia and Down syndrome.
what causes down syndrome | trisomy 21 | extra chromosome => Down syndrome is caused by having an extra copy of chromosome 21, so 47 chromosomes instead of 46. People with Down syndrome have a wide range of abilities.
what is genetic engineering | gmo meaning | what are genetically modified organisms => Genetic engineering is changing an organism's DNA directly, often by adding a gene from another species. GMOs include crops that resist pests and bacteria that make human insulin.
how is insulin made with bacteria | genetically engineered insulin | recombinant dna => Scientists put the human insulin gene into bacteria or yeast, which then make human insulin. It was first approved in 1982 and is used by millions of people with diabetes.
what is crispr | how does crispr work | gene editing => CRISPR is a gene editing tool that finds a specific DNA sequence and cuts it, so genes can be removed, fixed or changed. Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier won the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for it.
what is cloning | how was dolly the sheep cloned | first cloned animal => Cloning makes a genetically identical copy of an organism. Dolly the sheep, born in 1996 in Scotland, was the first mammal cloned from an adult cell.
are identical twins clones | identical vs fraternal twins | why are identical twins identical => Identical twins come from one fertilized egg that splits in two, so they share almost the same DNA. Fraternal twins come from two eggs and are like regular siblings.
what is dna fingerprinting | who invented dna fingerprinting => DNA fingerprinting compares parts of DNA that vary a lot between people. Alec Jeffreys invented it in 1984, and it's used to solve crimes and test family relationships.
what is epigenetics | can genes be switched on and off | epigenetics explained => Epigenetics is about chemical tags on DNA that switch genes on or off without changing the DNA sequence. Diet, stress and environment can affect some of these tags.
what is selective breeding | artificial selection | examples of selective breeding => Selective breeding is when people choose plants or animals with useful traits to breed. All dog breeds came from wolves this way, and wild cabbage was bred into broccoli, cauliflower and kale.
who came up with natural selection | charles darwin | alfred russel wallace => Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace both came up with natural selection. Their papers were presented together in 1858, and Darwin published On the Origin of Species in 1859.
what did darwin find in the galapagos | darwins finches | galapagos islands evolution => On the Galápagos Islands in 1835, Darwin noticed finches, tortoises and mockingbirds that differed from island to island. Later he realized they had adapted from common ancestors.
what is an adaptation | examples of adaptations | how do animals adapt => An adaptation is an inherited trait that helps a living thing survive in its environment, like a cactus's spines, a polar bear's thick fur or a giraffe's long neck.
what is the peppered moth | peppered moth evolution | industrial melanism => In England in the 1800s, soot darkened trees, and dark peppered moths became more common because birds spotted the light ones. When air got cleaner, light moths came back.
what is the evidence for evolution | how do we know evolution happened | proof of evolution => Evidence includes fossils showing changes over time, similar bone structures in different animals, DNA similarities, and evolution seen happening today, like antibiotic resistance.
what are homologous structures | similar bones in different animals | arm bones in whales and bats => Homologous structures are body parts with the same basic bones but different uses, like a human arm, bat wing, whale flipper and cat leg. They point to a common ancestor.
what are vestigial structures | vestigial organs examples | whale hip bones => Vestigial structures are leftovers that have lost most of their original use, like tiny hip bones in whales and wings on flightless birds.
what is a transitional fossil | tiktaalik | archaeopteryx => Transitional fossils show in-between forms. Tiktaalik, about 375 million years old, was a fish with leg-like fins. Archaeopteryx, about 150 million years old, had feathers and teeth, part dinosaur and part bird.
what is speciation | how do new species form => A species is a group that can breed and have fertile offspring. New species form when groups get separated, by mountains, water or behavior, and change until they can't interbreed.
do humans come from monkeys | did humans evolve from chimps => Humans didn't evolve from living monkeys or chimps. We share a common ancestor with chimpanzees that lived roughly 6 to 7 million years ago, and both lines changed since then.
what is antibiotic resistance | how do bacteria become resistant | superbugs => When antibiotics are used, any bacteria with mutations that let them survive multiply. Over time, the drugs stop working. Finishing prescribed courses and not overusing antibiotics helps.
what is convergent evolution | why do sharks and dolphins look alike | similar but unrelated animals => Convergent evolution is when unrelated living things evolve similar features because they face similar challenges. Sharks (fish) and dolphins (mammals) both evolved streamlined bodies and fins.
what is coevolution | flowers and bees evolution | coevolution examples => Coevolution is when two species evolve in response to each other, like flowers and the insects that pollinate them, or predators getting faster as prey get faster.
how old is the earth evolution | how long has evolution taken | timeline of life => Life has been evolving for at least 3.5 billion years. Simple cells came first, complex cells about 2 billion years ago, animals around 600 million years ago, and modern humans about 300,000 years ago.
what is taxonomy | what is classification in biology | why do we classify living things => Taxonomy is the science of naming and classifying living things into groups based on shared features and ancestry. It helps scientists study and talk about millions of species.
who invented the classification system | carl linnaeus | father of taxonomy => Carl Linnaeus, a Swedish scientist, created the modern system of naming and grouping living things in the 1750s. He's called the father of taxonomy.
what is binomial nomenclature | what is a scientific name | how do you write a scientific name => Binomial nomenclature gives each species a two-part Latin name: genus then species, like Homo sapiens. The genus starts with a capital letter, and the whole name is written in italics.
what are the levels of classification | kingdom phylum class order family genus species | taxonomy ranks => The main ranks, from biggest to smallest, are domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species.
how do you remember the classification order | mnemonic for taxonomy | dear king philip came over => Try Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species.
what are the three domains of life | bacteria archaea eukarya | three domains => All life is grouped into three domains: Bacteria, Archaea (tough single cells often found in extreme places) and Eukarya (everything with a nucleus, including plants, animals and fungi).
who proposed the three domains | carl woese | what are archaea => Carl Woese proposed the three domains in 1990 after studying RNA. Archaea look like bacteria but are a separate group, and many live in extreme places like hot springs.
what are the kingdoms of life | six kingdoms | how many kingdoms are there => A common system uses six kingdoms: Animals, Plants, Fungi, Protists, Archaea and Bacteria. Some schools teach five, putting Archaea and Bacteria together as Monera.
what is the kingdom fungi | why are fungi not plants => Fungi, like mushrooms, molds and yeast, are their own kingdom. They can't make food by photosynthesis. They absorb food from what they grow on, and their cell walls are made of chitin.
are fungi closer to animals or plants | fungi related to animals | fungi evolution => Surprisingly, fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants, according to DNA evidence.
what are protists | examples of protists | is algae a protist => Protists are mostly single-celled eukaryotes that aren't plants, animals or fungi. Examples include amoebas, paramecia and many algae. Kelp is a giant protist.
how many species are there on earth | number of species | how many species are undiscovered => About 2 million species have been named, but scientists estimate there may be around 8 to 10 million species of plants, animals and fungi, plus many more microbes.
what is a dichotomous key | how to use a dichotomous key | identification key => A dichotomous key identifies living things by asking a series of either-or questions, like has wings or no wings, until you reach the name.
what is a vertebrate vs invertebrate | what percentage of animals are invertebrates | backbone or no backbone => Vertebrates have a backbone, like fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals. Invertebrates don't, like insects, worms and jellyfish, and they make up about 97% of animal species.
what are the main groups of plants | types of plants classification | mosses ferns conifers flowering plants => Main plant groups are mosses (no veins or seeds), ferns (veins but spores, no seeds), conifers (seeds in cones) and flowering plants (seeds in fruits).
what is a hybrid animal | can different species have babies => A hybrid is the offspring of two different species. A mule is a cross of a female horse and male donkey, and mules are almost always unable to have babies of their own.
what is a phylogenetic tree | tree of life | cladogram => A phylogenetic tree, or cladogram, is a branching diagram showing how species are related through common ancestors, based on traits and DNA.
