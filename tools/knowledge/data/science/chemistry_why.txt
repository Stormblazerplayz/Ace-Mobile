why do apples turn brown when cut | why do sliced apples go brown => Cutting an apple lets oxygen reach an enzyme called polyphenol oxidase, which turns chemicals in the fruit brown. Lemon juice slows it because its acid and vitamin C block the reaction.
why do bananas get spots | banana ripening chemistry => As bananas ripen, starch turns into sugar and enzymes break down the green pigment. Brown spots form as enzymes react with oxygen. Cold makes the peel brown faster, though the inside stays fine.
what makes bread stale | why does bread get hard => Bread goes stale mainly because its starch slowly recrystallizes and pushes out water, not just because it dries out. Warming stale bread in an oven reverses this for a while.
what does baking soda do in cakes | why use baking powder => Baking soda is a base that releases carbon dioxide when it meets an acid like buttermilk or lemon juice. Baking powder is baking soda already mixed with a dry acid, so it only needs water and heat.
why does gum make water feel cold | menthol cooling => Mint contains menthol, which triggers your cold-sensing nerves, so your mouth feels cool even though its temperature hasn't changed.
toothpaste and orange juice | why does toothpaste change taste => Most toothpaste contains a foaming agent called sodium lauryl sulfate. It temporarily dulls your sweet taste and makes bitter flavors stronger, so juice tastes awful.
why does shaking soda make it explode | why does shaken soda spray | shaking a soda bottle => Shaking makes lots of tiny bubbles in the soda. When you open it, the gas rushes into these bubbles, which grow fast and push the soda out.
mentos and coke | diet coke and mentos experiment => Mentos have millions of tiny pits on their surface, which give carbon dioxide lots of places to form bubbles all at once. It's a physical reaction, not a chemical one.
why does salt melt ice | how does salt melt snow on roads | why do we put salt on icy roads => Salt dissolves in the thin layer of water on ice and lowers the freezing point. The salty water can stay liquid below 0 °C, so ice melts. It stops working well below about minus 10 °C.
why does salt make ice colder | how does ice cream in a bag work | salt and ice freezing => When salt makes ice melt, melting soaks up heat from its surroundings. That can drop the temperature of a salt and ice mix to around minus 20 °C, cold enough to freeze ice cream.
why does sugar dissolve faster in hot water | hot vs cold water dissolving | dissolving speed temperature => In hot water, molecules move faster, bumping into the sugar more often and pulling it apart more quickly. Hot water can also dissolve much more sugar in total.
why does soap kill viruses | does hand washing kill coronavirus | soap vs virus => Many viruses, including flu and coronaviruses, have a fatty outer envelope. Soap pulls this layer apart, breaking the virus. Scrubbing for at least 20 seconds gives soap time to work.
does hand sanitizer kill germs | alcohol hand gel => Hand sanitizers with at least 60% alcohol kill many germs by breaking down their proteins and outer layers. Soap and water work better on dirty hands and some germs.
how does toothpaste protect teeth | what does fluoride do => Fluoride helps rebuild tooth enamel and makes it harder for acids from bacteria to dissolve. It turns some enamel into fluorapatite, which resists acid better.
why do teeth get cavities | what causes tooth decay | how do cavities form => Bacteria in your mouth eat sugar and make acid. The acid dissolves tooth enamel little by little, and over time a hole forms, called a cavity.
why does a battery die | what happens inside a battery | how does a battery make electricity => A battery has two different materials that react through a chemical in between, pushing electrons through your device. When the chemicals are used up, the reaction stops and the battery is dead.
how do rechargeable batteries work | why can some batteries recharge | lithium ion battery => In rechargeable batteries, charging pushes the chemical reaction backward, storing energy again. Lithium-ion batteries move lithium ions back and forth between two electrodes.
why do batteries swell | why do phone batteries puff up => Old or damaged lithium-ion batteries can produce gas inside, making them puff up. A swollen battery can catch fire, so it should be safely recycled, not used or punctured.
how do you stop iron from rusting | ways to prevent rust | galvanizing => Rust is stopped by keeping out water and oxygen with paint, oil or plastic, or by galvanizing, coating iron with zinc. Zinc corrodes first, protecting the iron.
why do copper roofs turn green | copper patina => Copper slowly reacts with air, water and carbon dioxide, forming a green coating called patina. The Statue of Liberty turned green over about 30 years and the patina protects the copper beneath.
how to clean tarnished silver | black silver => Silver reacts with sulfur compounds in the air, forming a dark layer of silver sulfide. Aluminum foil, baking soda and hot water can reverse it chemically.
why do glow in the dark stickers glow | phosphorescence => Glow-in-the-dark materials absorb light energy and slowly release it as a soft glow, called phosphorescence. Most modern ones use strontium aluminate, and they're not radioactive.
what is inside a hand warmer | chemical heat pack => Disposable hand warmers contain iron powder that rusts quickly when exposed to air, releasing heat. Salt and charcoal help speed it up.
how do instant ice packs work | endothermic cold pack => Squeezing an instant cold pack breaks an inner pouch of water, which dissolves a salt like ammonium nitrate. Dissolving it absorbs heat, so the pack gets cold.
why does nail polish remover smell | what is in nail polish remover | acetone smell => Most nail polish removers contain acetone, a solvent that dissolves the polish. It evaporates very quickly, so you smell it right away.
why do markers dry out | why do felt tip pens dry up | how to save dried markers => Marker ink is pigment or dye in a solvent that evaporates. Leaving the cap off lets the solvent escape, so the tip dries. A few drops of water can revive water-based markers.
why does milk go sour | what makes milk spoil | why does milk curdle => Bacteria in milk eat its sugar, lactose, and make lactic acid. The acid makes milk taste sour and causes its proteins to clump into curds.
what bacteria make yogurt | yogurt chemistry => Yogurt is made by adding bacteria such as Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus to warm milk. They turn lactose into lactic acid, which thickens the milk and gives it a tangy taste.
how does jelly set | what makes jelly wobbly => Gelatin is made of long protein chains. In hot water they unwind, and as it cools they tangle into a net that traps water, making a wobbly gel.
why does fresh pineapple ruin jello | pineapple and gelatin | kiwi in jelly => Fresh pineapple and kiwi contain enzymes that cut up gelatin's proteins, so the jelly won't set. Canned pineapple works because heating destroys the enzyme.
why do eggs go hard when cooked | what happens to egg white when cooked | protein denaturing => Heat makes egg proteins unfold and then link up with each other, a change called denaturing. The runny clear white becomes a solid white, and it can't change back.
why does a boiled egg get a green ring | overcooked egg => Overcooking makes sulfur from the white react with iron in the yolk, forming a greenish layer of iron sulfide. It's harmless, and cooling the egg quickly helps prevent it.
why does salt bring out flavor | salt and bitterness => Salt boosts many flavors and also blocks bitter tastes, which makes other flavors seem stronger and sweeter.
why do cut flowers last longer with sugar | flower food | how to keep flowers fresh => Flower food usually has sugar for energy, an acid to help water flow up the stem, and a bit of bleach to stop bacteria clogging the stem.
why shouldnt you put water on a grease fire | oil fire water => Water sinks under burning oil and flashes into steam, which throws burning oil everywhere. Turn off the heat and cover the pan with a lid to cut off the oxygen.
why is smoke bad for you | what is in smoke | wood smoke chemistry => Smoke contains tiny particles and gases like carbon monoxide that irritate your lungs and can get deep inside them. That's why smoke inhalation is so dangerous in fires.
why does gas smell like rotten eggs | mercaptan => Natural gas has no smell, so companies add a strong-smelling chemical, usually a mercaptan, so leaks can be noticed. If you smell it, leave and call for help.
what makes a skunk smell | skunk spray chemistry | how to get rid of skunk smell => Skunk spray contains sulfur chemicals called thiols, which people can smell in tiny amounts. A mix of hydrogen peroxide, baking soda and dish soap breaks them down better than tomato juice.
what makes garlic smelly | allicin => Crushing garlic triggers an enzyme that makes allicin, which gives fresh garlic its sharp smell and breaks down into other sulfur compounds.
why do some plastics get brittle in the sun | uv damage plastic | why does plastic fade outside => Ultraviolet light from the Sun breaks the long chains in many plastics, making them faded, weak and brittle. Additives called UV stabilizers slow this down.
why is plastic so hard to break down | how long does plastic take to decompose | plastic in landfills => Most plastics are long chains of carbon atoms that very few microbes can digest. Many last hundreds of years, slowly breaking into smaller pieces called microplastics.
how is paper recycled | paper recycling process | how many times can paper be recycled => Used paper is soaked into a pulp, cleaned, de-inked and pressed into new sheets. Paper fibers get shorter each time, so paper can be recycled about 5 to 7 times.
how is glass recycled | can glass be recycled forever | glass recycling => Glass is crushed, melted and reshaped into new glass. It can be recycled again and again without losing quality.
how is aluminium recycled | why recycle cans | aluminium can recycling energy => Aluminium cans are shredded, melted and rolled into new metal. Recycling uses about 95% less energy than making aluminium from ore, and the metal can be recycled forever.
why does red cabbage change color | red cabbage indicator | cabbage juice ph => Red cabbage contains pigments called anthocyanins, which change color with pH. Its juice turns pink or red in acids, purple when neutral, and green or yellow in bases.
why do hydrangeas change color | blue and pink hydrangeas | soil ph flowers => Some hydrangeas are blue in acidic soil, because they can take up aluminium, and pink in alkaline soil, where they can't.
why does lemon juice make invisible ink | invisible ink experiment | how does lemon juice ink work => Lemon juice is a weak acid that dries clear. Heating the paper makes the juice brown faster than the paper, revealing the message.
what is inside a lava lamp | lava lamp science => A lava lamp has blobs of wax in a liquid. The bulb heats the wax so it expands and rises, then it cools at the top, gets denser, and sinks again.
why does slime stretch | how does slime work | glue and borax slime => Glue contains long polymer chains. Borax or contact lens solution links these chains loosely together, making a stretchy, gooey substance that flows slowly.
what is oobleck | cornstarch and water | why does oobleck get hard when you hit it => Oobleck is cornstarch mixed with water. It flows like a liquid when handled gently, but turns firm when you squeeze or punch it, because the starch grains jam together.
how do pop rocks work | why do pop rocks pop | popping candy => Pop Rocks are made by mixing hot candy with pressurized carbon dioxide. When the candy melts in your mouth, the trapped gas bubbles burst with a crackle.
how does cotton candy work | how is cotton candy made | candy floss science => A cotton candy machine melts sugar and spins it through tiny holes. The thin streams cool instantly into glassy threads of sugar.
why does honey crystallize | is crystallized honey bad | how to fix crystallized honey => Honey is a supersaturated sugar solution, so glucose slowly comes out as crystals. It's still good to eat, and gently warming it in water makes it smooth again.
why is honey good for a long time | why doesnt honey spoil => Honey has very little water and is acidic, so bacteria and molds can't grow in it. Sealed honey can last for many years, and edible honey has been found in ancient Egyptian tombs.
