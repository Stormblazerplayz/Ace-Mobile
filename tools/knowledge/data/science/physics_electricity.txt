what is electric current | what is current in electricity | what does current mean => Electric current is the flow of electric charge, usually electrons moving through a wire. It's measured in amperes, or amps (A).
what is voltage | what does voltage do | voltage explained simply => Voltage is the push that drives current around a circuit, a bit like water pressure in a pipe. It's measured in volts (V).
what is resistance | what does resistance mean in electricity | electrical resistance explained => Resistance is how much something opposes the flow of current. It's measured in ohms (Ω). Thin, long or hot wires have more resistance.
what is ohms law | ohms law formula | v equals ir => Ohm's law says voltage = current x resistance (V = IR). So a 6 volt battery across a 3 ohm resistor pushes 2 amps.
what is an amp | what is an ampere | how much is one amp => An ampere (amp) is the unit of electric current. One amp means about 6 quintillion electrons flowing past a point every second.
what is an ohm | ohm unit | who was ohm => The ohm (Ω) is the unit of resistance, named after Georg Ohm, a German physicist who discovered Ohm's law in 1827.
what is electric charge | positive and negative charge | what is charge in physics => Charge is a property of particles. Protons are positive, electrons are negative, and neutrons have no charge. Opposite charges attract and like charges repel.
what is a coulomb | unit of electric charge | how much charge is a coulomb => The coulomb (C) is the unit of electric charge. One amp is one coulomb flowing past a point each second.
what is a series circuit | how does a series circuit work | series circuit facts => In a series circuit, parts are connected in one loop. The same current goes through everything, and if one bulb breaks, they all go off.
what is a parallel circuit | how does a parallel circuit work | parallel circuit facts => In a parallel circuit, parts are on separate branches. Each branch gets the full voltage, and if one bulb breaks, the others stay on.
series vs parallel circuit | difference between series and parallel | which is better series or parallel => In series there's one path, so one break stops everything. In parallel there are several paths, so each part works on its own. Houses are wired in parallel.
why are houses wired in parallel | why are home circuits parallel | house wiring circuit type => Parallel wiring gives every outlet and light the full voltage, and lets you switch each one on and off without affecting the others.
what are circuit symbols | how do you draw a circuit diagram | circuit diagram symbols => Circuit diagrams use symbols: a long and short line for a cell, a circle with a cross for a bulb, a gap with a lever for a switch, and a rectangle for a resistor.
what is a cell vs a battery | is a cell the same as a battery | difference between cell and battery => A cell is a single unit that makes electricity from chemicals. A battery is two or more cells joined together, though people call single cells batteries too.
how does a battery work | what happens inside a battery | how do batteries make electricity => A battery has two different materials (electrodes) in a chemical paste or liquid. A chemical reaction pushes electrons out of the negative end, through your circuit and back to the positive end.

what is a switch | how does a switch work | what does a switch do in a circuit => A switch opens or closes a gap in a circuit. When it's closed, current flows. When it's open, the circuit is broken and current stops.
why are short circuits dangerous | what happens in a short circuit => A short circuit is when current finds an easy path with almost no resistance. A huge current flows, which can melt wires and start fires.
what is a fuse | how does a fuse work | why do plugs have fuses => A fuse is a thin wire that melts if too much current flows, breaking the circuit. It protects wires and devices from overheating and fires.
what is a circuit breaker | fuse vs circuit breaker | why did my breaker trip => A circuit breaker is a switch that turns off automatically when too much current flows. Unlike a fuse, you can reset it after fixing the problem.
what is an open circuit | what is a closed circuit | open vs closed circuit => A closed circuit is a complete loop, so current flows. An open circuit has a gap somewhere, so no current flows.
what is an ammeter | how do you measure current | where do you put an ammeter => An ammeter measures current in amps. You connect it in series, in the path of the electric current.
what is a voltmeter | how do you measure voltage | where do you put a voltmeter => A voltmeter measures voltage. You connect it in parallel, across the part you're measuring.
what is a multimeter | what does a multimeter measure | how to use a multimeter => A multimeter measures voltage, current and resistance. You turn the dial to what you want to measure and touch the two probes to the circuit.
what is a resistor | what do resistors do | why do circuits need resistors => A resistor is a part that limits current. For example, an LED needs a resistor so too much current doesn't burn it out.
what is a variable resistor | what is a potentiometer | how does a dimmer switch work => A variable resistor lets you change the resistance, often by turning a knob. Volume knobs and dimmer switches work this way.
what is an led | how does an led work | what does led stand for => LED stands for light emitting diode. It glows when current flows through it in the right direction. LEDs are very efficient and last a long time.
what is a diode | what does a diode do | one way valve electricity => A diode lets current flow in only one direction, like a one-way valve. It's used to turn AC into DC and protect circuits.
what is a capacitor | what does a capacitor do | how do capacitors work => A capacitor stores electric charge for a short time and can release it quickly. Camera flashes use one to make a sudden burst of light.
what is a transistor | what does a transistor do | why are transistors important => A transistor is a tiny electronic switch or amplifier. Computer chips have billions of them, switching on and off to process information.
what is a semiconductor | what are semiconductors used for | is silicon a semiconductor => A semiconductor conducts electricity better than an insulator but worse than a metal, and its conductivity can be controlled. Silicon is the main one, used in chips and solar cells.
what is an ldr | what is a light dependent resistor | how does a light sensor resistor work => An LDR (light dependent resistor) has less resistance in bright light and more in the dark. It's used in night lights that switch on automatically.
what is a thermistor | how does a thermistor work | temperature sensor resistor => A thermistor's resistance changes with temperature. Most types have lower resistance when hot, so they're used in thermostats and digital thermometers.
what is ac and dc | difference between ac and dc | ac vs dc current => DC (direct current) flows one way, like from a battery. AC (alternating current) switches direction many times a second, like the electricity from wall outlets.

A key on the string gave off sparks. It was very dangerous, and others who tried similar experiments were killed.
