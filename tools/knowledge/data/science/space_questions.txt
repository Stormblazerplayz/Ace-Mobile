# tag: science.space.questions
what does space smell like | do astronauts smell space | smell of space => Astronauts say their suits smell like seared steak, hot metal or welding fumes after spacewalks, probably from oxygen atoms clinging to the suits.
how cold is space | what is the temperature of space | is space cold or hot => Deep space is about minus 270 °C, just 2.7 degrees above absolute zero. But in sunlight near Earth, objects can heat to over 120 °C, so spacecraft need heat shields and insulation.
is space completely empty | is there anything between stars => No. Even deep space has a few atoms per cubic meter, plus dust, light, magnetic fields, neutrinos and the faint leftover glow of the Big Bang.
where does space start | how high up is outer space | what height is space => Space is often said to begin at the Kármán line, 100 km above sea level, though the atmosphere fades out gradually beyond that.
why is space black | why is space dark if there are stars | olbers paradox => Space looks black because there's no air to scatter light, and because the universe has a finite age, so light from very distant stars hasn't reached us yet and the universe's expansion stretches it out of sight.
what is outside the universe | is there an end to space => As far as scientists know, the universe has no edge or outside. It may be infinite, or curve back on itself, but there's no wall at the end.
how many stars are there in the universe | how many stars are in the sky => Roughly 10^22 to 10^24 stars exist in the observable universe, likely more than all the grains of sand on Earth's beaches.
number of stars in our galaxy | how big is the milky way => The Milky Way has about 100 to 400 billion stars, and it's about 100,000 light years across.
can we see other galaxies without a telescope | which galaxies can you see with your eyes | naked eye galaxies => Yes. From dark places you can see the Andromeda Galaxy, and from the Southern Hemisphere the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds. Andromeda is the farthest thing most people can see with their eyes.
what are exoplanets | how many exoplanets have been found => Exoplanets are planets orbiting other stars. As of 2025, over 6,000 have been confirmed, the first around a Sun-like star in 1995.
what is proxima b | nearest exoplanet | planet around proxima centauri => Proxima b is a planet about as massive as Earth or a bit more, orbiting in the habitable zone of Proxima Centauri, the nearest star, about 4.2 light years away. It was found in 2016.
seven earth sized planets | trappist planets => TRAPPIST-1 is a small, cool red dwarf star about 40 light years away with seven Earth-sized planets, several in its habitable zone. All seven were announced by 2017.
hot jupiter planets | gas giants close to their stars => A hot Jupiter is a giant gas planet orbiting very close to its star, often in just a few days, so it's scorching hot. They surprised astronomers, since our solar system has nothing like them.
what is a super earth | super earth planet | what is a mini neptune => A super-Earth is a planet bigger than Earth but smaller than Neptune. They're very common around other stars, though our solar system has none.
what is a rogue planet | planets without a star | free floating planets => A rogue planet drifts through space without orbiting a star, probably flung out of its home system. There may be billions of them in the Milky Way.
how long would it take to get to the nearest star | how long to fly to alpha centauri | travel time to proxima centauri => At the speed of the fastest spacecraft so far, it would take thousands of years. Voyager 1, at about 61,000 km/h, would need roughly 75,000 years to travel 4.2 light years.
how long to fly to the moon | travel time to the moon => Apollo astronauts took about 3 days to reach the Moon. The fastest trip, New Horizons, passed the Moon's orbit in about 9 hours, but it didn't stop.
how long to fly to mars | travel time to mars => A trip to Mars takes about 7 to 9 months with the rockets used as of 2025. Launches happen about every 26 months, when Earth and Mars line up well.
how long would it take to drive to the moon | drive to the moon by car | car to the moon => At 100 km/h without stopping, driving the 384,400 km to the Moon would take about 160 days, around 5 months.
how long would it take to drive to the sun | drive a car to the sun | car to the sun => At 100 km/h nonstop, the 150 million km to the Sun would take about 170 years.
how long would it take to walk to the moon | walk to the moon | walking to the moon time => At a steady 5 km/h without stopping, walking 384,400 km would take almost 9 years.
how far have humans traveled from earth | farthest humans have gone | apollo 13 distance record => The Apollo 13 crew set the record in 1970, about 400,171 km from Earth, as they swung around the far side of the Moon.
how many satellites are orbiting earth | number of satellites in space => As of 2025, there are over 11,000 active satellites in orbit, and more than 7,000 of them are SpaceX Starlink satellites.
what is kessler syndrome | can space junk trap us on earth | space debris chain reaction => Kessler syndrome is the idea that collisions between space junk could create more and more debris in a chain reaction, making some orbits too dangerous to use.
how do satellites stay up | why dont satellites fall | how do satellites orbit => Satellites are actually falling around Earth. They move sideways so fast, about 28,000 km/h in low orbit, that as they fall, Earth's surface curves away beneath them.
how fast does the iss travel | how many times a day does the iss orbit | iss speed => The ISS travels about 28,000 km/h and circles Earth about every 90 minutes, so its crew sees around 16 sunrises and sunsets a day.
how much does the iss weigh | size of the space station => The ISS is about 109 meters long, about the size of an American football field, and has a mass of about 420,000 kg.
how do astronauts shower | washing in space => There are no showers on the ISS. Astronauts wipe themselves with wet towels and use no-rinse shampoo, since water would float around in blobs.
do astronauts get taller in space | why do astronauts grow in space => Yes, by up to about 5 cm, because without gravity the discs in their spine expand. They shrink back after returning to Earth.
what is a spacesuit made of | why do astronauts need spacesuits => A spacesuit is like a tiny spacecraft. It provides oxygen and pressure, removes carbon dioxide, controls temperature, and protects from radiation and tiny space rocks. Each has many layers.
how long can astronauts stay in space | record for most time in space => Valeri Polyakov holds the record for a single flight, about 437 days on Mir in 1994 and 1995. As of 2025, Oleg Kononenko holds the record for total time, over 1,100 days.
how do rockets get to space | how fast must a rocket go | how do rockets leave earth => Rockets burn huge amounts of fuel to push hot gas downward, which pushes them up. To reach orbit, they must go about 28,000 km/h sideways, not just straight up.
why are rockets launched near the equator | where are rockets launched from | why launch from florida => Launching near the equator and heading east gives a boost from Earth's spin, which is fastest there, at about 1,670 km/h. That saves fuel.
what is a reusable rocket | how do rockets land => A reusable rocket flies back and lands to fly again, which cuts costs. SpaceX first landed a Falcon 9 first stage in December 2015, and its boosters now land and refly routinely.
spacex starship | biggest rocket ever => Starship is SpaceX's giant fully reusable rocket, about 120 meters tall, the biggest and most powerful ever flown. As of 2025, it's still in test flights, and its booster has been caught by the launch tower.
how hot does a spacecraft get on reentry | why do spacecraft need heat shields | reentry heat => Coming back from orbit at about 28,000 km/h, a spacecraft squeezes the air in front of it so hard that it heats to over 1,500 °C. Heat shields protect it.
what is a gravity assist | slingshot around a planet | how do probes speed up => A gravity assist uses a planet's gravity and motion to speed up or redirect a spacecraft without fuel. Voyager 2 used Jupiter, Saturn and Uranus to reach Neptune.
what is a light second | how far is a light minute | light distance units => A light second is about 300,000 km, the distance light travels in a second. The Moon is about 1.3 light seconds away and the Sun about 8.3 light minutes.
how fast is the moon moving away | moon drifting away => Yes. The Moon is moving away from Earth by about 3.8 cm a year, measured by bouncing lasers off reflectors left by Apollo astronauts.
how often is there a blue moon | once in a blue moon => A blue moon usually means the second full moon in one calendar month, which happens about every 2 to 3 years. The Moon doesn't actually look blue.
why does the moon look bigger sometimes | supermoon distance => A supermoon is a full moon near its closest point to Earth, looking up to about 14% bigger and 30% brighter than when farthest.
why do we have different moon phases | how do moon phases work | moon phases explained => As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of its sunlit half. It goes from new moon to full moon and back in about 29.5 days.
