# tag: science.experiments
what was galileos leaning tower experiment | did galileo drop balls from the tower of pisa | heavy and light objects fall same speed => Galileo showed that heavy and light objects fall at the same rate if air resistance is small. The famous Tower of Pisa drop may be a legend, but he did roll balls down ramps to study falling.
what was the hammer and feather experiment | apollo 15 feather drop => In 1971, Apollo 15 astronaut David Scott dropped a hammer and a falcon feather on the Moon. With no air, they hit the ground at the same time, just as Galileo predicted.
what was eratosthenes experiment | how did eratosthenes measure the earth | first measurement of earth size => Around 240 BC, Eratosthenes compared shadow angles at noon in Syene and Alexandria and the distance between them, and worked out Earth's circumference quite close to the real value.
what was newtons prism experiment | how did newton split light | white light is made of colors => In the 1660s, Isaac Newton split sunlight into a rainbow with a prism, then used a second prism to combine the colors back into white light, proving white light is a mix of colors.
did franklin get struck by lightning | franklin kite 1752 => In 1752, Benjamin Franklin reportedly flew a kite in a storm and drew sparks from a key on the string, showing lightning is electricity. He wasn't struck, and others were killed trying similar experiments.
what was the cavendish experiment | how was the earth weighed | measuring gravity in the lab => In 1798, Henry Cavendish measured the tiny gravitational pull between lead balls using a twisting wire. His result let scientists work out Earth's mass and density.
what was youngs double slit experiment | double slit experiment explained | light is a wave proof => Around 1801, Thomas Young shone light through two narrow slits and saw stripes of light and dark, an interference pattern, proving light behaves like a wave.
what is the weird part of the double slit experiment | double slit with electrons | wave particle duality experiment => When single electrons are fired at two slits one at a time, they still build up a wave pattern, as if each passes through both. Checking which slit they go through makes the pattern disappear.
what was oersteds discovery | compass needle and wire => In 1820, Hans Christian Ørsted noticed a compass needle move near a wire carrying electric current, discovering that electricity and magnetism are linked.
what was faradays induction experiment | how did faraday make electricity with a magnet | electromagnetic induction discovery => In 1831, Michael Faraday showed that moving a magnet near a coil of wire makes an electric current. This electromagnetic induction is how generators make most of our electricity.
what was joules experiment | mechanical equivalent of heat | falling weight paddle wheel => In the 1840s, James Joule used falling weights to spin a paddle in water and measured the water warming slightly, showing that work and heat are forms of the same thing, energy.
what was the michelson morley experiment | did they find the ether | speed of light experiment 1887 => In 1887, Albert Michelson and Edward Morley tried to detect the ether, a substance light was thought to travel through. They found no sign of it, which later helped support Einstein's relativity.
what was the cathode ray experiment | jj thomson experiment => In 1897, J.J. Thomson showed that cathode rays are made of tiny negatively charged particles much lighter than atoms. He had discovered the electron.
what was the oil drop experiment | millikan oil drop | how was the charge of an electron measured => Around 1909, Robert Millikan and Harvey Fletcher balanced tiny charged oil droplets between electric plates and measured the charge of a single electron.
becquerel photographic plate | discovery of radioactivity 1896 => In 1896, Henri Becquerel found that uranium salts fogged photographic plates wrapped in black paper, even in the dark. Marie and Pierre Curie went on to study this radioactivity.
wilhelm rontgen | first x ray picture => In 1895, Wilhelm Röntgen noticed a screen glowing near a covered tube and discovered X-rays. One of his first X-ray pictures showed the bones of his wife's hand and her ring.
what was the pavlov dog experiment | classical conditioning | pavlov bell => Ivan Pavlov noticed dogs drooling before food arrived. By pairing a signal with feeding, he showed dogs learn to drool at the signal alone. This is called classical conditioning.
what was the miller urey experiment | origin of life experiment | making amino acids from gases => In 1952, Stanley Miller and Harold Urey sparked a mix of gases and water to imitate early Earth and lightning. Within days, amino acids, the building blocks of proteins, formed.
what was the meselson stahl experiment | how dna copies itself proof | semiconservative replication => In 1958, Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl used heavy and light nitrogen to show that when DNA copies itself, each new molecule keeps one old strand and one new one.
what was the hershey chase experiment | how do we know dna is genetic material | dna not protein => In 1952, Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase used viruses with labeled parts to show that DNA, not protein, enters bacteria and carries the genetic instructions.
what was griffiths experiment | transforming principle | griffith mice experiment => In 1928, Frederick Griffith found that harmless bacteria could become deadly after mixing with dead deadly bacteria. Something passed between them, later shown to be DNA.
what was jenners smallpox experiment | first vaccine | cowpox and smallpox => In 1796, Edward Jenner infected a boy with cowpox and showed he was then protected from smallpox. This led to the first vaccine, and smallpox was declared wiped out in 1980.
what was van helmonts tree experiment | willow tree experiment | where does plant mass come from experiment => In the 1600s, Jan van Helmont grew a willow tree for five years. It gained about 74 kg, but the soil lost only about 60 grams, so he concluded its mass came from water. We now know carbon dioxide matters too.
what was priestleys mouse experiment | joseph priestley oxygen | plant and mouse in a jar => In 1771, Joseph Priestley found that a mint plant could restore air in a sealed jar so a candle could burn and a mouse could breathe. Plants make the oxygen that animals use.
what was lavoisiers experiment | conservation of mass experiment | lavoisier burning in sealed containers => Antoine Lavoisier carefully weighed substances before and after reactions in sealed containers and found the total mass stayed the same, establishing the law of conservation of mass.
what was the stanford marshmallow experiment | delayed gratification experiment => In the late 1960s and 1970s, Walter Mischel offered children one marshmallow now or two if they waited. Later studies suggest family background explains much of the link to later success.
how did foucault prove the earth spins | pendulum earth rotation => In 1851, Léon Foucault hung a long pendulum in Paris. Its swing slowly turned around during the day, showing that Earth is rotating underneath it.
what was the pitch drop experiment | slowest experiment in the world | pitch drop => The pitch drop experiment, started in 1927 at the University of Queensland, shows that tar pitch, which seems solid, is actually a very thick liquid. Only nine drops have fallen in nearly a century.
what was the first detection of gravitational waves | ligo 2015 | how were gravitational waves found => On 14 September 2015, the LIGO detectors in the US picked up gravitational waves from two black holes merging over a billion light years away, confirming a century-old prediction by Einstein.
what was the discovery of the higgs boson | cern 2012 | large hadron collider discovery => In 2012, scientists at CERN's Large Hadron Collider announced they had found the Higgs boson, a particle linked to how other particles get mass, predicted in 1964.
how big is the lhc | what does the lhc do => The Large Hadron Collider at CERN, near Geneva, is the world's biggest particle accelerator: a ring 27 km around where protons are smashed together at nearly the speed of light.
what was the mentos and diet coke discovery | why does mentos make coke explode | diet coke and mentos science => Mentos candies have tiny bumps that give carbon dioxide bubbles lots of places to form all at once, so the soda foams out in a big fountain. It's physical, not a chemical reaction.
how does the baking soda volcano work | why does the volcano fizz => Baking soda (a base) reacts with vinegar (an acid) to make carbon dioxide gas, water and sodium acetate. The gas bubbles make the foam pour out like lava.
what is the egg in a bottle experiment | how does the egg get sucked into the bottle | egg bottle air pressure => Heat the air in a bottle (with an adult, often using a lit match), then set a peeled hard-boiled egg on top. As the air inside cools, its pressure drops, and the higher air pressure outside pushes the egg in.
what is the naked egg experiment | how to dissolve an eggshell | egg in vinegar => Soak a raw egg in vinegar for a day or two. The acid dissolves the calcium carbonate shell, leaving a rubbery egg held together by its membrane.
what is the walking water experiment | rainbow walking water | capillary action experiment => Put cups of colored water next to empty cups and link them with paper towel strips. Water climbs the towels by capillary action and mixes into new colors in the empty cups.
what is the skittles experiment | skittles rainbow in water | why do skittles colors not mix => Put Skittles in a circle on a plate and pour warm water in the middle. The colored sugar coating dissolves and spreads out in stripes that don't mix much at first.
what is the cartesian diver experiment | how does a cartesian diver work | bottle diver => Put a pen cap or dropper with an air bubble inside a sealed water bottle. Squeezing the bottle compresses the bubble so the diver gets denser and sinks. Letting go makes it float back up.
what is the lava lamp experiment | homemade lava lamp | oil water and alka seltzer => Fill a bottle mostly with oil, add water and food coloring, then drop in an effervescent tablet. Gas bubbles carry colored water blobs up through the oil, and they sink when the gas escapes.
what is the oobleck experiment | how to make oobleck | why is oobleck solid and liquid => Mix about 2 parts cornstarch with 1 part water. Oobleck acts solid when you hit or squeeze it and flows like a liquid when you go slowly. It's a non-Newtonian fluid.
what is the red cabbage indicator experiment | how to make ph indicator | cabbage juice acid base => Boil or blend red cabbage and strain the purple juice. Add it to household liquids: acids like vinegar turn it pink or red and bases like baking soda turn it blue, green or yellow.
lemon juice invisible ink | how does lemon ink work => Write with lemon juice and let it dry. Warming the paper with a lamp or iron (with an adult) makes the writing turn brown, because the juice browns at a lower temperature than paper.
what is the static electricity balloon experiment | balloon on hair science | why does a balloon stick to the wall => Rubbing a balloon on your hair moves electrons onto the balloon, making it negatively charged. It then attracts your hair or sticks to a wall by pulling on charges in it.
what is the bending water experiment | comb bends water | static bends water stream => Charge a plastic comb by running it through dry hair, then hold it near a thin stream of tap water. The stream bends toward the comb because water molecules are attracted to the charge.
what is the lemon battery experiment | can a lemon power a light => Push a copper coin and a zinc-coated (galvanized) nail into a lemon, and it makes a small voltage, about 0.9 volts. Several lemons linked together can light a small LED.
what is the paper airplane experiment | which paper plane flies farthest | paper airplane science fair => Test different paper airplane designs by throwing each the same way several times and measuring distance. Keep the paper and throw the same so only the design changes.
what is the egg drop challenge | how to protect an egg from a drop | egg drop ideas => Design a container that stops a raw egg from breaking when dropped. Good designs spread the impact over more time with padding, straws, or a parachute to slow the fall.
what is the density tower experiment | layers of liquids experiment | rainbow density column => Carefully pour honey, corn syrup, dish soap, water and oil into a tall glass. They stack in layers from densest at the bottom to least dense on top.
what is the floating egg experiment | why does an egg float in salt water | egg salt water density => A fresh egg sinks in plain water, but if you dissolve enough salt in the water, the egg floats, because salt water is denser than the egg.
what is the ice and salt experiment | ice fishing with string and salt => Salt lowers the freezing point of water, so ice with salt on it melts even below 0 °C. Lay a string on an ice cube and sprinkle salt: it refreezes around the string so you can lift the cube.
what is the milk and food coloring experiment | magic milk experiment | dish soap on milk => Drip food coloring on milk, then touch it with a cotton swab dipped in dish soap. The soap breaks the surface tension and grabs fats, making the colors swirl wildly.
what is the pepper and soap experiment | pepper runs away from soap | surface tension trick => Sprinkle pepper on water and touch the middle with a soapy finger. The pepper shoots to the edges because soap weakens surface tension in the middle and the stronger tension at the edges pulls the water outward.
how many drops of water fit on a coin | penny drop experiment | surface tension coin => Using a dropper, see how many water drops fit on a coin before it spills. Surface tension makes a dome, and you can often fit 20 or more on a US penny.
what is the rubber band car | balloon powered car | how to build a balloon car => Attach a balloon to a straw on a light car made of cardboard and bottle caps. Air rushing out the back pushes the car forward, showing Newton's third law.
what is the bottle rocket experiment | water rocket | how does a bottle rocket work => Partly fill a plastic bottle with water and pump air in. When it releases, the pressure blasts water down and pushes the rocket up, by Newton's third law. Wear eye protection and use a launcher.
what is the dancing raisins experiment | why do raisins dance in soda | raisins in fizzy water => Drop raisins in clear soda. Bubbles stick to their wrinkles and float them up. At the top, the bubbles pop and the raisins sink, then it repeats.
what is the cloud in a jar experiment | make a cloud in a bottle | how to make a cloud => Put a little warm water in a jar, add a puff of smoke from a blown-out match (with an adult) and put ice on the lid. The cooled vapor condenses on the smoke particles, making a cloud.
what is the tornado in a bottle | how to make a vortex in a bottle | bottle tornado experiment => Tape two bottles together at the necks with one partly full of water. Flip and swirl it, and the water drains in a spinning vortex that looks like a tornado.
what is the crystal snowflake experiment | borax crystals | pipe cleaner snowflake => Bend pipe cleaners into a snowflake, hang it in a hot, very concentrated borax solution and leave it overnight with an adult. Crystals grow as it cools. Don't eat borax.
what is the rainbow paper experiment | chromatography experiment | separate marker colors => Draw a dot of washable marker near the bottom of a strip of coffee filter, and dip the end in water. As water climbs, it separates the ink into the different colors mixed to make it.
what is the bean in a bag experiment | grow a seed in a bag | watch roots grow => Put a bean seed on a damp paper towel inside a clear plastic bag and tape it to a window. In a few days you can watch the root and shoot grow.
what is the sound and rice drum experiment | seeing sound vibrations | rice on a drum => Stretch plastic wrap over a bowl and sprinkle rice on top. Bang a pot nearby and the rice jumps, showing that sound is vibration traveling through the air.
what is the string telephone experiment | tin can phone | how does a cup phone work => Connect two cups with a tight string. Talking into one cup vibrates the bottom, the vibrations travel along the string, and the other cup turns them back into sound.
what is the straw oboe experiment | make an instrument from a straw | straw whistle => Flatten the end of a drinking straw and cut it to a point. Blow hard and the flaps vibrate, making a sound. Cutting the straw shorter raises the pitch.
what is the water glass xylophone | glasses of water music | why do water glasses make different notes => Fill glasses with different amounts of water and tap them. More water means a lower note, because there's more mass to vibrate. Blowing over bottles works the opposite way.
what is the coin and card trick | inertia experiment | coin on a card over a cup => Put a card on a cup and a coin on the card, then flick the card away fast. The coin drops into the cup because of inertia: it tends to stay where it is.
what is the tablecloth trick | pulling a tablecloth from under dishes | inertia tablecloth => A quick, straight pull can remove a smooth tablecloth while dishes stay put, because of inertia and the short time friction acts on them. Practice with plastic, not real dishes.
what is the potato electricity experiment | potato clock | how does a potato battery work => A potato with a copper and zinc electrode makes a small current, because the metals react differently in the potato's juices. Two or more potatoes can run a small clock.
what is the homemade compass experiment | make a compass with a needle | floating needle compass => Stroke a needle in one direction with a magnet about 30 times, then float it on a bit of cork or leaf in water. It turns to point north and south.
what is the electromagnet experiment | nail and battery magnet => Wrap insulated wire many times around an iron nail and connect the ends to a battery with an adult. The nail becomes a magnet that can pick up paper clips. More coils make it stronger.
what is the leaf chromatography experiment | hidden colors in leaves | separate leaf pigments => Crush leaves in rubbing alcohol with an adult, then dip a coffee filter strip in it. Over an hour or so, the pigments separate into green chlorophyll and yellow and orange bands.
what is the plant maze experiment | shoebox plant maze | plant grows through a maze => Put a sprouting bean in a shoebox with cardboard walls inside and a hole at one end. Over a couple of weeks, the shoot winds through the maze toward the light.
what is the bread mold experiment | where does mold grow fastest | mold growth experiment => Put slices of bread in sealed bags in different conditions, like dark, light, warm, cold, damp or dry, and check daily. Mold usually grows fastest when it's warm, dark and damp. Don't open moldy bags.
what is the hand washing glitter experiment | glitter germs experiment | how soap gets rid of germs => Put glitter on your hands as pretend germs, then try rinsing with water only, then with soap. Soap removes far more, just like real germs.
what is the strawberry dna activity | can you see dna with your eyes | dna in a test tube => When strawberry cells are broken open with soap and salt and then cold alcohol is added, millions of DNA strands clump together into a white, stringy mass big enough to see.
