# tag: science.compare
what is the difference between a virus and bacteria | are viruses bacteria => Bacteria are living single cells that can reproduce on their own, and many are harmless. Viruses are much smaller, aren't cells, and can only multiply inside a host's cells. Antibiotics work on bacteria, not viruses.
what is the difference between an atom and a molecule | atom vs molecule | is a molecule an atom => An atom is a single particle of an element, like one oxygen atom. A molecule is two or more atoms bonded together, like O₂ or H₂O.
what is the difference between an element and a compound | is water an element => An element is made of only one kind of atom, like gold or oxygen. A compound is two or more elements chemically joined, like water, which is hydrogen and oxygen.
what is the difference between a mixture and a compound | is salt water a compound => In a mixture, like salt water, the parts aren't chemically joined and can be separated physically. In a compound, like salt itself, the elements are bonded and need a chemical reaction to separate.
what is the difference between a physical and chemical change | physical vs chemical change | is melting a chemical change => A physical change, like melting ice or tearing paper, doesn't make a new substance. A chemical change, like burning or rusting, makes new substances and is usually hard to reverse.
what is the difference between melting and dissolving | melting vs dissolving | does sugar melt in water => Melting is a solid turning to liquid from heat, like ice becoming water. Dissolving is a solid mixing evenly into a liquid, so sugar dissolves in tea rather than melting.
evaporation vs boiling | does water evaporate without boiling => Evaporation happens only at a liquid's surface and at any temperature, like puddles drying. Boiling happens throughout the liquid at one temperature, making bubbles of vapor.
what is the difference between steam and water vapor | is steam visible | why can you see steam => Water vapor is an invisible gas. The white cloud above a kettle is really tiny droplets of liquid water that formed when the vapor cooled. Right at the spout, the gap that looks clear is the true invisible vapor.
what is the difference between an acid and a base | acid vs base | acid vs alkali => Acids have a pH below 7 and taste sour, like lemon juice. Bases have a pH above 7 and feel slippery, like soap. An alkali is a base that dissolves in water.
what is the difference between a conductor and an insulator | conductor vs insulator | examples of insulators and conductors => Conductors, like copper and other metals, let electricity or heat flow easily. Insulators, like plastic, rubber, wood and glass, block it.
what is the difference between voltage and current | voltage vs current | volts vs amps => Voltage is the push that drives electricity, measured in volts. Current is how much electric charge flows each second, measured in amps. A higher voltage can push more current through the same wire.
what is the difference between nuclear fission and fusion | which is used in power plants => Fission splits heavy atoms like uranium, and it powers today's nuclear plants. Fusion joins light atoms like hydrogen, which powers the Sun. Fusion power plants are still being developed as of 2025.
what is the difference between renewable and nonrenewable energy | examples of renewable energy => Renewable energy comes from sources that naturally refill, like sunlight, wind, flowing water and geothermal heat. Nonrenewable sources, like coal, oil, natural gas and uranium, run out because they take millions of years to form or are limited.
what is the difference between reflection and refraction | reflection vs refraction | mirror vs lens => Reflection is light bouncing off a surface, like a mirror. Refraction is light bending as it passes from one material into another, like through a lens or water.
what is the difference between a convex and concave lens | convex vs concave | which lens magnifies => A convex lens is thicker in the middle and brings light together, so it can magnify. A concave lens is thinner in the middle and spreads light out. Concave lenses help nearsighted people.
what is the difference between a solar and lunar eclipse | solar vs lunar eclipse | which eclipse is safe to look at => In a solar eclipse, the Moon blocks the Sun, and you need eye protection. In a lunar eclipse, Earth's shadow falls on the Moon, which is safe to look at.
what is the difference between a planet and a star | planet vs star | how is a star different from a planet => A star is a huge ball of hot gas that makes its own light through nuclear fusion. A planet is smaller, doesn't make its own light, and shines by reflecting light from its star.
what is the difference between a moon and a planet | moon vs planet | can a moon be bigger than a planet => A planet orbits a star, while a moon orbits a planet. Moons can be big: Ganymede and Titan are bigger than the planet Mercury.
what is the difference between an asteroid and a comet | asteroid vs comet | are comets asteroids => Asteroids are mostly rock or metal and usually orbit closer to the Sun. Comets are icy and dusty, and when they come near the Sun, their ice turns to gas, making a glowing coma and tails.
what is the difference between a galaxy and a solar system | galaxy vs solar system | is the solar system a galaxy => A solar system is one star with its planets and other objects. A galaxy is an enormous group of billions of stars, many with their own planets. Our solar system is one tiny part of the Milky Way galaxy.
what is the difference between a galaxy and a universe | galaxy vs universe | is the universe bigger than a galaxy => The universe is everything that exists, including all space, time, matter and energy. It holds hundreds of billions or more galaxies.
what is the difference between astronomy and astrology | astronomy vs astrology | is astrology science => Astronomy is the science of stars, planets and space, based on observation and evidence. Astrology claims star positions affect people's lives, but tests haven't supported it, so it isn't science.
what is the difference between a hurricane and a tornado | hurricane vs tornado | which is bigger tornado or hurricane => Hurricanes are huge storms, hundreds of kilometers wide, that form over warm oceans and last for days. Tornadoes are narrow, spinning columns of air from thunderstorms, usually less than 1 km wide and lasting minutes.
what is the difference between a hurricane typhoon and cyclone | hurricane vs typhoon | are typhoons and hurricanes the same => They're the same kind of storm with different names by region. It's a hurricane in the Atlantic and northeast Pacific, a typhoon in the northwest Pacific, and a cyclone in the Indian Ocean and South Pacific.
what is the difference between sleet and hail | sleet vs hail => Sleet is raindrops that freeze into ice pellets before hitting the ground, in winter. Hail is layered balls of ice from thunderstorms, often in summer. Freezing rain falls as liquid and freezes on contact.
what is the difference between fog and mist | is mist a cloud => Both are tiny water droplets floating near the ground. It's called fog when you can see less than 1 km, and mist when you can see farther.
what is the difference between a lake and a pond | lake vs pond | when is a pond a lake => There's no strict rule. Ponds are usually small and shallow enough for sunlight to reach the bottom so plants can grow all across them. Lakes are bigger and deeper.
what is the difference between a sea and an ocean | sea vs ocean | is a sea smaller than an ocean => Oceans are the five huge connected bodies of salt water. Seas are smaller parts of oceans, usually partly surrounded by land, like the Mediterranean Sea.
what is the difference between rocks and minerals | is a rock a mineral => A mineral is a natural, solid substance with a set chemical makeup and crystal structure, like quartz. A rock is usually a mixture of minerals, like granite, made of quartz, feldspar and mica.
what is the difference between weathering and erosion | erosion meaning => Weathering breaks rock into smaller pieces where it is. Erosion carries those pieces away, by water, wind, ice or gravity.
what is the difference between an ice sheet and a glacier | ice sheet vs glacier | ice shelf meaning => A glacier is any large mass of ice that slowly flows. An ice sheet is a continent-sized glacier, like those covering Antarctica and Greenland. An ice shelf is the floating edge of an ice sheet over the sea.
what is the difference between an iceberg and sea ice | sea ice vs iceberg | does sea ice raise sea level => Icebergs break off glaciers, so they're made of fresh water that fell as snow. Sea ice forms when seawater freezes. Melting sea ice barely changes sea level, but melting ice on land raises it.
what is the difference between global warming and climate change | global warming vs climate change => Global warming is the rise in Earth's average temperature. Climate change includes that plus its effects, like changing rainfall, rising seas and more heatwaves.
what is the difference between the ozone hole and global warming | is the ozone hole causing global warming | ozone vs climate change => They're separate problems. The ozone hole is thinning of the ozone layer from CFC chemicals, which lets in more UV. Global warming comes mainly from greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide trapping heat.
what is the difference between dna and rna | is rna the same as dna => DNA is a double-stranded molecule that stores genetic information long term. RNA is usually single-stranded and carries copies of instructions to make proteins. RNA uses uracil instead of thymine.
what is the difference between genes and chromosomes | gene vs chromosome | gene vs dna => DNA is the molecule that carries instructions. A gene is a section of DNA with instructions for one thing, usually a protein. A chromosome is a long, tightly packed strand of DNA holding many genes.
what is the difference between mitosis and meiosis | why do we need meiosis => Mitosis makes two identical cells for growth and repair. Meiosis makes four sex cells, like eggs and sperm, each with half the chromosomes, so offspring get a mix from both parents.
what is the difference between respiration and breathing | breathing vs respiration | is breathing respiration => Breathing moves air in and out of your lungs. Cellular respiration is the chemical process inside cells that uses oxygen and food to release energy.
what is the difference between a vaccine and an antibiotic | vaccine vs antibiotic | vaccine vs medicine => A vaccine trains your immune system to recognize a germ before you get sick, preventing disease. An antibiotic is medicine that kills bacteria once you're already infected.
what is the difference between a seed and a spore | do all plants have seeds => A seed holds a tiny plant embryo with a food supply and a protective coat. A spore is a single cell with no stored food. Ferns, mosses and fungi use spores.
what is the difference between deciduous and evergreen | deciduous vs evergreen | are all conifers evergreen => Deciduous trees, like oaks and maples, lose all their leaves each year. Evergreens keep leaves all year, replacing them gradually. Most conifers are evergreen, but larches drop their needles.
what is the difference between a hypothesis and a theory | hypothesis vs theory | is a theory just a guess => A hypothesis is a testable idea. A scientific theory is a well-tested explanation backed by lots of evidence, like the theory of evolution. In science, a theory is not just a guess.
what is the difference between a theory and a law in science | scientific law vs theory | does a theory become a law => A scientific law describes what happens, often with a formula, like Newton's law of gravity. A theory explains why it happens. Theories don't turn into laws, since they do different jobs.
what is the difference between accuracy and precision | precise but not accurate => Accuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value. Precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other. Darts clustered tightly away from the bullseye are precise but not accurate.
what is the difference between independent and dependent variables | independent vs dependent variable | what is a controlled variable => The independent variable is what you change on purpose. The dependent variable is what you measure. Controlled variables are kept the same so the test is fair.
what is the difference between a solid liquid and gas | solid liquid gas particles | states of matter particles => In a solid, particles are packed tightly and vibrate in place, so it keeps its shape. In a liquid, they're close but slide past each other. In a gas, they're far apart and move freely, filling any container.
what is the difference between a compass and a gps | compass vs gps | does a compass need batteries => A magnetic compass needs no power. It just points toward magnetic north. GPS uses signals from satellites to work out your exact position on Earth.
what is the difference between a telescope and a microscope | telescope vs microscope => A telescope gathers light from distant objects to make them look closer and brighter. A microscope magnifies very small, close objects. Both use lenses or mirrors.
what is the difference between magnetic north and true north | true north vs magnetic north | does a compass point to the north pole => True north is the direction to the geographic North Pole. A compass points to magnetic north, which is in a different spot and slowly moves, so maps show the angle between them.
what is the difference between an amphibian and a reptile | amphibian vs reptile | is a frog a reptile => Amphibians, like frogs and salamanders, have moist skin and usually lay jelly-like eggs in water, with young that live in water first. Reptiles, like lizards and snakes, have dry, scaly skin and lay eggs with shells on land.
are all insects bugs | true bugs => In everyday speech, bug means any small crawling creature. To scientists, true bugs are one group of insects with straw-like mouthparts, like stink bugs and aphids. A ladybug isn't a true bug, it's a beetle.
what is the difference between a herbivore carnivore and omnivore | herbivore vs carnivore | what is an omnivore => Herbivores eat only plants, like cows. Carnivores mostly eat meat, like lions. Omnivores eat both, like bears and most humans.
what is the difference between a dinosaur and a pterosaur | flying reptiles dinosaurs => Pterosaurs, like Pteranodon, were flying reptiles that lived alongside dinosaurs, but they weren't dinosaurs. Swimming reptiles like plesiosaurs weren't dinosaurs either. Birds, however, are living dinosaurs.
