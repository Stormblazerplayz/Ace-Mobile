# tag: science.physics.atoms
how big is an atom | how small are atoms | size of an atom => Atoms are tiny, about 0.1 to 0.5 nanometers across. About a million atoms lined up would be as wide as a human hair.
what is the nucleus of an atom | what is in the nucleus | how big is the nucleus => The nucleus is the tiny, dense center of an atom, made of protons and neutrons. If an atom were a football stadium, the nucleus would be like a pea in the middle.
is an atom mostly empty space | why are atoms mostly empty | how empty are atoms => Yes. Almost all of an atom's mass is in the tiny nucleus, and the electrons are spread out around it, so an atom is mostly empty space.
what is a proton | proton charge and mass | what does a proton do => A proton is a positively charged particle in the nucleus. The number of protons decides which element an atom is.
what is a neutron | neutron charge | what do neutrons do => A neutron is a particle in the nucleus with no charge. Neutrons help hold the nucleus together with the strong nuclear force.
what is an electron | electron charge and mass | where are electrons => An electron is a tiny negatively charged particle that moves around the nucleus. It's about 1,836 times lighter than a proton.
who discovered the electron | when was the electron discovered | jj thomson electron => J. J. Thomson discovered the electron in 1897, using cathode ray tubes. It was the first subatomic particle found.
who discovered the proton | when was the proton discovered | rutherford proton => Ernest Rutherford is credited with discovering the proton, around 1917 to 1919, by knocking protons out of nitrogen atoms.
who discovered the neutron | when was the neutron discovered | james chadwick neutron => James Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932 and won the Nobel Prize in Physics for it in 1935.
what was the gold foil experiment | rutherford gold foil experiment | how was the nucleus discovered => In 1909, Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden fired alpha particles at thin gold foil under Rutherford's direction. A few bounced back, which showed atoms have a tiny, dense nucleus.
what is the plum pudding model | thomson atom model | what did thomson think atoms looked like => J. J. Thomson's plum pudding model pictured an atom as a ball of positive charge with electrons dotted through it like plums in a pudding. The gold foil experiment proved it wrong.
what is the bohr model | bohr model of the atom | niels bohr atom => In 1913, Niels Bohr pictured electrons orbiting the nucleus in fixed energy levels, or shells. It's still used in school to show electron arrangements.
how many electrons fit in each shell | electron shell rules | 2 8 8 rule => For the first 20 elements, the first shell holds up to 2 electrons, the second up to 8 and the third up to 8. Later shells can hold more.
what is the modern model of the atom | electron cloud model | quantum model of the atom => In the modern model, electrons don't follow neat orbits. They're found in fuzzy regions called orbitals, where they're likely to be.
who first said everything is made of atoms | democritus atoms | where does the word atom come from => The ancient Greek thinker Democritus said matter is made of tiny uncuttable bits around 400 BC. Atom comes from atomos, Greek for uncuttable.
what was daltons atomic theory | john dalton atoms | who proved atoms exist => In the early 1800s, John Dalton proposed that elements are made of atoms, that atoms of one element are identical, and that compounds form when atoms combine in fixed ratios.
what are quarks | what are protons made of | are protons made of smaller things => Protons and neutrons are made of smaller particles called quarks. A proton has two up quarks and one down quark, and a neutron has one up and two down.
what is a subatomic particle | list of subatomic particles | particles smaller than an atom => Subatomic particles are smaller than atoms. The main ones are protons, neutrons and electrons. Others include quarks, neutrinos and photons.
what is an isotope | isotope definition | what are isotopes of carbon => Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. Carbon-12 has 6 neutrons and carbon-14 has 8, but both are carbon.
what is mass number | mass number vs atomic number | how do you find the mass number => The mass number is the number of protons plus neutrons in an atom. The atomic number is just the protons.
how do you find the number of neutrons | neutrons formula | how to calculate neutrons => Number of neutrons = mass number minus atomic number. Carbon-14 has 14 - 6 = 8 neutrons.
what is an ion | how do atoms become ions | positive and negative ions => An ion is an atom that has gained or lost electrons. Losing electrons makes a positive ion, and gaining electrons makes a negative ion.
what is radioactivity | what does radioactive mean | why are some atoms radioactive => Radioactivity is when an unstable nucleus breaks down and gives off radiation. It happens because the nucleus has the wrong mix of protons and neutrons to stay stable.
who discovered radioactivity | when was radioactivity discovered | henri becquerel uranium => Henri Becquerel discovered radioactivity in 1896 when uranium salts darkened a photographic plate. Marie and Pierre Curie then studied it and coined the word radioactivity.
what are alpha beta and gamma radiation | types of nuclear radiation | alpha vs beta vs gamma => Alpha particles are 2 protons and 2 neutrons, stopped by paper. Beta particles are fast electrons, stopped by thin aluminium. Gamma rays are high energy waves, reduced by thick lead or concrete.
what is half life | half life meaning | how does half life work => Half-life is the time it takes for half the radioactive atoms in a sample to decay. After two half-lives, a quarter is left.
what is carbon dating | how does carbon dating work | how do scientists date fossils with carbon => Living things take in carbon, including a tiny bit of radioactive carbon-14. After they die, it decays with a half-life of about 5,730 years, so measuring what's left tells their age.
how far back can carbon dating go | carbon dating limit | can you carbon date dinosaurs => Carbon dating works up to about 50,000 years. Dinosaur fossils are tens of millions of years old, so scientists date the rocks around them with other radioactive elements instead.
what is background radiation | where does background radiation come from | are we always exposed to radiation => Background radiation is low level radiation that's always around us. It comes from rocks, radon gas, space, food and even our own bodies, and normally isn't harmful.
how is radiation measured | what is a sievert | what is a geiger counter => A Geiger counter clicks when it detects radiation. The effect of radiation on the body is measured in sieverts, and a normal yearly dose is a few millisieverts.
what is nuclear fission | how does fission work | splitting the atom => Nuclear fission is splitting a heavy nucleus, like uranium-235, into smaller ones when a neutron hits it. It releases a lot of energy and more neutrons.
what is a chain reaction | nuclear chain reaction | how do chain reactions work => In a chain reaction, each fission releases neutrons that cause more fissions. Power plants control it with control rods, while a bomb lets it run out of control.
who discovered nuclear fission | when was fission discovered | otto hahn lise meitner => Otto Hahn and Fritz Strassmann discovered nuclear fission in 1938, and Lise Meitner and Otto Frisch explained it in early 1939.
what was the manhattan project | who built the first atomic bomb | first nuclear bomb => The Manhattan Project was the US effort during World War II to build atomic bombs. The first test, called Trinity, was in New Mexico on 16 July 1945.
what was the first nuclear reactor | chicago pile 1 | who built the first reactor => Enrico Fermi's team built Chicago Pile-1, the first nuclear reactor, under a stadium at the University of Chicago. It started the first controlled chain reaction on 2 December 1942.
fission vs fusion | difference between fission and fusion | which is more powerful fission or fusion => Fission splits heavy atoms like uranium. Fusion joins light atoms like hydrogen. Fusion releases more energy per kilogram of fuel and powers the Sun.
why dont we have fusion power yet | when will fusion power work | fusion reactor problems => Fusion needs temperatures over 100 million °C and strong magnets to hold the plasma. Reactors have produced fusion, but as of 2025 none makes more power than the whole plant uses.
what is iter | iter fusion reactor | where is iter => ITER is a giant international fusion experiment being built in southern France. It's a tokamak, a doughnut shaped machine that holds hot plasma with magnets.
what is antimatter | does antimatter exist | what happens when matter meets antimatter => Antimatter is made of particles like normal ones but with opposite charge, like the positron, which is a positive electron. When matter and antimatter meet, they destroy each other and release energy.
what is the large hadron collider | what is the lhc | what does cern do => The Large Hadron Collider at CERN, near Geneva, is the world's biggest particle accelerator. It's a 27 km ring that smashes particles together to study what matter is made of.
what is the higgs boson | god particle | when was the higgs boson found => The Higgs boson is a particle linked to the field that gives other particles their mass. It was discovered at the LHC in 2012, and Peter Higgs and François Englert won the Nobel Prize in 2013.
what is a neutrino | are neutrinos everywhere | ghost particles => Neutrinos are tiny, nearly massless particles that barely interact with anything. Trillions from the Sun pass through your body every second without you noticing.
what is the theory of relativity | what did einstein's relativity say | relativity explained simply => Einstein's special relativity (1905) says the speed of light is the same for everyone, and time and space stretch at high speeds. General relativity (1915) explains gravity as the bending of space and time by mass.
what is time dilation | does time slow down when you go fast | time dilation explained => Time dilation means time runs slower for things moving very fast or deep in strong gravity. GPS satellites have to correct for it, or they'd drift off by kilometers a day.
does gravity bend light | what is gravitational lensing | how does gravity bend light => Yes. Mass bends space, so light passing a big galaxy curves around it. This gravitational lensing can magnify faraway galaxies behind it.
how was general relativity proven | 1919 eclipse experiment | eddington eclipse => In 1919, Arthur Eddington photographed stars near the Sun during a solar eclipse. They appeared slightly shifted, just as Einstein predicted, which made Einstein famous worldwide.
what are gravitational waves | who detected gravitational waves | ligo discovery => Gravitational waves are ripples in space and time from huge events like black holes colliding. The LIGO detectors first detected them on 14 September 2015, a century after Einstein predicted them.
what is quantum physics | quantum mechanics explained simply | what does quantum mean => Quantum physics studies the tiny world of atoms and particles, where energy comes in small chunks called quanta and particles act like waves. Things there behave in strange ways.
what is the uncertainty principle | heisenberg uncertainty principle | can you know where an electron is => Werner Heisenberg's uncertainty principle says you can't know both the exact position and the exact speed of a particle at the same time.
what is schrodingers cat | schrodinger cat explained | is the cat dead or alive => Schrödinger's cat is a thought experiment from 1935. A cat in a box is linked to a random quantum event, so according to quantum rules it seems to be both alive and dead until you look. Schrödinger used it to show how weird quantum ideas seem.
what is a quantum computer | how do quantum computers work | qubits explained => A quantum computer uses qubits, which can be 0 and 1 at the same time. For certain problems, like simulating molecules, they could be far faster than normal computers.
what is the photoelectric effect | what did einstein win the nobel prize for | einstein nobel prize => The photoelectric effect is light knocking electrons out of metal. Einstein explained it in 1905 using light particles (photons) and won the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for it.
what is dark matter | what is dark energy | what is the universe made of => Normal matter is only about 5% of the universe. About 27% is dark matter, which we can't see but feel through its gravity, and about 68% is dark energy, which speeds up the universe's expansion.
what are the si units | what are the seven base units | list of si base units => The seven SI base units are the meter (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount of substance) and candela (brightness).
what is a meter defined as | how long is a meter exactly | definition of a meter => Since 1983, a meter has been defined as the distance light travels in a vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second.
how is a kilogram defined | what is the kilogram based on | did the kilogram change in 2019 => Since 2019, the kilogram is defined using the Planck constant, a fixed number of nature. Before that it was defined by a metal cylinder kept near Paris.
how is a second defined | what is a second based on | atomic clock second => A second is defined by caesium atoms: it's the time for 9,192,631,770 cycles of a certain radiation from a caesium-133 atom. Atomic clocks use this.
what is an atomic clock | how accurate are atomic clocks | how do atomic clocks work => Atomic clocks keep time using the steady vibrations of atoms like caesium. The best ones would lose less than a second in billions of years.
what is a mole in chemistry | avogadros number | what does mole mean => A mole is a counting unit for tiny particles: 6.022 x 10²³ of them. That huge number is called Avogadro's number. One mole of carbon-12 weighs exactly 12 grams.
what is the gravitational constant | big g in physics | what is g in newtons law => The gravitational constant, G, sets how strong gravity is. It's about 6.674 x 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg², a tiny number, which is why gravity is so weak.
what is plancks constant | planck constant value | what does plancks constant mean => Planck's constant (about 6.626 x 10⁻³⁴ joule seconds) links a photon's energy to its frequency. It's one of the key numbers of quantum physics.
what is the charge of an electron | elementary charge value | how much charge does an electron have => An electron's charge is about -1.602 x 10⁻¹⁹ coulombs. A proton has the same amount of charge, but positive.
what is the mass of an electron | how heavy is a proton | proton and electron mass => A proton weighs about 1.67 x 10⁻²⁷ kg and an electron about 9.11 x 10⁻³¹ kg. Neutrons are just a tiny bit heavier than protons.
what is scientific notation | how do you write numbers in standard form | why do scientists use powers of ten => Scientific notation writes huge or tiny numbers as a number times a power of ten. The speed of light, 300,000,000 m/s, is 3 x 10⁸ m/s.
what do kilo mega and giga mean | si prefixes => Kilo means 1,000, mega means a million and giga means a billion. Milli means a thousandth, micro a millionth and nano a billionth.
what is a nanometer | how small is a nanometer | nanometer size => A nanometer is a billionth of a meter. A human hair is about 80,000 to 100,000 nanometers wide.
what is nanotechnology | what are nanoparticles | uses of nanotechnology => Nanotechnology is building things at the scale of atoms and molecules, under 100 nanometers. It's used in computer chips, sunscreens, stain resistant fabrics and medicine.
what is a light year in km | how far is a light year in miles | light year distance => A light year is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion km (5.88 trillion miles). It measures distance, not time.
what is an astronomical unit | what does au mean in space | how far is 1 au => An astronomical unit (AU) is the average distance from Earth to the Sun, about 149.6 million km. It's used to measure distances in the solar system.
what is a parsec | how long is a parsec | parsec vs light year => A parsec is about 3.26 light years, a distance unit astronomers use. Despite what some movies say, it's a distance, not a time.
what is e equals mc squared used for | how does e mc2 explain nuclear power | mass into energy => E = mc² means a tiny bit of mass can turn into a huge amount of energy, because the speed of light squared is enormous. That's where the energy in nuclear power and the Sun comes from.
what is a black body | why do hot things glow different colors | red hot vs white hot => Hot objects glow because of their temperature. As something heats up, it glows red, then orange, yellow and white. Blue-white stars are hotter than red ones.
what is string theory | is string theory proven | string theory explained => String theory is an idea that the tiniest things in the universe are vibrating strings of energy. It tries to join gravity with quantum physics, but as of 2025 there's no experimental proof.
what is the standard model | standard model of particle physics | how many fundamental particles are there => The Standard Model is the theory that describes the known fundamental particles: six quarks, six leptons (including the electron), force-carrying particles like the photon, and the Higgs boson. It doesn't include gravity.
what is a tokamak | how do fusion reactors hold plasma | tokamak design => A tokamak is a doughnut shaped machine that uses powerful magnets to hold super hot plasma away from its walls, so fusion can happen.
what is cold fusion | is cold fusion real | 1989 cold fusion => In 1989 two chemists claimed they made fusion at room temperature, called cold fusion. Other scientists couldn't repeat it, so it's not accepted as real.
what is an electron volt | ev unit physics | what does ev mean in physics => An electron volt (eV) is a tiny unit of energy, the energy an electron gains moving through 1 volt. Particle physicists use it a lot, often in millions or billions (MeV, GeV).
what is nuclear waste | how is nuclear waste stored | why is nuclear waste dangerous => Nuclear waste is used fuel and materials that stay radioactive, some for thousands of years. It's cooled in pools, then sealed in steel and concrete casks, and some countries are building deep underground stores.
what happened at chernobyl | chernobyl disaster | when was chernobyl => On 26 April 1986, a reactor at the Chernobyl power plant in Soviet Ukraine exploded during a badly run safety test. It spread radiation across Europe, and the area around it is still closed.
what happened at fukushima | fukushima nuclear disaster | when was fukushima => In March 2011, a huge earthquake and tsunami in Japan knocked out cooling at the Fukushima Daiichi power plant. Three reactors melted down and released radiation, and many people were evacuated.
