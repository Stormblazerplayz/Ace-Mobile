# tag: science.bio.life
is a sponge an animal | are sea sponges alive | what kind of living thing is a sponge => Yes, sea sponges are animals, among the simplest there are. They have no brain, heart or muscles, and they filter food from water pumped through their bodies.
what kingdom are mushrooms in | is a mushroom a vegetable => No, a mushroom is a fungus. It's the fruiting body of a fungus that mostly lives underground as threads called mycelium. Cooks call it a vegetable, but biologists don't.
how big can a fungus get | largest living thing honey fungus => Mycelium is the network of thread-like hyphae that makes up most of a fungus. A honey fungus in Oregon spreads across about 9.6 square km, making it one of the largest living things on Earth.
do trees talk through fungi | mycorrhizal network => Many plant roots partner with fungi called mycorrhizae, which trade water and minerals for sugar. These fungal networks can link trees, though scientists debate how much trees actually share through them.
is fire alive | why isnt fire a living thing | does fire count as life => No. Fire grows, uses oxygen and spreads, but it has no cells, doesn't reproduce by itself with genes and can't respond or adapt, so it isn't alive.
is a seed alive | are seeds living things | how long can seeds stay alive => Yes. A seed holds a living plant embryo in a resting state. Some seeds can survive for centuries, and a date palm was grown from a seed about 2,000 years old.
doomsday seed vault | global seed vault => The Svalbard Global Seed Vault in Norway, opened in 2008, stores over a million seed samples from around the world inside a frozen mountain, as a backup for the world's crops.
what is asexual reproduction | asexual vs sexual reproduction | examples of asexual reproduction => Asexual reproduction needs just one parent, and the offspring are genetic copies. Sexual reproduction mixes genes from two parents, making each offspring different.
what is binary fission | what is budding | how do yeast reproduce => Binary fission is when one cell splits into two, as bacteria do. Budding is when a small new organism grows out of the parent and breaks off, as yeast and hydra do.
what is vegetative propagation | how do plants clone themselves | plant cuttings => Vegetative propagation is plants making copies of themselves from roots, stems or leaves, like strawberry runners, potato tubers and cuttings that grow roots in water.
how do ferns reproduce | spores vs seeds => Spores are tiny single cells that can grow into a new organism. Ferns, mosses and fungi use spores. Seeds are bigger and hold a baby plant with stored food.
what is regeneration in biology | which animals can regrow body parts | axolotl regeneration => Regeneration is regrowing lost body parts. Starfish can regrow arms, planarian flatworms can regrow a whole body from a small piece, and axolotls can regrow legs and even parts of their heart.
types of pathogens | what causes infectious diseases => A pathogen is a germ that causes disease. The main types are bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites, including protists like the one that causes malaria.
what causes malaria | is malaria a virus | plasmodium => Malaria is caused by Plasmodium, a single-celled parasite spread by bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. It's a protist, not a virus or bacterium.
what is an antibiotic | antibiotics vs antivirals => Antibiotics are medicines that kill bacteria or stop them growing, often by damaging their cell walls or ribosomes. They don't work on viruses, which need antiviral drugs.
what is a prion | prion disease | mad cow disease cause => A prion is a misfolded protein that makes other proteins misfold too, causing rare brain diseases like mad cow disease. Prions have no genes at all.
what is e coli | is e coli bad | escherichia coli => E. coli is a bacterium that normally lives harmlessly in the guts of people and animals. A few strains cause food poisoning. It's also one of the most studied organisms in science.
what are cyanobacteria | blue green algae | bacteria that make oxygen => Cyanobacteria are bacteria that do photosynthesis. Over billions of years, they filled Earth's air with oxygen, and chloroplasts in plants came from ancient cyanobacteria.
what was the great oxidation event | when did oxygen appear in the air | how did earth get oxygen => The Great Oxidation Event, around 2.4 billion years ago, is when oxygen made by cyanobacteria began building up in Earth's air. It changed the planet and let complex life evolve later.
what are stromatolites | oldest fossils of life | living stromatolites shark bay => Stromatolites are layered rocks built by mats of microbes, mostly cyanobacteria. Some fossil ones are about 3.5 billion years old, and living ones still grow in Shark Bay, Australia.
what is luca | last universal common ancestor | first common ancestor of life => LUCA, the last universal common ancestor, is the single-celled ancestor of all life today. It probably lived more than 3.5 billion years ago, maybe near hot vents.
what is abiogenesis | origin of life theories => Abiogenesis is how life first arose from nonliving chemistry. Scientists think simple molecules formed RNA-like molecules that could copy themselves, perhaps in hot springs or deep-sea vents, but the details are unknown.
what is the rna world hypothesis | did life start with rna | rna world => The RNA world idea suggests early life used RNA both to store information and to speed up reactions, before DNA and proteins took over those jobs.
what is biotechnology | examples of biotechnology | biotech meaning => Biotechnology uses living things or their parts to make useful products, like yeast for bread, bacteria that make insulin, and crops engineered to resist pests.
what is bioremediation | bacteria that eat oil | cleaning pollution with microbes => Bioremediation uses microbes or plants to clean up pollution. Some bacteria break down spilled oil, and certain plants pull heavy metals out of soil.
what is a model organism | why do scientists study fruit flies | lab organisms => Model organisms are species scientists study in detail because they're easy to grow and breed fast, like E. coli, yeast, fruit flies, nematode worms, zebrafish, mice and the plant Arabidopsis.
fruit flies in genetics | drosophila => Fruit flies breed in about 10 days, are easy to keep, and share many disease genes with humans. Studies of them led to several Nobel Prizes in genetics.
what is a life cycle | life cycle of a plant | stages of a life cycle => A life cycle is the series of stages a living thing goes through, from birth to reproduction to death. A flowering plant goes from seed to seedling to adult plant to flower to new seeds.
what does metabolism mean | metabolic rate => Metabolism is all the chemical reactions in a living thing, breaking down food for energy and building the molecules it needs.
what is an autotroph | what is a heterotroph | autotroph vs heterotroph => Autotrophs make their own food, usually from sunlight like plants, or from chemicals like vent bacteria. Heterotrophs get food by eating other living things, like animals and fungi.
what is a carbon sink | examples of carbon sinks | forests and oceans carbon => A carbon sink takes in more carbon dioxide than it releases. Forests, oceans, soils and peat bogs are Earth's main carbon sinks.
what is a peat bog | why are peatlands important | peat => Peat bogs are waterlogged wetlands where dead plants, mostly mosses, build up without fully rotting. They cover only about 3% of the land but store more carbon than all the world's forests.
what is rewilding | rewilding examples | bringing back nature => Rewilding means restoring natural processes, often by bringing back missing species like beavers or wolves and letting land recover with less human management.
why are beavers called ecosystem engineers | what is an ecosystem engineer | beaver dams wetlands => Ecosystem engineers reshape their habitat. Beaver dams create ponds and wetlands that store water, reduce floods and give homes to fish, birds and insects.
what is an indicator species | lichens and pollution | species that show environmental health => An indicator species shows the health of an environment. Many lichens can't survive air pollution, and mayfly larvae need clean water, so finding them is a good sign.
what is a food chain in the ocean | ocean food chain example | marine food web => A simple ocean food chain is phytoplankton, to zooplankton like krill, to small fish, to bigger fish, to a shark or orca. Blue whales skip steps and eat krill directly.
what is a quadrat | how do ecologists count plants | sampling in ecology => A quadrat is a square frame, often 1 meter on each side, placed on the ground to count plants or small animals. Ecologists use many quadrats to estimate populations over a big area.
what is a transect | line transect ecology | how to survey a habitat => A transect is a line across a habitat along which you record what lives at regular points. It shows how life changes, like from a beach up into sand dunes.
what is mark and recapture | how to estimate animal populations | capture recapture method => Catch and mark some animals, release them, then catch another group later. The share of marked animals in the second catch lets you estimate the whole population.
what is a biological control | using predators to control pests | ladybugs eat aphids => Biological control uses natural enemies to control pests, like ladybugs eating aphids. It can backfire if the new species becomes invasive, as cane toads did in Australia.
what are pesticides and herbicides | why are pesticides harmful | insecticide vs herbicide => Pesticides kill pests. Insecticides target insects and herbicides target weeds. They help farming but can harm helpful insects, water and wildlife if overused.
what is eutrophication | fertilizer runoff effects | algae bloom from fertilizer => Eutrophication is when extra nutrients, often from fertilizer or sewage, wash into water and feed huge algae blooms. When the algae die, bacteria use up the oxygen and fish suffocate.
