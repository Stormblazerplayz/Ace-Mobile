# tag: science.physics.forces
what is a force | force definition science | what does force mean in physics | what counts as a force => A force is a push or a pull on an object. Forces can make things start moving, stop, speed up, slow down, change direction or change shape.
what tool measures force | what is a spring scale | how does a force meter work => A force meter, also called a newton meter or spring scale, measures force. A spring inside stretches more when you pull harder, and a scale shows the force in newtons.
what are balanced forces | what happens when forces are balanced | balanced force examples => Balanced forces are equal in size and push in opposite directions, so they cancel out. The object keeps doing what it was doing, staying still or moving at a steady speed.
what are unbalanced forces | what happens when forces are unbalanced | unbalanced force examples => Unbalanced forces don't cancel out, so there's an overall force. That makes the object speed up, slow down or change direction.
what is net force | how do you find the resultant force | what is resultant force | how do you add forces => Net force is the overall force after you combine all the forces on an object. Forces in the same direction add, and forces in opposite directions subtract.
what are contact forces | examples of contact forces | what is a contact force => Contact forces only act when things touch. Examples are friction, air resistance, tension, the normal force and a push from your hand.
what are non contact forces | examples of non contact forces | forces that act at a distance => Non-contact forces act without touching. Gravity, magnetism and the electric force can all pull or push across empty space.
what is the normal force | why dont i fall through the floor | what is a support force => The normal force is the push a surface gives back on an object resting on it. The floor pushes up on you just as hard as gravity pulls you down, so you don't sink in.
what is tension | tension force meaning | what is tension in a rope => Tension is the pulling force in a rope, string, chain or cable when it's pulled tight from both ends.
what is air resistance | what is drag | why do things slow down in air => Air resistance, or drag, is friction from air pushing against a moving object. It gets bigger the faster you go and the bigger your front surface is.
what is upthrust | what is buoyancy | why do things float | what is the buoyant force => Buoyancy, or upthrust, is the upward push a liquid or gas gives an object. If the upthrust is as big as the object's weight, the object floats.
what is archimedes principle | archimedes law of buoyancy | how does archimedes principle work => Archimedes' principle says an object in a fluid is pushed up by a force equal to the weight of the fluid it pushes aside. That's why a heavy steel ship can float.
why do some things sink | what makes things sink in water | why does a rock sink => Something sinks when it's denser than the liquid. A rock is denser than water, so the water can't push up hard enough to hold it.
what is weight | how do you calculate weight | weight formula physics => Weight is the force of gravity pulling on your mass. Weight equals mass times gravity, so on Earth a 50 kg person weighs about 50 x 9.8 = 490 newtons.
why do astronauts float in the iss | is there no gravity on the space station | why are astronauts weightless => There's still lots of gravity at the ISS, about 90% as strong as on the ground. Astronauts float because they and the station are falling around Earth together, which is called free fall.
what is free fall | what does free fall mean | what is freefall in physics => Free fall is when the only force acting on something is gravity. A falling skydiver is close to free fall right after jumping, before air resistance builds up.
what is terminal velocity | why do skydivers stop speeding up | what is terminal speed => Terminal velocity is the top speed a falling object reaches when air resistance grows to equal its weight. A skydiver falling belly down reaches about 200 km/h.
how does a parachute work | why do parachutes slow you down | parachute physics => A parachute has a huge surface that catches lots of air. That makes air resistance much bigger, so your terminal velocity drops to a safe landing speed.
what is gravitational acceleration | what is g in physics | what does 9.8 m/s2 mean => Near Earth's surface, gravity makes falling things speed up by about 9.8 meters per second every second. Scientists call this g.
who came up with the law of gravity | what is newtons law of gravity | what is universal gravitation => Isaac Newton published his law of universal gravitation in 1687. It says every mass pulls on every other mass, and the pull gets weaker with distance.
why does the moon not fall to earth | why does the moon stay in orbit | why doesnt the moon crash into earth => The Moon is falling toward Earth all the time, but it's also moving sideways fast. It keeps missing, so it curves around Earth in an orbit.
what is an orbit | how do orbits work | what keeps satellites in orbit => An orbit is the curved path one object takes around another because of gravity. A satellite moves sideways so fast that as it falls, Earth's surface curves away beneath it.
does gravity get weaker farther away | does gravity change with distance | inverse square law gravity => Yes. Gravity follows an inverse square law: if you get twice as far from a planet's center, its pull drops to one quarter.
is there gravity in space | does space have gravity | is space zero gravity => Yes, gravity reaches through space forever, it just gets weaker with distance. It's what holds the Moon, planets and galaxies together.
what is inertia | inertia meaning => Inertia is an object's tendency to keep doing what it's doing. When a car stops suddenly, your body keeps moving forward, which is why seatbelts matter.
what is momentum | how do you calculate momentum | momentum formula => Momentum is mass times velocity (p = mv). A heavy truck or a fast bullet both have a lot of momentum, so they're hard to stop.
what is conservation of momentum | is momentum conserved | law of conservation of momentum => In a collision, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after, as long as no outside force acts. That's why a gun recoils when it fires.
what is impulse in physics | impulse formula | how do airbags work physics => Impulse is force times the time it acts. Airbags and crumple zones make a crash last longer, so the force on you is smaller for the same change in momentum.
what is speed | speed formula | speed distance time => Speed is how fast something moves: speed = distance / time. If you ride 30 km in 2 hours, your average speed is 15 km/h.
what is velocity | difference between speed and velocity | speed vs velocity | is velocity the same as speed => Velocity is speed in a certain direction. A car going 50 km/h north and one going 50 km/h south have the same speed but different velocities.
what is acceleration | how do you calculate acceleration | acceleration formula => Acceleration is how quickly velocity changes: acceleration = change in velocity / time. It's measured in meters per second squared (m/s²).
what is deceleration | what is negative acceleration | slowing down in physics => Deceleration means slowing down. In physics it's acceleration in the opposite direction to the motion.
can you accelerate at a constant speed | does turning count as acceleration | circular motion acceleration => Yes. Changing direction is acceleration too, so a car going around a bend at a steady speed is accelerating toward the center of the curve.
what is centripetal force | why do things move in circles | what keeps you in a loop on a roller coaster => Centripetal force is any force pulling something toward the center of a circle. For a ball on a string it's tension, and for a planet it's gravity.
is centrifugal force real | what is centrifugal force | why do i get pushed outward on a merry go round => The outward push you feel on a spinning ride isn't a real force. Your body is trying to go straight because of inertia, while the ride pulls you around in a circle.
what is a distance time graph | how do you read a distance time graph | what does a flat line on a distance time graph mean => A distance-time graph shows how far something has moved over time. The steeper the line, the faster it's going, and a flat line means it has stopped.
what is a velocity time graph | how do you read a speed time graph | area under velocity time graph => A velocity-time graph shows speed over time. The slope is the acceleration, and the area under the line is the distance traveled.
what is displacement | distance vs displacement | difference between distance and displacement => Distance is the total path you travel. Displacement is how far you end up from the start, in a straight line, with a direction.
what is pressure | how do you calculate pressure | pressure formula => Pressure is force spread over an area: pressure = force / area. It's measured in pascals (Pa), and one pascal is one newton per square meter.
why are knives sharp | why does a sharp knife cut better | pressure and knives => A sharp edge has a tiny area, so the same push gives a huge pressure. That's why it cuts easily.
why dont you sink in snow with snowshoes | how do snowshoes spread weight => Snowshoes spread your weight over a bigger area, which lowers the pressure on the snow, so you don't sink in as much.
why does air pressure drop as you go higher | is there less air pressure on a mountain | air pressure and altitude => The higher you go, the less air is above you pushing down, so pressure drops. At the top of Mount Everest it's only about a third of sea level pressure.
why does water pressure increase with depth | water pressure and depth => Deeper water has more water above it pushing down. Pressure goes up by about one atmosphere for every 10 meters you dive.
how does a straw work | why does a drinking straw work | physics of drinking with a straw => When you suck on a straw, you lower the air pressure inside it. The normal air pressure pushing on the drink outside then pushes the liquid up into your mouth.
what is hydraulics | how do hydraulic systems work | how does a hydraulic jack lift a car => Hydraulics use a liquid to pass on pressure. Pushing a small piston creates pressure that pushes a bigger piston with a much larger force, which can lift a car.
what is pascals law | pascal principle | what did blaise pascal discover => Pascal's law says pressure applied to a liquid in a closed container spreads equally in every direction. Hydraulic brakes and jacks use this idea.
what is hookes law | how do springs work physics | spring stretch and force => Hooke's law says a spring stretches in proportion to the force on it: F = kx, where k is the spring's stiffness. Pull twice as hard and it stretches twice as far, until it's overstretched.
what is elastic deformation | what does elastic mean in physics | elastic vs plastic deformation => An elastic material goes back to its shape when you let go, like a rubber band. Plastic deformation is when it stays bent or stretched, like modeling clay.
what is static friction | why is it harder to start pushing something => Static friction holds still things in place, and it's usually bigger than kinetic friction, the friction on moving things. That's why a heavy box is hardest to get started.
how can you reduce friction | ways to reduce friction | what reduces friction => You can reduce friction with lubricants like oil, ball bearings, wheels, smooth surfaces, or a cushion of air like on an air hockey table.
how can you increase friction | ways to increase friction | why do shoes have grip => Rough surfaces, rubber soles, tire treads, sand on icy roads and pressing surfaces together harder all increase friction.
is friction useful | why do we need friction | good things about friction => Yes. Without friction you couldn't walk, cars couldn't brake, nails would slide out and knots would come undone.
why does friction make heat | why do your hands get warm when you rub them | friction and heat => When surfaces rub, friction turns some of the movement energy into heat. That's why rubbing your hands warms them up.
what is rolling resistance | why do wheels make things easier to move | why are wheels useful => A wheel rolls instead of sliding, so there's much less friction to fight. Rolling resistance is the small leftover friction from squashing the tire and the ground.
what is mass | what does mass mean | how is mass measured => Mass is the amount of matter in something. It's measured in kilograms and stays the same anywhere in the universe.
what is center of gravity | what is center of mass | why do things fall over => The center of gravity is the point where an object's weight seems to act. Things tip over when their center of gravity moves outside their base.
why are low wide things more stable | what makes something stable | stability physics => Something is more stable when it has a wide base and a low center of gravity. That's why race cars are low and wide.
what is a moment in physics | what is torque | how do you calculate a moment => A moment, or torque, is a turning force. Moment = force x distance from the pivot, so pushing a door far from its hinges is easier.
why are door handles far from the hinges | why is it hard to push a door near the hinge | door handle physics => A door handle is far from the hinges so your push makes a bigger turning effect. Pushing near the hinge needs much more force.
what is the principle of moments | how does a seesaw balance | seesaw physics => A seesaw balances when the turning effects on both sides are equal. A heavy person sitting close to the middle can balance a light person sitting far out.
what is mechanical advantage | how do machines make work easier | what does mechanical advantage mean => Mechanical advantage is how much a machine multiplies your force. You push with less force, but you have to push over a longer distance.
what is a pulley | how does a pulley work | how do pulleys make lifting easier => A pulley is a wheel with a rope around it. One fixed pulley changes the direction of your pull, and using several pulleys together lets you lift heavy loads with less force.
what is an inclined plane | what is a ramp in science | how does a ramp make work easier => An inclined plane is a ramp. It lets you raise something with less force by spreading the work over a longer distance.
what is a wedge | wedge simple machine | examples of a wedge => A wedge is two ramps back to back. It splits or cuts things, like an axe, a knife or a doorstop.
what is a screw simple machine | how is a screw a ramp | screw examples => A screw is a ramp wrapped around a pole. Turning it many times moves it a short way with a lot of force, like a jar lid or a bolt.
what is a wheel and axle | wheel and axle examples | how does a wheel and axle work => A wheel and axle is a big wheel joined to a smaller rod so they turn together. Turning the big wheel gives a strong turn at the axle, like a doorknob or a steering wheel.
what are the three classes of levers | first second third class lever | types of levers => In a first class lever the pivot is in the middle (seesaw). In a second class lever the load is in the middle (wheelbarrow). In a third class lever the effort is in the middle (tweezers).
what is a fulcrum | fulcrum meaning | where is the fulcrum => The fulcrum is the pivot point that a lever turns on, like the middle of a seesaw.
what is a compound machine | examples of compound machines | is a bike a compound machine => A compound machine is made of two or more simple machines. A bicycle uses wheels and axles, levers and gears, and scissors use levers and wedges.
how do gears work | what do gears do | gear ratio meaning => Gears are toothed wheels that turn each other. A small gear driving a big gear gives more turning force but less speed, and the reverse gives more speed.
why do bikes have gears | low gear vs high gear bike => Low gears make pedaling easy for hills but you move slower. High gears are harder to push but carry you farther with each pedal turn on flat ground.
what did galileo discover about falling objects | galileo leaning tower of pisa experiment | did galileo drop balls from pisa => Galileo showed that, without air resistance, heavy and light objects fall at the same rate. The famous story of dropping balls from the Leaning Tower of Pisa may not have actually happened.
what was the hammer and feather drop | feather and hammer on the moon | apollo 15 feather experiment => In 1971, Apollo 15 astronaut David Scott dropped a hammer and a feather on the Moon. With no air, they hit the ground at the same time, just as Galileo predicted.
what is a projectile | what path does a thrown ball take | projectile motion => A projectile is anything thrown or launched that then only feels gravity (and air). It follows a curved path called a parabola.
what angle throws a ball the farthest | best angle to launch a projectile | 45 degree launch angle => Without air resistance, 45 degrees gives the longest throw. With real air resistance the best angle is a bit lower.
how fast do you need to go to escape earth | what is escape velocity | escape speed of earth => Escape velocity from Earth's surface is about 11.2 km per second, around 40,000 km/h. At that speed, something would never fall back, ignoring air.
how fast do satellites go | orbital speed of the iss | how fast does the space station travel => The ISS travels at about 28,000 km/h (7.7 km per second) and goes around Earth about every 90 minutes.
what is weightlessness | why do people feel weightless on roller coasters | why do i feel light going over a hill => You feel weightless when you and everything around you fall at the same rate, so nothing pushes on you. On a roller coaster hill you get a brief taste of it.
what is g force | what does 3 g mean | how many gs can a person take => G-force compares the push you feel to normal gravity. At 3 g you feel three times heavier. Fighter pilots can handle about 9 g briefly with special suits.
what is a reaction force | examples of newtons third law | action and reaction examples => When you push on something, it pushes back on you just as hard. A rocket pushes gas down and the gas pushes the rocket up. When you swim, you push water back and it pushes you forward.
how do rockets work | how does a rocket move in space | what pushes a rocket => A rocket burns fuel and blasts hot gas out the back very fast. By Newton's third law, the gas pushes the rocket forward. It works in space because it doesn't need air to push against.
how do airplanes fly | what keeps a plane in the air | how do planes stay up => Wings are shaped and tilted so they push air down as the plane moves forward. The air pushes the wing up, creating lift. Engines provide thrust to keep the plane moving.
what are the four forces of flight | forces on an airplane | lift weight thrust drag => The four forces of flight are lift (up), weight (down), thrust (forward) and drag (backward). To climb, lift must beat weight, and to speed up, thrust must beat drag.
what is bernoullis principle | bernoulli effect | does faster air have lower pressure => Bernoulli's principle says faster moving fluid has lower pressure. It helps explain how wings, perfume sprayers and curving balls work.
how does a curveball curve | why do spinning balls curve | what is the magnus effect => A spinning ball drags air around with it. That makes the air pressure uneven on the two sides, pushing the ball sideways. This is called the Magnus effect.
why do helium balloons float | why does a helium balloon go up | helium balloon physics => Helium is much less dense than air, so the air pushes up on the balloon more than gravity pulls it down. The balloon rises until the air gets too thin.
how do hot air balloons fly | hot air balloon physics => Heating air makes it expand and become less dense. The hot air inside the balloon is lighter than the cool air around it, so the balloon floats up.
how do submarines dive and surface | how does a submarine sink | submarine ballast tanks => Submarines have ballast tanks. Filling them with water makes the sub denser so it sinks, and blowing the water out with air makes it lighter so it rises.
what is surface tension | why can bugs walk on water | how do water striders walk on water => Surface tension is the way water molecules at the surface pull together, like a thin stretchy skin. Light bugs like water striders can stand on it.
why do things weigh less in water | do you weigh less in a pool | why can you lift people in water => Water pushes up on you with buoyancy, which cancels part of your weight. That's why you can lift a friend easily in a pool.
what is viscosity | what does viscous mean | why is honey thick => Viscosity is how thick and slow flowing a liquid is. Honey has high viscosity and water has low viscosity.
what is work in physics | how do you calculate work done | work formula => Work is done when a force moves something. Work = force x distance, measured in joules. Holding a heavy box still does no work in physics, even if it's tiring.
what is power in physics | power formula | how do you calculate power => Power is how fast work is done or energy is used: power = energy / time. It's measured in watts, and 1 watt is 1 joule per second.
what is a horsepower | how much is one horsepower | horsepower in watts => One horsepower is about 746 watts. James Watt came up with the unit to compare steam engines with horses.
what is efficiency | how do you calculate efficiency | efficiency formula => Efficiency is how much of the energy put in comes out as useful energy: efficiency = useful output / total input x 100%. No machine is 100% efficient.
can you make a perpetual motion machine | is perpetual motion possible | why cant a machine run forever => No. Every real machine loses some energy, mostly as heat from friction, so it can't keep running forever without energy being added.
why do you lean forward on a bike going fast | why do cyclists crouch | how do cyclists reduce drag => Crouching makes your body smaller and smoother to the wind, which cuts air resistance. At high speed, most of a cyclist's effort goes into pushing air out of the way.
what is streamlining | why are cars and planes shaped like that | aerodynamic shapes => Streamlining means shaping something so air or water flows smoothly around it. It lowers drag, which is why planes, fish and fast cars have smooth, pointed shapes.
why do seat belts save lives | how do seatbelts work physics | seatbelt inertia => In a crash the car stops suddenly, but your body keeps moving because of inertia. A seatbelt stops you more gently and spreads the force across your strong hip and chest bones.
what is stopping distance | thinking distance and braking distance | what affects stopping distance => Stopping distance is thinking distance (before you react) plus braking distance. Speed, wet or icy roads, worn tires and tiredness all make it longer.
why do things fall at the same speed in a vacuum | what happens to a feather in a vacuum | falling in a vacuum => In a vacuum there's no air resistance, so gravity speeds everything up at the same rate. A feather and a bowling ball dropped together land together.
what is a vacuum | is space a vacuum | what does vacuum mean in science => A vacuum is a space with no matter in it, not even air. Outer space is very close to a perfect vacuum.
how do magnets and gravity compare | is gravity stronger than magnetism | which force is the weakest => Gravity is by far the weakest of the four basic forces. A tiny fridge magnet can hold a paperclip up against the gravity of the whole Earth.
what are the four fundamental forces | what are the basic forces of nature | four forces of physics => The four fundamental forces are gravity, electromagnetism, the strong nuclear force (holds nuclei together) and the weak nuclear force (causes some radioactive decay).
what is the strong nuclear force | what holds the nucleus together | strong force => The strong nuclear force holds protons and neutrons together in the nucleus. It's the strongest force but only works over tiny distances.
what is the weak nuclear force | weak force physics | what does the weak force do => The weak nuclear force causes some kinds of radioactive decay, like turning a neutron into a proton. It also helps power the Sun's fusion.
how does a slingshot work | how do catapults work | catapult physics => A slingshot or catapult stores energy when you stretch or bend it. Letting go turns that stored elastic energy into the projectile's kinetic energy.
why do ice skaters spin faster when they pull their arms in | spinning skater physics => When a skater pulls their arms in, their spin speeds up to keep their angular momentum the same. Stretching their arms out slows them down again.

what is a foucault pendulum | how does a pendulum prove earth spins | foucault pendulum explained => A Foucault pendulum is a very long pendulum whose swing slowly turns during the day. It was first shown in 1851 by Léon Foucault and proves Earth is rotating.
