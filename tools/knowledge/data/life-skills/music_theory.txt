# tag: music.theory
what is a scale in music | music scales explained | what is a major scale => A scale is a set of notes going up or down in order. A major scale follows the step pattern whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half, like C D E F G A B C on the white piano keys.
what is a whole step and half step | semitone and tone | what is a half step on piano => A half step, or semitone, is the smallest distance in Western music, like from one piano key to the very next key, black or white. A whole step, or tone, is two half steps.
what are sharps and flats | what does a sharp mean | what is a flat in music => A sharp (♯) raises a note by a half step, and a flat (♭) lowers it by a half step. A natural sign (♮) cancels them. The black piano keys are sharps or flats.
what is a key in music | what key is this song in | key signature meaning => The key is the home note and scale a song is built on, like C major or A minor. The key signature at the start of the staff shows which notes are always sharp or flat.
what is the circle of fifths | how does the circle of fifths work | circle of fifths explained => The circle of fifths arranges the 12 keys so each step clockwise goes up a fifth and adds one sharp, and each step counterclockwise adds one flat. It shows which keys are closely related and helps with chords.
what is a minor scale | natural minor scale | how is a minor scale built => The natural minor scale follows the pattern whole, half, whole, whole, half, whole, whole. A minor uses the same notes as C major, just starting on A, so they're called relative keys.
what is a pentatonic scale | five note scale | why does the pentatonic scale sound good => A pentatonic scale has five notes per octave, like the black keys on a piano. It has no clashing half steps, so almost any combination sounds good, which is why it's used a lot in rock, blues and folk music.
what is the blues scale | blues scale notes | how to play the blues scale => The blues scale is a minor pentatonic scale plus one extra note, the "blue note" or flat fifth. In A, it's A, C, D, E flat, E, G.
what is a chromatic scale | all 12 notes | chromatic meaning => The chromatic scale uses all 12 notes in an octave, moving by half steps, like playing every key, white and black, in order on a piano.
12 notes in western music | why are there only 7 note names => Western music uses 12 different notes in each octave. Seven get letter names, A to G, and the other five are sharps or flats of those letters.
what is an interval | music intervals | what is a fifth in music => An interval is the distance between two notes. For example, C to E is a major third, C to G is a perfect fifth, and C to the next C is an octave.
what is a triad | major chord vs minor chord | how is a chord built => A triad is a three note chord made by stacking thirds. A major chord has the root, a major third and a fifth, like C E G. A minor chord lowers the middle note, like C E flat G, which sounds sadder.
what is a seventh chord | 7th chord meaning | dominant seventh => A seventh chord adds a fourth note, the seventh, on top of a triad. A dominant seventh, like G B D F, has a tense sound that wants to resolve to the home chord.
what is a power chord | power chord guitar | why use power chords => A power chord uses just the root and the fifth, often with the octave, and isn't major or minor. It sounds strong with distortion, which is why it's big in rock and metal.
what is a chord progression | common chord progressions | what does i iv v mean => A chord progression is a series of chords in a song. Roman numerals show chords by their place in the key, so in C major, I is C, IV is F and V is G. I IV V is the backbone of tons of rock and blues songs.
what is the four chord progression | i v vi iv | why do so many songs use the same chords => The I V vi IV progression, like C G Am F in C major, is used in hundreds of pop songs because it sounds satisfying and flexible.
what is a 12 bar blues | 12 bar blues pattern | blues chord progression => The 12 bar blues uses the I, IV and V chords over 12 measures: four bars of I, two of IV, two of I, one of V, one of IV, one of I and one of V. It's the base of blues and early rock and roll.
what is rhythm | rhythm vs beat | what is the beat of a song => The beat is the steady pulse you tap your foot to. Rhythm is the pattern of long and short sounds placed over that beat.
what are note values | whole note half note quarter note | how long is a quarter note => In 4/4 time, a whole note lasts 4 beats, a half note 2, a quarter note 1, an eighth note half a beat and a sixteenth note a quarter of a beat.
what is a rest in music | music rests | what does a rest look like => A rest is a symbol for silence, with values matching notes: whole rest, half rest, quarter rest and so on. Rests are just as important as notes in rhythm.
what is a dotted note | dotted half note | what does the dot after a note mean => A dot adds half of the note's value. A dotted half note lasts 3 beats (2 plus 1), and a dotted quarter note lasts 1.5 beats.
what is a tie in music | tie vs slur | what does the curved line mean in music => A tie joins two notes of the same pitch into one longer note. A slur is a curved line over different notes meaning play them smoothly connected.
what is a measure in music | bar line meaning | what is a bar in music => A measure, or bar, is a section of music between two bar lines containing a set number of beats, shown by the time signature.
what is 3 4 time | waltz time | what does 6 8 time mean => 3/4 time has three beats per measure, like a waltz. 6/8 has six eighth note beats per measure, often felt as two big beats, giving a swaying feel.
what is syncopation | syncopated rhythm | offbeat rhythm => Syncopation is putting accents on the weaker beats or between beats, which makes music feel bouncy and surprising. It's huge in jazz, funk and pop.
what is swing rhythm | swing feel jazz | swung eighth notes => Swing plays pairs of eighth notes unevenly, long then short, giving a bouncy, relaxed groove. It's the classic feel of jazz.
what is a triplet | triplet rhythm | how to count triplets => A triplet fits three notes into the time normally taken by two. Count quarter note triplets as "tri-pl-et" spread evenly over two beats.
how do you read sheet music | reading music for beginners | how to read notes on a staff => Music is written on a staff of five lines. Each line and space is a note, and the clef tells you which. Notes higher on the staff are higher pitches, and their shapes show how long they last.
what are the notes on the treble clef lines | every good boy does fine | face in the treble clef => On the treble clef, the lines from bottom to top are E G B D F (Every Good Boy Does Fine), and the spaces spell F A C E.
what are the notes on the bass clef | good boys do fine always | all cows eat grass => On the bass clef, the lines from bottom to top are G B D F A (Good Boys Do Fine Always), and the spaces are A C E G (All Cows Eat Grass).
what is middle c | where is middle c on the piano | middle c on the staff => Middle C is the C near the middle of a piano keyboard, usually right under the brand name. It sits on a small extra line, called a ledger line, between the treble and bass staves.
what are ledger lines | notes above the staff | extra lines in music => Ledger lines are short extra lines above or below the staff for notes too high or low to fit on the five lines.
what does piano and forte mean in music | music dynamics | what does ff mean in music => Dynamics show how loud to play: p (piano) means soft, f (forte) means loud, mp and mf are medium soft and medium loud, and pp and ff are very soft and very loud. They come from Italian.
what is a crescendo | crescendo vs decrescendo | what does diminuendo mean => A crescendo means gradually getting louder, and a decrescendo or diminuendo means gradually getting softer. They're often written as long wedge shapes.
what does allegro mean | music tempo words | what is adagio => Tempo markings are often Italian: largo is very slow, adagio slow, andante walking pace, moderato moderate, allegro fast and presto very fast.
what does staccato mean | staccato vs legato | music articulation => Staccato, shown by a dot above or below a note, means short and detached. Legato means smooth and connected.
what is a fermata | fermata symbol | what does the bird's eye mean in music => A fermata, an arc with a dot under it, means hold that note or rest longer than its usual value, as long as the performer or conductor decides.
what is a repeat sign | repeat bar in music | what does da capo mean => A repeat sign, two dots by a double bar line, means play that section again. D.C. (da capo) means go back to the beginning, and D.S. (dal segno) means go back to the sign.
what makes a good melody | writing a catchy tune | how to write a melody => A melody is a series of single notes heard as a tune you can hum. Good melodies often move mostly by small steps with a few leaps, repeat ideas, and end on a stable note.
what is a riff | riff vs lick | famous guitar riffs => A riff is a short repeated musical phrase, often the main hook of a rock song. A lick is a short phrase used in solos.
what is a hook in a song | catchy hook | what makes a song catchy => A hook is the catchiest part of a song, like a memorable chorus line or riff that sticks in your head. Repetition, a simple rhythm and a singable melody make hooks work.
what is song structure | verse chorus bridge | parts of a song => Many pop songs follow intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus, outro. Verses tell the story, the chorus repeats the main hook, and the bridge brings something new.
what is a key change | modulation in music | why do songs change key => A key change, or modulation, moves a song to a different key, often up near the end to add energy and excitement.
what is harmony in singing | how to sing harmony | singing in thirds => Singing harmony means singing different notes that blend with the melody, often a third above or below. It's easier when you know the melody well first.
what is perfect pitch | absolute pitch | can you learn perfect pitch => Perfect pitch is being able to name or sing a note without any reference. It's rare. Relative pitch, recognizing notes by their distance from other notes, can be trained by anyone and is more useful.
what is ear training | how to play by ear | learning music by ear => Ear training means practicing recognizing intervals, chords and rhythms by sound. Start by picking out simple melodies you know on an instrument, one note at a time.
what is transposing | how to transpose a song | move a song to another key => Transposing is moving a piece to a different key by shifting every note by the same interval. Singers often transpose songs to fit their voice range, and a guitar capo transposes chords up easily.
what is a capo | how does a capo work | why use a capo on guitar => A capo clamps across the guitar's strings at a fret, raising the pitch so you can play songs in higher keys using the same easy chord shapes.
what is a cadence in music | perfect cadence | why do songs sound finished => A cadence is a chord pattern that ends a phrase. A perfect cadence, V to I, sounds complete, like a period at the end of a sentence.
what is dissonance | consonance vs dissonance | why do some notes clash => Consonant notes sound stable and blend, like a major chord. Dissonant notes clash and create tension, like two notes a half step apart. Music often uses tension and release.
what is a time signature 5 4 | odd time signatures | songs in 7 8 => Odd time signatures have uneven groupings of beats. "Take Five" by Dave Brubeck is in 5/4, and many progressive rock and video game songs use 7/8.
what is bpm for different genres | typical tempo of songs | how fast is house music => Rough ranges: hip hop is often around 80 to 100 BPM, pop around 100 to 130, house around 120 to 130, drum and bass around 160 to 180. Ballads are slower.
what is a metronome | why practice with a metronome | metronome app => A metronome clicks a steady beat at a set tempo. Practicing with one builds solid timing. Start slow and raise the tempo as you get it right.
what is music theory | do i need to learn music theory | why learn music theory => Music theory explains how music works: notes, scales, chords, rhythm and form. You can play without it, but it helps you learn songs faster, write your own and talk with other musicians.
what is a mode in music | dorian mode | music modes explained => Modes are scales made by starting a major scale on different notes. Dorian, starting on the second note, sounds minor but brighter, and Mixolydian, starting on the fifth, sounds major but bluesy.
what is an arpeggio | broken chord | how to play arpeggios => An arpeggio is playing the notes of a chord one at a time instead of together, like C, E, G, C going up. They're great finger practice and sound flowing.
what is a glissando | piano glissando | what does gliss mean => A glissando is a slide from one note to another, like running your fingernail across piano keys or sliding up a trombone.
what is vibrato | how to do vibrato | vibrato singing => Vibrato is a slight, regular wobble in pitch that makes notes sound warm and alive. Singers and string players use it on long notes.
what is an octave higher | why do notes repeat | what makes an octave sound the same => A note an octave higher vibrates exactly twice as fast. That simple 2 to 1 ratio makes the two notes blend so well they get the same letter name.
what is a 440 hz | concert pitch | what is a440 => A440 is the standard tuning reference where the A above middle C vibrates 440 times per second. Most instruments and tuners use it.
what does tempo rubato mean | rubato | flexible tempo => Rubato means stretching and squeezing the tempo for expression, speeding up and slowing down slightly. It's common in romantic piano music.
what is counterpoint | polyphony | two melodies at once => Counterpoint is two or more independent melodies played at the same time that fit together. Bach was a master of it.
what is a motif in music | musical motif | beethoven fifth motif => A motif is a short musical idea that repeats and develops through a piece. The famous four note "da da da dum" opening of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony is a motif.
what is a leitmotif | character themes in music | star wars music themes => A leitmotif is a musical theme tied to a character, place or idea. Film composers like John Williams use them, such as Darth Vader's Imperial March.
