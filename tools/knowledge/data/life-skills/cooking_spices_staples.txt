# tag: life.cooking.spices
what is the difference between herbs and spices | herbs vs spices | is pepper a spice => Herbs are the leaves of plants, like basil, parsley and mint. Spices come from other parts, like seeds, bark, roots or berries, such as cumin, cinnamon, ginger and black pepper.
when do i add herbs when cooking | fresh herbs timing | when to add dried herbs => Add dried herbs and hardy fresh herbs like rosemary and thyme early so their flavor spreads. Add tender fresh herbs like basil, parsley and cilantro at the end so they stay bright.
how much dried herb equals fresh | dried vs fresh herb ratio | convert fresh herbs to dried => Use about one third as much dried herb as fresh, since dried is stronger. So 1 tablespoon fresh is about 1 teaspoon dried.
what is basil | how to use basil | basil in cooking => Basil is a sweet, fragrant herb used in Italian and Thai cooking, like pesto, tomato sauce and caprese salad. Add it at the end and tear rather than chop to avoid bruising.
what is rosemary | how to use rosemary | rosemary in cooking => Rosemary is a woody herb with needle like leaves and a piney flavor. It's great with roast potatoes, chicken and lamb. Strip the leaves off the tough stem and chop finely.
what is thyme | how to use thyme | thyme in cooking => Thyme has tiny leaves with an earthy, slightly lemony flavor. It's used in soups, stews, roasts and sauces. Run your fingers down the stem backward to pull off the leaves.
what is parsley | flat leaf vs curly parsley | how to use parsley => Parsley has a fresh, green flavor. Flat leaf (Italian) parsley is more flavorful for cooking, while curly parsley is often used as a garnish. Add it at the end of cooking.
what is mint used for in cooking | mint in food | cooking with mint => Mint adds a cool, fresh flavor to drinks, fruit salads, Middle Eastern dishes like tabbouleh, yogurt sauces and desserts. Add it fresh at the end.
what is dill | how to use dill | dill flavor => Dill is a feathery herb with a fresh, slightly tangy flavor. It goes with fish, potatoes, eggs, yogurt sauces and of course pickles.
what is chili powder | chili powder vs cayenne | is chili powder spicy => In the US, chili powder is usually a mild blend of ground chiles, cumin, garlic and oregano. Cayenne is pure ground hot red pepper and much spicier.
what is black pepper | where does pepper come from | peppercorns colors => Black pepper comes from dried berries of a vine called Piper nigrum, grown in tropical places like India and Vietnam. Green, white and black peppercorns come from the same plant at different stages.
what is garlic powder | garlic powder vs fresh garlic | garlic salt => Garlic powder is dried, ground garlic, milder and sweeter than fresh. About 1 eighth of a teaspoon equals one clove. Garlic salt is garlic powder mixed with salt, so cut back on other salt.
what is onion powder | how to use onion powder | onion powder vs onion => Onion powder is dried, ground onion. It adds savory flavor to rubs, dips and sauces without chunks. About 1 tablespoon equals one medium onion in flavor.
what is nutmeg | how to use nutmeg | nutmeg in food => Nutmeg is the seed of a tropical tree, with a warm, sweet flavor. A small pinch goes in eggnog, pumpkin pie, white sauces and spinach. Too much tastes bitter.
what is turmeric | turmeric flavor | why is curry yellow => Turmeric is a bright yellow spice from a root related to ginger, with an earthy, slightly bitter taste. It gives curry powder and some mustards their yellow color. It stains, so be careful.
what is curry powder | is curry a spice | curry vs curry powder => Curry powder is a spice blend, usually turmeric, coriander, cumin, ginger and chili, created for British cooking. In India, curry means a sauced dish, and cooks mix their own spices.
what is garam masala | garam masala spices | how to use garam masala => Garam masala is a warm Indian spice blend often with cumin, coriander, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves and black pepper. It's usually added near the end of cooking.
what is smoked paprika | sweet vs smoked paprika | hot paprika => Paprika is ground dried red peppers. Sweet paprika is mild, hot paprika is spicy, and smoked paprika is made from peppers dried over wood smoke, giving a smoky flavor.
what is cardamom | how to use cardamom | cardamom flavor => Cardamom is a fragrant spice from seed pods, with a sweet, floral, slightly minty taste. It's used in chai, Scandinavian pastries and Indian rice dishes.
what are cloves | how to use cloves | clove spice => Cloves are dried flower buds with a strong, warm, sweet flavor. A few go in ham, mulled drinks, pumpkin pie spice and rice. They're very strong, so use few.
what is allspice | is allspice a blend | allspice flavor => Allspice is a single spice from the dried berries of a Caribbean tree. It's named for tasting like a mix of cinnamon, nutmeg and cloves. It's key in Jamaican jerk seasoning.
what is pumpkin pie spice | pumpkin spice ingredients | make pumpkin pie spice => Pumpkin pie spice is a blend of cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, and cloves or allspice. Make your own with 4 parts cinnamon, 2 parts ginger and 1 part each nutmeg and cloves.
what is italian seasoning | italian seasoning ingredients | italian herbs blend => Italian seasoning is a dried herb mix, usually basil, oregano, thyme, rosemary and marjoram. It's great in pasta sauce, pizza and roasted vegetables.
what is cajun seasoning | cajun vs creole | cajun spices => Cajun seasoning from Louisiana usually includes paprika, cayenne, garlic, onion, black pepper and oregano or thyme. Creole blends are similar, often with more herbs.
what is taco seasoning | homemade taco seasoning | taco spice mix => Taco seasoning is usually chili powder, cumin, paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, oregano, salt and pepper. Making your own lets you control salt and spice.
what is everything bagel seasoning | everything seasoning ingredients | everything bagel spice => It's a mix of sesame seeds, poppy seeds, dried garlic, dried onion and coarse salt. It's great on eggs, avocado toast and roasted vegetables.
what is za'atar | zaatar spice | how to use zaatar => Za'atar is a Middle Eastern blend of dried thyme or oregano, toasted sesame seeds, tangy sumac and salt. Mix it with olive oil for dipping bread.
what is sumac spice | is sumac poisonous | sumac flavor => Culinary sumac is a dried, ground red berry with a tangy, lemony flavor, used in Middle Eastern food. It's a different plant from poison sumac.
what is vanilla bean | why is vanilla expensive | where does vanilla come from => Vanilla comes from the pods of an orchid, mostly grown in Madagascar. Each flower must be hand pollinated, and curing takes months, which makes it expensive.
how do i toast spices | toasting whole spices | bloom spices in oil => Heat whole spices in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, for 1 to 3 minutes until fragrant. Or bloom ground spices by stirring them in hot oil for 30 seconds. Both boost flavor.
what is kosher salt | kosher salt vs table salt | sea salt vs table salt => Kosher salt has bigger, flaky crystals that are easy to pinch, and no iodine. Table salt is fine and denser, so a teaspoon is saltier. Sea salt comes from evaporated seawater.
why is salt iodized | what is iodized salt | iodine in salt => Iodine was added to table salt starting in the 1920s because many people lacked it, which caused thyroid problems like goiter. Iodized salt helped fix that.
what is flaky salt | finishing salt | maldon salt => Flaky salt has large, thin crystals that add crunch and bursts of saltiness. Sprinkle it on food at the end, like on cookies, steak or salads.
what are the five basic tastes | sweet sour salty bitter umami => The five basic tastes are sweet, sour, salty, bitter and umami. Umami is a savory taste found in mushrooms, tomatoes, parmesan, soy sauce and meat.
# tag: life.cooking.cheese
what is cheddar cheese | mild vs sharp cheddar | where is cheddar from => Cheddar comes from the English village of Cheddar. Mild cheddar is aged a few months and creamy, while sharp and extra sharp are aged longer and taste stronger. Orange cheddar is colored with annatto.
what is parmesan | parmigiano reggiano | real parmesan => Parmigiano Reggiano is a hard Italian cheese aged at least 12 months, from a specific region in Italy. It's salty and nutty, great grated on pasta. Generic parmesan is a cheaper copy.
what is swiss cheese | why does swiss cheese have holes | emmental => Swiss cheese like Emmental has holes, called eyes, made by carbon dioxide gas bubbles from bacteria during aging. Tiny hay particles in the milk help the holes form.
what is brie | brie rind edible | brie cheese => Brie is a soft, creamy French cheese with a white, fuzzy rind made by a mold. The rind is edible. It's best served at room temperature.
what is blue cheese | is blue cheese mold safe | gorgonzola => Blue cheese has safe, edible blue mold (Penicillium) added on purpose, giving it a sharp, tangy taste. Gorgonzola, Roquefort and Stilton are famous types.
what is feta | feta cheese | what is feta made of => Feta is a salty, crumbly Greek cheese traditionally made from sheep's milk or a mix with goat's milk and stored in brine. It's great in salads.
what is ricotta | ricotta cheese uses | what is ricotta made from => Ricotta is a soft, mild, slightly grainy cheese traditionally made by reheating whey left from making other cheese. It's used in lasagna, cannoli and pancakes.
what is cottage cheese | cottage cheese vs ricotta | cottage cheese curds => Cottage cheese is fresh cheese curds mixed with a little cream, mild and lumpy. It's high in protein and often eaten with fruit. Ricotta is smoother.
what is american cheese | is american cheese real cheese | processed cheese => American cheese is a processed cheese made by melting cheeses like cheddar with emulsifying salts so it melts smoothly. That's why it's great on burgers and grilled cheese.
what is gouda | smoked gouda | gouda cheese => Gouda is a Dutch cheese named after the city of Gouda. Young gouda is mild and creamy, while aged gouda gets firm, sweet and crunchy with crystals.
what is goat cheese | chevre | goat cheese taste => Goat cheese, or chevre, is made from goat's milk and is soft, tangy and creamy when fresh. It's great crumbled on salads or spread on crackers.
what is halloumi | why doesnt halloumi melt => Halloumi is a firm, salty cheese from Cyprus that holds its shape when heated, so you can grill or pan fry it until golden.
what is paneer | indian cheese | paneer vs tofu => Paneer is a fresh Indian cheese made by curdling hot milk with lemon juice or vinegar. It doesn't melt, so it's cooked in curries like palak paneer.
what are the crunchy bits in aged cheese | crystals in cheese | tyrosine crystals => Crunchy crystals in aged cheese like parmesan and gouda are clusters of amino acids or calcium lactate formed over time. They're safe and a sign of age.
how do i make a cheese board | charcuterie board ideas | snack board tips => Pick 3 cheeses with different textures (soft, firm, hard), add crackers or bread, fruit, nuts, pickles and maybe meats. Take cheese out 30 to 60 minutes early for best flavor.
# tag: life.cooking.grains
how do i cook rice on the stove | stovetop rice | rice to water ratio => For long grain white rice, rinse 1 cup, add it to 1.5 to 2 cups boiling salted water, cover, simmer on low about 15 to 18 minutes, then rest 5 to 10 minutes covered before fluffing.
what are the types of rice | long grain vs short grain rice | jasmine vs basmati => Long grain rice like basmati and jasmine cooks fluffy and separate. Short grain like sushi rice and arborio is sticky or creamy. Brown rice keeps its bran layer, so it's chewier and takes longer.
how do i cook brown rice | brown rice cooking time | brown rice water ratio => Use about 1 cup brown rice to 2 to 2.5 cups water, simmer covered for about 40 to 45 minutes, then rest 10 minutes. It takes longer because of its bran layer.
what is arborio rice | how to make risotto | risotto rice => Arborio is a short grain Italian rice that releases starch for creamy risotto. Toast it in butter, then add hot broth a ladle at a time, stirring often, for about 18 to 20 minutes.
what are the different pasta shapes | pasta shapes and sauces | which pasta for which sauce => Long thin pastas like spaghetti suit oil or light tomato sauces. Ridged tubes like penne and rigatoni hold chunky sauces. Shapes like shells and fusilli grab cheesy or creamy sauces.
what is fresh pasta vs dried | fresh pasta | is fresh pasta better => Fresh pasta has eggs, is tender and cooks in 2 to 4 minutes. Dried pasta is firmer, cooks in 8 to 12 minutes and holds up to hearty sauces. Neither is always better.
how do i cook oatmeal | stovetop oatmeal | rolled oats vs steel cut => For rolled oats, simmer 1 part oats with 2 parts water or milk for about 5 minutes. Steel cut oats are chopped whole oats, chewier, and take about 20 to 30 minutes.
how do i make overnight oats | overnight oats recipe | no cook oatmeal => Mix equal parts rolled oats and milk, add a spoon of yogurt, chia seeds or fruit and a little honey, and refrigerate overnight. Eat it cold in the morning.
what is barley | how to cook barley | pearl barley => Barley is a chewy whole grain used in soups and salads. Pearl barley has the outer layer polished off and cooks in about 25 to 30 minutes.
what is polenta | polenta vs grits | how to make polenta => Polenta is Italian cornmeal porridge. Grits are a similar Southern US dish, often from hominy corn. Both are cooked by slowly stirring cornmeal into simmering water or milk.
what is farro | ancient grains | farro vs quinoa => Farro is an ancient wheat grain with a nutty taste and chewy texture, great in salads and soups. Other ancient grains include spelt, amaranth and millet.
what is bulgur | bulgur wheat | tabbouleh grain => Bulgur is cracked wheat that has been partly cooked, so it just needs soaking or a short simmer. It's the grain in tabbouleh.
what are the types of bread | different breads around the world | bread types => Examples include sourdough, baguette, ciabatta, rye, pita, naan, tortillas, focaccia, bagels and challah. Flatbreads like pita and naan are among the oldest kinds of bread.
what is a baguette | french bread | why are baguettes long => A baguette is a long, thin French bread with a crisp crust and chewy inside. Its shape gives lots of crust. In 2022, UNESCO added baguette making to its cultural heritage list.
