# tag: life.money.history
why cant the government just print more money | printing more money | why not print money for everyone => If a government prints lots of money without more goods being made, prices rise because more money chases the same stuff. Extreme cases, like Zimbabwe in 2008, caused hyperinflation where prices doubled in about a day.
what is hyperinflation | hyperinflation examples | weimar germany money => Hyperinflation is extremely fast price growth. In Germany in 1923, prices rose so fast that people carried cash in wheelbarrows, and money was worth less than the paper it was printed on.
what is fiat money | fiat currency meaning | why is paper money worth anything => Fiat money is money that has value because a government declares it legal tender and people trust it, not because it's backed by gold. The dollar and euro are fiat money.
who prints us money | bureau of engraving and printing | where is money made => Paper bills are printed by the Bureau of Engraving and Printing in Washington, D.C., and Fort Worth, Texas. Coins are made by the US Mint in places like Philadelphia and Denver.
is the penny being discontinued | did the us stop making pennies | penny production ending => Yes. Pennies cost more than a cent to make, and the US Mint struck its last circulating pennies in November 2025. Existing pennies are still legal money.
how much does it cost to make a penny | penny costs more than a cent | cost of making coins => In 2024, a penny cost the US Mint about 3.7 cents to make and a nickel about 14 cents, which is why the penny was stopped.
is the 2 dollar bill real | 2 dollar bill rare | are 2 dollar bills still made => Yes, 2 dollar bills are real, legal money and are still printed occasionally. They're just uncommon in everyday use, so many people think they're rare.
how do i spot a fake bill | counterfeit money | how to check if money is real => Real US bills have a watermark and a security thread visible when held to light, color shifting ink on the number in the corner on bills 10 dollars and up, and raised printing you can feel.
what happens to old worn out money | shredded money | how long does a dollar bill last => Banks send worn bills to the Federal Reserve, which shreds them and replaces them. A 1 dollar bill lasts roughly 6 to 7 years in circulation.
what are bills made of | is money paper | what is a dollar made of => US paper money is about 75 percent cotton and 25 percent linen, which is why it survives the washing machine better than regular paper.
what is the most valuable currency | strongest currency | highest value currency => As of 2025, the Kuwaiti dinar was the highest valued currency per unit, worth over 3 US dollars. The US dollar is the most widely used currency in world trade.
what currencies do other countries use | currency names | money in other countries => Examples include the euro in much of Europe, the pound in the UK, the yen in Japan, the yuan (renminbi) in China, the rupee in India, the peso in Mexico and the Canadian dollar in Canada.
what is a central bank | central bank meaning | what does a central bank do => A central bank manages a country's money supply and interest rates and acts as a bank for banks. Examples are the Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Bank of England.
what happens when interest rates go up | fed raises rates | higher interest rates effect => Higher interest rates make loans like mortgages and car loans more expensive, which slows spending and can lower inflation. Savers earn more on their savings.
# tag: life.money.econ2
what is specialization | division of labor | why do people specialize => Specialization means people or countries focus on what they do best and trade for the rest. Division of labor splits work into steps, like an assembly line, so more gets made.
what is an entrepreneur risk | why businesses fail | most startups fail => Many new businesses fail in their first few years because of low sales, high costs or tough competition. Entrepreneurs take a risk, which is why successful ones can earn a lot.
what is marketing | what do marketers do | marketing meaning => Marketing is figuring out what customers want and getting a product in front of them, through research, design, ads, social media and pricing.
what is advertising | how ads work | why companies advertise => Advertising is paid messages that try to get people to buy something or believe something. Ads often use emotions, celebrities and limited time offers.
how do ads target me | targeted ads | why do i see ads for things i searched => Many apps and sites track what you view and search to show you ads you're more likely to click. Privacy settings and fewer app permissions reduce tracking.
what is an influencer sponsorship | sponsored posts | how influencers make money => Influencers earn money when brands pay them to show products. In the US, they must disclose paid posts with labels like "ad" or "sponsored."
what is a loss leader | stores selling at a loss | loss leader example => A loss leader is a product sold cheaply, even at a loss, to bring customers in who will buy other things. Game consoles are sometimes sold this way, with profits from games.
what is dynamic pricing | why do prices change online | surge pricing => Dynamic pricing changes prices based on demand, time or the customer. Ride apps charge more during busy times, and airline tickets change daily.
how do free games make money | free to play business model | why are free games free => Free to play games earn money from in game purchases, battle passes, cosmetics and ads. They're designed to make spending feel easy, so setting a limit helps.
how do i get a refund for an app purchase | apple refund request | accidental in app purchase => For Apple purchases, a parent can request a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com within a reasonable time. Turning on Ask to Buy in Family Sharing prevents accidental purchases.
what is the economy of a game | in game economy | game currency exchange => Many games have economies with currencies, prices and trading, like Robux or V Bucks. Game currency usually can't be turned back into real money.
what is a black market | black market meaning | illegal market => A black market is illegal buying and selling, often of banned goods or to avoid taxes. Buying game items or accounts from shady sites is risky and breaks most games' rules.
what is a recession sign | how do you know a recession is coming | recession indicators => Signs include rising unemployment, falling spending, falling business profits and a shrinking economy. Officially, the NBER decides when US recessions start and end.
what is the cost of living | cost of living meaning | why are some places expensive => Cost of living is how much it costs to cover basics like housing, food and transport in a place. Big cities usually cost more, mostly because of housing.
what is rent | how does renting work | security deposit => Rent is money paid regularly to live in a home owned by someone else. Renters usually pay a security deposit, which is returned if the place is left in good condition.
what is a kilowatt hour | kwh meaning | how much electricity does my pc use => A kilowatt hour is using 1,000 watts for one hour. A 500 watt gaming PC running 2 hours uses 1 kWh, which often costs about 10 to 30 cents in the US.
what is a salary vs hourly | salary vs wage | hourly pay => Hourly workers are paid for each hour worked and often get overtime. Salaried workers get a set yearly amount, regardless of hours.
what is overtime pay | overtime rules | time and a half => In the US, many hourly workers get overtime of 1.5 times their normal pay for hours over 40 in a week, called time and a half.
what is a benefit at a job | job benefits | health insurance from work => Benefits are extras from a job beyond pay, like health insurance, paid vacation, sick days and retirement plans.
what are child labor laws | how many hours can a 14 year old work | teen work hours => US federal law generally lets 14 and 15 year olds work limited hours outside school, like up to 3 hours on school days and 18 hours in a school week. States can have stricter rules.
