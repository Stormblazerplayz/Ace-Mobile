# tag: life.money.saving
how much of my allowance should i save | saving part of allowance | what percent should i save => A good habit is saving at least 10 to 20 percent of any money you get, or try splitting it into spend, save and share. Saving first, before you spend, makes it easier.
what is the save spend share method | three jar money system | spend save give jars => Split your money into three jars or accounts: one to spend now, one to save for goals and one to give to charity or others. Many kids put the biggest share in save.
how do i save up for something big | saving for a goal | how long to save for something => Write down the price, decide how much you can save each week, and divide to see how many weeks it will take. Track progress on a chart, and put the money somewhere you won't be tempted to spend it.
what is an emergency fund | why have emergency savings | rainy day fund => An emergency fund is money set aside for surprises, like a broken phone screen or a bike repair. Adults often aim for 3 to 6 months of expenses. Even a small one helps kids avoid stress.
how do i stop impulse buying | stop spending money on stuff | impulse purchase tips => Wait 24 hours or even a week before buying anything that isn't planned. Ask if you'll still want it next month, and compare the price to how many hours of work or weeks of allowance it costs.
how do i save money on games | buying games cheaper | are game sales worth it => Wait for seasonal sales, check if a game is in a subscription you already have, buy used physical copies, and set a budget for in game purchases. Ask if you'll actually play it.
are in game purchases a waste of money | microtransactions | should i buy loot boxes => In game purchases add up fast, and loot boxes give random rewards, which works a bit like gambling. Set a monthly limit with a parent, and prefer purchases where you know exactly what you get.
how do i ask for an allowance | should kids get an allowance | how much allowance is normal => Talk to your parents calmly, suggest chores you could do, and explain you want to learn to manage money. A common rule of thumb is about a dollar per week for each year of age, but it really depends on the family.
what is a piggy bank | why use a piggy bank | clear jar for saving => A piggy bank is a container for saving coins and bills at home. A clear jar lets you see your money grow, which keeps you motivated.
how do i track my spending | spending tracker for kids | where does my money go => Write down every purchase in a notebook or app for a month, then add it up by category. Most people are surprised how much small things add up.
how do i save money on food | eating out less | cheap meals tips => Cooking at home, packing lunch and bringing a water bottle save a lot compared with buying food out. Buying store brands and planning meals helps too.
what are store brands | store brand vs name brand | is generic as good => Store brands, or generic brands, are sold under the store's own name and are often made in similar factories as name brands. They're usually cheaper and often just as good.
what is unit price | how to compare prices | which size is the better deal => Unit price is the price per ounce, gram or item, often on the shelf tag. Comparing unit prices shows which size is actually cheaper. Bigger isn't always a better deal.
what is a sale really | is a sale always a good deal | buy one get one free worth it => A sale is only a deal if you were going to buy it anyway. Spending $30 to save $10 on something you don't need costs you $30.
how do coupons work | what is a promo code | how to use coupons => Coupons and promo codes take money off the price at checkout. Use them only for things you already planned to buy.
what is delayed gratification | marshmallow test | why waiting is good with money => Delayed gratification means waiting for something better later instead of something smaller now. With money, it's skipping small buys now to afford a bigger goal later.
how do i make my money last | running out of money | budget my allowance => Plan what you'll spend before you get it, spread fun spending over the week or month, and keep your savings untouched. Check your balance before you buy.
what is a want vs a need example | is a phone a need or a want | needs and wants for kids => Needs keep you safe and healthy, like food, shelter and basic clothes. Wants are nice to have, like new games or brand name shoes. Some things, like a phone, can be both depending on how it's used.
what is minimalism | buy less stuff | how to want less => Minimalism means owning fewer things and focusing on what you really use and love. Many people find it saves money and makes cleaning easier.

# tag: life.money.budget
how do i make a budget as a kid | kid budget example | simple budget for teens => List all the money you expect to get, then plan where each dollar goes: savings first, then needs, then fun. If spending is more than income, cut something. Check it each month.
what is the 50 30 20 rule | 50 30 20 budget | budget rule of thumb => The 50/30/20 rule splits take home pay into about 50 percent for needs, 30 percent for wants and 20 percent for savings and paying off debt. It's a simple starting point for adults.
what is income | what is an expense | income vs expenses => Income is money coming in, like allowance, gifts or pay from jobs. Expenses are money going out, like food, games and gifts for others.
what is a fixed expense | fixed vs variable expenses | examples of fixed costs => A fixed expense stays the same every month, like rent or a phone plan. A variable expense changes, like groceries or entertainment.
what is a subscription | why subscriptions add up | how to cancel a subscription => A subscription charges you regularly, like monthly for streaming or games. Small monthly fees add up over a year, so check what you actually use and have a parent help cancel the rest.
what is a free trial trap | free trial auto renew | why do free trials need a card => Many free trials automatically start charging when the trial ends unless you cancel. Set a reminder before the end date, and don't sign up without a parent's okay.
what happens if you spend more than you earn | overspending | living beyond your means => If you spend more than you earn, you run out of money or have to borrow, which can lead to debt. A budget helps you notice early and adjust.
how much does it cost to have a pet | pet costs per year | can i afford a dog => Pets cost more than the purchase price: food, vet visits, vaccines, toys, grooming and boarding can add up to hundreds or thousands of dollars a year depending on the pet.
how much does a phone plan cost | phone bill cost | why is my phone bill so high => Costs vary a lot by carrier, data amount and country. Extra charges can come from going over data, buying apps or in app items, and paying off the phone itself monthly.
what is a bill you pay | how do bills work | paying bills on time => A bill is a request for money owed for something, like electricity, water or internet. Paying on time avoids late fees and keeps services running.

# tag: life.money.banking
how do i open a bank account as a kid | kids bank account | can a 12 year old have a bank account => Most banks let kids open a savings or youth checking account with a parent or guardian as a joint owner. Bring ID for both of you and a small deposit, and ask about fees and minimum balances.
what is a checking account | checking vs savings account | what is a checking account for => A checking account is for everyday spending, with a debit card and easy withdrawals. A savings account is for money you're setting aside, and usually earns more interest.
how do i deposit money | depositing cash at a bank | mobile check deposit => You can deposit cash or checks at a bank teller or ATM, or deposit checks with a phone photo in the bank's app. Keep the receipt until it shows in your account.
how do i write a check | parts of a check | filling out a check => Write the date, the name of who you're paying, the amount in numbers in the box and in words on the line, then sign at the bottom right. The memo line says what it's for.
what is a debit card | how does a debit card work | is a debit card safe => A debit card takes money straight from your bank account when you buy something. Keep your PIN secret, and tell a parent and the bank right away if it's lost.
what is a pin number | how to keep my pin safe | pin for bank card => A PIN is a secret number you type to use your card. Never share it, cover the keypad when typing, and don't write it on the card.
what is a prepaid card | prepaid card for kids | gift card vs prepaid card => A prepaid card is loaded with a set amount of money ahead of time, so you can only spend what's on it. Gift cards are similar but often only work at one store.
what is overdraft | what happens if you overdraw your account | overdraft fee => Overdraft is spending more than is in your account. Banks may decline the payment or allow it and charge a fee. Checking your balance before spending avoids it.
what is a bank statement | reading a bank statement | what is my balance => A bank statement lists all money in and out of your account over a month, with your starting and ending balance. Checking it helps you catch mistakes or fraud.
what is a credit union | bank vs credit union | are credit unions good => A credit union is like a bank, but owned by its members instead of shareholders. It often has lower fees and better rates, but you need to qualify to join.
what is fdic insurance | is my money safe in a bank | what if a bank fails => In the US, the FDIC insures bank deposits up to $250,000 per person per bank for each account ownership type, so your money is protected if the bank fails.
how do mobile payment apps work | apple pay how does it work | tap to pay => Apps like Apple Pay and Google Pay store a card securely on your phone or watch, so you tap to pay. They use a special code instead of your real card number, which is safer.
what is venmo | can kids use cash app | peer to peer payment apps => Venmo, Cash App and Zelle let people send money to each other with a phone. Most require users to be 18 or have a parent sponsored teen account. Only send money to people you know in real life.
what is interest on a savings account | how much interest do banks pay | apy meaning => Banks pay you interest for keeping money in savings. APY, annual percentage yield, shows how much you'd earn in a year including compounding. High yield savings accounts pay more than regular ones.
what is a cd at a bank | certificate of deposit | how does a cd work => A certificate of deposit holds your money for a set time, like 6 months or a year, at a fixed interest rate. Withdrawing early usually costs a penalty.

# tag: life.money.credit
credit card interest explained | what is credit card interest | why credit cards can be dangerous => A credit card lets you borrow money to buy things and pay later. If you pay the full balance each month, you usually pay no interest. If you don't, the bank charges high interest on what's left, and debt can grow fast.
what is a minimum payment | paying only the minimum | credit card minimum payment trap => The minimum payment is the smallest amount you must pay each month. Paying only that means interest keeps adding up, so a small balance can take years to pay off and cost much more.
what is apr | annual percentage rate | what is a high interest rate => APR is the yearly interest rate on borrowed money, like a credit card or loan. Credit card APRs are often over 20 percent, which is very high compared with savings interest.
how old do you have to be to get a credit card | can kids have credit cards | authorized user => In the US, you need to be 18 to get your own credit card, and under 21 you need to show your own income or have a co signer. Teens can sometimes be added as an authorized user on a parent's card.
what affects a credit score | how to build good credit | credit score factors => Paying bills on time matters most. Using only a small part of your credit limit, having a long credit history and not opening many new accounts at once also help.
what is a co signer | cosigning a loan | why do you need a co signer => A co signer is someone, often a parent, who agrees to pay a loan if the borrower can't. It helps people with no credit history get approved.
what is a mortgage | how does a mortgage work | home loan explained => A mortgage is a loan used to buy a home, usually paid back monthly over 15 to 30 years with interest. If payments stop, the lender can take the house.
what is a student loan | how do student loans work | paying for college => Student loans are money borrowed to pay for college, paid back with interest after school. Scholarships, grants and savings can reduce how much you need to borrow.
what is a scholarship | how to get a scholarship | scholarship vs grant => Scholarships and grants are money for school that you don't pay back. Scholarships are often for grades, talents or activities, and grants are often based on financial need.
what is a 529 plan | college savings account | saving for college => A 529 plan is a US savings account for education where money can grow without being taxed if it's used for school costs.

# tag: life.money.tax
what is income tax | how does income tax work | why do paychecks have taxes taken out => Income tax is a portion of what people earn paid to the government. Employers usually take it out of each paycheck automatically, and once a year people file a tax return to settle up.
what does tax money get spent on | where does tax money go | why are taxes important => Taxes pay for public things like schools, roads, police, firefighters, parks, the military, and programs like health care and Social Security.
what is a tax return | filing taxes => A tax return is a form people send the government each year reporting income and taxes paid. In the US, the deadline is usually April 15. If too much was taken out, you get a refund.
what is a tax refund | why do people get money back from taxes | tax refund meaning => A refund is money the government sends back when more tax was taken out of your pay during the year than you actually owed.
what is property tax | who pays property tax | property tax pays for schools => Property tax is paid by people who own land and buildings, based on their value. It often funds local schools, roads and services.
what is gross pay vs net pay | take home pay | why is my paycheck smaller => Gross pay is the total you earn before anything is taken out. Net pay, or take home pay, is what's left after taxes and other deductions.
social security taxes on my paycheck | social security tax | what is fica => Social Security is a US program that pays money to retired and disabled people. Workers pay into it through FICA taxes taken from their paychecks.
why is sales tax added at the register | price tag doesn't include tax | sales tax by state => In the US, most prices on tags don't include sales tax, which is added at checkout. Rates are set by states and cities, and a few states have no statewide sales tax.
what is a w2 form | what is a w4 | tax forms for a job => A W-4 is a form you fill out when starting a job to tell your employer how much tax to hold back. A W-2 is a form your employer gives you each year showing what you earned and the taxes withheld.

# tag: life.money.tipping
how much do you tip at a restaurant | restaurant tip percentage | tipping in the us => In the US, people usually tip around 15 to 20 percent of the bill before tax at sit down restaurants, more for great service. Tips are a big part of servers' pay.
do you tip for takeout | tipping at a counter | should i tip at a coffee shop => Tipping for takeout or counter service is optional in the US. Many people leave a small tip or round up, especially for big or complicated orders.
how much do you tip a pizza delivery driver | delivery tip | tipping food delivery => In the US, delivery drivers are commonly tipped around 15 to 20 percent, or a few dollars minimum, more in bad weather or for big orders.
who do you tip | what services do you tip for | tipping guide => In the US, people commonly tip servers, delivery drivers, hairstylists, taxi and ride share drivers, hotel housekeepers and tour guides. Tipping customs vary a lot by country.
do people tip in other countries | tipping around the world | is tipping rude in japan => Tipping varies by country. In Japan, tipping isn't expected and can confuse people. In much of Europe, service is often included and people round up or leave a little extra.
how do i split a bill with friends | splitting the check | who pays when eating out => Decide before ordering whether to split evenly or each pay for your own. Ask the server for separate checks early, and remember to add tip and tax to your share.
