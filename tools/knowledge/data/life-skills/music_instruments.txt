what are the names of guitar strings | guitar string notes | standard guitar tuning => In standard tuning, from the thickest string to the thinnest, the strings are E A D G B E. A memory trick is "Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie".
how do i tune a guitar | tuning a guitar for beginners | guitar tuner app => Use a clip on tuner or tuner app, pluck one string at a time, and turn its tuning peg until the tuner shows the right note: E A D G B E from thickest to thinnest. Tighten to raise pitch, loosen to lower it.
what are the easiest guitar chords | first guitar chords to learn | beginner guitar chords => Great first chords are E minor, A minor, C, G, D and E major. With G, C, D and E minor you can play hundreds of songs.
why do my fingers hurt playing guitar | guitar finger pain | how long until calluses form => Pressing strings is hard on new fingertips. Practice short sessions daily, and calluses usually form within a few weeks, after which it stops hurting. Lighter strings and a lower action help.
what is the difference between acoustic and electric guitar | acoustic vs electric | which guitar should i start with => An acoustic guitar has a hollow body and makes sound on its own. An electric guitar needs an amplifier, has lighter strings and is easier to press. Both work for beginners, so pick the music you want to play.
what is a bass guitar | bass vs guitar | what does the bass do in a band => A bass guitar usually has four thicker strings tuned E A D G, an octave below a guitar's lowest four. It plays low notes that lock in with the drums and hold the band's harmony and groove together.
how do i read guitar tabs | guitar tab explained | what do numbers on tabs mean => Tabs show six lines for the six strings, with the thinnest string on top. Numbers tell you which fret to press, and 0 means play the open string.
what is a fret | frets on a guitar | what does fretting mean => Frets are the metal strips on a guitar's neck. Pressing a string just behind a fret shortens the vibrating part, raising the pitch by a half step per fret.
what is strumming | strumming patterns for beginners | how to strum a guitar => Strumming is brushing a pick or fingers across the strings. A common beginner pattern is down, down, up, up, down, up. Keep your wrist loose and your arm moving steadily.
what is fingerpicking | fingerstyle guitar | how to fingerpick => Fingerpicking is plucking strings with your fingers instead of a pick, usually thumb on the bass strings and fingers on the higher strings, for flowing patterns.
what is a ukulele | ukulele strings | is ukulele easy to learn => A ukulele is a small four string instrument from Hawaii, usually tuned G C E A. It's one of the easiest instruments to start, with simple chord shapes and soft nylon strings.
how do i start learning piano | piano for beginners | how to learn piano at home => Learn where the notes are, starting with middle C, practice simple songs with both hands separately and then together, and play a little every day. A teacher or a learning app with a keyboard helps a lot.
what is the difference between a piano and a keyboard | keyboard vs piano | digital piano => An acoustic piano makes sound with hammers hitting strings. A keyboard or digital piano makes sound electronically, is lighter, can use headphones and often has many sounds. Weighted keys feel most like a real piano.
how many white and black keys are on a piano | white keys black keys | piano key count => A standard piano has 88 keys: 52 white and 36 black.
what do piano pedals do | sustain pedal | soft pedal piano => The right pedal is the sustain pedal, which lets notes ring after you lift your fingers. The left is the soft pedal. Grand pianos have a middle sostenuto pedal that holds only certain notes.
what is a synthesizer | how does a synth work | what is a synth used for => A synthesizer creates sounds electronically using oscillators, filters and envelopes. It can imitate instruments or make totally new sounds, and it's huge in electronic, pop and game music.
what are the parts of a drum kit | drum set pieces | what is a hi hat => A basic drum kit has a bass drum played with a foot pedal, a snare drum, toms, a hi hat (two cymbals opened and closed with a pedal), a crash cymbal and a ride cymbal.
how do i start learning drums | drums for beginners | basic drum beat => Start with a simple rock beat: hi hat on every eighth note, bass drum on beats 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4. Practice pads and electronic kits let you practice quietly.
what are drum rudiments | single stroke roll | paradiddle => Rudiments are basic drum sticking patterns. The single stroke roll alternates R L R L, the double stroke roll goes R R L L, and the paradiddle is R L R R L R L L.
how do i practice drums quietly | silent drum practice | practice pad => Use a practice pad, an electronic drum kit with headphones, or rubber mutes on an acoustic kit. Practice pads are cheap and great for rudiments.
what are the woodwind instruments | woodwind family | is a saxophone a woodwind => Woodwinds include the flute, piccolo, clarinet, oboe, bassoon and saxophone. The saxophone is metal but counts as a woodwind because it uses a reed.
what are the brass instruments | brass family | how do brass instruments make sound => Brass instruments include the trumpet, trombone, French horn, tuba and euphonium. Players buzz their lips into a cup shaped mouthpiece, and valves or a slide change the pitch.
what are the string instruments in an orchestra | string family | violin viola cello bass => The orchestra's string section has violins, violas, cellos and double basses, from highest to lowest. The harp is often included too.
what is the difference between a violin and a viola | violin vs viola | what is a cello => A viola is slightly bigger than a violin with a deeper, warmer sound and reads mostly alto clef. A cello is much bigger, played sitting with it between your knees, and sounds lower still.
what are percussion instruments | percussion family | is a piano percussion => Percussion instruments make sound by being struck, shaken or scraped, like drums, cymbals, xylophone, triangle and timpani. The piano is sometimes called percussion because hammers hit its strings.
what is a recorder | recorder for beginners | why do schools use recorders => A recorder is a simple woodwind with finger holes and no reed. Schools use it because it's cheap, easy to start and teaches note reading and breath control.
how does a flute make sound | playing the flute | why is the flute a woodwind => You blow across a hole on the flute's edge, splitting the air and making it vibrate inside the tube. It's a woodwind even though it's metal, because woodwinds were originally all wood.
what is a clarinet reed | how does a reed work | single vs double reed => A reed is a thin strip of cane that vibrates when you blow. Clarinets and saxophones use a single reed against a mouthpiece, and oboes and bassoons use two reeds vibrating together.
how does a trumpet change notes | trumpet valves | how many valves on a trumpet => A trumpet has three valves that send air through extra tubing to lower the pitch. Combined with changing lip tension, they let the player reach every note.
how does a trombone work | trombone slide | trombone positions => A trombone uses a slide instead of valves. Pushing the slide out makes the tube longer and the pitch lower, using seven main positions.
what is a french horn | why is the french horn hard | horn in an orchestra => The French horn is a coiled brass instrument with a big bell, played with the right hand inside the bell. It's known for a warm sound but is tricky because its notes sit close together.
what is a harmonica | how to play harmonica | blues harp => A harmonica is a small instrument with reeds you play by blowing and drawing air through holes. A 10 hole diatonic harmonica, often called a blues harp, is popular in blues and folk.
what is the xylophone vs glockenspiel | xylophone vs marimba | mallet instruments => A xylophone has wooden bars and a bright, short sound. A marimba also has wooden bars but with resonator tubes for a deeper, warmer sound. A glockenspiel has metal bars and a bell like sound.
what is a drum machine | what is a beat maker | 808 drum machine => A drum machine creates drum sounds and patterns electronically. The Roland TR-808 from 1980 became legendary, and its booming bass drum shaped hip hop and pop.
what is a daw | digital audio workstation | best daw for beginners => A DAW is software for recording, editing and producing music. GarageBand is free on Apple devices and great for beginners, and others include FL Studio, Ableton Live, Logic Pro and the free Cakewalk for Windows.
how do i make beats | making music on a computer | beat making for beginners => Start in a DAW like GarageBand or FL Studio: pick a tempo, make a drum pattern, add a bassline and chords, then a melody. Copying the structure of songs you like is a great way to learn.
what is midi | midi keyboard | what is a midi controller => MIDI is a way for instruments and computers to send note information, like which key was pressed and how hard. A MIDI controller is a keyboard or pad that controls sounds in software.
what is a sample in music | sampling meaning | is sampling legal => A sample is a piece of an existing recording reused in a new song. Hip hop producers famously sample old records. Using copyrighted samples in released music usually needs permission.
what is an audio interface | do i need an audio interface | recording guitar to computer => An audio interface connects microphones and instruments to a computer with better sound quality than a built in input. You need one to record a guitar or a good mic into a DAW.
what microphone should i use for singing | dynamic vs condenser mic | best mic for recording => Dynamic mics are tough and handle loud sound, great for live singing. Condenser mics capture more detail and are common in studios, but pick up more room noise.
how do i take care of my instrument | instrument care tips | cleaning a guitar => Keep it in its case away from heat, cold and humidity changes, wipe it after playing, change strings when they sound dull, and get it checked by a technician if something feels off.
how often should i change guitar strings | when to replace strings | old guitar strings sound => Many players change strings every couple of months, or when they sound dull, won't stay in tune or look rusty. Players who practice a lot change them more often.
how long does it take to learn an instrument | how long to get good at piano | learning guitar how long => With regular practice, most people can play simple songs within a few weeks to a few months. Getting really good takes years, but steady daily practice matters more than long sessions.
how long should i practice my instrument | practice tips for music | best way to practice => For beginners, 15 to 30 minutes of focused daily practice works well. Warm up, work on the hardest parts slowly, use a metronome, and end with something fun.
what is the hardest instrument to learn | most difficult instrument | hardest instrument ever => It depends on the person, but the violin, French horn, oboe, organ and bagpipes are often called among the hardest because of tricky pitch control and technique.
what is the biggest instrument | largest musical instrument | pipe organ size => Pipe organs are among the largest instruments, with thousands of pipes. The Boardwalk Hall Auditorium Organ in Atlantic City has over 33,000 pipes.
what is the smallest violin size | violin sizes for kids | what size violin do i need => Violins come in fractional sizes for kids, like 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and full size (4/4). Most 11 to 12 year olds use a 3/4 or full size. A teacher or shop can measure your arm.
what is a theremin | instrument you don't touch | spooky sci fi sound => A theremin is an electronic instrument played without touching it. Moving your hands near two antennas controls pitch and volume. It makes the eerie sound used in old sci fi movies.
what is a steel drum | steelpan | where are steel drums from => The steelpan is a pitched percussion instrument made from oil drums, created in Trinidad and Tobago in the 1930s and 1940s. Different dents in the surface play different notes.
what is a sitar | indian instruments | what does a sitar sound like => A sitar is a long necked string instrument from India with movable frets and extra strings that vibrate in sympathy, making a shimmering, buzzing sound. Ravi Shankar made it famous worldwide.
what are bagpipes | how do bagpipes work | where are bagpipes from => Bagpipes have a bag the player fills with air, which is squeezed to push air through reed pipes. One pipe, the chanter, plays the melody, and drones play constant notes. They're famous in Scotland but exist in many countries.
how do i sing better | singing tips for beginners | how to improve my voice => Breathe from your belly, stand tall, relax your jaw and shoulders, warm up with gentle scales, and drink water. Record yourself to hear what to improve, and don't push into strain.
what is my vocal range | soprano alto tenor bass | voice types => Voice types from highest to lowest are soprano, mezzo soprano, alto, tenor, baritone and bass. Kids' voices are usually higher, and voices change during puberty.
why does my voice crack | voice cracking during puberty | voice changing => During puberty the vocal cords grow longer and thicker, especially in boys, and the voice can crack while it adjusts. It's normal and settles down over time.
how do i warm up my voice | vocal warm ups | singing exercises => Try gentle humming, lip trills (blowing air through loose lips like a motorboat), sirens sliding from low to high, and easy scales. Start soft and gradually get higher and louder.
what is an orchestra | orchestra vs band | what does a conductor do => An orchestra is a large group with strings, woodwinds, brass and percussion. A concert band has no string section except sometimes a bass. The conductor keeps everyone together, sets the tempo and shapes the music.
what is a choir | choir parts | satb meaning => A choir is a group of singers, often split into parts. SATB stands for soprano, alto, tenor and bass, the four main voice parts.
what is a quartet | trio quartet quintet | string quartet meaning => A trio is three musicians, a quartet four and a quintet five. A string quartet has two violins, a viola and a cello.
