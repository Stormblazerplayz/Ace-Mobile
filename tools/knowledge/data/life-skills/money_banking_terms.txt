# tag: life.money.banking
what is a minimum balance | minimum balance fee | account maintenance fee => A minimum balance is the least amount of money some accounts require you to keep. Dropping below it can cause a monthly fee or lose you interest, so check the rules.
what is a debit card hold | pending transaction | pending charge => A hold is money your bank sets aside for a pending charge, like at gas stations or hotels. You can't spend it until the charge settles or the hold drops off.
what is a cashier's check | cashier's check vs personal check | certified check => A cashier's check is a check the bank pays from its own money, so it's trusted for big purchases. Scammers fake them, so wait for your bank to confirm one clears.
what is ach | ach transfer | bank transfer days => ACH is the US network banks use for electronic transfers like direct deposit, bill payments and moving money between banks. Transfers usually take 1 to 3 business days.
what is a grace period credit card | pay in full no interest | grace period => The grace period is the time between your statement and the due date. If you pay the full balance by the due date, you pay no interest on new purchases.
what is apy | annual percentage yield | apy vs apr => APY, annual percentage yield, is how much a savings account earns in a year, including compounding. A higher APY means your money grows faster.
what is a money market account | money market vs savings | money market fund => A money market account is a bank savings account that often pays more interest and may allow some checks, but can need a higher balance. A money market fund is a different thing, an investment.
what is ncua | credit union insurance | credit union safe => The NCUA is the US agency that insures money in credit unions, the same way the FDIC covers banks, usually up to $250,000 per person per account type.
what is an online bank | online only bank | high yield online savings => An online bank has no branches, so you do everything on an app or website. They often pay higher savings rates with lower fees. Check that it's FDIC insured.
what is a car loan | auto loan | car payment => A car loan is money borrowed to buy a car, repaid monthly with interest over several years. The car is collateral, so a lender can take it if payments stop.
what is fafsa | fafsa meaning | apply for college aid => FAFSA, the Free Application for Federal Student Aid, is the US form families fill out to get grants, loans and work study for college. It's filled out each year before and during college.
what is a pell grant | pell grant | free college money => A Pell Grant is US federal money for undergraduate college students with financial need. Unlike a loan, it usually doesn't have to be paid back.
what is payday loan | payday loans dangerous | payday lender => A payday loan is a small, short loan due on your next payday. Fees are so high that the yearly cost can be around 400% APR, so they're best avoided.
what is rent to own | rent to own expensive | rent to own furniture => Rent to own lets you pay weekly or monthly for an item and own it after many payments. The total often ends up costing much more than buying it outright.
what is a chargeback | dispute a charge | credit card dispute => A chargeback is when your card company reverses a charge after you dispute it, like for an item that never arrived. Try contacting the seller first.
what is sales tax holiday | tax free weekend | back to school tax holiday => A sales tax holiday is a few days when some US states drop sales tax on items like school clothes and supplies. Many happen in late summer before school.
what is a resume for teens | first resume | resume no experience => A teen resume is one page listing your contact info, school, skills, volunteering, clubs, awards and jobs like babysitting or yard work.
what is a job interview | interview tips teens | first job interview => A job interview is a meeting where an employer gets to know you. Dress neatly, arrive early, make eye contact, give real examples, ask a question and say thank you.
what is a reference for a job | job reference | who to use as reference => A reference is someone who can tell an employer you're reliable, like a teacher, coach or neighbor you've worked for. Ask them first, and don't use family.
what is dollar cost averaging | invest regularly | dca investing => Dollar cost averaging is investing the same amount on a regular schedule no matter the price. You automatically buy more shares when prices are low.
what is a ponzi scheme | ponzi scheme meaning | pyramid scheme vs ponzi => A Ponzi scheme is a scam that pays early investors with money from new investors instead of real profits. It always collapses. It's named after Charles Ponzi from the 1920s.
