# tag: life.money.basics
how does inflation affect savings | does inflation make savings worth less | inflation and my money => If your savings earn less interest than the inflation rate, they slowly buy less. That's one reason people put long term savings in accounts or investments that can grow.
what is a high yield savings account | hysa | online savings account interest => A high yield savings account pays a higher interest rate than a regular savings account, often at an online bank. It's still safe and insured up to limits at FDIC banks.
how do i build credit | building credit as a teen => Adults build credit by paying bills on time and keeping card balances low. Teens can sometimes become authorized users on a parent's card, and adults over 18 can get a student or secured card.
what is a secured credit card | secured card | credit card with deposit => A secured credit card requires a cash deposit that usually becomes your credit limit. It helps people with no credit history build it safely.
what is buy now pay later | bnpl | afterpay klarna => Buy now, pay later services split a purchase into installments, often four payments. They can be interest free, but late fees and overspending are common risks.
what is a credit limit | credit card limit | going over credit limit => A credit limit is the most you can borrow on a credit card. Using a small part of it, under about 30 percent, is better for your credit score.
what is a sinking fund | saving for planned expenses | sinking fund example => A sinking fund is money set aside bit by bit for a known future expense, like saving 10 dollars a month for a 120 dollar holiday gift budget.
how do i set a savings goal | savings goal tips | saving for a drone => Decide what you want and its price, set a date, and divide. For a 300 dollar drone in 6 months, save 50 dollars a month. Track progress somewhere you can see it.
what is a savings jar system | three jar method => Use three jars labeled spend, save and give. Split every bit of money you get between them, like 50 percent spend, 40 percent save and 10 percent give.
what is the envelope method | cash envelope budgeting | envelope budget => With the envelope method, you put cash for each category, like food or fun, in its own envelope. When an envelope is empty, you stop spending in that category.
what is pay yourself first | pay yourself first meaning | save first spend later => Pay yourself first means putting money into savings as soon as you get it, before spending on anything else. It makes saving automatic.
what is lifestyle creep | spending more as you earn more | lifestyle inflation => Lifestyle creep is when spending rises every time income rises, so you never save more. Keeping your spending steady when you earn more lets savings grow.
what is a net worth statement | assets minus liabilities => Net worth is what you own (assets) minus what you owe (liabilities). If you have 500 dollars saved and owe a friend 20, your net worth is 480 dollars.
what is a financial goal | money goals examples => Short term goals take weeks or months, like a new game. Long term goals take years, like a car or college. Writing them down with amounts and dates helps.
# tag: life.money.everyday
how do debit and credit cards differ | debit vs credit card | which is better debit or credit => A debit card takes money straight from your bank account. A credit card borrows money you must pay back, with interest if you don't pay in full by the due date.
what is contactless payment | how does tap to pay work => Contactless payment lets you tap a card, phone or watch on a reader. It uses short range radio (NFC) and sends a one time code, so it's very secure.
what is apple pay | is apple pay safe => Apple Pay lets you pay by tapping an iPhone or Apple Watch. It uses a device account number instead of your real card number and needs Face ID, Touch ID or a passcode, which makes it secure.
what is a gift card | are gift cards worth it | gift card tips => A gift card holds a set amount to spend at a store or service. Use them soon, keep them safe like cash, and only buy them from real stores. Anyone asking you to pay with gift cards is likely a scammer.
what is cash back | cash back rewards | getting cash back at a store => Cash back can mean a reward card returning a percentage of what you spend, or asking for extra cash at the register when paying with a debit card.
how do i use an atm | atm fees => An ATM lets you withdraw cash, deposit and check your balance using a debit card and PIN. Using one outside your bank's network can cost a fee. Cover your PIN when typing.
what is a routing number | routing vs account number | where to find routing number => A routing number identifies the bank, and the account number identifies your specific account. Keep your account number private, since it can be misused.
what is direct deposit | how does direct deposit work | paycheck direct deposit => Direct deposit sends your paycheck straight into your bank account electronically, so you don't need a paper check. It's usually available on payday morning.
what is a wire transfer | bank transfer | how do bank transfers work => A bank transfer moves money electronically between accounts. Wire transfers are fast but hard to reverse, which is why scammers like them.
what is a money order | money order vs check | cashiers check => A money order is a prepaid paper payment bought with cash from places like post offices. A cashier's check is guaranteed by a bank. Both are safer than personal checks for some payments.
what is a currency exchange rate example | how much is a dollar worth in euros | converting currency => Exchange rates change every day. If 1 US dollar equals 0.9 euros, then 100 dollars gets about 90 euros, minus any fees.
why does the price end in 99 | 9.99 pricing | charm pricing => Prices like 9.99 feel noticeably cheaper than 10 dollars because we read the first digit first. It's a common store trick called charm pricing.
what is a membership fee | is a membership worth it | warehouse club membership => A membership fee lets you use a store or service, like Costco or a streaming plan. It's worth it only if you save or use it enough to cover the fee.
how do streaming subscriptions add up | too many subscriptions | cancel unused subscriptions => Several 10 to 15 dollar subscriptions can add up to over 50 dollars a month. Review them every few months and cancel the ones you don't use.
what is a late fee | how to avoid late fees => A late fee is an extra charge when a bill is paid after the due date. Setting reminders or automatic payments helps avoid them.
what is sales tax in different states | states with no sales tax | sales tax rates => Sales tax varies by state and city. As of 2025, five states have no statewide sales tax: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon, though Alaska allows local ones.
how much is 20 percent off | sale price math => Multiply the price by the percent off as a decimal, then subtract. 20 percent off 50 dollars is 50 times 0.2, which is 10, so you pay 40 dollars before tax.
how do i calculate tax on a price | adding sales tax | price with tax => Multiply the price by the tax rate as a decimal and add it. With 8 percent tax, a 25 dollar item costs 25 plus 2, so 27 dollars.
what is a bogo deal | buy one get one | is bogo a good deal => BOGO means buy one, get one free or at a discount. It's only a good deal if you actually need two, or the second will get used.
what is a price match | price matching | do stores match prices => Price matching is when a store lowers its price to match a competitor's advertised price. Policies vary, so check the store's rules and bring proof.
what is a rain check at a store | sale item out of stock | rain check meaning => A rain check is a store's promise to sell you a sale item at the sale price later if it's out of stock. Not every store offers them.
what is a layaway | layaway plan | how does layaway work => Layaway lets you reserve an item by paying a deposit and then paying the rest in installments. You get the item once it's fully paid.
# tag: life.money.teens
how much should a 12 year old get for allowance | average allowance by age | allowance amount => There's no set amount. A common rule of thumb is about 1 dollar per week for each year of age, so around 12 dollars a week, but families vary a lot.
what can i buy with my own money | should i ask before spending my money | spending my own money => Even with your own money, check your family's rules for big purchases, online buying and in app purchases. Talking it over helps you avoid regrets.
how do i ask my parents to help me open an account | teen bank account | joint account with parent => Under 18, most banks need a parent or guardian on the account. Ask which bank they use, and look at teen accounts with no fees and a debit card with parent controls.
what is a teen debit card | greenlight | debit cards for kids => Teen debit cards like Greenlight, or kids' cards from regular banks, let parents add money, set limits and see spending. They help kids learn to manage money safely.
how do i save for a car | saving for my first car | how much does a first car cost => Start early, save part of every job paycheck and gift, and remember costs beyond the price, like insurance, gas, registration and repairs. Used cars are usually the smart first choice.
how much does car insurance cost for teens | teen car insurance | why is teen insurance expensive => Teen drivers have higher crash rates, so insurance is expensive, often thousands of dollars a year. Good grades discounts and driver training can lower it.
how much does college cost | college tuition | cost of college => As of 2025, average yearly tuition and fees in the US were roughly 11,000 dollars at in state public colleges and over 40,000 at private ones, before financial aid. Room and board add more.
what is financial aid | fafsa | grants vs loans => Financial aid helps pay for college. Grants and scholarships don't need to be repaid, while loans do. In the US, the FAFSA form is used to apply for most aid.
what is a work study | work study job | college work study => Work study is a financial aid program that gives college students part time jobs, often on campus, to help pay for school.
how do i make money mowing lawns | lawn mowing business | how much to charge for mowing => Ask neighbors first, get your parents' okay, and agree on a price per lawn, often 20 to 40 dollars depending on size. Show up on time, do neat edges and clean up clippings.
how do i make money pet sitting | pet sitting business | dog walking rates => Start with neighbors and family friends, learn each pet's routine and emergency contact, and charge per visit or per walk. Be reliable, since owners trust you with their pets.
how do i make money doing yard work | odd jobs for teens | ways to earn money in my neighborhood => Offer raking, weeding, shoveling snow, washing cars, watering plants or helping with garage cleanouts. Make a simple flyer and get parents' permission.
can i make money from 3d printing | selling prints at school | 3d printing side hustle => Yes, with care. Sell useful or fun prints like phone stands, keychains or custom name tags. Price for filament, electricity, time and failed prints, and get permission before selling at school.
can i make money from drawing | selling art as a kid | commissions for artists => You can sell prints, stickers or custom drawings (commissions) with a parent's help. Set clear prices, show examples of your work, and get payment through a parent's account.
how do i handle money from a job | first paycheck tips | what to do with my first paycheck => Save a good chunk, like half, set aside some for giving, and spend the rest on something you planned. Celebrate a little, but don't spend it all at once.
what is a w4 form | w4 vs w2 | filling out a w4 => A W 4 is a form you fill out when starting a job that tells your employer how much federal tax to withhold from each paycheck. A W 2 shows what you earned and paid in taxes for the year.
do teens have to file taxes | teen taxes | when does a minor need to file taxes => In the US, teens with a job may not need to file if they earn under the standard deduction, but filing can get withheld taxes refunded. Self employed teens who earn 400 dollars or more usually must file.
what is a 1099 | 1099 form | freelance income form => A 1099 form reports income that isn't from a regular job, like freelance work. People who get 1099 income usually have to set aside money for taxes themselves.
what is a roth ira for kids | custodial roth ira | can a teen have a roth ira => A teen with earned income from a job can have a custodial Roth IRA opened by a parent. Money grows tax free for retirement, and starting early gives decades of compounding.
# tag: life.money.econ
what is a market economy | market vs command economy | types of economies => In a market economy, prices and production are set mostly by buyers and sellers. In a command economy, the government decides. Most countries, including the US, have mixed economies.
what is a business cycle | economic boom and bust | expansion and recession => A business cycle is the economy's ups and downs over time: expansion, peak, contraction (recession) and trough, then recovery.
what is unemployment | unemployment rate | what happens when people lose jobs => Unemployment means people who want work can't find it. The unemployment rate is the share of the workforce without a job but looking. Many places offer unemployment benefits for a while.
what is a share of stock | stock vs share => A share is a small piece of ownership in a company. If a company has 1 million shares and you own 1,000, you own 0.1 percent of it.
what is a portfolio | investment portfolio | what is in a portfolio => A portfolio is the collection of investments someone owns, like stocks, bonds, funds and cash. A diverse portfolio spreads risk.
what is a stock split | why do companies split stock | 2 for 1 split => In a stock split, a company divides each share into more shares, like 2 for 1. The total value you own stays the same, but each share costs less.
what is a stock exchange | nyse vs nasdaq | where are stocks traded => A stock exchange is a marketplace for trading stocks. The two biggest in the US are the New York Stock Exchange and the Nasdaq.
what is compound growth example | 100 dollars invested for 50 years | how money grows over time => At about 7 percent average yearly growth, 100 dollars roughly doubles every 10 years, becoming about 3,000 dollars in 50 years. Real returns go up and down from year to year.
what is risk vs reward in money | higher return higher risk | why are stocks risky => Investments with higher possible returns usually have higher risk of losing money in the short run. Savings accounts are safe but grow slowly, while stocks grow more on average but swing a lot.
what is a fee on an investment | expense ratio | why fees matter in investing => An expense ratio is a yearly fee a fund charges, shown as a percent. A 1 percent fee versus 0.05 percent can cost tens of thousands of dollars over decades.
what is a pension | pension vs 401k | how retirement works => A pension is a retirement plan where an employer pays retirees a set amount. A 401(k) is a plan where workers save their own money, often with an employer match, and invest it.
what is an employer match | 401k match | free money from employer => An employer match is when a company adds money to a worker's retirement account, like 50 cents for each dollar the worker saves, up to a limit. It's like free money.
what is a credit card reward | points and miles | are credit card rewards worth it => Reward cards give points, miles or cash back. They're only worth it if you pay the full balance every month, since interest charges quickly cancel out rewards.
what is a financial advisor | what does a financial planner do | do i need a financial advisor => A financial advisor helps people plan their money, like saving, investing and retirement. Some charge a fee, others earn commissions, so it's important to understand how they're paid.
what is a fiduciary | fiduciary meaning | advisor acting in your best interest => A fiduciary is someone legally required to act in your best interest, like a fee only financial planner. Not all financial salespeople are fiduciaries.
