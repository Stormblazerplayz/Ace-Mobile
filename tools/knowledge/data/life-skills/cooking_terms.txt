# tag: life.cooking.terms
what does braise mean | braising meat | how do you braise => Braising means browning food, then cooking it slowly covered in a little liquid on low heat until very tender. It's great for tough cuts like pot roast and short ribs.
what does poach mean in cooking | poaching chicken | what is poaching => Poaching means gently cooking food in liquid just below a simmer, like eggs, fish or chicken in broth. It keeps food moist and tender.
what does it mean to baste | basting a turkey | why baste meat => Basting means spooning or brushing pan juices, butter or sauce over food while it cooks to add flavor and color.
what does dredge mean | dredging in flour | what is dredging => Dredging means coating food lightly in a dry ingredient like flour before frying, shaking off the extra.
what does it mean to beat eggs | beat vs whisk vs stir | beating ingredients => Beating means mixing hard and fast to blend or add air. Whisking is similar with a whisk. Stirring is gentle mixing to combine.
what does it mean to cut in butter | cutting butter into flour | pastry cutter use => Cutting in means working cold butter into flour until it's in small pea sized bits, using a pastry cutter, two knives or your fingertips. It makes flaky crusts and biscuits.
what does it mean to scald milk | scalded milk | why scald milk => Scalding means heating milk until it's steaming with small bubbles at the edge, just before it boils. Old recipes used it to kill bacteria, and some still use it for texture.
what does it mean to temper eggs | tempering eggs in custard | why temper eggs => Tempering means slowly whisking a little hot liquid into beaten eggs to warm them gently, so they don't scramble when added to the hot mixture.
what is a slurry | cornstarch slurry meaning | how to make a slurry => A slurry is a starch like cornstarch or flour mixed with cold water until smooth. It's stirred into hot liquid to thicken it without lumps.
what does it mean to let dough proof | proofing meaning | what is a proofing box => Proofing is the final rise of yeast dough after shaping, when yeast makes gas that puffs it up before baking.
what does it mean to rest dough | why rest pie dough | let the batter rest => Resting gives flour time to absorb liquid and lets gluten relax, so dough rolls out easier and batters cook more evenly.
what does it mean to garnish | what is a garnish | garnish ideas => A garnish is a small decoration on a dish, like chopped herbs, a lemon wedge or a sprinkle of cheese. It adds color and sometimes a bit of flavor.
what does al forno mean | italian cooking words | what does a la mean in recipes => Al forno means baked in the oven in Italian. A la means "in the style of" in French, so a la mode means served with ice cream in the US.
what is a dutch oven braise vs stew | stew vs soup | what is a stew => A stew has chunks of meat and vegetables cooked slowly in a small amount of thick liquid. Soup has more liquid. Both simmer gently.
what is a casserole | what does casserole mean | casserole dish => A casserole is a dish baked in a deep pan, often mixing a protein, vegetables, a starch and a sauce, sometimes topped with cheese or crumbs. The pan itself is also called a casserole dish.
what is a gratin | au gratin meaning | potatoes au gratin => Au gratin means a dish topped with cheese or breadcrumbs and baked until golden and crispy on top, like potatoes au gratin.
what is confit | duck confit meaning | what does confit mean => Confit means cooking food slowly in fat or sugar syrup at a low temperature. Duck confit is duck cooked gently in its own fat.
what is sous vide | how does sous vide work | sous vide cooking => Sous vide means sealing food in a bag and cooking it in a water bath held at an exact temperature. It cooks food perfectly evenly, then it's often seared for a crust.
what does flambe mean | why set food on fire | flambe cooking => Flambe means adding alcohol to a hot pan and lighting it, which burns off the alcohol and adds flavor and drama. It's a job for experienced adults only.
what is a reduction | balsamic reduction | what does a reduction sauce mean => A reduction is a liquid simmered until much of the water evaporates, leaving a thicker, stronger sauce. Balsamic reduction is balsamic vinegar boiled down until syrupy.
what does medium heat mean | low medium high heat stove | which stove setting is medium => On most stove dials, medium is around the middle. Low is gentle for melting and simmering, medium for most cooking, and high for boiling water and searing.
what does a dash of mean in cooking | what does scant mean | what is a heaping tablespoon => Scant means a little less than the full amount, so a scant cup is just under a cup. A dash is a tiny quick splash or shake.
what does crumb mean in baking | tender crumb | open crumb bread => The crumb is the inside texture of baked goods. A tender crumb is soft and fine, and an open crumb in bread has big, irregular holes.
what does fork tender mean | how do i know meat is tender | fall apart tender => Fork tender means a fork slides in and twists easily with little resistance. Braised meats are often cooked until fork tender.
what does golden brown mean | how brown is golden brown | cook until golden => Golden brown is a light to medium toasty color, like a perfect piece of toast or a cooked pancake. It's a sign of browning flavor without burning.
what does blind bake mean | blind baking pie crust | pie weights => Blind baking means baking a pie crust empty before adding the filling, using pie weights or dried beans on parchment so it doesn't puff up. It keeps the bottom from getting soggy.
what does chiffonade mean | how to cut basil in ribbons | ribbon cut herbs => Chiffonade means stacking leaves like basil, rolling them up tight and slicing across into thin ribbons.
what does render mean in cooking | rendering bacon fat | rendered fat => Rendering means cooking fatty meat slowly so the fat melts out, like bacon or chicken skin, leaving crispy bits and liquid fat.
what does parboil mean | parboiling potatoes | partially cooking food => Parboiling means boiling food partway, then finishing it another way, like roasting parboiled potatoes for crispy outsides and fluffy insides.
what does shock mean in cooking | ice bath for vegetables | shocking pasta => Shocking means plunging cooked food into ice water to stop the cooking right away, keeping vegetables bright and crisp.
what does sweat mean in cooking | sweating onions | sweat vs saute => Sweating means cooking vegetables gently on low heat with a little fat, often covered, until soft but not browned.
what does whip mean | whipping vs beating | what is whipped => Whipping means beating fast to add air and make something light and fluffy, like whipped cream or egg whites.
what does score mean in cooking | scoring bread | scoring meat => Scoring means making shallow cuts in the surface of food, like slashes on bread so it expands neatly, or crosshatch cuts on meat so marinade soaks in.
what does broil mean | broiling steak | what is the broiler setting => Broiling cooks food with intense direct heat from above, browning it quickly. Keep food a few inches from the heat and watch it constantly.
what is the difference between grill and barbecue | bbq vs grilling | what is smoking meat => Grilling cooks food quickly over high direct heat. Barbecue usually means cooking meat low and slow with smoke for hours until it's very tender.

# tag: life.cooking.world
what is ramen | what is real ramen | ramen vs instant noodles => Ramen is a Japanese noodle soup with wheat noodles in a rich broth, often topped with pork, a soft egg, green onions and seaweed. Instant ramen is a quick dried version invented in Japan in 1958.
what is sushi rice | why is sushi rice sticky | how do you make sushi rice => Sushi rice is short grain rice that's naturally sticky, seasoned while warm with rice vinegar, sugar and salt.
what is a burrito vs a taco | difference between taco and burrito | what is a quesadilla => A taco is a small folded tortilla with fillings, a burrito is a big flour tortilla wrapped fully closed around fillings, and a quesadilla is a tortilla folded with melted cheese and toasted.
what is paella | where is paella from | what is in paella => Paella is a rice dish from Valencia, Spain, cooked in a wide shallow pan with saffron. Versions include chicken and rabbit, or seafood. A crispy rice layer on the bottom is called socarrat.
what is curry | where does curry come from | is all curry spicy => Curry is a broad word for many sauced dishes, especially from South and Southeast Asia, made with spices like turmeric, cumin and coriander. Many curries are mild, it's not all spicy.
what is dim sum | what do you eat at dim sum | dim sum meaning => Dim sum is a Cantonese style meal of many small dishes, like dumplings, buns and rolls, often served with tea. You pick plates to share.
what is pho | how do you say pho | what is in pho => Pho is a Vietnamese noodle soup with rice noodles in a clear, spiced broth, usually with beef or chicken, and fresh herbs, lime and bean sprouts. It's said roughly like "fuh".
what is pad thai | what is in pad thai | where is pad thai from => Pad thai is a Thai stir fried rice noodle dish with egg, tofu or shrimp, bean sprouts and a sweet, sour and salty sauce with tamarind, topped with peanuts and lime.
what is a croissant | how are croissants made | why is a croissant flaky => A croissant is a French pastry made by folding butter into dough again and again to create many thin layers. In the oven, steam puffs the layers apart, making it flaky.
what is a bagel | why are bagels chewy | how are bagels made => Bagels are ring shaped breads that are boiled briefly before baking, which gives them a shiny, chewy crust.
what is a pretzel | why are pretzels brown | how are soft pretzels made => Soft pretzels are dough twisted into a knot and dipped in an alkaline baking soda or lye bath before baking, which gives them their deep brown color and special taste.
what is a dumpling | types of dumplings | what are potstickers => Dumplings are pieces of dough wrapped around a filling or cooked on their own. Examples include Chinese jiaozi, Japanese gyoza, Polish pierogi and Nepalese momo. Potstickers are pan fried on one side, then steamed.
what is a falafel | what is falafel made of | where is falafel from => Falafel are crispy fried balls or patties of ground chickpeas or fava beans with herbs and spices, popular in the Middle East and often served in pita.
what is kimchi | what does kimchi taste like | how is kimchi made => Kimchi is a Korean side dish of fermented vegetables, most often napa cabbage, with chili, garlic and ginger. It's spicy, sour and full of flavor.
what is bibimbap | korean rice bowl | what is in bibimbap => Bibimbap is a Korean rice bowl topped with seasoned vegetables, meat or tofu, a fried egg and spicy gochujang sauce, mixed together before eating.
what is a gyro | gyro vs shawarma | how do you say gyro => A gyro is Greek meat cooked on a vertical rotisserie, shaved and served in pita with tzatziki. Shawarma is a similar Middle Eastern dish. Gyro is often said "YEE-roh".
what is tzatziki | tzatziki sauce ingredients | greek yogurt sauce => Tzatziki is a Greek sauce of yogurt, grated cucumber, garlic, olive oil and herbs like dill. It's cool and fresh, great on gyros and with veggies.
what is a calzone | calzone vs pizza | what is stromboli => A calzone is a pizza folded in half and sealed before baking, with fillings inside. A stromboli is rolled up like a log instead.
what is lasagna | how is lasagna made | lasagna layers => Lasagna is an Italian dish of flat pasta sheets layered with sauce, cheese like ricotta and mozzarella, and often meat, baked until bubbly.
what is risotto | how is risotto made | why stir risotto => Risotto is an Italian rice dish made by adding hot broth a little at a time to short grain rice like arborio while stirring, which releases starch and makes it creamy.
what is gnocchi | how do you say gnocchi | what is gnocchi made of => Gnocchi are soft Italian dumplings usually made from potato and flour. It's said "NYOH-kee". They cook in boiling water and are done when they float.
what is a crumpet vs english muffin | english muffin nooks | crumpet meaning => Both are griddle cooked breads. English muffins are split to show nooks and crannies, while crumpets are spongy with holes on top and aren't split.
what is fish and chips | where are fish and chips from | british fish and chips => Fish and chips is a British dish of battered fried white fish, like cod or haddock, with thick cut fried potatoes, often with salt and malt vinegar.
what is poutine | where is poutine from | poutine ingredients => Poutine is a dish from Quebec, Canada, of french fries topped with cheese curds and hot gravy.
what is a samosa | samosa filling | where are samosas from => A samosa is a fried or baked triangle pastry filled with spiced potatoes, peas or meat, popular in South Asia and many other places.
what is naan | naan vs pita | how is naan cooked => Naan is a soft, leavened flatbread from South and Central Asia, traditionally baked on the hot wall of a tandoor clay oven. Pita is a Middle Eastern flatbread that often puffs into a pocket.
what is tempura | what is tempura batter | japanese fried food => Tempura is a Japanese dish of seafood or vegetables in a very light, thin batter, fried until crisp. Ice cold water and barely mixing the batter keep it light.
what is teriyaki | teriyaki sauce ingredients | what does teriyaki mean => Teriyaki is a Japanese cooking style where food is glazed with a sweet soy based sauce, often soy sauce, mirin and sugar, and grilled or broiled. Teri means shine and yaki means grilled.
what is a churro | where are churros from | how are churros made => Churros are fried dough sticks piped through a star shaped tip, rolled in cinnamon sugar and often dipped in chocolate. They're popular in Spain, Portugal and Latin America.
what is mochi | what is mochi made of | mochi ice cream => Mochi is a Japanese treat made from sticky glutinous rice pounded into a soft, chewy dough. Mochi ice cream wraps the dough around a ball of ice cream.
what is a galette | buckwheat crepe | galette vs crepe => In Brittany, France, a galette is a savory crepe made with buckwheat flour, often filled with ham, cheese and an egg. The word can also mean a free form rustic tart.
what is goulash | hungarian goulash | what is in goulash => Goulash is a Hungarian stew of beef, onions and lots of paprika. In the US, goulash often means a pasta dish with ground beef and tomato sauce.
what is jollof rice | where is jollof from | jollof ingredients => Jollof rice is a West African dish of rice cooked in a tomato, pepper and onion sauce with spices. Nigeria and Ghana have a friendly rivalry over whose is best.
what is a tamale | how are tamales made | tamale wrapper => Tamales are a Mesoamerican food of corn based dough, called masa, filled with meat, cheese or other fillings, wrapped in a corn husk or banana leaf and steamed. Don't eat the husk.
what is ceviche | is ceviche raw | how does lime cook fish => Ceviche is a Latin American dish, famous in Peru, of raw fish soaked in lime or lemon juice. The acid firms the fish and makes it look cooked, but it doesn't kill germs like heat, so very fresh fish matters.
what is fondue | how do you eat fondue | swiss cheese fondue => Fondue is a Swiss dish of melted cheese in a shared pot over a small flame. You dip bread on long forks into it. Chocolate fondue is a dessert version.
what is a scone | scone vs biscuit | british scones => A scone is a British baked good like a slightly sweet biscuit, often eaten with jam and clotted cream. American biscuits are usually savory and flakier.
what is tiramisu | what does tiramisu mean | tiramisu ingredients => Tiramisu is an Italian dessert of ladyfinger cookies dipped in coffee and layered with a creamy mascarpone mixture, topped with cocoa. The name means "pick me up".
what is baklava | baklava ingredients | where is baklava from => Baklava is a sweet pastry made of many thin layers of phyllo dough with chopped nuts, soaked in honey or syrup. It's popular in Turkey, Greece and the Middle East.
what is shakshuka | eggs in tomato sauce dish | shakshuka ingredients => Shakshuka is a dish of eggs poached in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce, popular in North Africa and the Middle East. It's eaten with bread for dipping.
