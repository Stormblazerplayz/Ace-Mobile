# tag: art.animation
what are the 12 principles of animation | disney animation principles | list the principles of animation => The 12 principles, from Disney animators Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston's 1981 book The Illusion of Life, are squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, straight ahead and pose to pose, follow through and overlapping action, slow in and slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, solid drawing and appeal.
what is squash and stretch | squash and stretch animation | bouncing ball squash => Squash and stretch means objects squish when they hit something and stretch when moving fast, while keeping the same volume. A bouncing ball flattens on impact and stretches as it falls, making it feel alive and springy.
what is anticipation in animation | anticipation principle | wind up before an action => Anticipation is a small move in the opposite direction before a big action, like crouching before a jump or pulling back before a throw. It prepares the audience for what's coming.
what is staging in animation | staging principle | clear poses in animation => Staging means presenting an action so it's clear and easy to read, through camera angle, pose, silhouette and what's in focus. The audience should instantly know what to look at.
what is straight ahead vs pose to pose | pose to pose animation | straight ahead action => Straight ahead means drawing each frame in order from start to finish, good for fire, water and wild action. Pose to pose means drawing the key poses first, then filling in between, which gives more control.
what is follow through and overlapping action | overlapping action animation | follow through principle => Follow through means parts keep moving after the main body stops, like hair or a cape swinging forward. Overlapping action means different parts move at different rates, so motion looks natural.
what is ease in and ease out | slow in slow out | easing animation => Most movements speed up gradually and slow down before stopping. Ease in and ease out means putting more frames near the start and end of a move and fewer in the middle. Animation software often calls these easing curves.
what are arcs in animation | animate in arcs | why do movements follow arcs => Most natural motion follows curved paths, not straight lines, like a swinging arm or a turning head. Moving things along arcs makes animation look smooth and organic.
what is secondary action | secondary action animation | small extra movements => Secondary action is a smaller motion that supports the main one, like a character whistling while walking or tapping fingers while thinking. It adds personality without stealing focus.
what is timing in animation | how many frames for an action | animation timing tips => Timing is the number of frames an action takes, which controls speed and weight. More frames feel slow or heavy, fewer feel fast or light. Acting it out with a stopwatch helps.
what is exaggeration in animation | exaggerate poses | why exaggerate in cartoons => Exaggeration pushes poses, expressions and motion beyond realistic to make them read clearly and feel more alive. Even realistic animation uses a little.
what is solid drawing in animation | solid drawing principle | volume in animation => Solid drawing means drawing characters with a sense of weight, volume and 3D form so they look real in space, not flat. Good anatomy and perspective help.
what is appeal in animation | character appeal | making characters likable => Appeal is what makes a character interesting to watch, whether heroes or villains. Clear shapes, a strong design and charisma create appeal.
what is a keyframe | keyframes vs in betweens | what is tweening => Keyframes are the main poses that define an action. In betweens, or tweens, are the frames that fill the gaps. In digital software, the computer can create tweens between keyframes.
animating on twos | what does on twos mean in animation | drawings per second in cartoons => Films run at 24 frames per second. Many hand drawn animations are done on twos, holding each drawing for two frames, so 12 drawings per second. Fast action is often on ones.
what is onion skinning | onion skin animation | see previous frame => Onion skinning shows faint versions of the frames before and after the one you're drawing, so you can line up movement smoothly. Almost every animation app has it.
how do i animate a walk cycle | walk cycle animation | walk cycle key poses => A walk cycle is a loop of steps. The main poses are contact (heel touches), down (weight lowers), passing (one leg passes the other) and up (body rises). Animate one step, then mirror it for the other leg and loop.
how do i animate a run cycle | run cycle vs walk cycle | running animation => A run has a moment where both feet are off the ground, more body lean forward, bigger arm swings and faster timing. Runs are often about 8 to 16 frames per cycle at 24 fps.
how do i animate a bouncing ball | bouncing ball exercise | first animation exercise => Draw the ball's arc, space frames closer at the top where it slows and farther apart near the ground where it's fast, squash it on contact and stretch it while falling. Each bounce gets lower.
how do i do lip sync animation | mouth shapes for animation | animating talking => Break the dialogue into sounds and use a set of mouth shapes, often around 8 to 10, like closed for M, B and P, open for A, and rounded for O. Time them to the audio and hit consonants a frame early.
what is a storyboard | how to make a storyboard | storyboard for animation => A storyboard is a series of rough sketches showing each shot of a story, like a comic, with notes on camera moves and dialogue. It's planned before animating anything.
what is an animatic | animatic vs storyboard | how to make an animatic => An animatic is a storyboard edited into a video with timing, sound and dialogue. It shows whether the story and pacing work before the hard animation begins.
what is rotoscoping | rotoscope animation | tracing over video => Rotoscoping is tracing over live action footage frame by frame to create realistic animated motion. It was invented by Max Fleischer in the 1910s.
what is stop motion animation | how to make a stop motion | claymation how to => Stop motion means moving real objects, like clay figures or LEGO, a tiny bit between each photo, then playing the photos in sequence. Use a tripod, steady lighting and an app like Stop Motion Studio.
what is claymation | famous claymation movies | wallace and gromit => Claymation is stop motion with clay figures. Aardman Animations made Wallace and Gromit and Chicken Run, and Laika made Coraline using puppets.
what is 2d vs 3d animation | difference between 2d and 3d animation | which is easier 2d or 3d => 2D animation uses flat drawings, frame by frame or with rigs. 3D animation moves digital models in 3D software like Blender. Both take practice. 2D can start with just a tablet, while 3D needs modeling and rigging too.
what is rigging in animation | character rig | bones in 3d animation => Rigging is building a skeleton of bones and controls inside a character so animators can pose it like a puppet. Good rigs have controls for the body, face and fingers.
what is motion capture | mocap animation | how does mocap work => Motion capture records a real actor's movements with special suits, markers or cameras and applies them to a digital character. It's used in games and films like Avatar.
what apps can i use to animate on ipad | animation apps for ipad | best animation app for beginners => Popular iPad apps include Procreate Dreams, FlipaClip, Callipeg, RoughAnimator and Stop Motion Studio. FlipaClip is free to start and easy to learn.
what free software can i use for animation | free animation programs | blender for animation => Free options on computers include Blender for 3D and 2D Grease Pencil, Krita and OpenToonz for 2D frame by frame, and Pencil2D for simple 2D.
what was the first animated feature film | first disney movie | snow white 1937 => Disney's Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937) was the first full length cel animated feature film made in the US and a huge success. Earlier animated features were made in Argentina and Germany.
what was the first fully cgi movie | toy story first cgi movie | first computer animated film => Pixar's Toy Story (1995) was the first feature length film made entirely with computer animation.
what is studio ghibli | studio ghibli movies | hayao miyazaki films => Studio Ghibli is a Japanese animation studio co founded by Hayao Miyazaki, known for hand drawn films like My Neighbor Totoro, Spirited Away and The Boy and the Heron. Spirited Away won an Oscar in 2003.
what is cel animation | how were old cartoons made | animation cels => In traditional cel animation, each drawing was traced onto a clear plastic sheet called a cel and painted on the back, then layered over a painted background and photographed frame by frame.
what is a smear frame | smear animation | motion blur in 2d animation => A smear frame is a stretched, blurry drawing on one or two frames of a very fast move, giving the feeling of motion blur. Cartoons use them a lot.
what is a hold in animation | moving hold | why characters never freeze completely => A hold keeps a pose still for several frames so the audience can read it. A moving hold adds tiny movements, like breathing or blinking, so the character doesn't look frozen.
how do i make my animation smoother | choppy animation fix | smoother animation tips => Add more in betweens, use easing so moves don't start and stop suddenly, keep shapes consistent between frames, follow arcs, and check timing by playing it back often.
how long does it take to animate | how long to make a 1 minute animation | animation takes forever => It depends on style. A minute of hand drawn animation at 12 drawings per second is 720 drawings. Simple styles, limited animation and reusing loops make it much faster for solo creators.
how do i animate for youtube shorts | vertical animation size | animation format for shorts => Animate at 1080 by 1920 pixels in a 9:16 vertical frame, keep important action in the center, start with a hook in the first second, and keep it fast. As of 2025, Shorts can be up to 3 minutes long.
what is a background artist | painting backgrounds for animation | background design tips => Background artists paint the settings characters move through. Use perspective, simpler detail where characters act, and colors that make characters stand out.
what is limited animation | how cartoons save time | limited animation tricks => Limited animation reuses drawings, animates only parts that move, like a mouth, and uses holds and loops. Many TV cartoons use it to save time while still looking good.
what is a turnaround in 3d modeling | modeling sheet | front and side reference for 3d => A turnaround shows a character from the front, side and back with matching proportions. 3D modelers place these as reference images to model accurately.
what is a frame by frame animation | frame by frame vs tween | traditional animation => Frame by frame animation means drawing every frame by hand, like traditional cartoons. Tweening lets software create in between frames automatically from keyframes.
what is a graph editor in animation | animation curves | f curves blender => A graph editor shows how a value, like position or rotation, changes over time as a curve. Adjusting the curve's shape changes easing and timing.
what is a camera move in animation | pan zoom truck | camera shots in animation => Common camera moves are pans (sliding side to side), tilts, zooms, and trucking in or out. Shot types include wide shots to show a place and close ups for emotion.
how do i add sound to my animation | sound effects for animation | free sound effects => Add sound in your animation app or a video editor like DaVinci Resolve or CapCut. Sound effects and music make animation feel much more alive. Use royalty free sound libraries.
how do i come up with ideas for animations | short animation ideas | what should i animate => Start with a simple, funny moment with one character and one problem, like a robot trying to catch a fly. Short and focused beats long and unfinished.
