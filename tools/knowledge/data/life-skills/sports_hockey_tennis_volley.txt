# tag: sports.hockey
what is icing in hockey | icing rule explained | why is icing called => Icing is when a team shoots the puck from its own half across the other team's goal line without anyone touching it. Play stops and the faceoff goes back to the offending team's end. A team on the penalty kill is allowed to ice the puck.
what is offside in hockey | hockey offside rule | blue line offside => An attacking player can't cross the other team's blue line into the offensive zone before the puck does. If they do, it's offside and there's a faceoff outside the zone.
what is a power play | penalty kill meaning | how does a power play work => When a player gets a penalty, they sit in the penalty box and their team plays a player short. The other team has a power play, and the short handed team is on the penalty kill.
how long is a hockey penalty | minor vs major penalty | what is a two minute penalty => A minor penalty is 2 minutes and ends early if the other team scores. A major penalty is 5 minutes, often for fighting, and doesn't end early.
what are hockey positions | hockey positions explained | what does a center do in hockey => Each team has a goalie, two defensemen and three forwards: a center and two wingers. The center takes faceoffs and plays both ends, wingers attack along the sides, and defensemen protect their own zone.
what is a faceoff | how does a faceoff work | faceoff dots => A faceoff starts play: the referee drops the puck between two players, usually centers, who try to win it to a teammate. There are nine faceoff spots on the rink.
how big is a hockey puck | what is a puck made of | why are pucks frozen => A regulation puck is made of hard vulcanized rubber, 1 inch thick, 3 inches across and about 6 ounces. Pucks are frozen before games so they bounce less.
how big is a hockey rink | nhl rink size | olympic hockey rink => An NHL rink is 200 feet long and 85 feet wide. International rinks are traditionally wider, about 60 by 30 m.
how does overtime work in the nhl | nhl shootout | 3 on 3 overtime => In the NHL regular season, a tie after three periods goes to a 5 minute, 3 on 3 sudden death overtime, then a shootout if still tied. In the playoffs, teams play full 20 minute 5 on 5 periods until someone scores.
what is the stanley cup | who has won the most stanley cups | stanley cup history => The Stanley Cup is the NHL's championship trophy, first awarded in 1893. As of 2025, the Montreal Canadiens have won it the most, 24 times.
who won the 2025 stanley cup | 2025 stanley cup champion | stanley cup winner 2025 => The Florida Panthers won the 2025 Stanley Cup, beating the Edmonton Oilers in the Final for the second year in a row.
who has the most goals in nhl history | ovechkin vs gretzky goals | nhl goal record => Alex Ovechkin passed Wayne Gretzky's record of 894 career goals in April 2025 and became the NHL's all time leading goal scorer.
why is wayne gretzky called the great one | gretzky records | how many points did gretzky have => Wayne Gretzky holds dozens of NHL records, including 2,857 career points and 1,963 assists. He has more assists alone than any other player has total points.
how many teams are in the nhl | number of nhl teams | newest nhl team => As of 2025, the NHL has 32 teams. The newest are the Seattle Kraken, who joined in 2021, and the Utah team, which moved from Arizona in 2024.
what is a hat trick in hockey | why do fans throw hats | hockey hat trick tradition => A hat trick is three goals by one player in a game. Hockey fans celebrate by throwing hats onto the ice.
what is a slap shot | slap shot vs wrist shot | types of hockey shots => A slap shot is a powerful shot with a big wind up where the stick hits the ice just before the puck. A wrist shot is quicker and more accurate, flicked with the wrists, and a snap shot is in between.
what is a zamboni | why do they resurface the ice | what does a zamboni do => A Zamboni is a machine that smooths the ice by shaving the top layer, collecting the snow and laying down a thin coat of warm water that freezes smooth. Frank Zamboni invented it in 1949.
what is checking in hockey | body checking rules | when can kids body check => Body checking is using your body to knock an opponent off the puck. USA Hockey doesn't allow it until the 13U to 14U level and never in girls' and women's hockey.
what is a hockey assist | primary and secondary assist | how assists work in hockey => In hockey, up to two players can get an assist on a goal: the last two teammates to touch the puck before the scorer.
what equipment do you need for hockey | hockey gear list | what pads do hockey players wear => Players wear skates, a helmet with a face cage or shield, a mouthguard, shoulder pads, elbow pads, gloves, padded hockey pants, shin guards, a neck guard and a protective cup or jill, plus a stick.
how do i learn to skate | ice skating for beginners | how to stop on ice skates => Bend your knees, lean slightly forward, and push out to the side with one skate at a time, gliding on the other. To stop, push your heels out and toes in for a snowplow stop. Falling is normal, so wear a helmet.
what is a hockey shootout | shootout rules | how does a shootout work => In a shootout, three players from each team take turns skating in alone against the goalie. If still tied, it goes to sudden death rounds.
what is a five hole | five hole meaning | where to shoot on a goalie => The five hole is the gap between a goalie's legs. Shooters also aim for high corners, called top shelf, or low corners near the posts.
what is a hockey line change | changing on the fly | why do hockey players switch so often => Hockey is so tiring that forwards usually play shifts of about 45 seconds before switching with teammates. They can change on the fly while play keeps going.

# tag: sports.tennis
what are the four grand slams in tennis | tennis grand slam tournaments | what is a grand slam in tennis => The four Grand Slams are the Australian Open (hard court, January), the French Open (clay, May to June), Wimbledon (grass, June to July) and the US Open (hard court, August to September). Winning all four in one year is a calendar Grand Slam.
who has the most grand slam titles in tennis | most grand slams men | djokovic grand slams => As of 2025, Novak Djokovic has the most men's Grand Slam singles titles with 24. Rafael Nadal won 22 and Roger Federer 20.
who has the most women's grand slam titles | serena williams grand slams | margaret court record => Margaret Court holds the all time record with 24 women's singles Grand Slams. Serena Williams won 23, the most in the Open Era since 1968.
who won the 2025 grand slams | tennis grand slam winners 2025 | who won wimbledon 2025 => In 2025 men's singles, Jannik Sinner won the Australian Open and Wimbledon, and Carlos Alcaraz won the French Open and US Open. In women's singles, Madison Keys, Coco Gauff, Iga Swiatek and Aryna Sabalenka won them in that order.
how does a tennis tiebreak work | tiebreaker rules tennis | what is a tie break => At 6 games all, players play a tiebreak: points go 1, 2, 3 instead of 15, 30, 40, and the first to 7 points, winning by 2, wins the set. Players switch serves every two points.
what is deuce in tennis | advantage in tennis | how do you win from deuce => Deuce is 40 all. A player must then win two points in a row: the first gives them the advantage, and the second wins the game. Losing the next point goes back to deuce.
how many sets are in a tennis match | best of three vs best of five | grand slam sets => Most matches are best of three sets. Men's singles at Grand Slams are best of five. You win a set by winning six games by two, or by a tiebreak.
what is a let in tennis | let serve | when do you replay a serve => A let is when a serve hits the net and still lands in the correct service box. The serve is replayed with no penalty.
what is a double fault | foot fault tennis | how many serves do you get => You get two tries to serve. Missing both is a double fault and the other player wins the point. A foot fault is stepping on or over the baseline before hitting the serve.
what is an ace in tennis | service winner | fastest serve in tennis => An ace is a legal serve the opponent doesn't touch. As of 2025, Sam Groth's 263 km/h (163.7 mph) serve in 2012 is the fastest recorded, though it wasn't on the official ATP tour list.
how big is a tennis court | tennis court dimensions | how high is a tennis net => A tennis court is 78 feet (23.77 m) long. It's 27 feet wide for singles and 36 feet for doubles. The net is 3 feet high at the center and 3.5 feet at the posts.
what are the different tennis court surfaces | clay vs grass vs hard court | which surface is fastest => Grass is the fastest with low bounces, clay is the slowest with high bounces and long rallies, and hard courts are in between.
what is a forehand and backhand | tennis strokes | one handed vs two handed backhand => A forehand is hit on your dominant side with your palm facing forward. A backhand is hit on the other side, with one or two hands. Volleys are hit before the ball bounces, near the net.
how do i serve in tennis | tennis serve for beginners | how to toss for a serve => Stand sideways behind the baseline, toss the ball a bit in front and slightly above your reach, swing your racket up and over, and hit it at the highest point. A consistent toss is the key.
what does love mean in tennis score | why is zero called love | love all => Love means zero. Nobody knows for sure where it came from, but a popular theory is the French word l'oeuf, meaning egg, because a zero looks like an egg.
what is hawk eye | electronic line calling | how do they check if the ball is in => Hawk-Eye uses many high speed cameras to track the ball and show exactly where it landed. Many big tournaments now use electronic line calling instead of human line judges.
why do players wear white at wimbledon | wimbledon dress code | wimbledon rules => Wimbledon has a strict tradition that players wear almost all white clothing. Since 2023, women have been allowed to wear dark undershorts.
what is pickleball | pickleball rules | pickleball vs tennis => Pickleball is a paddle sport played on a badminton sized court with a low net and a plastic ball with holes. Serves are underhand, and players can't volley from the non volley zone near the net, called the kitchen.
what is padel | padel vs tennis | how is padel played => Padel is a racket sport played in doubles on an enclosed court with glass walls that the ball can bounce off, like squash and tennis mixed. Serves are underhand.
what is table tennis | ping pong rules | how many points to win ping pong => In table tennis, games go to 11 points, win by 2, and players switch serve every 2 points. The ball must bounce once on each side of the table during a serve.
how do i get better at tennis | tennis tips for kids | tennis practice => Take lessons to learn good form early, practice footwork and staying on your toes, hit against a wall to build consistency, and play lots of points. Focus on getting the ball in before hitting hard.

# tag: sports.volleyball
how does volleyball scoring work | how many points to win a volleyball set | volleyball rules for beginners => Indoor volleyball uses rally scoring, so a point is scored on every serve. Sets go to 25, win by 2, and matches are best of five, with a fifth set to 15.
how many touches do you get in volleyball | three hit rule | can you hit the ball twice in volleyball => Each team gets up to three touches to send the ball over, and one player can't touch it twice in a row. A block doesn't count as one of the three touches.
what are the positions in volleyball | volleyball positions explained | what does a setter do => The main positions are setter, outside hitter, opposite hitter, middle blocker and libero. The setter takes the second touch and sets up hitters, like a point guard in basketball.
what is a libero | libero rules | why does the libero wear a different jersey => The libero is a defensive specialist who wears a different color jersey. They play back row only, can't attack the ball above the net, and can sub in and out without counting as a normal substitution.
what is rotation in volleyball | why do volleyball players rotate | rotation rules => When your team wins the serve back, players rotate one position clockwise. Players must be in the correct order when the ball is served.
what is a spike in volleyball | how to spike a volleyball | attack hit => A spike is a hard downward hit over the net. Take a fast approach of three or four steps, jump with both arms swinging up, and snap your wrist to hit the top of the ball.
what is a bump in volleyball | how to pass a volleyball | forearm pass => A bump, or forearm pass, uses your joined forearms to pass the ball. Make a flat platform with your arms, bend your knees and move your feet to get under the ball, using your legs, not your arms, to lift it.
what is a set in volleyball | how to set a volleyball | overhead pass => A set is an overhead pass with your fingertips, usually the second touch, placing the ball up for a hitter. Form a triangle with your thumbs and fingers above your forehead and push up with your legs and arms.
what is a dig in volleyball | dig meaning | how to dig a hard spike => A dig is passing a hard hit or spiked ball to keep it from hitting the floor. Stay low, get in the ball's path and keep your platform angled toward the target.
what is an ace in volleyball | service ace | how to serve overhand => An ace is a serve that scores directly without the other team keeping it in play. For an overhand serve, toss the ball in front of your hitting shoulder and hit it with a firm open palm.
what is a block in volleyball | how to block a spike | blocking rules => A block is jumping at the net with arms up to stop or slow an attack. You can reach over the net to block as long as you don't touch the net. Only front row players can block.
what are common volleyball faults | net violation | what is a lift in volleyball => Faults include touching the net during play, crossing the center line, a lift or carry where the ball rests in your hands, a double hit, and stepping on the end line while serving.
how big is a volleyball court | volleyball court dimensions | what is the attack line => An indoor court is 18 m long and 9 m wide. The attack line is 3 m from the net, and back row players must jump from behind it to attack above the net.
what is beach volleyball | beach volleyball rules | how many players in beach volleyball => Beach volleyball is played on sand with two players per team. Sets go to 21, win by 2, and matches are best of three, with a third set to 15.
how do i get better at volleyball | volleyball drills at home | volleyball tips for beginners => Practice passing against a wall, set to yourself to build touch, work on serves until they're consistent, and play with others as much as you can. Stay low and move your feet to the ball.
