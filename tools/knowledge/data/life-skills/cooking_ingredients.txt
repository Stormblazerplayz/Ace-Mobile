# tag: life.cooking.ingredients
what do eggs do in baking | why do recipes need eggs | role of eggs in cake => Eggs hold ingredients together, help baked goods rise, add moisture and richness, and give color. Yolks add fat and whites add structure.
what does baking soda do | why use baking soda | how does baking soda make things rise => Baking soda is a base that reacts with acids like buttermilk, yogurt, brown sugar or lemon juice to make carbon dioxide bubbles, which make dough rise. It also helps browning.
what does baking powder do | is baking powder the same as yeast | double acting baking powder => Baking powder is baking soda already mixed with a dry acid, so it makes bubbles when wet and again when heated. It's for quick breads, cakes and pancakes, while yeast is a living fungus used for bread.
what does salt do in baking | why add salt to cookies | do you need salt in sweets => Salt brings out flavor and balances sweetness, so sweets taste less flat. In bread, it also controls yeast and strengthens the dough.
what does sugar do in baking | why is sugar in bread | can i use less sugar in a recipe => Sugar adds sweetness, keeps baked goods moist and tender, helps browning and feeds yeast. You can often cut sugar by about a quarter, but more than that changes texture.
what does butter do in baking | why is butter important in cookies | cold butter vs soft butter => Butter adds flavor, tenderness and richness. Cold butter makes flaky layers in pie crust and biscuits, softened butter traps air when creamed for cakes and cookies, and melted butter makes chewier cookies.
what does flour do in baking | what is gluten in flour | types of flour => Flour gives structure. When wheat flour gets wet and mixed, its proteins form gluten, a stretchy net that traps gas. More mixing and higher protein flour make chewier results.
why do you sift flour | do i need to sift flour | what does sifting do => Sifting breaks up lumps and adds air. Many modern recipes don't need it, but it helps for very light cakes or when mixing in cocoa powder, which clumps.
what is the difference between white and brown sugar | brown sugar vs white sugar | why is brown sugar moist => Brown sugar is white sugar with molasses, which makes it moist, a bit acidic and caramel tasting. It makes cookies softer and chewier.
what does vanilla extract do | why add vanilla to recipes | what is vanilla made from => Vanilla extract adds a warm, sweet flavor and makes other flavors like chocolate taste fuller. It's made by soaking vanilla bean pods, from an orchid plant, in alcohol and water.
what is cocoa powder | natural vs dutch process cocoa | cocoa powder vs hot chocolate mix => Cocoa powder is chocolate with most of the fat removed, unsweetened. Dutch process cocoa is treated to be less acidic and darker. Hot chocolate mix is cocoa with sugar and milk powder added.
what is the difference between baking chocolate types | semisweet vs bittersweet | milk vs dark chocolate => Unsweetened chocolate has no sugar, bittersweet and semisweet have some sugar with bittersweet usually darker, milk chocolate adds milk and more sugar, and white chocolate has cocoa butter but no cocoa solids.
what is olive oil good for | extra virgin olive oil meaning | cooking with olive oil => Extra virgin olive oil is the least processed with the most flavor, great for dressings, drizzling and medium heat cooking. Light or regular olive oil has a milder taste.
what is a smoke point | which oil is best for frying | high heat cooking oil => The smoke point is the temperature where oil starts to smoke and break down. For high heat frying, use oils with high smoke points like canola, vegetable, peanut or avocado oil.
what is vegetable oil | what is canola oil | neutral oil meaning => Vegetable oil is a neutral tasting oil, often soybean or a blend. Canola oil comes from rapeseed. Both are called neutral oils because they don't add much flavor.
what is cornstarch | what is cornstarch used for | cornstarch vs flour thickening => Cornstarch is a fine powder from corn that thickens sauces, puddings and gravies. It thickens about twice as much as flour and turns sauces glossy instead of cloudy.
what is gelatin | how does jello set | is gelatin vegetarian => Gelatin is a protein made from animal collagen that sets liquids into a jiggly gel when cooled. It isn't vegetarian, but agar agar, from seaweed, works similarly.
what is cream cheese | what is cream cheese used for | cream cheese vs regular cheese => Cream cheese is a soft, mild, spreadable fresh cheese used on bagels, in frosting, dips and cheesecake.
what is mozzarella | why does mozzarella stretch | best cheese for pizza => Mozzarella is a soft Italian cheese famous for stretching when melted, because of how its proteins line up when it's made. Low moisture mozzarella is the classic pizza cheese.
what cheese melts best | best melting cheese | cheese for grilled cheese => Young, moist cheeses like American, mozzarella, Monterey Jack, mild cheddar, Gruyere and fontina melt smoothly. Aged dry cheeses like parmesan don't melt as smoothly.
what is buttermilk | why use buttermilk | what does buttermilk taste like => Store buttermilk is milk that's been cultured with bacteria, making it tangy and thick. Its acid reacts with baking soda to make fluffy pancakes, biscuits and tender fried chicken.
what is heavy cream | heavy cream vs whipping cream | half and half meaning => Heavy cream has at least 36 percent fat and whips the firmest. Whipping cream has a bit less fat. Half and half is half milk and half cream and won't whip.
what is yogurt made of | how is yogurt made | what are live cultures => Yogurt is milk fermented by helpful bacteria, which turn milk sugar into acid so it thickens and tastes tangy. Live cultures means those bacteria are still alive.
what is greek yogurt | greek yogurt vs regular | why is greek yogurt thick => Greek yogurt is strained to remove much of the liquid whey, making it thicker, tangier and higher in protein.
what is broth vs stock | chicken stock vs broth | what is bouillon => Stock is usually made by simmering bones and is richer in body, while broth is made mostly with meat and is often seasoned. Bouillon cubes are dried, concentrated broth. They can swap in most recipes.
what is soy sauce made of | how is soy sauce made | is soy sauce gluten free => Soy sauce is made by fermenting soybeans, wheat, salt and water. Regular soy sauce contains wheat, so it isn't gluten free, but tamari often is.
what is worcestershire sauce | how do you say worcestershire | what's in worcestershire sauce => Worcestershire sauce is a tangy, savory British sauce with vinegar, molasses, tamarind, anchovies and spices. It's said "WUSS-ter-sher".
what is vinegar | types of vinegar | what is vinegar used for in cooking => Vinegar is a sour liquid made when bacteria turn alcohol into acetic acid. White vinegar is sharp, apple cider vinegar is fruity, rice vinegar is mild and balsamic is sweet. It adds tang to dressings, pickles and sauces.
what is mustard made of | yellow vs dijon mustard | how is mustard made => Mustard is made from ground mustard seeds mixed with liquid like vinegar or water. Yellow mustard gets its color from turmeric and is mild, while Dijon is sharper and creamier.
what is paprika | smoked paprika vs regular | is paprika spicy => Paprika is ground dried peppers. Sweet paprika is mild, hot paprika has a kick, and smoked paprika has a smoky barbecue flavor.
what is cumin | what does cumin taste like | what dishes use cumin => Cumin is a warm, earthy, slightly smoky spice from seeds. It's big in tacos, chili, curry and Middle Eastern food.
what is cinnamon | where does cinnamon come from | cinnamon on what => Cinnamon is the dried inner bark of certain trees, rolled into sticks or ground. It's sweet and warm, great in oatmeal, baking, French toast and some savory dishes.
what is oregano | oregano vs basil | herbs for pizza => Oregano is a strong, earthy herb used in pizza, pasta sauce and Greek food. Basil is sweeter and fresher, and is best added at the end or raw.
what is cilantro | why does cilantro taste like soap | coriander vs cilantro => Cilantro is the leaves of the coriander plant, and coriander usually means its seeds. Some people have genes that make cilantro taste soapy to them.
what is ginger | fresh ginger how to use | how to peel ginger => Ginger is a spicy, zingy root. Scrape off the skin with the edge of a spoon, then grate, mince or slice it for stir fries, tea and baking.
what is msg | is msg bad for you | monosodium glutamate => MSG, monosodium glutamate, adds savory umami flavor. It's found naturally in foods like tomatoes and parmesan. The US FDA considers it generally recognized as safe, though a few people say it bothers them.
what is nutritional yeast | what does nutritional yeast taste like | nooch => Nutritional yeast is deactivated yeast sold as yellow flakes. It tastes cheesy and nutty, so people sprinkle it on popcorn, pasta and vegan dishes.
what is tofu | how is tofu made | how do you cook tofu => Tofu is made by curdling soy milk and pressing the curds into blocks, kind of like cheese. It soaks up flavors. Press out water, then bake, fry or stir fry it until crispy.
what is quinoa | how do you cook quinoa | is quinoa a grain => Quinoa is a seed eaten like a grain, high in protein. Rinse it, then simmer 1 cup in about 2 cups of water for around 15 minutes until the water is absorbed.
what is couscous | how do you make couscous | is couscous pasta => Couscous is tiny pasta made from wheat. Most store couscous just needs boiling water poured over it, covered for 5 minutes, then fluffed with a fork.
what are lentils | how do you cook lentils | do lentils need soaking => Lentils are small legumes full of protein and fiber. They don't need soaking: rinse and simmer in water, about 20 to 30 minutes for brown or green lentils.
do i need to soak dried beans | how do i cook dried beans | canned vs dried beans => Soaking overnight helps beans cook faster and more evenly, but isn't strictly required. Simmer until tender, usually 1 to 2 hours. Canned beans are already cooked, just rinse and heat.
what is panko | panko vs breadcrumbs | why is panko crispier => Panko is a Japanese style breadcrumb made from crustless bread into big, airy flakes. It soaks up less oil, so food comes out lighter and crunchier.
what is pesto | what is pesto made of | traditional pesto ingredients => Classic pesto from Genoa, Italy, is basil, pine nuts, garlic, parmesan, salt and olive oil blended or pounded into a sauce. It's great on pasta and sandwiches.
what is salsa | pico de gallo vs salsa | salsa ingredients => Salsa means sauce in Spanish. Tomato salsa usually has tomatoes, onion, chili, cilantro, lime and salt. Pico de gallo is a fresh, chunky, uncooked version.
what is hummus | what is hummus made of | is hummus healthy => Hummus is a dip from chickpeas blended with tahini, lemon juice, garlic and olive oil. It's a good source of protein and fiber.
what is tahini | what is tahini made from | tahini uses => Tahini is a paste made from ground sesame seeds. It's used in hummus, dressings and some desserts. People with a sesame allergy must avoid it.
what is sourdough | how is sourdough made | sourdough starter meaning => Sourdough is bread risen with a starter, a mix of flour and water full of wild yeast and bacteria, instead of store yeast. It has a tangy flavor and needs to be fed regularly.
what is brioche | what makes brioche different | brioche bread => Brioche is a French bread with lots of eggs and butter, making it soft, rich and slightly sweet. It's great for French toast and burger buns.
what is a roux based sauce | mother sauces | what are the five french sauces => The five French mother sauces are bechamel (milk), veloute (light stock), espagnole (brown stock), hollandaise (butter and egg yolk) and tomato. Many other sauces are made from them.
what is aioli | aioli vs mayonnaise | garlic mayo => Traditional aioli is a garlic and olive oil sauce from the Mediterranean. Today it often means garlic flavored mayonnaise.
what is an emulsion | why don't oil and vinegar mix | how does mayonnaise stay mixed => An emulsion is a mix of two liquids that normally don't blend, like oil and water. In mayo, egg yolk acts as an emulsifier that keeps tiny oil drops spread through the liquid.
what makes food crispy | how to make things crunchy | why does food go soggy => Crispiness comes from water leaving the surface quickly in high heat. Drying food first, using hot oil or a hot oven and not crowding the pan helps. Steam trapped under a lid makes things soggy.
why does meat get tough | why is my steak chewy | tender vs tough meat => Hard working muscles have more connective tissue. Quick cooking works for tender cuts, while tough cuts like chuck need long, slow cooking to melt the tissue. Overcooking tender cuts also makes them dry and tough.
what is the difference between a fruit and vegetable in cooking | culinary vs botanical fruit | is a cucumber a fruit => In botany, a fruit is the part that holds seeds, so cucumbers, peppers and tomatoes are fruits. Cooks call them vegetables because they're used in savory dishes.
why do apples turn brown | stop apple slices browning | browning cut fruit => When cut, an enzyme in the apple reacts with oxygen and turns it brown. Squeezing on lemon juice or dipping in lightly salted water slows it down.
why does bread go stale | how to fix stale bread | stale bread uses => Bread goes stale mostly because its starch hardens as it cools and ages, not just from drying out. A quick warm up in the oven softens it for a while, and stale bread is great for French toast and croutons.
why do bananas turn brown | should bananas go in the fridge | ripe banana spots => Bananas ripen and brown as starch turns to sugar. The fridge turns the peel black but keeps the inside firm longer once ripe. Spotty bananas are perfect for banana bread.
what is the difference between sweet potatoes and yams | are yams sweet potatoes | sweet potato vs yam => In US stores, "yams" are usually orange sweet potatoes. True yams are a different plant with rough, bark like skin, more common in Africa and Asia.
what does it mean when a recipe says room temperature eggs | why room temperature ingredients | warming eggs quickly => Room temperature eggs and butter mix together more smoothly and trap more air, giving fluffier cakes. To warm eggs fast, set them in a bowl of warm tap water for 5 to 10 minutes.
what is the difference between cocoa and cacao | cacao nibs | raw cacao meaning => Cacao usually refers to the raw or less processed bean and products like cacao nibs. Cocoa usually refers to roasted, processed powder. They come from the same tree.
what is a sprinkle of salt vs a dash | dash pinch smidgen meaning | cooking measurement words => These are tiny, informal amounts. A dash is a quick shake or splash, a pinch is what fits between thumb and finger, and a smidgen is even smaller. Recipes using them expect you to taste and adjust.
