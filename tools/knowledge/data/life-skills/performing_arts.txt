# tag: arts.dance
what are the five ballet positions | ballet positions feet | first position ballet => The five basic ballet positions describe how the feet are placed, all turned out from the hips. First has heels together, second feet apart, third one heel at the other's middle, fourth one foot in front with space, and fifth feet tight together heel to toe.
what is a plie | ballet terms | plie releve tendu => A plie is a bend of the knees. A releve is rising onto the balls of the feet or toes. A tendu stretches the foot along the floor until only the toes touch. Ballet terms are in French.
what is pointe in ballet | pointe shoes | when can you go on pointe => Dancing en pointe means on the tips of the toes in special stiff shoes. Dancers usually start only after several years of training and with their teacher's approval, often around age 11 to 12, to protect growing feet.
what is a pirouette | how to do a pirouette | ballet spin => A pirouette is a spin on one foot. Keep your core tight, arms rounded, and spot by keeping your eyes on one point and whipping your head around quickly to avoid dizziness.
what is spotting in dance | how dancers dont get dizzy | spotting turns => Spotting means focusing your eyes on one spot and turning your head quickly at the last moment as you spin. It helps balance and reduces dizziness.
what is hip hop dance | hip hop dance styles | popping locking => Hip hop dance includes styles like breaking, popping, locking, krumping and freestyle. It started in New York and California in the 1970s alongside hip hop music.
what are breakdancing moves | breaking moves | toprock downrock freeze => Breaking has four main parts: toprock (standing steps), downrock or footwork on the floor, power moves like windmills and head spins, and freezes, where you hold a pose.
what is tap dancing | tap shoes | how does tap dance work => Tap dancers wear shoes with metal plates on the heel and toe, using their feet as percussion instruments to make rhythms.
what is contemporary dance | contemporary vs modern dance | lyrical dance => Contemporary dance blends ballet, modern and jazz with free, expressive movement. Lyrical dance is similar, often interpreting song lyrics with emotion.
what is jazz dance | jazz dance style | jazz hands => Jazz dance is energetic with sharp moves, kicks, leaps and turns, often seen in musicals and competitions. Jazz hands are open palms with spread fingers, shaken for flair.
what is salsa dancing | latin dances | salsa basic step => Salsa is a lively partner dance from Cuban and Puerto Rican roots, popularized in New York. Its basic step follows 8 counts, stepping on 1, 2, 3 and 5, 6, 7.
what is ballroom dancing | ballroom dances list | waltz tango foxtrot => Ballroom dances include the waltz, foxtrot, quickstep, tango and Viennese waltz, plus Latin styles like cha cha, samba, rumba and paso doble.
what is a waltz | waltz count | 3 4 dance => The waltz is a smooth, turning partner dance in 3/4 time, counted "1, 2, 3," with a strong step on 1.
what is a flash mob | flash mob dance | organizing a flash mob => A flash mob is a group that suddenly performs a dance or song in a public place, then disperses. Planning one needs practice and permission from the place.
how do i learn to dance | dancing for beginners | learn dance moves at home => Start with simple steps from beginner videos or a class, practice to music you like, use a mirror, learn the counts, and don't worry about looking silly. Rhythm comes with practice.
how do i dance at a school dance | dancing when you dont know how | nervous to dance => Just move to the beat with simple steps side to side, bob your head and relax. Dance with friends, and remember most people are focused on themselves, not you.
what is choreography | choreographer | how to choreograph a dance => Choreography is the planned set of moves in a dance. A choreographer creates it by listening to the music, matching moves to counts, and building sections.
what is a dance recital | dance competition | dance team => A dance recital is a show where students perform what they learned. Competitions are judged on technique, performance and choreography.
# tag: arts.theater
what are stage directions | stage left and right | upstage downstage => Stage directions are from the actor's view facing the audience. Stage left is the actor's left, and downstage is toward the audience. Upstage is away from them, because old stages sloped upward at the back.
why do actors say break a leg | break a leg meaning | theater superstitions => Actors say "break a leg" for good luck because saying "good luck" is considered bad luck in theater. Another superstition is not saying "Macbeth" inside a theater.
how do i memorize lines for a play | memorizing a script | learn lines fast => Read the whole scene to understand it, learn small chunks, say lines out loud with your cue lines, record and listen to them, and practice with a friend.
how do i do a good audition | acting audition tips | drama club audition => Prepare your piece well, choose something that fits you, speak clearly and loudly, make bold choices, stay calm if you mess up, and thank the panel.
how do i project my voice on stage | speak louder on stage | stage voice => Breathe from your belly, open your mouth more, speak to the back row, and slow down. Projecting isn't yelling: it's supported, clear speech.
what do the people backstage do | theater crew jobs | stage crew => Backstage crew include the stage manager, who runs the show, set builders, lighting and sound operators, costume and props teams, and stagehands who move scenery.
what is improv | improv games | how to do improv => Improv is acting without a script, making scenes up on the spot. The key rule is "yes, and": accept what your partner offers and add something new.
what is a monologue | monologue vs soliloquy | monologue for auditions => A monologue is a long speech by one character. A soliloquy is a monologue spoken alone, sharing inner thoughts, like Hamlet's "To be or not to be."
what is a script format | how to write a play script | screenplay format => Plays list the character's name, then their lines, with stage directions in italics or brackets. Screenplays use a standard format with scene headings like INT. HOUSE - NIGHT.
what is the fourth wall | breaking the fourth wall | fourth wall meaning => The fourth wall is the imaginary wall between performers and the audience. Breaking it means a character speaks directly to the audience.
what are the tony awards | tony awards meaning | oscars emmys tonys grammys => The Tonys honor Broadway theater, the Oscars movies, the Emmys television and the Grammys music. Winning all four is called an EGOT.
what is stage makeup | why do actors wear makeup | theater makeup => Bright stage lights wash out faces, so actors wear stronger makeup to keep their features visible from far away.
what is a puppet show | types of puppets | marionette vs hand puppet => Hand puppets fit over the hand, marionettes are moved by strings from above, rod puppets use sticks, and shadow puppets cast shapes on a screen.
what is stand up comedy | how to tell a joke | comedy tips => Stand up comedy is one person telling jokes to an audience. A joke usually has a setup and a punchline, and timing, especially a short pause before the punchline, matters a lot.
how do i do a magic show | performing magic tricks | magic show tips => Practice tricks until they're smooth, never repeat a trick for the same audience, never reveal the secret, keep talking to direct attention, and plan an opening and a big finish.
how do i make a short film | filming a movie with friends | short film tips => Write a short script, plan shots with a storyboard, film with steady shots and good sound, use natural light, and edit tightly. Keep it under a few minutes for your first film.
what are camera shot types | wide medium close up | film shots explained => A wide shot shows the whole scene and setting, a medium shot shows a person from the waist up, and a close up shows a face or detail to show emotion.
what is a b roll | a roll vs b roll => A roll is the main footage, like someone talking. B roll is extra footage, like close ups and scenery, cut in to make the video more interesting.
what is the 180 degree rule | 180 rule film | crossing the line in film => The 180 degree rule says to keep the camera on one side of an imaginary line between two characters, so they stay on the same sides of the screen and viewers don't get confused.
what is a green screen | how does green screen work | chroma key => A green screen lets editors replace a solid green background with any image or video, using a technique called chroma keying. Light it evenly and don't wear green.
how do i make a stop motion with lego | lego stop motion | brickfilm tips => Use a tripod or fixed phone, steady lighting without daylight changes, move figures a tiny bit each frame, and shoot about 12 frames per second. Stick the baseplate down so it doesn't shift.
