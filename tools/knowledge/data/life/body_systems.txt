# tag: body.systems
what are the body systems | how many systems are in the human body | name the organ systems => The main body systems are the circulatory, respiratory, digestive, nervous, skeletal, muscular, immune, endocrine, urinary, integumentary (skin) and reproductive systems. They all work together.
what is the circulatory system | what does the circulatory system do => The circulatory system is your heart, blood and blood vessels. It pumps blood around your body to deliver oxygen and nutrients and carry away waste.
what is the respiratory system | what does the respiratory system do => The respiratory system is your nose, throat, windpipe and lungs. It brings oxygen into your body and gets rid of carbon dioxide.
what is the digestive system | what does the digestive system do => The digestive system breaks food down into nutrients your body can absorb. It includes your mouth, esophagus, stomach, intestines, liver and pancreas.
what is the nervous system | what does the nervous system do => The nervous system is your brain, spinal cord and nerves. It carries messages around your body so you can sense, think, move and react.
what is the skeletal system | what does the skeleton do => Your skeleton holds you up, protects organs like your brain and heart, lets you move with your muscles, stores minerals and makes blood cells in your bone marrow.
what is the muscular system | what do muscles do => The muscular system is all your muscles. They pull on bones to move you, keep your heart beating and push food through your gut.
what is the endocrine system | what are hormones => The endocrine system is a set of glands that release hormones, chemical messengers in your blood that control growth, energy, mood, sleep and puberty.
what is the urinary system | how does the body make pee => The urinary system is your kidneys, bladder and the tubes between them. Kidneys filter waste from your blood into pee, which is stored in the bladder.
what is the integumentary system | is skin an organ system => The integumentary system is your skin, hair and nails. It protects you from germs, helps control your temperature and lets you feel touch.
what is the lymphatic system | what are lymph nodes => The lymphatic system is a network of vessels and small bean-shaped lymph nodes that drains fluid from tissues and helps your immune system fight germs. Swollen nodes often mean you're fighting an infection.
what is the reproductive system | what does the reproductive system do => The reproductive system is the set of organs that lets people have babies when they are grown up. It also makes hormones that cause the changes of puberty.
how do body systems work together | do organs work together => Your systems team up all the time. For example, your lungs load oxygen into blood, your heart pumps it, and your muscles use it to move.
what is the biggest internal organ | what is the largest organ inside the body => The liver is the biggest organ inside your body, weighing about 1.5 kg in adults. Your skin is the biggest organ overall.
what organs can you live without | can you live without a kidney => You can live without your appendix, gallbladder, spleen, one kidney, one lung, tonsils and parts of your liver and intestines, though some of these make life harder.
how many organs are in the human body | how many organs do we have => There's no exact count, because experts define organs differently, but people usually say there are about 78 organs.
what is an organ | what is the difference between a tissue and an organ => Cells of the same kind group together to make tissues, like muscle tissue. Different tissues working together make an organ, like the heart.

# tag: body.organs
what does the small intestine do | how long is the small intestine => The small intestine is a twisty tube about 6 to 7 m long where most digestion and nutrient absorption happens. Its inside is covered in tiny fingers called villi.
what does the large intestine do | what does the colon do => The large intestine, or colon, soaks up water and salts from leftover food and turns it into poop. It's wider but shorter than the small intestine, about 1.5 m.
what does the pancreas do | where is your pancreas => The pancreas sits behind your stomach. It makes digestive juices for the small intestine and hormones like insulin that control blood sugar.
what is insulin | what does insulin do => Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas. It helps sugar from your blood get into your cells to be used for energy.
what does the gallbladder do | what is bile => The gallbladder is a small pouch under the liver that stores bile, a green-yellow liquid that helps break down fats in your food.
what does the spleen do | where is your spleen => The spleen is a fist-sized organ on your upper left side, under your ribs. It filters old red blood cells out of your blood and helps fight infections.
what does the appendix do | why do we have an appendix => The appendix is a small pouch attached to the large intestine. Scientists think it may store good gut bacteria. If it gets infected, it can cause appendicitis, which needs a doctor fast.
what is appendicitis | what are signs of appendicitis => Appendicitis is when the appendix gets swollen and infected. Signs include pain that starts near the belly button and moves to the lower right, fever and throwing up. It needs a doctor right away.
what does the esophagus do | how does food get to your stomach => The esophagus is the tube from your throat to your stomach, about 25 cm long. Its muscles squeeze food down in waves, so you could even swallow upside down.
what is the epiglottis | why does food sometimes go down the wrong pipe => The epiglottis is a flap that covers your windpipe when you swallow so food goes down your esophagus. If you talk or laugh while swallowing, food can slip the wrong way and make you cough.
what does the bladder do | how much pee can your bladder hold => The bladder is a stretchy bag that stores pee. An adult bladder can comfortably hold around 300 to 500 ml before you really need to go.
what does the thyroid do | where is the thyroid gland => The thyroid is a butterfly-shaped gland at the front of your neck. Its hormones control how fast your body uses energy, which affects growth, heart rate and temperature.
what do adrenal glands do | what is adrenaline => Adrenal glands sit on top of your kidneys. They make adrenaline, the hormone that speeds up your heart and breathing when you're scared or excited.
what is the pituitary gland | what is the master gland => The pituitary is a pea-sized gland under your brain. It's called the master gland because it controls many other glands and makes growth hormone.
what are tonsils | what do tonsils do => Tonsils are two lumps of tissue at the back of your throat. They help catch germs, and if they get infected you have tonsillitis, with a sore throat and fever.
what is the diaphragm | how does the diaphragm help you breathe => The diaphragm is a dome-shaped muscle under your lungs. When it tightens and flattens, it pulls air into your lungs, and when it relaxes, air goes out.
where is your liver | what side is the liver on => Your liver is on the upper right side of your belly, mostly under your ribs.
can your liver grow back | can the liver regenerate => Yes. The liver can regrow to nearly full size even after a big part is removed, which is why people can donate part of their liver.
where are your kidneys | how big are your kidneys => Your kidneys are on either side of your spine, just below your ribs toward your back. Each is about the size of a fist.
how much blood do the kidneys filter | how much does the kidney clean => Your kidneys filter about 180 liters of blood fluid a day, but most is returned to your blood and only about 1 to 2 liters becomes pee.
why is pee yellow | what color should pee be => Pee is yellow because of a pigment called urobilin, left from breaking down old red blood cells. Pale yellow usually means you're drinking enough, and dark yellow means drink more water.
why is poop brown | what makes poop brown => Poop is brown mostly because of bilirubin, a pigment from old red blood cells that gets changed by gut bacteria. Bile also adds color.
what is poop made of | what is in poop => Poop is mostly water, plus bacteria, undigested fiber, dead cells from your gut lining, fats and salts.
why do we fart | what causes gas => Farts are gas from swallowed air and from bacteria in your large intestine breaking down food. Beans, broccoli and fizzy drinks can make more gas. Everyone farts, often over 10 times a day.
why do farts smell | why do some farts stink => Most fart gas has no smell. The stink comes from tiny amounts of sulfur gases made by gut bacteria, especially from foods like eggs, meat and broccoli.
why does my stomach growl | what makes your tummy rumble => Stomach growling is the sound of muscles in your stomach and intestines squeezing gas and liquid along. It's louder when your stomach is empty.
why do we burp | what causes burping => Burping lets out air you swallowed while eating, drinking or talking, and gas from fizzy drinks.
what are gut bacteria | what is the microbiome => Your gut holds trillions of bacteria, called your gut microbiome. They help digest food, make some vitamins and help train your immune system.
what is stomach acid | how strong is stomach acid => Your stomach makes hydrochloric acid that's very strong, around pH 1 to 2. A thick layer of mucus protects the stomach wall from being digested.
how big is your stomach | how much food can your stomach hold => An empty adult stomach is about the size of a fist, but it stretches to hold about 1 to 1.5 liters of food comfortably.
what is digestion | how does digestion work => Digestion is breaking food into tiny pieces your body can absorb. It starts with chewing and saliva, continues with acid in the stomach and finishes in the small intestine.
what does saliva do | why does your mouth water => Saliva wets food so you can swallow it and starts breaking down starches. Your mouth makes about 1 to 1.5 liters a day, and it waters when you smell or think about food.
what are enzymes | what do digestive enzymes do => Enzymes are proteins that speed up chemical reactions. Digestive enzymes cut food into small pieces, like amylase for starch, protease for protein and lipase for fat.
what are nutrients | how does the body absorb food => Nutrients are the useful parts of food, like carbs, protein, fat, vitamins and minerals. They pass through the walls of your small intestine into your blood.

# tag: body.lungs
how many lungs do you have | why is the left lung smaller => You have two lungs. The left one is a bit smaller to make room for your heart.
how many breaths do you take a day | how often do we breathe => An adult at rest breathes about 12 to 20 times a minute, which adds up to roughly 20,000 breaths a day. Kids breathe a little faster.
how does breathing work | what happens when you breathe in => When you breathe in, your diaphragm pulls down and your ribs move out, so air rushes into your lungs. Oxygen passes into your blood and carbon dioxide passes out to be breathed away.
what are alveoli | what happens in the lungs => Alveoli are millions of tiny air sacs in your lungs, surrounded by blood vessels. That's where oxygen moves into your blood and carbon dioxide moves out.
what is the windpipe | what is the trachea => The trachea, or windpipe, is the tube that carries air from your throat to your lungs. It's held open by rings of cartilage you can feel at the front of your neck.
how long can you hold your breath | how long can a person hold their breath => Most people can hold their breath for about 30 seconds to 2 minutes. Never practice holding your breath underwater, because you can pass out without warning.
why do we breathe faster when we exercise | why do i get out of breath when running => Working muscles need more oxygen and make more carbon dioxide, so your brain makes you breathe faster and deeper to keep up.
why cant we breathe underwater | can humans breathe water => Our lungs can only take oxygen from air. Fish gills pull oxygen out of water, but our lungs can't, and water in the lungs stops them working.
what does smoking do to your lungs | why is smoking bad => Smoking damages the tiny air sacs and hairs that clean your lungs, causes lung disease and cancer, and harms your heart. Vaping also exposes lungs to harmful chemicals and addictive nicotine.
why is vaping bad | is vaping safe for kids => Vaping isn't safe for kids. Most vapes contain nicotine, which is very addictive and can harm the developing brain, plus chemicals that irritate the lungs.
what is asthma | what happens in an asthma attack => Asthma is a condition where the airways get swollen and narrow, making it hard to breathe, with coughing, wheezing and a tight chest. Inhalers from a doctor help open the airways.
what is the voice box | how do we talk => Your voice box, or larynx, has vocal cords that vibrate when air from your lungs passes through. Your tongue, lips and mouth shape the sounds into words.
what is an adams apple | why do boys get adams apples => The Adam's apple is the bump of cartilage at the front of the throat around the voice box. It grows bigger in most boys during puberty, which also deepens their voice.
why do we sneeze | how fast is a sneeze => Sneezing blasts irritants like dust or pollen out of your nose. The air can shoot out fast, and droplets can spread far, so sneeze into your elbow.
why do we cough | what is a cough for => Coughing is a reflex that clears mucus, dust or food out of your airways. A cough that lasts weeks or makes it hard to breathe needs a doctor.
what is mucus | why do we make snot => Mucus, or snot, is a sticky goo that traps dust, germs and pollen in your nose and airways. Your body makes a lot of it every day, and you swallow most without noticing.
why does snot change color | what does green snot mean => Snot can turn yellow or green when white blood cells are fighting germs. It doesn't always mean a bacterial infection, and most colds clear up on their own.
why does your nose run when its cold | why do i get a runny nose outside in winter => Cold air makes your nose produce extra moisture to warm and wet the air you breathe. Some of it condenses and drips out.

# tag: body.cells
what are the parts of a cell | what is inside a cell => Animal cells have a cell membrane around the outside, a nucleus holding DNA, mitochondria that make energy, and jelly-like cytoplasm filling the inside.
what is the nucleus of a cell | what does the nucleus do => The nucleus is the cell's control center. It holds your DNA, which has the instructions for running the cell.
what do mitochondria do | what is the powerhouse of the cell => Mitochondria are the powerhouse of the cell. They use oxygen and sugar to make energy the cell can use.
what is the cell membrane | what does the cell membrane do => The cell membrane is a thin, flexible layer around the cell that controls what goes in and out.
how many types of cells are in the body | what kinds of cells do humans have => Your body has around 200 main types of cells, like muscle cells, nerve cells, skin cells, fat cells and blood cells.
what is the biggest cell in the human body | what is the smallest cell => The egg cell is the largest human cell, about the size of a period on a page. The smallest include sperm cells and red blood cells. Some nerve cells are the longest, about 1 m.
how often does the body replace cells | do your cells regenerate => Different cells renew at different speeds. Skin cells last a few weeks, red blood cells about 4 months, and some brain and heart cells may last your whole life.
what are stem cells | what can stem cells do => Stem cells are special cells that can turn into other types of cells. They help the body grow and repair itself, and scientists study them to treat diseases.
what are genes | what does a gene do => Genes are sections of DNA that hold instructions for making proteins, which build and run your body. You have about 20,000 genes.
what are chromosomes | how many chromosomes do humans have => Chromosomes are tightly wound packages of DNA. Humans usually have 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, getting one of each pair from each parent.
why do kids look like their parents | how do we inherit traits => You get half your DNA from each parent, so you inherit a mix of their traits, like eye color and height. The mix is different for each child, except identical twins.
are identical twins exactly the same | do identical twins have the same dna => Identical twins come from one egg that split, so they start with almost the same DNA. Small differences and life experiences still make them unique, and their fingerprints differ.
what is a dominant gene | what is a recessive gene => A dominant gene shows its trait even if you have only one copy. A recessive gene only shows if you get a copy from both parents.
how long is your dna | how much dna is in one cell => If you stretched out the DNA from just one of your cells, it would be about 2 m long, tightly packed into a nucleus far too small to see.
how much dna do humans share with bananas | are humans 60 percent banana => People often say we share about 60 percent of our genes with bananas. It really means many basic genes that run cells are similar in both, not that we're part banana.
what is a dna test | how does a dna test work => A DNA test reads parts of your DNA from a cheek swab or spit. It can show family relationships, ancestry and some health risks.
