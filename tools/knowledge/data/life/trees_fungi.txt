# tag: plants.trees
how do you tell how old a tree is | how do tree rings work | what do tree rings tell you => Count the rings in a tree stump. Most trees grow one light and one dark ring each year, and wide rings mean good growing years while thin ones mean droughts or cold.
why do trees have bark | what is bark for => Bark protects a tree like skin. It keeps out insects, germs and fire and stops the tree from drying out.
why do leaves change color in the fall | why do leaves turn red and yellow | why do leaves turn orange in autumn => In fall, trees stop making green chlorophyll. As it fades, yellow and orange pigments that were hidden show through, and some trees make new red pigments.
why do trees lose their leaves | why do leaves fall off trees in autumn => Trees drop leaves to save water and energy in winter, when there's little light and the ground may be frozen. A layer of cells cuts each leaf off at the stem.
whats the difference between deciduous and evergreen trees | what is an evergreen tree => Deciduous trees, like maples and oaks, lose all their leaves each fall. Evergreens, like pines and spruces, keep leaves or needles all year and replace them a few at a time.
why do pine trees have needles | why dont pine trees lose their needles => Needles are thin leaves with a waxy coating that lose little water and shed snow easily, so pines can keep them through cold, dry winters.
how many trees are on earth | how many trees are there in the world => A 2015 study estimated there are about 3 trillion trees on Earth, roughly 400 for every person.
how do trees help the planet | why are trees important => Trees make oxygen, soak up carbon dioxide, clean the air, cool cities with shade, hold soil in place and give homes to countless animals.
how much water does a tree drink | how much water does a big tree use => A large tree can pull up hundreds of liters of water from the ground on a hot summer day, most of which evaporates from its leaves.
how long do trees live | which trees live the longest => It depends on the type. Many trees live 100 to 300 years, while bristlecone pines and some giant sequoias can live thousands of years.
what is an acorn | what tree do acorns come from => An acorn is the nut of an oak tree. Each acorn holds one seed that can grow into a new oak, and squirrels and jays bury many of them.
how long do oak trees live | how big do oak trees get => Many oaks live 200 to 300 years or more, and some have lived over 1,000. Big oaks can grow over 30 m tall.
what is a sequoia | whats the difference between a redwood and a sequoia => Coast redwoods are the tallest trees and grow along the foggy California coast. Giant sequoias, in the Sierra Nevada mountains, are shorter but much thicker and bulkier.
what is a baobab tree | why do baobab trees look upside down => Baobab trees from Africa and Madagascar have huge, thick trunks that store water, and bare branches that look like roots, so people call them upside-down trees.
what is a banyan tree | how does a banyan tree grow => A banyan is a fig tree that drops roots from its branches. They grow into new trunks, so one tree can spread into what looks like a whole forest.
are palm trees really trees | what kind of plant is a palm tree => Palms aren't true trees in the scientific sense. They're more closely related to grasses and have no growth rings or real bark.
what is a ginkgo tree | why is the ginkgo a living fossil => The ginkgo is an ancient tree whose family goes back over 200 million years, before many dinosaurs. It has fan-shaped leaves, and female trees drop very smelly seeds.
why do willow trees grow near water | what medicine came from willow trees => Willows love wet soil and grow along rivers. Their bark contains salicin, a chemical that led to the invention of aspirin.
what is a rainbow eucalyptus | which tree has a rainbow trunk => The rainbow eucalyptus has bark that peels off in strips, showing streaks of green, blue, purple, orange and red underneath.
why are cherry blossoms famous | what is hanami | when do cherry blossoms bloom => Cherry blossoms, called sakura in Japan, bloom in pink and white for just a week or two each spring. People in Japan hold picnics under them, a tradition called hanami.
can you grow an apple tree from a seed | why dont apple seeds make the same apple => You can, but the apples will be different from the parent, often sour. That's why apple trees are grown by grafting a branch of a good variety onto another tree.
what is grafting | how do you graft a tree => Grafting joins a cut branch from one plant onto the roots and trunk of another so they grow together. It's how most fruit trees are grown.
what kind of tree is a christmas tree | what trees are used for christmas trees => Popular Christmas trees include firs like the Fraser fir and noble fir, spruces and pines. Most are grown on farms for about 7 to 10 years.
what is a rainforest | why are rainforests important => Rainforests are warm, wet forests with huge numbers of plants and animals. They cover a small part of Earth but are home to around half of all land species.
where is the amazon rainforest | which countries is the amazon in | how much of the amazon is in brazil => The Amazon is the biggest rainforest on Earth, about 5.5 million square kilometres, mostly in Brazil and spreading into eight other countries.
what is deforestation | why are forests being cut down => Deforestation is clearing forests for farms, cattle ranches, logging, mining and roads. It destroys animal homes and adds carbon dioxide to the air.
what are the layers of a rainforest | what is the rainforest canopy => A rainforest has four layers: the forest floor, the understory, the thick canopy where most animals live, and the emergent layer of the tallest trees poking out above.
what is a boreal forest | what is the taiga => The boreal forest, or taiga, is a huge band of cold evergreen forest across Canada, Alaska, Scandinavia and Russia. It's the biggest land biome on Earth.
what is a mangrove | how do mangroves grow in salt water => Mangroves are trees that grow in salty water along tropical coasts. Their tangled roots stick up out of the mud to breathe and protect coasts from storms.
how do trees survive winter | why dont trees freeze => Trees go dormant in winter, stopping growth. Their cells move water out so ice forms between cells instead of inside, and sugars act like antifreeze.
can trees get sick | what is dutch elm disease => Yes. Trees get diseases from fungi, bacteria and insects. Dutch elm disease, spread by beetles, killed millions of elm trees in Europe and North America.
what is the national tree of the usa | what is americas national tree => The oak was named the national tree of the United States in 2004.
what is petrified wood | how does wood turn to stone => Petrified wood forms when a buried tree's wood is slowly replaced by minerals like quartz over millions of years, turning it into stone while keeping its shape.

# tag: fungi
what is a fungus | what are fungi => Fungi are living things like mushrooms, molds and yeast. They aren't plants or animals. They feed by breaking down other living or dead things.
what is a mushroom | is a mushroom the whole fungus => A mushroom is just the fruiting part of a fungus, like an apple on a tree. Most of the fungus is a hidden web of threads called mycelium in the soil or wood.
how do mushrooms reproduce | what are spores => Mushrooms release millions of tiny spores, like seeds, from their gills or pores. Wind, water and animals carry the spores to new places where they grow.
is it safe to eat wild mushrooms | can i eat mushrooms i find outside => No. Never eat wild mushrooms unless an expert has identified them. Some deadly ones, like the death cap, look like edible ones, and eating them can kill.
what is the most poisonous mushroom | what is a death cap => The death cap is the deadliest mushroom and causes most mushroom poisoning deaths. Even a small piece can badly damage the liver.
what is the biggest living thing on earth | what is the humongous fungus => A honey fungus in Oregon's Blue Mountains spreads underground over about 9 square kilometres and may be thousands of years old. It's one of the biggest living things on Earth.
what is mycelium | what does mycelium do => Mycelium is the network of thin threads that makes up most of a fungus. It spreads through soil, breaks down dead stuff and links up with plant roots.
how do fungi help plants | what is the wood wide web => Many fungi grow on plant roots and trade water and nutrients for sugar from the plant. These underground networks are nicknamed the wood wide web.
why are fungi important | what would happen without fungi => Fungi break down dead plants and animals and recycle their nutrients into the soil. Without them, the world would pile up with dead stuff.
what is mold | why does food get moldy => Mold is a fungus that grows from tiny spores floating in the air. It loves damp food, and the fuzzy spots are its spore-making parts.
can you cut mold off bread | is moldy bread safe if you cut it off => No. Throw out moldy bread and soft foods, because mold threads spread deep inside where you can't see them. Hard cheese is different, since mold can be cut off with a margin.
what is lichen | is lichen a plant => Lichen isn't a plant. It's a fungus and an alga, or bacteria, living together as a team. The fungus gives shelter, and the partner makes food from sunlight.
do mushrooms glow in the dark | what is foxfire => Yes. Around 100 kinds of fungi glow with a soft green light called bioluminescence. Glowing rotting wood is sometimes called foxfire.
what is the zombie ant fungus | can a fungus control ants => A fungus called Ophiocordyceps infects certain ants and takes over their behavior, making them climb a plant and bite down before it kills them and sprouts from their head.
what are truffles | why are truffles so expensive => Truffles are fungi that grow underground near tree roots. They're rare, hard to find and must be sniffed out by trained dogs or pigs, so they cost a lot.
what is penicillin made from | which medicine comes from mold => Penicillin, the first antibiotic, comes from a Penicillium mold. Alexander Fleming noticed it killing bacteria in 1928.
are fungi more like plants or animals | are mushrooms related to animals => Surprisingly, fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants. Like animals, they can't make their own food and must take it in from other things.
how many kinds of fungi are there | how many species of mushrooms are there => Scientists have named about 150,000 kinds of fungi, but they estimate there could be a few million species in total.
what is a puffball | why do puffballs puff => Puffballs are round fungi that release a cloud of spores like smoke when something presses on them.
what is a fairy ring | why do mushrooms grow in circles => A fairy ring is a circle of mushrooms. It happens when a fungus spreads outward underground from a center, and mushrooms pop up at its growing edge.
