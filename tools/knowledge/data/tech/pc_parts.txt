# tag: tech.parts.overview
what parts do you need to build a pc | list of pc parts | what goes in a gaming pc => A PC needs a CPU, a CPU cooler, a motherboard, RAM, storage (an SSD), a graphics card for gaming, a power supply and a case. You also need Windows, a monitor, keyboard and mouse.
what does each pc part do | explain pc parts simply => The CPU thinks, the GPU draws the graphics, RAM is short-term memory, the SSD stores files, the motherboard connects everything, the power supply feeds power, and the cooler and fans keep it all cool.
what part matters most for gaming | most important part for fps => The graphics card matters most for gaming FPS, then the CPU. Spend the biggest share of a gaming budget on the GPU, while keeping a decent CPU so it doesn't hold the GPU back.
is it cheaper to build a pc or buy one | build vs prebuilt => Building is often a bit cheaper for the same parts and lets you pick quality parts like the power supply. Prebuilts can be good deals during sales and come with a warranty for the whole system.
# tag: tech.parts.cpu
what do cpu cores and threads mean | cores vs threads => Cores are separate workers inside the CPU. Threads are the tasks it can juggle; with SMT or Hyper-Threading, each core runs two threads. More cores help with video editing and multitasking.
how many cores do i need for gaming | is 6 cores enough for gaming => As of 2025, 6 to 8 modern cores is great for gaming. More cores mostly help with rendering, video editing and streaming at the same time.
what does ghz mean on a cpu | what is clock speed => GHz is how many billion cycles per second the CPU runs. Higher is faster, but newer CPUs do more per cycle, so you can't compare different generations by GHz alone.
what is cpu cache | what is l3 cache => Cache is tiny, super-fast memory inside the CPU that holds data it needs soon. More L3 cache helps a lot in games, which is why X3D chips are fast.
what is x3d | what is 3d v cache | why is the 7800x3d good for gaming => X3D Ryzen CPUs have extra cache stacked on top of the chip (3D V-Cache). Games love that cache, so chips like the 7800X3D and 9800X3D are among the fastest gaming CPUs as of 2025.
what do amd ryzen names mean | ryzen 5 vs ryzen 7 => In a name like Ryzen 7 9700X, the 7 is the tier (3, 5, 7, 9 from low to high), 9 is the generation, 700 is the model, and X means higher clocks. X3D adds extra gaming cache.
what do intel cpu names mean | i5 vs i7 => In older names like Core i5-14600K, i5 is the tier, 14 is the generation and K means unlocked for overclocking. Newer Core Ultra chips use names like Core Ultra 7 265K.
what does the k mean on intel cpus | k vs non k => K means the CPU is unlocked for overclocking and usually clocks higher. F means it has no built-in graphics, so you need a graphics card. KF means both.
what is a cpu socket | what is am5 => The socket is where the CPU sits on the motherboard. AM5 is AMD's socket for Ryzen 7000, 8000 and 9000 desktop CPUs, and it only uses DDR5 RAM. CPU and board sockets must match.
what cpus work on am5 | can i put a ryzen 5000 on am5 => AM5 takes Ryzen 7000, 8000G and 9000 series desktop chips. Ryzen 5000 and older use AM4 and don't fit AM5 boards.
will future cpus fit my am5 board | how long will am5 last => AMD said it plans to support AM5 into at least 2027, so newer Ryzen chips should drop in with a BIOS update. Check your board's CPU support list before upgrading.
what is am4 | am4 vs am5 => AM4 is AMD's older socket for Ryzen 1000 to 5000 chips and DDR4 RAM. AM5 is newer, uses DDR5 and supports PCIe 5.0 on some boards.
what intel socket is lga1700 | lga1700 vs lga1851 => LGA1700 fits Intel 12th, 13th and 14th gen Core CPUs. LGA1851 is for the newer Core Ultra 200S chips. They aren't swappable.
do i need a graphics card if my cpu has graphics | integrated graphics gaming => Built-in graphics can run light games like Minecraft or Roblox at low settings. For most modern games you need a graphics card. Most Ryzen 7000 and 9000 chips have basic graphics for display output.
what are apus | ryzen 8700g => APUs are CPUs with stronger built-in graphics, like the Ryzen 8000G chips. They can play light games without a graphics card but are much slower than a real GPU.
what is overclocking | is overclocking safe => Overclocking runs parts faster than stock settings. It can add a little speed but makes heat and crashes more likely. Modern CPUs already boost themselves, so most gamers don't need it.
what is precision boost overdrive | what is pbo => PBO lets AMD Ryzen CPUs boost higher when cooling and power allow. It's in the BIOS. With Curve Optimizer it can also lower voltage, but test for stability.
what is tdp | cpu watts meaning => TDP is roughly how much heat the cooler must handle, in watts. Real power use can be higher when the chip boosts, so pick a cooler with some headroom.
why are intel 13th and 14th gen chips crashing | intel instability problem => Some 13th and 14th gen Intel CPUs had a voltage problem that caused crashes and damage. Intel released microcode fixes in BIOS updates in 2024, so keep those boards updated.
how do i install a cpu | putting cpu in motherboard => Lift the socket lever, line up the gold triangle on the CPU with the triangle on the socket, lower it in gently with no force, then close the lever. Never touch the pins or pads.
# tag: tech.parts.gpu
what is vram | how much vram do i need => VRAM is the graphics card's own memory for textures and frames. As of 2025, 8 GB is the minimum for 1080p, 12 to 16 GB is better for 1440p and new games.
what does rtx mean | rtx vs gtx => RTX is NVIDIA's name for cards with ray tracing and Tensor cores for DLSS. Older GTX cards lack them.
what is ray tracing | is ray tracing worth it => Ray tracing simulates how light bounces for realistic shadows, reflections and lighting. It looks great but costs a lot of FPS, so it's often paired with DLSS or FSR.
what is dlss | how does dlss work => DLSS is NVIDIA's AI upscaling. The game renders at a lower resolution and AI builds a sharp higher resolution image, giving more FPS. It needs an RTX card.
what is fsr | amd fsr => FSR is AMD's upscaling tech. It renders at a lower resolution and upscales for more FPS. Older FSR versions work on most GPUs; FSR 4 uses AI and needs Radeon RX 9000 cards as of 2025.
what is xess | intel xess upscaling => XeSS is Intel's upscaling tech. It works best on Intel Arc cards and also runs on many others, giving more FPS by upscaling.
what is frame generation | is frame gen real fps => Frame generation uses AI to create extra frames between real ones, so motion looks smoother. It doesn't lower input lag like real FPS, and it works best when you already have around 60 FPS or more.
what is nvidia reflex | should i turn on reflex => Reflex cuts system latency by keeping the CPU and GPU in sync. Turn it on (On or On + Boost) in games that have it, especially shooters.
what is amd anti lag | anti lag 2 => Anti-Lag is AMD's feature to lower input latency, like NVIDIA Reflex. Turn it on in AMD Software or in games that support Anti-Lag 2.
nvidia vs amd graphics card | should i get nvidia or amd => Both make great cards. NVIDIA leads in ray tracing, DLSS and streaming features. AMD often gives more VRAM and raster performance for the money. Compare reviews for cards in your budget.
what do nvidia gpu numbers mean | rtx 5070 vs 5060 => In RTX 5070, 50 is the generation and 70 is the tier. Higher tiers like 80 and 90 are faster. Ti and Super versions are a step up from the plain card.
what do amd gpu numbers mean | rx 9070 xt meaning => In RX 9070 XT, 9 is the generation, 070 the tier, and XT is a faster version. Higher tier numbers are faster.
what is intel arc | are intel gpus good => Intel Arc cards, like the B580, are budget graphics cards with good value at 1080p and 1440p as of 2025. Turn on Resizable BAR for them to run well.
how do i install a graphics card | put gpu in pc => Remove the slot covers on the case, push the card into the top long PCIe x16 slot until it clicks, screw it to the case, and plug in its power cables from the PSU.
what is the 12vhpwr connector | 12v 2x6 cable => It's the 16 pin power plug on many high-end GPUs. Push it in fully until it clicks and avoid sharp bends right at the plug, since a loose connection can overheat.
what is gpu sag | do i need a gpu support bracket => Heavy graphics cards can bend down over time. A support bracket or stand under the far end keeps it straight and protects the slot.
how much power does my graphics card need | psu for my gpu => Check the card maker's recommended power supply size on its spec page. Use a quality PSU at that size or a bit bigger.
what is pcie | pcie 4 vs 5 for gpu => PCIe is the fast slot connection for graphics cards and SSDs. Newer versions double the speed, but most GPUs barely lose any FPS on PCIe 4.0 vs 5.0.
what is a founders edition card | nvidia fe gpu => It's NVIDIA's own version of its graphics cards. Other brands like ASUS, MSI and Gigabyte make their own versions with different coolers and clocks.
# tag: tech.parts.ram
how much ram do i need for gaming | is 16gb ram enough => As of 2025, 16 GB works for most games, and 32 GB is the comfy choice for gaming with Discord, a browser and streaming or editing at the same time.
ddr4 vs ddr5 | what is ddr5 => DDR5 is newer and faster than DDR4. They don't fit the same slots, so your motherboard decides which one you use. AM5 boards use DDR5 only.
what ram speed is best for am5 | ddr5 6000 cl30 => DDR5-6000 with CL30 is the common sweet spot for Ryzen 7000 and 9000 CPUs. Faster kits often bring little gain and can be harder to run stable.
what does cl mean on ram | what is cas latency => CL (CAS latency) is how many clock cycles the RAM waits before answering. Lower is better at the same speed, so DDR5-6000 CL30 beats DDR5-6000 CL36.
what is dual channel ram | should i get 2 sticks of ram => Two matched sticks in the right slots run in dual channel, doubling memory bandwidth. Use 2 sticks instead of 1 for better gaming performance.
which slots do i put ram in | 2 sticks of ram which slots => With two sticks, most boards use the second and fourth slots from the CPU, often labeled A2 and B2. Check your motherboard manual to be sure.
can i mix different ram sticks | mixing ram brands => It can work, but mismatched kits often won't run at their rated speed or stay stable. Buying one matched kit is safer.
is 4 sticks of ram worse than 2 | 4 dimms ddr5 => On DDR5 boards, 4 sticks usually must run at lower speeds than 2. If you need lots of RAM, two bigger sticks (like 2 x 32 GB) are better.
what is xmp | what is expo => XMP (Intel) and EXPO (AMD) are saved speed profiles on the RAM. Without turning one on in the BIOS, the RAM runs at a slower default speed.
how do i install ram | putting ram in => Open the slot clips, line up the notch in the stick with the bump in the slot, and press both ends down firmly until the clips snap. It takes more force than people expect.
what is ecc ram | do i need ecc memory => ECC RAM can fix small memory errors, which matters for servers. Gaming PCs don't need it, and many consumer boards don't fully support it.
what is ram vs storage | ram vs ssd => RAM is fast short-term memory that's cleared when the PC turns off. Storage (SSD or hard drive) keeps files and games even when it's off.
# tag: tech.parts.storage
what is an nvme ssd | nvme vs sata ssd => An NVMe SSD is a small stick that plugs into an M.2 slot and talks over PCIe, so it's much faster than a SATA SSD. Both are far faster than a hard drive.
what is an m.2 slot | m2 ssd => M.2 is a small slot on the motherboard for stick-shaped SSDs. Most use the 2280 size (22 mm wide, 80 mm long). Check whether the slot supports NVMe or only SATA.
how much storage do i need for gaming | is 1tb enough => As of 2025, many big games take 100 GB or more, so 1 TB fills quickly. 2 TB is a comfortable choice for a gaming PC.
pcie 4 vs pcie 5 ssd | is a gen 5 ssd worth it => Gen 5 SSDs are faster on paper but run hotter and cost more. For games, a good Gen 4 NVMe loads almost as fast, so it's usually the better value.
what is a hard drive | hdd vs ssd => A hard drive (HDD) stores data on spinning disks, so it's cheap per terabyte but slow. Use an SSD for Windows and games and an HDD for backups and big video files.
what is a dram cache on an ssd | dramless ssd => Some SSDs have a small DRAM chip that helps them keep speed during heavy use. DRAM-less drives are cheaper and fine for most gamers, especially with HMB support.
what is tbw on an ssd | how long does an ssd last => TBW (terabytes written) is how much data the maker says you can write before wear-out. Normal gaming use takes many years to reach it.
do i need an m.2 heatsink | ssd heatsink => Fast Gen 4 and Gen 5 drives can get hot. Most motherboards include an M.2 heatsink, so use it if there's one.
how do i install an m.2 ssd | put nvme in motherboard => Remove the M.2 heatsink or cover, slide the SSD into the slot at an angle, press it down, and fix it with the screw or latch. Peel the plastic off any thermal pad first.
what is directstorage | directstorage games => DirectStorage is a Windows feature that lets games load data from fast NVMe SSDs more efficiently. Only some games use it.
how do i clone my old drive to a new ssd | move windows to new ssd => Use a cloning tool, often free from the SSD's maker, to copy everything to the new drive. Then set the new drive first in the BIOS boot order.
# tag: tech.parts.psu
what does a power supply do | what is a psu => The power supply (PSU) turns wall power into the steady low voltages your PC parts use. A good one protects your parts if something goes wrong.
how many watts do i need | what size power supply do i need => Add up the CPU and GPU power, then pick a quality PSU with room to spare. As of 2025, a midrange gaming PC usually does well with 650 to 850 watts. Use a PSU calculator to check.
what is 80 plus | 80 plus gold meaning => 80 Plus ratings (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Titanium) show how efficient a PSU is at turning wall power into usable power. Gold wastes less as heat than Bronze, but rating isn't the same as build quality.
what is a modular power supply | modular vs non modular => On a modular PSU, you plug in only the cables you need, which makes building neater. Semi-modular has the main cables attached and the rest removable.
can i use cables from another power supply | mixing modular cables => No. Modular cables are often wired differently between brands and even models, and the wrong one can destroy your parts. Only use the cables that came with your PSU.
what is atx 3.1 | atx 3.0 psu => ATX 3.0 and 3.1 are newer power supply standards that handle the quick power spikes of modern GPUs and include the 12V-2x6 GPU cable.
what are the cables from the power supply | 24 pin and 8 pin cpu => The 24 pin powers the motherboard, the 8 pin (EPS) powers the CPU, PCIe or 12V-2x6 cables power the GPU, and SATA cables power drives and some fans or lights.
is a cheap power supply bad | dont cheap out on psu => A low quality PSU can be unstable, noisy, or fail and take parts with it. Buy from a known brand and check reviews, since it powers every other part.
which way should the psu fan face | psu fan up or down => If the case has a vent under the PSU, face its fan down so it pulls in cool air from outside. Keep that dust filter clean.
# tag: tech.parts.motherboard
why does the motherboard matter | does a better motherboard give more fps => The motherboard connects the CPU, RAM, storage, GPU and everything else. It decides which CPUs and RAM work, how many drives and USB ports you get, and features like Wi-Fi.
what are motherboard sizes | atx vs micro atx vs mini itx => ATX is the standard size with the most slots. Micro-ATX is shorter with fewer slots. Mini-ITX is tiny with one PCIe slot and two RAM slots, for small builds.
what is a chipset | b650 vs x670 => The chipset decides a board's features like how many USB ports, drives and PCIe lanes it has. On AM5, B650 and B850 are great for gaming, while X670 and X870 add more connections.
what does the e mean in b650e | b650 vs b650e => The E boards (B650E, X670E, X870E) have PCIe 5.0 for the graphics card slot as well as an M.2 slot. Regular versions give PCIe 5.0 to at least one M.2 slot instead.
what are vrms on a motherboard | do vrms matter => VRMs turn power into the exact voltage the CPU needs. Strong VRMs with good heatsinks matter for high core count CPUs; most B650 boards handle a gaming CPU fine.
does my motherboard have wifi | wifi motherboard => Boards with Wi-Fi often have WIFI in the name and come with antennas that screw into the back. Without it, use Ethernet or a PCIe or USB Wi-Fi adapter.
what are the front panel connectors | power switch pins => They're the small pins at the bottom edge of the board for the case's power button, reset button and lights. The manual shows which pin goes where.
what is a usb header | front usb not working => Headers are pin plugs on the motherboard for the case's front USB ports and fans. Plug the case's USB 3 and USB-C cables into the matching headers.
what is a pcie slot | what goes in pcie slots => PCIe slots hold expansion cards. The top long x16 slot is for the graphics card; smaller ones take Wi-Fi, capture or sound cards.
what is the cmos battery | motherboard coin battery => It's a small coin cell (usually CR2032) that keeps the BIOS clock and settings when the PC is unplugged. If the time keeps resetting, replace it.
what are motherboard standoffs | do i need standoffs => Standoffs are small metal posts that hold the motherboard off the case so it doesn't short. Most cases come with them pre-installed; only use ones that line up with board holes.
# tag: tech.parts.cooling
air cooler vs aio | do i need liquid cooling => A good air cooler is quiet, cheap and can't leak. An AIO liquid cooler can look cleaner and handle hotter CPUs. For most gaming CPUs, a solid dual tower or single tower air cooler is enough.
what is an aio cooler | how do aio coolers work => An AIO (all in one) is a sealed liquid cooler. A pump moves liquid from a block on the CPU to a radiator with fans that blow the heat away.
where should the aio radiator go | aio pump at the top bad => Mount the radiator at the top or front of the case. Keep the pump lower than the top of the radiator so air bubbles collect in the radiator, not the pump.
how much thermal paste should i use | how do i apply thermal paste => Put a pea-sized dot (or a small X) in the middle of the CPU, then press the cooler straight down. The pressure spreads it. Too much makes a mess but rarely hurts temps much.
do i need to add thermal paste | cooler already has paste => Many coolers come with paste already on the base. If it's there, peel the plastic cover off and don't add more.
how often should i replace thermal paste | thermal paste dried out => Good paste lasts many years. Replace it if temperatures creep up a lot, or any time you take the cooler off.
how do i clean old thermal paste off | remove thermal paste => Wipe it off with a coffee filter or lint-free cloth and isopropyl alcohol (90 percent or higher) until the metal is shiny, then let it dry.
what is the best fan setup for a pc | case airflow setup => Pull cool air in at the front and bottom, and push hot air out at the back and top. Slightly more intake than exhaust (positive pressure) keeps dust out better.
which way does air flow through a fan | fan direction arrow => Air flows out of the side with the fan's frame and arms (the struts). Many fans have small arrows on the side showing airflow and spin direction.
120mm vs 140mm fans | what size fans => Bigger fans move the same air at lower speed, so 140 mm fans are often quieter. Use whatever sizes your case and radiator support.
what is a fan hub | too many fans not enough headers => A fan hub plugs into one motherboard header and powers several fans. Many come with RGB control too.
what is pwm vs dc fan | 3 pin vs 4 pin fan => PWM fans have 4 pin plugs and change speed smoothly with a control signal. DC fans have 3 pins and change speed by voltage. Both work; set the header mode to match in the BIOS.
do rgb lights make my pc faster | is rgb worth it => No, RGB only changes looks. It does add cost and software, so get it if you like it.
how do i sync my rgb | control rgb lights => Use your motherboard maker's app or a free tool like OpenRGB or SignalRGB. Plug ARGB devices into the 3 pin 5V ARGB headers, not the 4 pin 12V RGB ones.
what is the difference between rgb and argb headers | 12v vs 5v rgb => ARGB (addressable) uses 3 pin 5V headers and lets each LED change color separately. Plain RGB uses 4 pin 12V headers. Plugging ARGB into 12V can burn out the lights.
what is a liquid metal | should i use liquid metal => Liquid metal is a super-conductive thermal compound that can lower temps, but it conducts electricity and can damage parts if it spills. Regular paste is the safe choice.
# tag: tech.parts.case
how do i choose a pc case | what to look for in a case => Check that your motherboard size, GPU length, cooler height and radiator sizes fit. Pick one with mesh front panels for good airflow and dust filters.
what is a mesh front case | mesh vs glass front => A mesh front lets fans pull in plenty of air, keeping parts cooler. Solid glass fronts look sleek but often run hotter.
what is cable management | how do i hide cables => It's routing cables behind the motherboard tray and tying them with zip or velcro ties. It looks cleaner and makes the PC easier to work on.
what are dust filters | should i clean dust filters => Dust filters are mesh screens over the fans that catch dust. Rinse or vacuum them every month or two so airflow stays strong.
how big a gpu fits my case | gpu clearance => Check the case's maximum GPU length in its specs and compare with the card's length. Front fans or radiators can take some of that space.
